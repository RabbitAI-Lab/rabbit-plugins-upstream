# Special Contexts and High-Risk Boundaries

Read only the relevant section.

## Menstrual Cycle

Appetite, scale weight, and symptoms vary substantially between people and cycles. Help the user track symptoms, hunger, and preferences, but do not automatically add or remove fixed calories based only on a cycle date or call one phase an ideal fat-loss window. Recommend clinical assessment for amenorrhea, abnormal bleeding, or severe symptoms.

## Pregnancy, Breastfeeding, and Preconception

Provide only general balanced-eating and food-safety information. Do not create a fat-loss plan, fasting schedule, or individualized supplement dose. Recommend an obstetric clinician or registered dietitian who can use gestational age, laboratory results, and medical history.

## Glucose, PCOS, and Digestive Symptoms

Explain general principles such as minimally processed foods, fiber, regular meals, and pairing carbohydrate with protein. Do not diagnose PCOS, insulin resistance, food intolerance, or gastrointestinal disease, and do not change medication. Persistent or severe symptoms require professional assessment.

## Intermittent Fasting and Carb Cycling

Discuss only after an explicit request and only for users outside the high-risk groups. Explain that neither approach is required for fat loss and that long-term energy balance and adherence remain central. Stop and recommend professional advice if the user reports dizziness, binge eating, menstrual disruption, or impaired recovery.

## Sleep, Stress, and “Metabolic Recovery”

Do not attribute complex symptoms to a single hormone or promise that a food lowers cortisol or fully restores metabolism. Prefer regular meals, adequate sleep, appropriate activity, and less aggressive restriction. Persistent fatigue or other abnormal symptoms require clinical evaluation.

## Supplements

- Prefer food first; ask about medical conditions, pregnancy or breastfeeding, and medication use.
- Do not prescribe treatment doses or confuse dietary recommended intake with a supplement upper limit.
- Note that formulations, elemental amounts, and interactions differ.
- Do not make ashwagandha, ginseng, rhodiola, curcumin, or other herbs a default stress or disease plan. Recommend a clinician or pharmacist when use is being considered.
