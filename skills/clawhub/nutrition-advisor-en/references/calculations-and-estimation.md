# Calculations and Estimation

Read this file only when the task needs numerical estimates.

## Resting and Total Energy

For generally healthy adults, the Mifflin–St Jeor equations can provide an initial BMR estimate:

```text
Male: BMR = 10 × weight_kg + 6.25 × height_cm - 5 × age + 5
Female: BMR = 10 × weight_kg + 6.25 × height_cm - 5 × age - 161
```

Use `TDEE = BMR × activity factor` only as a starting estimate:

| Activity description | Initial factor |
| --- | ---: |
| Mostly sedentary | 1.2 |
| Light activity 1–3 times/week | 1.35–1.4 |
| Moderate activity 3–5 times/week | 1.5–1.6 |
| Frequent training or physical work | 1.7–1.9 |

Activity factors are coarse. Adjust from two to four weeks of intake, weight trend, hunger, and training performance rather than treating one calculation as ground truth.

## Intake Targets

- Maintenance: begin near estimated TDEE.
- Fat loss: begin with a modest deficit; do not recommend aggressive restriction when information is incomplete.
- Muscle gain: begin with a small surplus and adjust using strength and weight trends.
- A single-food or single-meal question does not require a complete body profile.

Do not substitute fixed sex-based calorie floors for individual assessment. If a result is unusually low or the user reports persistent fatigue, feeling cold, menstrual disruption, or declining training performance, stop reducing intake and recommend professional assessment.

## Protein and Macronutrients

Give ranges with assumptions. Do not claim that every user needs one universal per-meal protein threshold, and do not apply invented “effective protein” multipliers to plant foods.

For users without relevant medical restrictions, set an initial range from goal, body weight, training, and dietary preferences, then allocate remaining energy to carbohydrate and fat. Do not set individualized protein targets for kidney disease, pregnancy, or another clinical condition.

## Food and Restaurant Estimates

Use sources in this order:

1. a user-supplied package label or official restaurant nutrition listing;
2. weighed ingredients and a known cooking method;
3. a reputable portion database;
4. a range based on a photo or description.

Do not use cuisine-wide calorie multipliers. Estimate staple food, protein food, vegetables, cooking oil, sauces, and drinks separately. State that restaurant estimates commonly have about 20% or more uncertainty depending on portion and oil.
