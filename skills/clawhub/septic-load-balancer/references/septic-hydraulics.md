# Septic Load Balancer — Reference

## 1. Why hydraulic spacing works

The septic tank is anaerobic digestion + settling: it needs **retention time** (typically 24–36 h at design flow) to let solids settle and scum float. The drainfield is a fixed-area biological filter: soil bacteria aerobically digest the effluent, at a rate fixed by soil type and mature bio-mat.

Two distinct failure flows:

- **Daily total over field capacity, sustained**: the soil stays saturated, anaerobic conditions spread, the bio-mat thickens faster than it degrades. This is progressive, permanent damage.
- **Hourly slugs**: big single-hour volumes stir the tank, pushing suspended solids out to the field — the #1 accelerant of bio-mat clogging from *inside the tank*.

Hence the two caps this tool computes: daily budget and hourly surge cap.

## 2. Sizing numbers

| Quantity | Typical value |
|---|---|
| Design flow | ~150 gal/bedroom/day (state code classic) |
| Tank working volume | ~80% of tank total (rest = scum/sludge storage) |
| Conservative sustained daily flow | ≤ working volume ÷ 3 |
| Single-hour slug cap | ≤ 15% of working volume |
| Top-load washer | 30–50 gal/load |
| Front-load (HE) washer | 12–20 gal/load |
| Dishwasher | 4–10 gal/run |
| Shower | 2.1 gpm (older heads 2.5–3.0) |
| Bath | 30–40 gal |
| Toilet | 1.28–1.6 (post-1994) vs 3.5+ (older) |
| Water softener regen | 20–100+ gal event |

## 3. Symptom triage: overload vs clog vs pump failure

| Pattern | Diagnosis | Action |
|---|---|---|
| All fixtures slow together, worse evenings, lawn soggy/dark over field, odor | **Hydraulic overload / failing field** | cut + space water use; professional inspection |
| One fixture slow, others fine | local clog | plumbing fix, not septic |
| Gurgling at the lowest drain when others drain | partial line/tee blockage | service call |
| Sudden total stoppage, alarm on (aerobic/pump systems) | pump/float failure | service call now |
| Sewage backup at floor drain | system at capacity/failure | septic pro NOW; stop all water use |
| Bright green strip over field lines, even in drought | field receiving concentrated flow (normal-ish) but check for surface wetness | inspect |

## 4. The weekly schedule shape that works

- **Laundry**: ≤1 top-load load/day or ≤2 front-load loads/day, never 6 on Sunday. Back-to-back loads get a ≥1 h gap when possible.
- **Dishwasher**: once daily, after dinner (single 8-gal event is fine; it's the stacking that kills).
- **Showers**: household staggered across morning/evening; guests spaced 30 min.
- **Softener**: regenerate on demand-count (not calendar), timed for a low-use morning, never laundry day.
- **Big one-off events** (hot tub drain, pool backwash, carpet cleaner waste): **never** to septic — bypass or haul.

## 5. What kills fields early (ranked)

1. Sustained over-budget flow (leaky toilets are the classic silent driver — a stuck flapper wastes 200+ gal/day straight through the field).
2. Solids carryover from missed pumping (pump on measured sludge depth, ~2–5 yr cadence; garbage disposal users: halve it).
3. Grease and non-biodegradables ("flushable" wipes are not).
4. Driving/parking on the field (soil compaction crushes absorption).
5. Tree roots over field lines (willows, poplars).
6. Water treatment backwash routed to septic.

## 6. Guest-day playbook

1. Before guests arrive: pump-check date known, no laundry that day, dishwasher skipped or run late.
2. Showers spaced; bath vs shower guidance (a bath ≈ 2 showers).
3. Next 2 days: reduced laundry, let the field catch down.
4. If slow drains appear: full water stop 2–4 h usually restores drainage if it's pure overload — that restoration IS the confirmation of the diagnosis.

## 7. Additives

Commercial septic "starters"/"boosters" are generally unnecessary (enough bacteria arrive with waste); some solvent-based cleaners can damage soil structure. Save the money for pumping.
