# Kids Allowance Ledger — Reference

## The Problem

Most allowance systems fail in one of three ways: money arrives and disappears
with no structure (no jars), the structure is pure accounting with no growth
(no interest/match, so saving looks pointless at $2/week scale), or the parent
improvises and the rules change weekly (kid stops trusting the system).
Financial-literacy research (CFPB, Jump$tart, and the "Money as You Grow" /
Kobliner frameworks) consistently points at three active ingredients:
**partitioning** (visual split the moment money arrives), **visible growth**
(compound interest the child can watch beat spending), and **goal friction**
(a real wishlist with real ETAs). This skill operationalizes all three.

## Allowance sizing heuristics

Common US heuristic (Kobliner / Pocketsmith survey norms): **$1 per year of age
per week** — an 8-year-old gets $8. Conservative start: age/2. Teen premium:
1.5–2× age as spending responsibilities shift from parent to child (clothes,
phone, outings). Raise on every birthday, and say why: "you're a year more
capable, so you manage a year more money."

## Jars: why three, and what each teaches

| Jar | Default | Lesson |
|-----|---------|--------|
| Spend | 50% | discretion, trade-offs, buyer's remorse at low stakes |
| Save | 40% | delayed gratification + the interest/match engine |
| Share | 10% | charity as a habit, not an afterthought |

Three jars is the sweet spot: two (spend/save) drop the charity lesson; five
gets fiddly at age 7. Physical jars for under-10s (coin visibility beats app
numbers), virtual ledger acceptable from ~10.

## The growth engine, quantified

**Interest mode**: 5%/month on Save. Effective annual: (1.05^12 − 1) ≈ **79.6%**.
At $2/wk into Save (~$8.67/mo), after 12 months: deposits $104, interest ≈ $27
— the child's money earned a full month of deposits "for free". That moment is
the entire point. Deliberately hyperinflationary: say so explicitly ("our family
bank pays way more than a real bank — real ones pay ~0.1%/mo"). The lesson is the
*shape* of compounding, not the market rate.

**Match mode**: parent matches 100% (or 50%) of Save deposits up to a monthly
cap. This is a literal 401(k) demo. Show the math: "every $1 you saved became
$2 — if your job did that, you'd never leave." Keep the cap small enough that
the kid can hit it (match-cap ≈ their weekly Save inflow × 2).

**Both**: interest on balance + match on deposits. Runs a touch rich; use for
12+ kids who can handle two moving parts.

## Raiding Save — the future-cost moment

When the child spends from Save, always print the future-value cost: FV of the
raided amount at the family rate for 12 months. A $20 raid at 5%/mo ≈ $35.9 of
year-end value. Do not forbid raids (forbidden fruit effect) — price them.
Log every raid; 3+ per quarter in the audit is a signal to talk, not punish.

## Chore commission design

- Commission = above-baseline work only. Baseline (unpaid): self-care, homework,
  family citizenship (dishes if it's your night, your own room).
- Payout ladder: 50¢ (5min task) → $1–2 (15–30min) → $5 (big jobs: car wash,
  garage sweep). Cap weekly commission ≈ 50–100% of base allowance so the base
  still teaches unconditional flow.
- Never dock allowance as punishment for unrelated behavior — it corrupts the
  ledger's neutrality. (Withhold commission for the specific chore not done, fine.)

## Statement + audit outputs

Monthly statement (kid-facing, short sentences, emojis ok):
- In this month / Saved / Spent / Shared / Interest-or-match earned
- Goal progress bar + weeks-to-goal at current rate
- One-line "money win" highlight (biggest interest month, fastest goal, etc.)

Parent audit:
- Raid count and sizes (trend across months)
- Share jar staleness (unused > 90 days → schedule a use)
- Match utilization (kid leaving free money on the table → show them)
- Effective save rate vs target band for age

## Common failure modes

1. **The parent forgets deposits for 3 weeks** — then dumps $60 in at once.
   Compounding demos die from irregularity. Use a weekly phone reminder; the
   ledger's `audit` flags gap > 21 days.
2. **Kid hoards in Save, never spends** — also a failure mode (money is for
   living). Audit flags save-rate > 70% two months running for under-14s.
3. **Sibling parity wars** — size by formula (age-based), publish the formula,
   never negotiate per-child exceptions off-ledger.
4. **Inflation of chore prices** — renegotiate the commission menu twice a
   year max, like a real market.
5. **Share jar guilt** — let the child pick the cause; parent-picked causes
   read as tax.

## Graduation path

- 5–7: counting, coins, 3 jars, no growth engine.
- 8–11: ledger + interest; first long goal (10+ weeks).
- 12–15: match mode, checkbook-style statement, clothing budget line.
- 16–18: near-adult: debit card ledger import (manual), budget categories,
  match cap becomes "employer plan", introduce a "tax" line for family
  vacation fund at a flat 10% — with representation: they vote on the trip.
