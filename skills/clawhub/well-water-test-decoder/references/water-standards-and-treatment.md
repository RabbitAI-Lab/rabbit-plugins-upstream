# Well Water — Standards, Sampling & Treatment Reference

## 1. Who is responsible (the core fact)
Private wells are **not regulated under the Safe Drinking Water Act**. No utility tests your water; EPA standards are your benchmark but enforcement is you. Testing cadence: coliform + nitrate **annually** (spring, after snowmelt, is most revealing); arsenic/lead/Mn every 3–5 yr; full panel at property transfer, after flooding, well repairs, or nearby construction/drilling.

## 2. Health standards (EPA MCL / action levels)
| Parameter | Limit | Health meaning | Source patterns |
|---|---|---|---|
| E. coli | 0 | fecal contamination — acute GI illness | septic proximity, well defect, livestock |
| Total coliform | 0 | indicator of surface intrusion | cap/casing defects, grading, floods |
| Nitrate (as N) | 10 mg/L | blue-baby syndrome (infants <6 mo) | septic, manure, fertilizer, old shallow wells |
| Arsenic | 10 µg/L | chronic — skin/bladder/lung cancer | New England, upper Midwest, Southwest aquifers |
| Lead | 15 µg/L AL | neuro, children | plumbing + corrosive water (rarely the aquifer) |
| Manganese | 0.12 mg/L (adv.) | neuro development, children | Northeast/Appalachian aquifers |
| Uranium | 30 µg/L | kidney | granite aquifers (New England) |
| Radon | varies (no federal MCL) | lung (inhalation route) | granite aquifers; test air alongside |

## 3. Secondary (aesthetic) standards
| Parameter | Secondary | Symptoms |
|---|---|---|
| Iron | 0.3 mg/L | red-brown stains, metallic taste, appliance rust |
| Manganese | 0.05 mg/L | black stains, bitter taste |
| Hardness | ~120 mg/L "hard" | scale, soap scum, water heater wear |
| TDS | 500 mg/L | brackish taste |
| pH | 6.5–8.5 | <6.5 corrosion; >8.5 scale + taste |
| Sulfate | 250 mg/L | laxative (unaccustomed drinkers) |
| Chloride | 250 mg/L | salty taste |

## 4. Sampling protocol (bad samples = wasted money)
1. Use lab-provided bottles; sterile bottle for bacteria; preserve arsenic/nitrate vials if specified.
2. Run cold tap 2–3 min; sample from the tap, not a hose or faucet aerator caked with biofilm.
3. Never sample within ~2 weeks of shock-chlorination (false negatives); run water 15+ min after any stagnation.
4. Ship/transport same day; refrigerate if delayed; bacteria plates die in heat.
5. For lead/copper: first-draw sample (6 hr stagnation) per the LCR protocol — different question than "well water quality."
6. Location matters: raw well water at the pressure tank vs post-treatment tap tell different stories; test raw for the well, post-treatment to verify equipment.

## 5. Well-construction audit (before blaming water chemistry)
- Well cap sealed and vented? Cracked/sloped grading funneling surface water to casing?
- Casing depth vs local water table; shallow dug wells (old farms) are coliform machines.
- Back-siphonage: hose bibs + chemical sprayers without vacuum breakers.
- Adjacent septic (within 50–100 ft), fuel tanks, livestock — nitrate/coliform sources.
- After floods: assume bacteria; shock-chlorinate, flush, wait 2 weeks, test.

## 6. Treatment classes (what each actually does)
| Class | Removes | Cost (installed) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shock chlorination | bacteria (one-shot) | $0–50 DIY | fix defect first or it returns |
| UV sterilizer | bacteria (continuous) | $800–1,500 | needs clear water (turbidity shields microbes); pre-filter |
| Neutralizer (calcite/soda-ash) | raises pH | $800–1,800 | must precede softener/iron units |
| Oxidizing filter (air/greensand) | Fe, Mn, H₂S | $900–2,200 | the correct class for Fe >3 ppm |
| Chlorination + retention + filter | Fe>10, Fe bacteria, H₂S, bacteria | $1,500–3,500 | heavy-duty wells |
| Softener (ion exchange) | hardness; Fe ≤3 incidental | $600–1,500 | size to grain load; resin fouls on high Fe/Mn |
| Anion exchange | nitrate, arsenic(V) | $1,200–2,500 | selective resin; verify with post-test |
| POU RO (undersink) | As, nitrate, lead, TDS, sodium at one tap | $200–600 | the honest drinking-water fix; 3:1 waste, check certification |
| Whole-house RO | everything, everywhere | $4,000–15,000 | only for extreme whole-house TDS/sodium/sulfate |

## 7. The oversell playbook (how treatment sales work)
1. Free test = strips with low resolution; everything reads "bad."
2. Fear framing: "you've been DRINKING this" — most secondary exceedances are aesthetic.
3. Bundle pitch: whole-house RO or one-size systems regardless of the actual report.
4. Pressure: "special price today only."
Defenses: accredited lab first; decode against MCL/secondary yourself; sequence per §6 order (bacteria → pH → iron → hardness); get itemized quotes in the right classes; POU RO for drinking-water-only parameters; second opinion on any >$2,000 proposal.

## 8. Retest & maintenance calendar
- Annually (spring): coliform + nitrate.
- Every 3–5 yr: arsenic, lead (first-draw), Mn, uranium if regional.
- After: floods, pump replacement, casing work, new nearby drilling/construction, or any change in taste/odor/color.
- Equipment: softener salt monthly check; neutralizer calcite top-up 6–12 mo; oxidizing media 3–7 yr; RO membrane 2–3 yr; UV lamp annually (even if still lit — intensity fades).
- Keep a water log: date, parameter, value, lab — trends beat snapshots, and a clean log is gold at property sale.
