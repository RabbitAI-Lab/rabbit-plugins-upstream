# UGC ad scripts

A UGC ad is a short video that looks like a person filmed it on their phone: one creator, one product, a real
setting. It works because it does not look like an ad for the first two seconds. Most bad UGC scripts fail in the same
three ways: they open slowly ("Hi guys, so today..."), they are the same talking-head testimonial everyone else runs,
and they hold more words than the video has time for.

## 1. Pick the format first

The talking-head testimonial is the most overexposed format in paid social. Viewers recognise it as an ad almost
instantly. Use it, but rotate in the others:

| Format | What happens | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Testimonial | Creator tells the camera what changed for them | Products with a clear before and after |
| Scenario drop-in | Open in the middle of the problem moment, no setup | Everyday pains (sleep, cleaning, commuting) |
| Hot take | "Unpopular opinion: ..." then the product as the proof | Crowded categories with a default choice |
| Diagnostic | "If you have this exact problem, watch this" | Narrow audiences who self-identify |
| Demo | Hands and product, the creator narrating what they do | Anything with a satisfying physical action or a fast app flow |
| Third-person proof | Creator reacts to someone else's result (a partner, a friend, reviews) | Gifts, family products, B2B |
| List | "3 things I stopped buying after..." with the product as one | Bundles, routines, comparisons |
| Us vs them | The usual way side by side with this way | A product that replaces a habit |

Make 3 to 5 scripts in different formats for the same product before you make 5 variations of one.

## 2. Size it to the length

A creator speaking naturally to camera says about **2 words per second**; a fast, punchy read reaches about 2.4. Budget
for the slower rate and leave half a second at the end:

| Video length | Spoken words |
|---|---|
| 9 to 10 s | 15 to 18 |
| 15 s | 25 to 30 |
| 20 s | 35 to 40 |
| 30 s | 55 to 60 |
| 45 s | 85 to 90 |
| 60 s | 110 to 120 |

A script over budget gets read too fast, cut off, or (by an AI video model) silently drifts. Cut words, never speed.

## 3. Structure, beat by beat

1. **Hook (0 to 2 s).** The visual and the first line land together. No greeting, no name, no logo intro. See the
   `ad-hooks` skill for 17 mechanics by name.
2. **Problem (2 to 5 s).** One specific, relatable moment. "My back hurt after every flight" beats "back pain is common".
3. **The product, on screen by second 3 to 5.** Shown in use, not held up next to the face.
4. **Proof (the middle).** One concrete result with a number or a detail only a user would know. "I went from 6
   alarms to 1", "it fits under the seat in front". One proof point lands; four are a list nobody remembers.
5. **The ask (last 2 to 3 s).** One action, said plainly: "link's below", "it's 30% off till Sunday". If there is an
   offer, say it here, once.

Write each beat as **what the camera sees** plus **the line**, so whoever films or generates it knows both:

```
HOOK  [Close-up: she pours coffee, it overflows because she's staring at her phone]
      "I used to lose the first hour of every day to my inbox."
PROOF [Over the shoulder: the app sorts 40 emails into 3 piles in one tap]
      "Now it's sorted before my coffee's done. Forty emails, three piles."
ASK   [Back to her, sipping, relaxed]
      "Free for two weeks. Link's below."
```

## 4. Sound off, and voice-safe

- **Most feeds start muted.** The first frame and the captions have to carry the hook on their own. Put the key line on
  screen as captions (burned in during editing, short lines of 5 words or fewer).
- **Write numbers as they are spoken** when a voice or an AI video model will read the line: "ninety-nine dollars",
  "thirty percent off", "two times faster". A model reading "$99" or "2x" may say it wrong or skip it. Keep the digits for
  the on-screen caption.
- **Keep on-screen text out of AI-generated footage.** Video models garble text on signs, labels and screens. Add text,
  prices and logos in the edit.
- **One action per shot.** "She opens the box, smiles, and pours a glass" is three shots. Each extra action in one shot is
  one more thing that can go wrong on camera or in a generated clip.

## 5. Casting and voice

- Cast the person the target viewer would trust, not a default. Match age, situation and register to the buyer.
- Write in the creator's register: contractions, short sentences, one aside. Read it out loud; if it sounds like a
  brochure, rewrite it.
- No claims the product cannot back up, and nothing a regulated category forbids (health, finance, weight loss).

## Checklist before handing it off

- [ ] The hook works on mute, in the first frame
- [ ] Word count fits the length table above
- [ ] Product on screen by second 3 to 5
- [ ] One proof point, with a number or a specific detail
- [ ] One ask
- [ ] Numbers written out for the voice, digits kept for captions
- [ ] At least three formats tried, not three wordings of one
