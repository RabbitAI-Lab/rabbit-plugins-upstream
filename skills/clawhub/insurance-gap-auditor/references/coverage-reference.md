# Insurance Coverage Reference

## 1. How personal policies are structured

### Homeowners (HO-3 typical in the US)
- **A — Dwelling**: rebuild cost of the structure (NOT market value).
- **B — Other structures**: 10 % of A (fence, shed).
- **C — Personal property**: 50–70 % of A; **sub-limits** for theft of
  jewelry ($1–2k), furs, firearms, silver, electronics (business property
  $2.5k on/premises).
- **D — Loss of use**: temp housing while uninhabitable.
- **E — Personal liability**: 100–500k typical.
- **F — Medical payments to others**: small no-fault guest injuries.

Perils: HO-3 covers dwelling all-risk, contents named-peril. The big
named exclusions: **flood, earthquake, sewer backup (rider), war,
neglect, ordinance-or-law (rider)**.

### Renters (HO-4)
Contents + liability, same sub-limit structure; often $100k liability
default — usually too low.

### Auto
- Liability (BI/PD) — often state minimums by default; 100/300/100 is
  the common expert recommendation floor.
- UM/UIM (uninsured/underinsured motorist) — in many states ~13–30 % of
  drivers are uninsured; UM/UIM is the only thing that pays when they
  hit you.
- Collision/comprehensive with deductible; PIP/MedPay where applicable.
- Gap coverage for financed new cars (payout = ACV < loan balance).

### Umbrella
$1M+ excess liability over auto+home. Requires underlying limits
(typically 250/500 auto, $300k home) — dropping underlying voids it.
Cost $150–400/yr for the first $1M.

## 2. The water-damage triangle (the #1 confusion)

| Water event | Covered by standard home? |
|---|---|
| Sudden accidental pipe burst (inside) | YES |
| Storm wind rips roof, rain enters | YES (wind peril) |
| **Flood — external surface water** | **NO — NFIP/private flood only** |
| **Sewer/drain backup** | **Only with water-backup rider** |
| **Sump pump failure/overflow** | **Only with sump rider (same endorsement)** |
| Long-term slow leak / mold from neglect | NO (maintenance exclusion) |

The same storm can produce three different answers. The rider
("water backup & sump overflow", $50–250/yr) is one of the highest-value
endorsements in personal lines for anyone with a finished basement.

## 3. Wind/hail & catastrophic deductibles

Hurricane-prone and hail-prone states commonly replace the flat
deductible with a **percentage deductible** on wind/hail: 1–5 % of
Coverage A. On a $400k dwelling, a 2 % wind deductible = $8,000 out of
pocket per roof claim. People discover this at claim time. Ask for the
**dollar figure** of every deductible type.

## 4. ACV vs replacement cost

- **ACV (actual cash value)** = replacement cost − depreciation. A
  12-year-old roof might pay 25 % of new; a 5-year-old TV pays a
  fraction.
- **RC (replacement cost)** endorsements pay to replace at today's
  prices. Roof claims in hail states increasingly default to ACV or
  "schedule roof by age" formulas.
- Contents RC endorsement is cheap; verify it exists.

## 5. Liability sizing logic

Liability limit should approximate what a lawsuit could reach:
**net worth + a few years of income** (future wages are garnishable).
Rules of thumb:

| Net worth | Suggested stack |
|---|---|
| < $100k | state-min auto + renter liability (still raise to 100/300) |
| $100–300k | auto 100/300, home 300k |
| $300k–1M | auto 250/500 + $1M umbrella |
| $1M+ | umbrella stacked to ~net worth |

Umbrella also extends coverage to some non-auto non-home situations
(libel, rental liability abroad in some policies) — read the "additional
coverages" section.

## 6. Scheduling valuables

Jewelry, watches, furs, fine art, cameras, musical instruments, bikes:
schedule them (floater endorsement) with appraisals — no deductible
option, all-risk coverage including mysterious disappearance. Costs
~$1–3 per $100 of value per year. Do it BEFORE the loss; the sub-limit
applies regardless of total contents coverage otherwise.

## 7. UK / EU differences worth knowing

- UK home insurance splits buildings/contents as separate products;
  "accidental damage" is an add-on (standard covers are perils-based).
- Flood is insurable in the UK (Flood Re scheme for high-risk homes) —
  the US-style "never covered" rule doesn't apply, but excesses rise.
- EU varies by country; liability for the *homeowner* often sits in a
  separate "family liability" (familienhaftpflicht) product in DE/AT/CH
  — check it exists; it's the continental equivalent of umbrella.
- Motor: EU minimums lower than sensible; green-card system for driving
  abroad.

## 8. The annual audit ritual (what this tool automates)

1. Life changes: renovations (dwelling rebuild cost!), home business,
   new pet, teen driver, valuables acquired, jewelry inherited.
2. Re-verify: dwelling = current rebuild estimate; contents limit after
   inventory; liability vs current net worth; deductibles in dollars.
3. Riders present: water backup, scheduled property, RC contents,
   earthquake (region-dependent), umbrella.
4. Get every change as a **written endorsement** — binders and verbal
   promises don't pay claims.

## 9. Scope & disclaimer

Market-typical patterns for personal lines, primarily US, with UK/EU
notes. Not legal or coverage advice; your contract, endorsements, and
jurisdiction control. For disputes, state insurance regulators (US) and
the Financial Ombudsman (UK) are the escalation paths.
