# Toxin Reference — Dogs & Cats

Onset windows and decontamination rules. When this table and a hotline
disagree, the hotline wins — they have the case detail.

## Quick Hazard Grid

| Toxin | Dog severity | Cat severity | Onset | Lag trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chocolate/theobromine | dose-dependent | moderate | 6-12h | 17.5h half-life — symptoms persist days |
| Xylitol | severe ≥0.1 g/kg | minimal | 10-60 min | asymptomatic at 2h ≠ safe (AKI at ≥0.5) |
| Grapes/raisins | severe, any amount | moderate | 24h | kidney signs at 24-72h |
| True lilies | mild | DEADLY | 2-12h | kidney values spike 12-24h; 18h+ = worse prognosis |
| Sago palm | deadly (1-2 seeds) | deadly | 15 min-2h | false improvement, liver failure day 2-3 |
| Antifreeze (EG) | deadly 4-5 mL/kg | deadly 1.4 mL/kg | 30 min ("drunk") | fake recovery 12-24h, kidneys 24-72h |
| Ibuprofen | severe | deadly | 4-6h | GI bleed silent; kidney injury cumulative |
| Acetaminophen | severe | deadly (10 mg/kg) | 1-4h | cats: face swelling = late sign |
| Rodenticides | severe | severe | 3-5 DAYS | anticoagulant type: perfectly fine-looking for days |
| Onions/garlic | moderate | severe | 1-5 days | RBC damage delayed; powder concentrated |
| Alcohol/yeast dough | moderate | moderate | 15-60 min | dough also expands (GDV risk) |
| Caffeine | moderate | moderate | 30-60 min | additive with chocolate |
| Macadamia nuts | moderate | unknown | 3-12h | usually paired with chocolate (cookies) |
| Lily of the valley | severe (cardiac) | severe | 30 min-2h | confused with true lilies — different toxin |
| Azalea/rhododendron | moderate | moderate | 0-4h | large amounts = cardiac |
| Dieffenbachia/pothos | mild | mild | immediate | oral pain looks dramatic, rarely systemic |
| Avocado | mild (pit = obstruction) | mild | — | real victims are birds/rabbits/horses |

## Decontamination Rules

1. **Inducing vomiting — only on instruction.** 3% hydrogen peroxide,
   1 mL/lb (max 45 mL) for DOGS only; never cats (laryngospasm risk),
   never with corrosives/acids/alkalis/hydrocarbons/petroleum, never
   if already symptomatic (aspiration).
2. **Activated charcoal** — vet-administered; repeated doses for
   theobromine (enterohepatic recirculation) and roditenticides.
3. **Fur/skin contamination** — dish soap + lukewarm water, rinse,
   dry. Prevent grooming (onesie/e-collar) until fully clean.
4. **Eye splash** — saline rinse 10-15 min, eye on the way to vet.
5. **Corrosive (cleaner/battery)** — NO vomiting; rinse mouth with
   water, offer water or milk, ER.

## What to Bring to the ER

- The product in its packaging (xylitol content, rodenticide class,
   concentration of everything)
- Ingestion time estimate and amount estimate (count what's missing)
- Pet weight (they'll re-weigh, but your dose math needs it now)
- Any vomit sample (photo at least — identify chunks)
- Medication list (interactions change treatment)

## Hotlines

- **ASPCA Animal Poison Control:** (888) 426-4435 — US, 24/7, ~$75
- **Pet Poison Helpline:** (855) 764-7661 — US/Canada/Caribbean, ~$85,
  includes follow-ups
- UK: Animal PoisonLine (01202 509000) — free triage
- AU: Animal Poisons Centre (1300 869 738)
- Find the nearest 24/7 ER BEFORE you need it (regular vets close;
  poisonings cluster at night/weekends — that's when people bake and clean)

## The "Wait, That's Toxic?" List

Commonly underestimated:

- **Sugar-free peanut butter** (xylitol) — people dose dogs with it
- **Grapes in trail mix** — the raisins are the toxin
- **Onion/garlic powder** in baby food, soup, jerky — concentrated
- **One ibuprofen dropped on the floor** — cat finds it, 10 mg/kg
- **Tulips/hyacinth bulbs** — dug up by dogs in fall planting season
- **Cocoa mulch** in gardens — smells like chocolate, is chocolate
- **Hand sanitizer, nicotine gum, ADHD patches** in bags left at dog level
- **Batteries** (remote controls chewed) — corrosive + heavy metal
- **Human meds in a weekly pill organizer** — the dog eats the whole
  Sunday slot (multi-drug exposure; bring EVERYTHING)

Commonly over-feared:

- White chocolate (theobromine ≈ 0), avocado flesh for dogs/cats,
  plain cooked salmon, plain rice cakes, most spider bites in the US
  (brown recluse/black widow territory, but "random brown spider" ≠
  emergency)

## Safe-Plant Shortlist (still supervise — obstruction exists)

Spider plant, Boston fern, calathea, peperomia, ponytail palm,
African violet, some succulents (echeveria; NOT jade — mild-moderate).
Toxic bouquets to refuse as a cat owner: ANY with true lilies.
Substitutes people gift instead: alstroemeria (Peruvian lily —
irritant, not kidney-toxic), roses, gerbera daisies, orchids.
