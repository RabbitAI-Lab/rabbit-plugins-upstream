# Topic selection, writing and expression training

## From materials to brief

Determine the reader, concrete question and current facts. Select allowed event cards that directly explain this topic's judgment, tradeoff or boundary. A few relevant cards usually suffice; personal storytelling is not mandatory.

Extract practice methods from `x-writing-dna` or supplied references. Record genre, source works, scope and gaps. If an older profile lacks the three content-context dimensions, say so. A method from insufficient sampling may still be an experimental practice goal, but cannot be called validated author DNA.

Include in the project brief:

- Event IDs, current version/checked date, allowed claims and contribution boundaries.
- Method/rule IDs, source, genre and fit to this topic.
- One main organizing principle and a few practice goals.
- Newly proposed user opinions requiring claiming, and conclusions not yet supported.

Recheck public facts for the current topic. Confirmed materials do not make dynamic data permanently current. Honor project rules for format, number of drafts, discovery-first research, user topic selection and publication.

## Output

Apply methods separately to posts and long form. Alternatives share facts, personal-material basis and claim strength while varying entry, progression and ending. If project rules require P1/P2/P3, provide three complete candidates. If they require one L, choose by evidence fit. Do not redefine project styles.

Personalization adds concrete background, reasons, real questions and bounded judgments. Without lived experience, use public research, real evaluation criteria or proposed judgments. Do not add unprovided emotions, failures, clients, achievements or earnings.

Keep the clean body reader-facing. Put IDs, source mapping, attribution, new-opinion claiming and practice explanations in the review appendix.

## Feedback and adoption

After selection, deletion, revision or rejection, record the actual draft/passage, before and after, user reason, method and unsuitable conditions. A selection without explanation has no inferred reason. Without real feedback, do not add an adopted record.

Use `trial` for practice. Cross-topic feedback or explicit user adoption supports `adopted`, with evidence. Use `retired` for discarded or obsolete rules. Preferences may vary by genre/topic; do not turn them into unconditional personality traits.

Retain feedback and usage history in related cards or rules, avoiding duplicate ledgers. Generated drafts cannot prove their personal experiences. New facts return to capture checks; reference-author material retains attribution.

## Review and effects

Check sourced and permitted personal claims, concrete information gain, delivery of the reader promise, method fit, excessive imitation, repeated stories and factual loss.

Report format validation, actual user adoption and real distribution data independently. One selection or high-interaction post does not prove a stable style or causal reach effect. Unrecorded time, editing effort and feedback remain unknown.
