# Find jobs the person could realistically do

Use this workflow when the user wants JobGenie to find opportunities. Read their résumé, full career reference, skills matrix and saved feedback before choosing search terms. The question is: **Could this person reasonably do the main day-to-day work, and what could they bring that would help this company?** Search from their actual capabilities, including relevant transferable experience, rather than limiting them to previous titles or one industry.

Discovery comes before choosing an application. It is not a sixth writing round: once a job is selected, use the existing five-round pipeline. A user who already has a posting can skip discovery. One Claw can do both workflows; additional bots are optional.

## Establish useful search preferences

Read the existing answers first. Ask concise questions about missing preferences that would materially change the search: location and work authorization/geographic eligibility, remote/hybrid/on-site arrangements, employment type, compensation preferences, travel, role interests and explicit exclusions. Ask which are nonnegotiable and which are flexible; do not turn a preference into a hard stop yourself. Do not import another applicant's requirements or make the user repeat settled answers.

Agree the useful initial batch or search scope before prolonged work. An exploratory user can start with broad role suggestions and then choose a direction. If they have no résumé/reference, use [guided discovery](skills-matrix.md) to establish enough experience to search; do not require an exhaustive career inventory first. Suggest missing related capabilities for the user to accept/correct/reject and save those answers before treating suggestions as established facts.

## Search from capabilities and the work

Identify plausible role families from the whole reference: responsibilities owned, practical skills/tools, independent work, leadership, delivery, writing, research, commercial work and other accepted capabilities. Include adjacent titles and industries when the actual work fits. Do not let an impressive but unrelated accomplishment dominate the search, and do not overlook substantial implementation or independent work because it lacks a familiar employer/title.

Use existing web search, fetch and browser tools to find real current openings. Search with relevant activities, deliverables and tools as well as title variants. Read the actual posting and relevant public company material; a search snippet or taxonomy suggestion is not a reviewed vacancy. Prefer the employer's careers page or its linked ATS listing. Aggregators can identify leads, but follow through to the original when available. Career/skills taxonomies can suggest vocabulary; they are optional aids, not a new integration or authority over the person's lived experience.

For each promising opening, establish what the employer does, what this hire would spend time doing, and the useful contribution the person could make. Use the JD's substantial clues and relevant company context. Save source URLs, date checked, requisition ID and posting date when available. Check the posting's current status and remote/location terms rather than assuming a search result is still open or globally remote. Mark inaccessible or unresolved details plainly; do not invent pay, availability, projects or links.

## Judge relevance without shrinking the person

Read the duties against the person's full reference, not just a keyword overlap. Explain the useful work they could own and identify the closest relevant experience supporting it. Where they have automation capability, consider how it improves that role's work. Do not assign automation experience to a person who has not supplied or accepted it.

Honor the user's explicit nonnegotiable filters. A title, seniority label, salary level, stated years, degree wishlist, missing exact tool or unfamiliar industry is not by itself a reason to discard a role. Assess the actual work and meaningful transfer from what the person has done. Surface concrete practical conditions that need a decision without recasting the person's career as a list of deficiencies. Do not add percentage-match thresholds, a claim-verification stage or a new eligibility judge.

Use qualitative priorities and concise reasons. Prefer roles where the applicant's useful contribution is easy to explain through relevant experience; unfamiliar titles may still be strong opportunities. Keep sensible exploratory opportunities visible when they fit the user's goals. Missing salary or an inaccessible detail can be reported without inventing certainty or blocking all progress.

## Return a useful, reusable shortlist

Show the user:

| Information | What to include |
|---|---|
| Employer and role | Actual title, company and requisition ID when available |
| Work and company context | What the person would do and the relevant work/problems the employer needs help with |
| Why this person | What they could contribute and brief closely relevant experience from their reference |
| Practical terms | Stated location/remote scope, employment type, pay/currency and other user-important terms; identify unknowns plainly |
| Original listing | Direct employer/ATS URL, date checked and observed status |
| Next step | Recommended priority and any concrete question the user needs answered |

Save the shortlist, useful search directions, posting text and applicable user preferences in the user's project directory. Reuse existing job lists/trackers when suitable. Deduplicate by employer/requisition/original URL; respect the user's existing application-history preferences. Do not overwrite another project or change production application states merely to record a discovery result. Update changed findings incrementally and retain useful prior work.

When the user chooses a role—or has already authorized preparation of an agreed batch—pass its actual JD, source URLs and collected company material into round 1. That round researches the selected employer's work without reverse-engineering it around the candidate. Rounds 2–5 then use the full current career reference and feedback. Reuse applicable collected sources, but do not copy one employer's argument into another application.

Deliver the agreed shortlist and stop at the agreed scope. Searching and drafting do not authorize employer contact, applications, account creation, background schedules or a new service. If live search is unavailable, say so and offer to evaluate supplied postings; do not label that as a completed live search.
