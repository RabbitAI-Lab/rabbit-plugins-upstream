# Manuals, Model Plates, and Error Codes

## Where the model/serial plate lives

| Unit type | Plate location |
|---|---|
| Gas furnace (upflow) | inside the burner compartment door (front lower panel) |
| Gas furnace (downflow) | side panel, sometimes inside blower door |
| High-eff (90%+) | inside both front doors; rating sticker on the side |
| Oil furnace | front door over the burner (Beckett/Carlin burner has its own tag) |
| Wall boiler | under the front cover or on the side panel |
| Air handler | filter door or upper panel |

Photograph it — the tech will ask, and warranty lookups need it. The model
number encodes capacity (e.g., a gas furnace model containing `080` ≈ 80
kBTU/h input).

## Finding the manual

1. Google: `"<brand> <model>" furnace installation manual pdf` — most
   manufacturers (Carrier, Trane, Lennox, Goodman, Rheem, York, Bryant,
   Coleman, Heil) publish them.
2. The door panel itself often has the lighting/relight instructions and
   a blink-code legend — the single most useful sticker in your basement.
3. Lennox hides some behind dealer login; use the generic family manual
   (e.g., G26/G32/DLSC series guides float around).

## Control-board LED conventions (families differ — check the door legend)

- **Goodman/Amana**: 1 blink = gas valve sense wait… no — 1 blink lockout,
  2 = pressure switch open, 3 = limit. Legend ON THE DOOR.
- **Carrier/Bryant**: short/long blink code (e.g., 3 short 1 long =
  limit). Legend on the reverse of the blower door.
- **Trane/American Standard**: 2 flashes = pressure switch, 4 = limit,
  9 = igniter. Legend on panel.
- **Rheem/Ruud**: steady = OK; slow flash = lockout.
- **White-Rodgers / Honeywell boards**: status via blink count only.

Count blinks BEFORE power-cycling (the code history often clears).

## Flame sensor cleaning — the classic DIY-tease

Old advice says "sand the flame sensor with a dollar bill/emery." Modern
technicians warn: abrasive sanding leaves microscopic grooves that carbon
up FASTER; proper fix is steel wool or fine Scotch-Brite at most, and if
the rod is discolored/blackened it's often just aged out ($20-60 part).
If you're comfortable removing two screws and one wire: gentle polish,
refit, test one cycle. If it fails again within weeks — replace the rod,
it's cheap.

## Igniter handling (if you get that far)

Hot-surface igniters are FRAGILE — never touch the gray silicon-carbide or
blue silicon-nitride element with bare fingers (skin oils create hot
spots). Grip the metal base. A visible crack or blister = dead; universal
replacements rarely fit right — buy by part number.

## What to say when you call a pro (gets you better service, faster)

1. Symptom in one sentence: "Gas furnace, blower runs, burners light and
   die after 5 seconds."
2. What you checked: "Filter's new, vent's clear, power-cycled the
   switch, blink code is 5 flashes = lockout per the door sticker."
3. Model + serial from your photo.
4. Ask: "What's your diagnostic fee, and is it credited against the
   repair?"
5. Emergency or not: say so explicitly — "House is at 14°C and holding;
   can this wait for morning rates?" (If they hear panic, some will sell
   you panic pricing.)

## Warranty quick-check

- Serial decoders: first 2-4 characters usually encode manufacture
  date/year+week (varies by brand — check the plate or the manual).
- Parts warranties run 5-10 years (registers sometimes 20 on heat
  exchangers — but labor is never included).
- Have your install receipt handy; registration gaps can halve coverage
  in some US states.
