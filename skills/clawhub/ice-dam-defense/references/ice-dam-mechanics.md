# Ice-Dam Mechanics Reference

## How a Dam Forms (the 3-zone roof)

1. **Snow-covered upper roof** sits above a warmed attic → its underside is above 0 °C →
   bottom layer of snow melts.
2. Meltwater runs down the roof under the snow until it reaches…
3. **The eave/overhang**, which hangs past the warm wall and is at outdoor temperature →
   water refreezes into a growing ice ridge.
4. Subsequent melt pools BEHIND the ridge (water only needs a few mm of head) and wicks
   under shingle laps/through nail holes into the sheathing → ceiling stain.

Dams are a **heat-leak symptom**, not an act of weather. Same storm, same street: the
well-sealed attic gets icicle-free eaves; the leaky one gets a dam.

| Factor | Weight | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Poor attic insulation | 25 | primary driver: heat flux to the deck |
| Previous ice dams | 20 | best predictor — the leaks are still there |
| Recessed ceiling lights | 15 | each can = a 15 cm hole in the thermal envelope |
| Bath/kitchen fan venting into attic | 15 | dumps warm HUMID air → frost on deck, melts later |
| Unsealed attic hatch | 10 | stack-effect chimney |
| Unsealed flue/duct chases | 10 | same |
| Weak soffit/ridge ventilation | 10 | can't flush what heat gets in |
| Valleys/dormers/wall junctions | 10 | geometry traps snow + heat at intersections |
| Frozen/full gutters | 10 | gives backup a ledge to start on |
| Deep snow on roof | 10 | insulates the deck, keeping meltwater liquid; fuel |

## Snow Load (weight side)

Density by snow type (kg/m³): fresh 70 · settled 150 · packed 250 · wet 300 · ice-layered 400.

- Load per m² = depth(m) × density. 45 cm settled = 68 kg/m².
- Typical modern residential capacity ≈ 180 kg/m² (varies widely by code, span, age, and
  construction — older or engineered-light roofs can be lower).
- **Danger signals while loaded:** new door jams, drywall cracks at openings, sagging
  ridgeline/soffit, popping sounds → evacuate the room below, get a professional.
- Wet snow is the killer: 30 cm of wet ≈ 90 kg/m², plus rain-on-snow events can double loads
  in hours. Ice-layered (multiple thaw-freeze crusts) approaches masonry weights.
- Drifts: gable ends, valleys, and leeward sides can hold 2-3× the average depth — rake the
  drifts first.

## Safe Removal Ladder (do in this order)

1. **Roof rake from the ground** for the lower 1-1.5 m and above valleys/dormers. This breaks
   the dam's water supply without touching the roof.
2. **Do NOT** climb the roof, and do NOT chop the ice — shingle and deck damage turn a dam
   into a rebuild.
3. **Active leak:** fill a sock/stocking with calcium chloride, lay it across the dam
   perpendicular to the eave; it melts a drain channel through the ridge within hours
   (keep it off planting beds; not NaCl — corrodes fasteners).
4. **Professional steam removal** exists for big dams — steam cuts, chips destroy.
5. **Interior damage control:** buckets + plastic; if the ceiling bulges, puncture it at the
   low point to drain into a bucket (a controlled hole beats a collapsed saturated ceiling).
   Wet-dry within 48 h — then a humidity meter, not just eyes — mold risk.

## The Permanent Fix List (spring project)

1. Air-seal the attic floor: hatch weatherstrip, can-light covers, foam/flue-seal every
   top-wall penetration (blower-door guided if possible).
2. Insulate to modern code: R-49 (~40 cm blown) in cold climates.
3. Verify/instal soffit baffles + ridge or gable vents — a cold, ventilated deck stays frozen.
4. Reroute bath/kitchen fans to the exterior (never into the attic).
5. Where geometry can't be fixed (cathedral ceilings, skylight valleys): self-regulating
   heated eave cables, ON before the storm, OFF after melt — and a metal-roof or ice-and-water
   membrane upgrade at reroof time.
