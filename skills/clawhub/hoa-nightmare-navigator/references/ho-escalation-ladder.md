# HOA Escalation Ladder & Evidence Reference

## The ladder (in order — skipping rungs loses)

1. **Written response to the violation** (certified + email) — demand covenant text, evidence, hearing, cure period. Never phone. Never open-forum-only.
2. **Hearing** (in person or telephonic) — bring the paper trail; record if state law allows (one-party consent states: yes; two-party: announce and ask). Outcome in writing requested.
3. **Board meeting open forum** — 2-3 minute statement, submit written version for minutes. Bring neighbors; boards count heads.
4. **Internal dispute resolution / ADR** — CA Civ. Code §5930 (ADR offer required pre-suit), FL §720.511 (pre-suit mediation for parcel disputes). This is where associations get reasonable.
5. **Regulator / licensing complaints** — FL DBPR (licenses community association managers, CAMs), state AG consumer protection (unfair/deceptive practices against management companies), state real-estate division where applicable.
6. **Small claims / superior court** — specific money demands only ($50 fine refund, $300 damage). Fee-shifting under your act's prevailing-party clause cuts both ways: a paper trail of YOUR procedural correctness is what makes the association's attorney advise settlement.

## Fine schedules — the cheat most owners miss
A fine that is not in a **published, adopted schedule** is generally unenforceable. Ask: "please provide the adopted fine schedule and the minutes of the meeting adopting it." Boards that levied ad-hoc fines frequently reverse everything when asked this in writing.

## Special assessments — the vote is the leverage
- Governing docs typically require a **majority or supermajority of the entire membership** (not just attendees) to approve special assessments above a threshold.
- Demand: the meeting notice, the quorum count, the vote tally, and the minutes. A special assessment approved by 12 people in a room of 200-unit association is voidable.
- Reserve studies: an underfunded reserve study that "suddenly" requires $4,800/owner is a governance failure — the study is a member-record (request it under the records statute).

## Assessment increase patterns worth auditing
- Increases compounding at 2-3× CPI for consecutive years.
- Management contract renewals with >CPI escalators (it's a member record — request it).
- Insurance premium spikes after a claim — ask for the claims history and quotes.
- "Deferred maintenance" surcharges that recur annually (that's an assessment rename).

## When to actually hire the lawyer
- Liens/foreclosure threats (immediately — most states have strict notice and amount-verification rules; TX §209.0063, CA §5720 notice obligations).
- Construction defect or major property damage.
- Fair-housing overlay (disability accommodation denials — file HUD within 1 year, and the association knows federal fee exposure changes the math).
Everything else: the ladder above, in writing, with dates.
