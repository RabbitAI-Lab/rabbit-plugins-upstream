# Timeshare Exit Reference

## The fee machine (why the math always turns)
- Maintenance fees historically escalate 5-8%/yr; special assessments land on top (roofs, hurricanes, refurbishment cycles).
- At 6% growth a $1,200 fee becomes $3,860/yr in 20 years — $50k+ paid over two decades for one week a year.
- The purchase price is SUNK. The only live comparison: future fees − realized usage value vs exit cost. Never let anyone (including yourself) justify keeping by what you paid in 1998.

## Exit paths in detail
### 1. Developer deed-back / take-back
Ask owner services IN WRITING: "Do you operate a deed-back, surrender, or exit program, and what are its terms?" Works best when: fees current, loan paid, you're willing to forfeit the week. Some developers run quiet programs (especially when they can resell inventory); some charge a fee; some refuse. Cost: $0-2,000-ish. ALWAYS first.

### 2. Resale / giveaway
- The honest market: most deeded weeks resell at $0-10% of purchase. That's not a scam; that's the market.
- List free/cheap; the moment a "buyer" wants YOU to pay transfer/closing/title "fees" or a "Mexican title transfer tax" upfront, it's the classic recovery scam walking the other direction.
- Closing must go through a licensed title/escrow company. Redweek, TUG (Timeshare Users Group) classifieds, and licensed resale brokers (commission on success) are the legit channels.

### 3. Licensed attorney — when there's a real defect
Viable legal theories (consult the timeshare bar; the American Resort Development Residential lawyer ecosystem is small and searchable):
- **Fraud/misrepresentation at sale**: "investment," "guaranteed rental income," "we'll buy it back," points "never expire" — sales-center misrepresentations were endemic; some states' AGs extracted settlement programs.
- **Truth-in-Lending / RESPA issues** on the purchase loan.
- **Developer breach**: maintenance of the unit/common areas materially failing.
- **Mexico/foreign timeshares**: different and worse; contract choice-of-law clauses, and the "Mexican bank trust" (fideicomiso) cancellation involves Mexican counsel — a favorite hunting ground for scammers.

### 4. Calculated default (know the blast radius)
- Consequences: collections, credit damage (both the loan and the fee obligation can report), possible lien/foreclosure on a deeded week (they take the week back — sometimes that's the goal), and possible deficiency judgment on the loan depending on state law and whether it's purchase-money.
- Some states restrict deficiencies on purchase-money timeshare paper; verify YOUR state before deciding. Never default casually — and never pay a company to tell you to "just stop paying."

## The exit-scam industry (why it exists)
Desperate owners + no legitimate resale market = perfect prey. The FTC and multiple state AGs (including multi-state actions) have repeatedly targeted "timeshare exit" outfits charging $4k-10k upfront. The pattern: robocall or direct mail after a resort visit/maintenance-fee complaint → sales pitch at a hotel conference room → huge upfront fee → 12-24 month "process" → a letter the developer ignores → company dissolved/rebranded. Industry body ARDA even runs consumer warnings because the scams give exits a bad name. If flagged: report to your state AG and reportfraud.ftc.gov; if you paid by card recently, dispute within the chargeback window (~120 days).

## Inheritance playbook
- Perpetual deeded timeshares pass to heirs WITH the obligation. Heirs who don't want it:
  1. **Disclaimer** under federal law (25 USC § 2518 analog: IRC § 2518): must generally be executed within 9 months of the death date (minors extended), in writing, irrevocable, before accepting benefits. STATE probate law governs the property itself — timing varies; move fast.
  2. If the 9 months passed: negotiate with the estate's executor to have the ESTATE dispose/renegotiate (deed-back) before distribution; distributing the week to unwilling heirs is the executor's problem too.
  3. Never "ignore it into going away" — fee accruals and liens follow the titled owner.
- Some points/club programs terminate the fee obligation at death (the contract's successor clause governs) — send the death certificate to owner services certified, and get written confirmation the account is closed.

## Talking to the developer (script)
"I'm exploring my options with the week. Does [Resort] operate a deed-back, surrender, or buy-back program? What are the eligibility requirements, costs, and timeline? Please respond in writing to this email." — This one email outperforms most $6,000 exit packages.
