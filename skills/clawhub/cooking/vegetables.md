# Vegetables, Beans, and Potatoes

Vegetables fail for three reasons and only three: **water** (they release it and then steam), **pH** (acid firms and dulls, alkali softens and browns), and **starch structure** (which decides whether a potato mashes or holds). Pick the one that applies and the method follows.

## Water Content Decides the Method

| Group | Examples | Method that works | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Very wet | Mushroom, courgette, spinach, tomato, aubergine | High heat, small batches, or drive the water off first then brown | They flood any pan they are crowded in (`heat.md`) |
| Dense and starchy | Potato, sweet potato, winter squash, carrot, parsnip, beetroot | Roast 200-220°C, or par-boil then roast | Need long heat to soften; brown well once dry |
| Cruciferous | Broccoli, cauliflower, sprouts, cabbage, kale | High dry heat, or fast blanch | Long wet cooking releases the sulfur compounds that make them smell |
| Leafy and delicate | Lettuce, herbs, pea shoots, chard leaves | Barely cook, or dress raw | Collapse in seconds |
| Alliums | Onion, leek, shallot, garlic | Sweat slow for sweetness, or brown for depth | Garlic burns in seconds at browning temperatures |
| Green and tender | Green beans, asparagus, peas, mangetout | Blanch and shock, or roast hot and fast | Colour is lost to time and acid, not to heat itself |

## Roasting

- 200-220°C, convection if available, on a preheated tray. A cold tray costs the first several minutes of browning on the underside.
- Cut for uniformity, and cut *larger* than feels right for wet vegetables — small pieces dry out before they caramelize.
- Oil enough to coat (roughly 1 tbsp per 400 g) and salt before roasting. Under-oiled vegetables dehydrate and go leathery instead of browning.
- Single layer with gaps. Two half-full trays beat one crowded tray (`heat.md`).
- Sugar-heavy vegetables (carrot, onion, beetroot, squash) burn before they soften above ~230°C; dense low-sugar ones (potato, celeriac) take it.
- Add anything delicate — garlic, herbs, cherry tomatoes, cheese — in the last 5-10 minutes.

## Roast Potatoes, With the Chemistry

The best-in-class method, and each step has a mechanism:

1. Choose a **floury** variety (Maris Piper, Russet, King Edward; >20% starch). Waxy potatoes will not fluff.
2. Par-boil in salted water with **¼ tsp baking soda per litre**. Raising the pH accelerates the breakdown of pectin on the surface and speeds Maillard later — this is what produces the shaggy, glassy crust.
3. Drain and **rough them up** in the pan. The damaged surface is the surface that crisps.
4. Into hot fat in a preheated tray, 200-220°C, turned once, roughly 45-60 minutes.

Same principle in reverse: a potato salad wants a **waxy** variety (Charlotte, new potatoes) boiled in salted water and dressed while warm so it absorbs the dressing.

## Blanching, Shocking, and Green Colour

- Blanch in water salted to ~1% at a hard boil, uncovered, then shock in ice water. Uncovered matters: the volatile acids that turn chlorophyll olive-green escape rather than condensing back into the pot.
- Times from boiling: green beans 2-4 min, broccoli florets 2-3, asparagus 1-3 by thickness, peas 1-2, spinach 30 seconds.
- Chlorophyll degrades with acid and with time. Never add lemon or vinegar to green vegetables until the moment they are served.
- Baking soda in the water keeps green vividly green and turns the texture to mush at the same time. It is a photograph technique, not a cooking one.

## Beans and Pulses

- **Soaking is optional** for texture but cuts cook time by roughly a quarter and gives more even results. Salted soaking water (about 1-2%) improves the skins — the old rule that salt toughens beans is wrong.
- **Acid genuinely does firm them.** Tomato, wine, vinegar, and molasses go in only after the beans are tender, or they will stay firm for hours.
- Old beans never soften. If a batch is still hard after twice the expected time, they are old, not undercooked. A pinch of baking soda (¼ tsp per litre) can rescue them at some cost to flavor and vitamin content.
- Cook at a bare simmer. A rolling boil breaks the skins and produces a pot of purée with bald beans in it.
- Cooking liquid from beans is a thickener and a stock, not waste. Chickpea liquid (aquafaba) whips like egg white, at roughly 3 tbsp per whole egg for structure.
- **Red kidney beans must be boiled hard for at least 10 minutes** before simmering: raw and undercooked ones contain phytohaemagglutinin, and a slow cooker alone does not reach a temperature that destroys it (`safety.md`).
- Lentils: red and split ones collapse (soups, dals); brown, green and Puy hold shape (salads). No soaking needed for any of them.

## Alliums

- **Sweating**: low heat, lid on, 8-12 min — translucent, sweet, no colour. The base of most European cooking.
- **Browning**: medium heat, uncovered, stirring. Real caramelized onions take 40-60 minutes, not the 10 that recipes claim. A pinch of baking soda speeds it noticeably and softens the texture; a pinch of salt draws water out and speeds the early phase.
- Garlic goes in *after* the onion, and off the heat if the pan is already hot. It burns and turns bitter within seconds at browning temperature (`rescue.md`).
- Softening the sharpness of raw onion: soak sliced onion in cold water for 10 minutes, or dress it in acid for 20 — both remove the aggressive sulfur compounds without cooking it.

## Salads and Raw

- Dry the leaves completely; dressing slides off wet leaves and pools at the bottom.
- Dress at the last moment, with roughly 1 tbsp of dressing per 50 g of leaves, tossed with hands rather than tongs.
- Salt draws water out of anything cut. Salt tomatoes and cucumber 10-15 minutes ahead and drain them, or salt them at the table — never salt a raw salad twenty minutes before serving and expect it to be crisp.
- Vinaigrette holds at 3:1 oil to acid with an emulsifier (mustard, honey, egg yolk); without one it will separate within minutes, which is fine if it is whisked again at the table.

## Common Vegetable Rescues

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Soggy roast vegetables | Crowded tray, cold tray, or too little oil | Split across two trays, preheat the tray, more oil |
| Mushrooms grey and boiled | Crowded; they are ~90% water | Cook in batches at high heat, or drive off the water first then brown |
| Aubergine oily | Its open structure absorbs oil instantly | Salt and rest 30 min, or roast/grill it dry first and add oil afterward |
| Cauliflower and sprouts sulfurous | Boiled too long | Roast, or blanch briefly; the smell is time-dependent |
| Bitter greens too aggressive | Nature of the plant | Fat, salt, acid, and a little sweetness — all four |
| Sweet potato never crisp | High sugar, high moisture | Higher heat, smaller pieces, cornstarch dusting, and accept less crispness than a potato |
| Vegetables done at different times | Different densities on one tray | Stagger by density, or cut the dense ones smaller |
