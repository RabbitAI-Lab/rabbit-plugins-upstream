# Rescue — The Dish Is Wrong Right Now

Triage order, always: **can it still be corrected in the pan → can it be corrected on the plate → can it be repurposed into something else → is it a loss**. Most dishes are recoverable up to the point where a texture has been destroyed; flavor is nearly always fixable, texture usually is not.

**Before proposing a fix**, read `## Pain Points` and `## Repertoire` in `~/Clawic/data/cooking/memory.md` (or `repertoire.md` if `## Boxes` points there): if this dish has failed the same way before, the fix that already worked beats a fresh diagnosis, and a third occurrence means the method is wrong, not the execution.

## Too Salty

Salt cannot be removed; it can only be diluted, balanced, or bound. In order of how much of the dish survives:

1. **Add unsalted bulk.** More of the base — stock, water, tomato, cream, unsalted beans, extra vegetables, a second batch of rice. Formula: to bring a dish from measured `S%` down to a target `T%`, add `weight × (S/T − 1)` of unsalted material. A 1 kg braise at 2% going to 1% needs another kilo. This is the only fix that reliably works and the reason to season late (SKILL.md Rule 5).
2. **Balance with acid and fat.** Lemon, vinegar, or a spoon of unsalted yogurt, cream, or butter reduces perceived saltiness without touching the actual concentration. Works over about a 0.3-percentage-point range — enough for "slightly too salty", useless for a brine accident.
3. **Add sweetness, carefully.** A teaspoon of sugar or a grated carrot in a tomato sauce hides salt; overshoot and the dish becomes a different, worse problem.
4. **Serve it over something unsalted.** Plain rice, plain pasta, bread, or potato under a too-salty sauce restores the ratio on the plate at zero risk. The most underused rescue there is.

**The potato does not work.** A potato absorbs salted liquid at the same concentration as everything else — it removes water and salt in proportion, so the remaining dish is exactly as salty. Discard the technique, not the potato.

Over-salted from a brine or a cure: soak in fresh water, 30-60 min per exposure, changing the water; for salt cod and heavily cured pork, 12-24 h with three changes. Then cook a small piece and taste before committing the batch.

## Bland, Flat, or Muddy

Run the axes in order — each answers a different sensation, and adding the wrong one makes the dish worse:

| Sensation | Missing | Add | How much |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nothing tastes of anything | Salt | Salt, soy, fish sauce, miso, parmesan | Toward 0.8-1.0% of finished weight, in two steps |
| Heavy, dull, sweet, or "muddy" | Acid | Lemon, vinegar, wine, tamarind, yogurt, tomato | Start at 1 tsp per litre and re-taste |
| Harsh, thin, or the flavors do not join | Fat | Butter, oil, cream, coconut milk, egg yolk | 10-20 g per portion, off heat |
| Tastes like separate ingredients | Umami or time | Anchovy, tomato paste, mushroom, soy, parmesan rind; or 20 more minutes | One addition, then re-taste |
| Flat but already salty enough | Aromatics or heat | Fresh herbs, citrus zest, black pepper, chili, toasted spice | At the end; heat destroys volatile aromatics |
| Everything is there but it is boring | Contrast | Something crunchy, something raw, something acidic on top | A garnish is a fix, not decoration |

Two multipliers worth knowing: **umami compounds synergize** — glutamate (tomato, parmesan, soy, kombu) plus inosinate or guanylate (cured fish, meat, dried mushroom) reads as several times more savory than either alone, which is why anchovy in a tomato sauce disappears and doubles its depth. And **salt at the end is not the same as salt at the start**: salt added during cooking penetrates and seasons, salt at the end sits on the surface and reads louder for less.

## Broken and Curdled

| What broke | Why | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| Mayonnaise or aioli split | Oil added faster than the lecithin could emulsify, or too much oil per yolk (~200 ml is one yolk's ceiling) | New bowl, 1 fresh yolk or 1 tbsp water/mustard, then drizzle the broken mixture in slowly |
| Hollandaise or beurre blanc split | Over 85°C, or the butter went in too fast | Off heat, whisk in 1 tbsp cold water or an ice cube; if that fails, restart on a fresh yolk |
| Butter pan sauce turned oily | Boiled after the butter went in | Off heat, splash of cold liquid, whisk hard; add remaining butter cold, off heat |
| Cream sauce curdled | Boiled, or acid met hot dairy | Off heat, whisk; blend if needed. Prevent: temper the dairy, or stabilize with a starch slurry before the acid |
| Cheese sauce grainy or stringy | Over ~82°C, or aged cheese with no starch | Off heat, add cheese in handfuls; a pinch of cornstarch or a spoonful of evaporated milk re-smooths it |
| Yogurt split in a curry | Cold yogurt into a boiling pot | Temper: stir hot liquid into the yogurt, then the yogurt into the pot, off the boil. Full-fat only |
| Chocolate seized into a paste | A *small* amount of water reached it | Counter-intuitively, add *more* liquid: 1 tbsp boiling water or cream per 60 g, stirred until smooth. It will never re-temper, so use it for ganache or sauce |
| Ganache split | Emulsion broke on cooling or from wrong ratio | Warm to 35-40°C and blend, or add 1 tbsp warm milk and stir from the centre outward |
| Custard scrambled | Passed ~85°C | Blend and strain immediately — recovers a pourable sauce, never a silky one |

Prevention beats all of it: dairy and eggs meet acid and high heat off the burner, and every emulsion is built by adding the fat to the base, never the base to the fat.

## Too Thin or Too Thick

- **Too thin, time available**: reduce uncovered at a strong simmer. Season *after* (Rule 5). Reduction concentrates everything, including bitterness from a scorched base.
- **Too thin, no time**: cornstarch slurry (1 part starch : 2 parts cold water), stirred in and brought to ~95°C for a full minute. It thickens only when it gets there; a sauce pulled at 80°C thins again on the plate. Roux, beurre manié (equal soft butter and flour kneaded together, whisked in cold), or a puréed ladle of the dish's own solids all work without a raw-starch taste.
- **Too thin and dairy-based**: a beurre manié or an egg-yolk liaison (yolk tempered with hot liquid, returned off heat, never boiled again).
- **Too thick**: thin with the same liquid the dish is built on, not with water — water dilutes flavor exactly as fast as it dilutes body. Re-taste for salt after, since thinning drops the percentage.
- **Gluey or gummy from too much starch**: no repair for the texture. Thin heavily, strain if possible, and repurpose (below).

## Burnt

The rule is whether the burn touched the food or only the pan.

- **Scorched bottom, food above it unaffected**: transfer immediately to a clean pot **without scraping the bottom**. One scraped bit re-contaminates the whole batch, and the acrid compounds are not removable afterward.
- **Burnt aromatics (garlic, spices)**: bitterness spreads within seconds and is permanent. Start those again separately and combine; garlic goes in late, at lower heat, for exactly this reason.
- **Burnt crust on a roast or a bake**: cut or grate it off. Cake and bread lose the burnt layer with a fine grater; the crumb underneath is fine.
- **Slightly bitter, not truly burnt**: a small amount of sugar, dairy, or acid masks it. A spoon of peanut butter or a splash of cream in a stew are the classic covers.
- **Milk, cream, or chocolate scorched**: unrecoverable. The compounds are in the liquid, not on the pan.

## Overcooked and Undercooked

- **Undercooked, still on the heat**: lower the heat and cover so it cooks through without further surface damage. For roasts and birds, back into a 150°C oven and cook to temperature; higher heat only widens the gap between the outside and the middle.
- **Undercooked in the middle, outside already done**: slice or portion it and finish the pieces in a pan or in liquid. A whole item cannot be saved intact.
- **Overcooked meat**: it cannot be un-dried. Slice thin across the grain, and serve it in something wet — a sauce, a broth, a mayonnaise-based salad, tacos, a ragù. Shredding overcooked pork or chicken into a sauce is a legitimately good dish.
- **Overcooked vegetables**: purée them into a soup with stock and cream, or roast them harder into something deliberately caramelized.
- **Overcooked pasta**: shock in cold water to stop it, then pan-fry it into something crisp. It will not become al dente again.
- **Overcooked fish**: flake it into a fishcake, a rillette with butter, or a salad. Bound with fat it reads as intentional.

## Texture Disasters

| Problem | Cause | What is left |
|---|---|---|
| Soggy crust on a pie or pizza | Wet filling, no bottom heat, or steamed in a covered container | Return to the hottest bottom heat available; next time blind-bake, drain the filling, or use a preheated surface (`baking.md`) |
| Gummy rice or gluey risotto | Excess starch and agitation | Spread on a tray to steam-dry; or embrace it — fry into rice cakes or arancini (`rice-and-pasta.md`) |
| Rubbery eggs | Held above ~72°C | None; scramble slower and lower next time (`eggs-and-dairy.md`) |
| Tough braise | Not enough time above 70°C, not too much | Keep cooking — this is the one texture failure that reverses (`meat.md`) |
| Bread dense and gummy inside | Under-baked or cut hot | Slice and toast; measure internal temperature next time (`bread.md`) |
| Watery sauce on a stir-fry | Pan crowded, ingredients wet, heat too low | Drain, raise heat, finish in batches (`heat.md`) |
| Greasy fried food | Oil below ~160°C or too much per batch | Re-fry hot for 60-90 seconds; it genuinely helps (`frying.md`) |

## Repurpose or Bin

Repurpose when flavor is intact and only structure failed: broken cake → trifle or cake pops; failed custard → ice-cream base; gluey rice → arancini; over-reduced sauce → a spread or a glaze; split ganache → hot chocolate. Bin when a bitter burn has entered the liquid, when a dish has been in the danger zone past its budget (`safety.md`), or when the salt is more than double the target and there is not enough of the base to dilute it.

**Write after any rescue that worked or failed**: a line in `## Pain Points` of `~/Clawic/data/cooking/memory.md` naming the cause, and the fix in the dish's row in `## Repertoire` under `Change next time` (`memory-template.md`). The second time the same dish fails the same way, that stops being a rescue and earns a runbook in `artifacts/` — a symptom-check-fix text for this kitchen, with its `## Boxes` line written in the same turn.
