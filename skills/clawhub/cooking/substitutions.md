# Substitutions, Scaling, and Conversions

Two different problems live here. **Substitution** is "what can stand in for this", and the honest answer always names what changes. **Scaling** is "make it for six instead of two", and it fails because parts of a recipe scale linearly and parts do not.

**Read `## Swaps` in `~/Clawic/data/cooking/memory.md` (or `swaps.md` if `## Boxes` points there) before proposing a substitution**: what has already been tried in this kitchen, and how it turned out, beats any general table — including the failures, which are the rows that stop a bad swap being suggested twice.

## Dairy

| Missing | Use | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Buttermilk (240 ml) | 1 tbsp lemon juice or vinegar + milk to 240 ml, rest 10 min | Supplies the acid, not the thickness or the tang |
| Buttermilk, better version | 180 ml plain yogurt + 60 ml milk | Closer on both counts |
| Heavy cream, for cooking | Full-fat coconut milk, or evaporated milk | Will not whip; coconut is a flavour decision |
| Heavy cream, for whipping | No true substitute below 30% fat | Chilled evaporated milk whips weakly and collapses |
| Milk | Any unsweetened plant milk | Soy and oat behave closest in baking; almond and rice are thinner and lower in protein, so browning suffers |
| Sour cream | Full-fat Greek yogurt | Splits more readily when heated — temper it (`eggs-and-dairy.md`) |
| Crème fraîche | Sour cream, or yogurt + cream | Lower fat means less heat stability |
| Butter, in cooking | Oil, ghee, or a neutral fat | Butter is ~16-18% water; oil browns differently and adds no dairy flavour |
| Butter, in baking | Oil at 75-80% of the weight | Denser, moister, no creamed air — do not use where creaming is the leavening |
| Parmesan | Pecorino (saltier), grana padano, aged gouda | Adjust the salt down for pecorino |
| Ricotta | Cottage cheese blended smooth, or drained yogurt | Wetter; drain it |

## Eggs

One large egg is ~50 g out of the shell: 30 g white, 20 g yolk. The substitute depends on the job the egg was doing.

| Job | Substitute per egg | Works in |
|---|---|---|
| Binding | 60 g applesauce or mashed banana | Muffins, quick breads; adds sweetness and flavour |
| Binding, neutral | 1 tbsp ground flax + 3 tbsp water, rested 10 min | Cookies, dense bakes; slightly gummy |
| Binding, neutral | 1 tbsp chia + 3 tbsp water | As flax, more visible |
| Moisture and structure | 60 g silken tofu, blended | Custards, brownies, quiches |
| Leavening | ½ tsp baking powder + 1 tbsp water + 1 tbsp oil | Light cakes; no more than two eggs' worth |
| Whipped foam | 45 ml aquafaba (chickpea liquid) | Meringue, mousse; genuinely whips, less stable |
| Emulsifying (mayonnaise) | 45 ml aquafaba, or 1 tbsp mustard as a partial helper | Aquafaba mayonnaise works and is thinner |
| Wash for colour | Milk, cream, or oil | Less shine and less browning |

Above three eggs in a recipe, substitutes stop working — at that point the eggs are the structure, and a recipe designed without them is the better route.

## Fats

- **Oil for butter** in baking: 75-80% of the weight. **Butter for oil**: 125% of the weight, and expect a slightly drier, more tender crumb.
- Solid fats (butter, shortening, lard, coconut oil) create flakiness and hold air when creamed; liquid fats cannot do either. Never swap across that line in pastry.
- Lard and beef dripping make the flakiest pastry and the best roast potatoes. Shortening has no water and no flavour, so it gives maximum tenderness and minimum taste.
- Frying: match by smoke point, not by name (`heat.md`).
- Reducing fat in a baked recipe by more than about a quarter changes the structure, not just the calories.

## Flours and Starches

| Missing | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cake flour (120 g) | 105 g all-purpose + 15 g cornstarch | Approximates the lower protein |
| Self-raising flour (120 g) | 120 g plain + 1½ tsp baking powder + ¼ tsp salt | — |
| Bread flour | All-purpose | Less chew and less rise; add 1 tsp vital wheat gluten per 120 g to approximate |
| All-purpose, gluten-free | A tested 1:1 blend with xanthan gum | Single flours do not work alone; rice flour alone is gritty |
| Cornstarch (1 tbsp) | 2 tbsp flour, or 1 tbsp arrowroot | Arrowroot is clearer and freeze-stable, and goes slimy if boiled |
| Breadcrumbs | Crushed crackers, oats, panko, ground nuts | Panko is drier and crisper; nuts brown much faster |
| Semolina for dusting | Cornmeal or polenta | Flour burns; both of these roll |

Gluten-free baking is a different system, not a swap: gluten provides structure and gas retention, and blends replace it with a mix of starches, a protein source, and a hydrocolloid (xanthan or psyllium). Expect more liquid, longer resting, and different mixing — a 1:1 blend in a wheat recipe is a starting point, not a translation.

## Sweeteners

| Missing | Use | Adjust |
|---|---|---|
| White sugar (200 g) | 200 g brown sugar | More moisture, more acid (reacts with soda), chewier |
| White sugar | 160 ml honey | Reduce other liquid by ~60 ml, drop the oven 15°C — honey browns faster |
| White sugar | 160 ml maple syrup | As honey; distinct flavour |
| Brown sugar (200 g) | 200 g white + 1 tbsp molasses | Effectively the definition of brown sugar |
| Caster sugar | White sugar pulsed briefly | Not powdered sugar — that has cornstarch in it |
| Icing sugar (120 g) | 100 g granulated + 1 tbsp cornstarch, blended | Never fully as fine |

Sugar is structural: it tenderizes, retains moisture, browns, and lowers the freezing point. Cutting it changes texture. Sugar alternatives that are not sugars (erythritol, allulose, stevia) do not brown or tenderize, so a baked good built on them behaves differently in every respect.

## Leavening and Acids

- 1 tsp baking powder = ¼ tsp baking soda + ½ tsp cream of tartar.
- Baking soda needs an acid: buttermilk, yogurt, sour cream, brown sugar, molasses, honey, natural cocoa, citrus, vinegar. Dutch-processed cocoa is alkalized and will not do it (`baking.md`).
- Yeast: 1 instant : 1.25 active dry : 3 fresh (`bread.md`).
- Vinegar for wine, in cooking: use half the volume and top up with stock or water — vinegar is far more acidic than wine and lacks the sugars.
- Wine for deglazing, when there is none or it is excluded: stock plus a splash of vinegar or lemon; verjus; or a non-alcoholic wine. Say what changes rather than pretending it is identical.

## Aromatics and Liquids

- Fresh to dried herbs: **1 tbsp fresh = 1 tsp dried** for hardy herbs only. Dried basil, parsley, cilantro, and chives are weaker, different ingredients and are usually better omitted.
- Fresh garlic: 1 clove ≈ ⅛ tsp garlic powder ≈ ½ tsp granulated. Powder brings no sweetness and no bite.
- Stock cube or bouillon: usually 1.5-2 g of salt per 250 ml of made stock. Subtract it from the dish's salt budget, or the dish lands over 1.5% (`seasoning.md`).
- Shallot: half a small onion plus a little garlic. Onion for leek: fine in a braise, wrong in anything where the leek's mildness is the point.
- Fish sauce: soy plus a pinch of salt, or miso, for a vegetarian version — less complexity, similar function.
- Buttermilk, yogurt, lemon, and vinegar are interchangeable *as acid*, never as texture.

## Dietary Swaps

| Constraint | Practical route | Watch |
|---|---|---|
| Vegetarian | Eggs, dairy, pulses, tofu, tempeh, mushroom, seitan | Parmesan, Worcestershire, many cheeses and gelatin are not vegetarian |
| Vegan | Plant milk, aquafaba, flax egg, nutritional yeast, oil for butter | Structure in baking, and umami — build it with mushroom, tomato, miso, soy |
| Gluten-free | Tested GF blend, naturally GF grains | Cross-contamination is a real exposure for celiac disease (`safety.md`); soy sauce, stock cubes, and oats commonly contain gluten |
| Dairy-free | Plant milks, coconut cream, oil, cashew cream | Browning and body both drop; add fat back |
| Low-sodium | Acid, umami, herbs, spice, toasted aromatics | Salt substitutes taste metallic to many people; deficit is best filled with acid |
| Low-FODMAP | Garlic-infused oil instead of garlic, green parts of spring onion, hard cheeses | The infused oil trick works because the FODMAPs are water-soluble and the flavour compounds are not |
| Nut allergy | Seeds, toasted oats, crisped rice | Marzipan, pesto, some curries, and many commercial baked goods carry nuts |
| Halal / kosher | Substitute the meat and the alcohol; check gelatin, rennet, and enzymes | Wine and stock made with wine are the usual oversights |

An allergy is a hard constraint from `health/profile.md`; a diet is a preference in `config.yaml`. The substitution logic is the same, the failure consequence is not.

## Scaling

Multiply the ingredients by weight, then correct the four things that do not scale:

1. **Time does not scale with quantity.** Conduction time rises with roughly the **square of thickness**, so twice as much food in a wider pan takes nearly the same time, and a piece twice as thick takes about four times as long (SKILL.md Rule 7).
2. **Evaporation scales with surface area, not volume.** A doubled braise, soup, sauce, or jam in the same pot reduces at the same rate and comes out thin. Use a wider pot, split into two, or finish uncovered for longer.
3. **Salt and spice scale linearly with weight; heat and aromatics do not scale linearly with perception.** Double the salt exactly, and scale chilli and strong spices by about 1.5× for a 2× batch, then correct by tasting.
4. **Pan capacity scales with area, and batter depth must stay constant** (`baking.md` has the pan table). A doubled cake batter in a pan of the same depth needs a lower temperature and a longer time, or it domes and cracks.

Halving is easier than doubling but has its own trap: a half batch in the same pan is a shallow layer that overcooks. Halved eggs are the other one — beat a whole egg and weigh out 25 g.

Set `default_servings` once and every quantity is scaled to it without arithmetic in the conversation.

## Conversions

| Item | Weight |
|---|---|
| Flour, all-purpose, spooned and levelled | 1 cup = 120 g |
| Granulated sugar | 1 cup = 200 g |
| Brown sugar, packed | 1 cup = 220 g |
| Icing sugar | 1 cup = 120 g |
| Butter | 1 cup = 227 g (1 US stick = 113 g) |
| Water and most liquids | 1 cup = 240 ml ≈ 240 g |
| Honey, syrup | 1 cup = 340 g |
| Rice, uncooked long-grain | 1 cup = 185 g |
| Rolled oats | 1 cup = 90 g |
| Cocoa powder | 1 cup = 85 g |
| Salt, fine | 1 tsp = 6 g (see `seasoning.md` for the brand table) |
| Large egg, out of shell | 50 g |

Temperature: `°C = (°F − 32) × 5/9`. Useful anchors: 160°C/325°F · 180°C/350°F · 200°C/400°F · 220°C/425°F · 230°C/450°F. Gas marks: 4 = 180°C, 6 = 200°C, 7 = 220°C.

Volume: 1 tbsp = 15 ml = 3 tsp (Australia is 20 ml) · 1 cup = 240 ml US, 250 ml metric, 284 ml UK Imperial pint-based · 1 fl oz = 30 ml · 1 lb = 454 g · 1 oz = 28 g.

The cup discrepancy between US, metric, and old UK measures is 4-18%, which is invisible in a stew and decisive in a cake. When a recipe's origin is unknown and the result matters, weigh.
