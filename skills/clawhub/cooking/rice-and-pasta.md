# Rice, Grains, Pasta, and Noodles

Starch behaves in exactly two ways that matter: it **absorbs water and swells** (which is cooking), and it **leaches out and thickens the surrounding liquid** (which is either a gluey mistake or the sauce, depending on the dish). Every technique here is about choosing which one you want.

**Read `## Kitchen` in `~/Clawic/data/cooking/memory.md`**: the ratio that works for the pot, the hob, and the specific rice in this cupboard is recorded there, and it beats any general table — rice ratios vary with the pot's lid seal and the burner's low setting.

## Rice by Absorption

Ratios by volume, for the absorption method: rinse until the water runs mostly clear (removes surface starch, prevents gumminess), combine with cold water, bring to a boil, cover, drop to the lowest heat, then **rest 10 minutes off the heat, lid on**. The rest is not optional — it lets the moisture gradient even out, and skipping it is the single most common cause of wet-bottomed, dry-topped rice.

| Rice | Water : rice | Simmer | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long-grain white | 1.5 : 1 | 15 min | The default |
| Basmati, rinsed and soaked 30 min | 1.25 : 1 | 12 min | Soaking gives longer, separate grains |
| Jasmine | 1.25 : 1 | 12 min | Naturally sticky-soft; rinse well |
| Short-grain / sushi | 1.1 : 1 | 12 min | Rinse until clear; season the cooked rice with vinegar, sugar, salt |
| Brown, long-grain | 2.25 : 1 | 40-45 min | The bran slows water entry, not the starch |
| Wild rice | 3 : 1 | 45-55 min | Cook like pasta and drain if unsure |
| Parboiled / converted | 2 : 1 | 20 min | The most forgiving rice there is |
| Risotto (arborio, carnaroli) | 3-4 : 1, added in stages | 18-20 min | Stirring is the point: it rubs starch off the grains |
| Paella (bomba) | 2.5-3 : 1 | 18 min | Never stirred — the opposite discipline to risotto |

Salt the water at about 1%. Above 1,000 m altitude add roughly 10% more water and time, because the boiling point is lower.

**Pilaf** method: toast the dry rice in fat with aromatics for 1-2 minutes before the liquid. It coats the grains and keeps them separate, and it is why restaurant rice is not sticky.

**Risotto**: warm stock, added a ladle at a time, kept at a simmer with near-constant stirring so grains abrade each other and release amylopectin. Finish off heat with cold butter and cheese (*mantecatura*) for the creaminess — cream is not part of it. Al dente means a faint firmness at the core; it keeps cooking on the plate, so serve it slightly looser than you want it.

**Rice cookers and pressure cookers** shift the ratios down slightly (less evaporation): typically 1.2-1.25 : 1 for white rice in a sealed cooker. Record the number that worked in `## Kitchen` once and stop re-deriving it.

## Other Grains

| Grain | Liquid : grain | Time | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quinoa | 1.75 : 1 | 15 min + 5 rest | Rinse or it is bitter (saponins) |
| Couscous (instant) | 1 : 1 | Off heat, covered, 5 min | Not a grain — it is tiny pasta |
| Bulgur | 1.5 : 1 | Soak 20-30 min in boiling water | Pre-cooked and cracked |
| Farro, pearled | 2.5 : 1 | 25-30 min | Cook like pasta and drain |
| Polenta, coarse | 4-5 : 1 | 35-45 min | Whisk into simmering liquid to avoid lumps; instant is 5 min and thinner in flavor |
| Oats, steel-cut | 3-4 : 1 | 25-30 min | Overnight soak halves it |
| Barley, pearl | 3 : 1 | 25-35 min | Releases starch — good in soups, thickens them |
| Freekeh, cracked | 2.5 : 1 | 20 min | Smoky; behaves like bulgur |

Toast any grain dry or in fat before adding liquid for a noticeable gain in flavor, and salt the cooking liquid — a grain salted afterward tastes seasoned on the outside only.

## Pasta

- **Water**: 1% salt (10 g/L). Less water than the traditional 4-5 litres is not a compromise — the starchier water it produces is better sauce material. 2 litres per 500 g is ample.
- **No oil in the water.** It coats the pasta and stops sauce adhering; stirring in the first minute is what prevents sticking.
- **Timing**: pull 1-2 minutes before the package time and finish in the sauce. Dried pasta at al dente still shows a thin pale core when bitten.
- **Finish in the pan**: pasta, sauce, and a ladle of the cooking water, tossed hard over heat for 30-60 seconds. The starch and salt in that water emulsify the fat into a sauce that clings. This step is the difference between pasta with sauce on it and a pasta dish.
- **Never rinse**, except for cold pasta salad, where you want the surface starch gone.
- **Fresh pasta** is a different ingredient: eggy, tender, cooks in 2-4 minutes, and suits butter and cream sauces. Dried semolina pasta suits oil and tomato and has more bite. Fresh is not an upgrade — it is another product.
- **Shape matching**: long thin shapes for smooth or oily sauces; tubes and ridges for chunky sauces; small shapes for soups; wide ribbons for rich meat ragù. Ridged (rigate) holds more sauce than smooth.
- Cooking water is worth keeping a cup of before draining, every single time. The one habit that most improves home pasta.

## Asian Noodles

| Noodle | Cook | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Rice noodles, thin | Soak in hot water 5-10 min, no boiling | Boiling turns them to mush; finish them in the wok |
| Rice noodles, wide fresh | Loosen in warm water, then stir-fry | They break if fried cold and stiff |
| Ramen (alkaline wheat) | Boil 1-3 min | Rinse only for cold dishes; the alkalinity gives the bite |
| Soba (buckwheat) | Boil 4-5 min | Rinse under cold water — always; surface starch makes them gluey |
| Udon | Boil 8-10 min fresh or frozen | Frozen is usually better than dried |
| Glass / cellophane (mung bean) | Soak 10 min | Absorbent — they will drink a sauce dry |
| Egg noodles, fresh | Boil 2-3 min | Toss in oil after draining if not used immediately |

Stir-fried noodles want to be slightly under-cooked, drained well, and added to a hot wok with the sauce already reduced (`heat.md`).

## Diagnostics

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Rice gummy and clumped | Not rinsed, too much water, or stirred while cooking | Rinse until nearly clear, exact ratio, lid stays on |
| Rice crunchy on top, wet below | Heat too high, or lid lifted so steam escaped | Lowest heat, lid on, 10-minute rest off heat |
| Rice burnt on the bottom | Heat too high or a thin pan | Heavy pan, lowest setting; the crisp bottom is *desirable* in tahdig and socarrat |
| Risotto gluey and heavy | Too much stirring at the end, or boiled hard | Stop stirring in the last minutes; finish off heat |
| Risotto soupy and separated | Not enough starch released, or stock added too fast | A ladle at a time, kept at a simmer |
| Pasta sticky and clumped | Not stirred in the first minute, or drained and left dry | Stir early, sauce immediately, keep some water |
| Sauce slides off the pasta | Never emulsified in the pan | Finish pasta in the sauce with starchy water |
| Quinoa bitter | Saponins not rinsed off | Rinse in a fine sieve for 30 seconds |
| Polenta lumpy | Poured in too fast | Rain it in while whisking, or mix with cold water first |
| Noodles broke apart in the wok | Too cold, too dry, or over-boiled first | Under-cook, loosen with a splash of water, use tongs not a spatula |
