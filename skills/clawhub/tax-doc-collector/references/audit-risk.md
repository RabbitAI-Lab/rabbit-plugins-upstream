# Audit Risk Factors

## Overview

The IRS uses automated screening (DIF scores — Discriminant Information
Function) to flag returns for audit. Understanding these triggers helps you
prepare documentation before you need it.

**Disclaimer:** This tool provides general guidance based on publicly known
IRS audit factors. It is NOT tax advice. Always consult a qualified tax
professional.

## High-Risk Categories

### 1. Large Meals & Entertainment Deductions
- **Trigger**: Meals exceeding 2-3% of gross revenue (Schedule C)
- **Why**: IRS knows this category is heavily abused
- **Mitigation**: Log date, attendees, business purpose, and amount
- **Rule**: Only 50% deductible; keep itemized receipts (not credit card totals)

### 2. Charitable Contributions Disproportionate to Income
- **Trigger**: Charity > 5-10% of AGI (varies by income bracket)
- **Why**: Overstating donations is a common evasion tactic
- **Mitigation**: Keep written acknowledgments for gifts ≥$250
- **Non-cash**: Document item descriptions and fair market value; get receipt
  from organization

### 3. Home Office Deduction with No Profit
- **Trigger**: Claiming home office when business shows a loss 3+ out of 5 years
- **Why**: IRS may reclassify "business" as a hobby
- **Mitigation**: Demonstrate profit motive (marketing, business plan, growth)
- **Hobby loss rule**: 2 of 5 years must show profit

### 4. Vehicle Expenses Without Mileage Log
- **Trigger**: Claiming 100% business use, or large vehicle deduction
- **Why**: Impossible to verify without a contemporaneous log
- **Mitigation**: Keep a log with date, miles, starting/ending locations, purpose
- **Standard mileage** is generally safer than actual expenses

### 5. Cash Transactions Over $10,000
- **Trigger**: Any cash transaction ≥$10,000 (Form 8300 required)
- **Why**: Anti-money laundering rules
- **Mitigation**: Report properly; never "structure" transactions to avoid

### 6. Round Numbers
- **Trigger**: Deductions like $5,000 exactly, $1,200 exactly
- **Why**: Looks estimated rather than actual
- **Mitigation**: Log actual amounts ($4,987.32 not $5,000)

### 7. Independent Contractor (1099) Income
- **Trigger**: High 1099 income relative to W-2, especially in cash-heavy industries
- **Why**: Income underreporting is the largest tax gap component
- **Mitigation**: Report all income including cash; match 1099s to your records

### 8. Claiming Dependents Incorrectly
- **Trigger**: Multiple returns claiming same dependent
- **Why**: Custody disputes, divorce situations
- **Mitigation**: Only one parent can claim; have custody agreement documented

### 9. Cryptocurrency Transactions
- **Trigger**: Any crypto activity (now asked on Form 1040)
- **Why**: IRS has increased enforcement on crypto
- **Mitigation**: Track every transaction; report gains/losses on Schedule D

### 10. Foreign Accounts
- **Trigger**: FBAR (FinCEN 114) if aggregate >$10,000 in foreign accounts
- **Why**: Anti-tax-evasion (FATCA)
- **Mitigation**: Report all foreign accounts; FBAR penalties are severe

## This Tool's Risk Scoring

The tool assigns risk points based on:

| Factor | Risk Points |
|--------|-------------|
| Meals > 3% of gross revenue | 3 |
| Charity > 5% of AGI | 2 |
| Round number expense | 1 each |
| Mileage without purpose logged | 2 |
| Cash expense > $200 | 1 |
| Home office without business income | 3 |
| Category "other" used excessively | 2 |

### Risk Levels
- **0-3 points**: Low risk — standard documentation sufficient
- **4-7 points**: Moderate risk — keep detailed records
- **8+ points**: High risk — consult a CPA before filing

## Best Practices for Audit Protection

1. **Contemporaneous records**: Log expenses when they happen, not later
2. **Receipts**: Keep all receipts over $75 (IRS requirement for travel/meals)
3. **Business purpose**: Always note WHY an expense is deductible
4. **Separate accounts**: Never mix personal and business expenses
5. **Mileage log**: Track date, miles, start/end location, purpose
6. **Documentation**: Bank statements + receipts + calendar entries
7. **Consistency**: Same categories year over year
8. **Professional help**: A CPA is worth the cost for complex situations
