## 5. Resistance Management

### Typical Resistance Patterns & Responses

| Likely Resistance | Who Says It | Response Strategy | Messenger |
|-------------------|-------------|-------------------|-----------|
| "I'm too busy -- no time to learn" | Store Manager / Head Chef | "Exactly because you're busy, the system saves you time. You'll be less busy after you learn it." | VP of Operations |
| "I'm too old -- can't learn" | Senior Manager / Senior Chef | "You only need to learn 3 things you already do every day. Two taps on the phone." | Super-user (similar age) |
| "The system keeps lagging -- unusable" | Various (excuse + actual) | Investigate immediately: is it network, hardware, or the system? | IT |
| "Using the system didn't make us more money" | Store Manager | "Store XX improved labor efficiency by 15% after adoption. What you save is what you earn." | Champion Store Manager |
| "We were doing fine before" | High-performing Store Manager | "Nobody's dismissing what you did before. We're helping good become even better." | VP of Operations |
| "HQ is spying on us" | Store Manager / Head Chef | 1-on-1: "Honestly, no. Think about it -- does the boss even yell at you in meetings anymore? They want numbers, not to watch you." | VP of Operations |

### Resistance Escalation Path

```
Mild Resistance (verbal complaints)
  -> Peer persuasion + champion case study + give them a face-saving way in
  -> Monitor for 1--2 weeks

Moderate Resistance (skipping training / refusing to use the system)
  -> 1-on-1 conversation + understand the real reason + solve actual problems
  -> If unreasonable demands -> clarify: "This is part of the job"

Severe Resistance (inciting others / deliberate sabotage)
  -> Regional Manager / VP of Operations intervention meeting
  -> Repeated and unrepentant -> per company disciplinary process (last resort; 99% of cases never reach here)
```
