# Case 1: A Street Food Stall's Digital Start

## Background

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Name** | Wang's Kitchen (pseudonym) |
| **Type** | Mom-and-pop street food stall |
| **Scale** | 1 location, 2 people (husband and wife) |
| **Size** | 40 m2 |
| **Location** | Neighborhood storefront, tier-2 city |
| **Daily Footfall** | 80--120 customers |
| **Avg. Ticket** | $2 |
| **Monthly Revenue** | ~$5,500 |

## Pre-Digital State

### Pain Points
1. **Fully paper-based ordering**: During peak hours, customers shout orders; the couple makes mistakes and a missed item means a 30-minute wait for the customer.
2. **Manual accounting**: Every night after closing, the wife spends 30+ minutes reconciling, often with errors.
3. **Scared of delivery apps**: DoorDash / Uber Eats felt overwhelming -- "We don't know how to set it up."
4. **Cash-heavy**: Making change eats up time; impatient customers in line walk away.
5. **No idea about profit**: Only at month-end do they know whether they made or lost money.

### R-DMM Assessment: L1.0
- Technology: 1.0 -- No systems at all, fully manual
- Operations: 1.0 -- Verbal coordination only
- Data: 1.0 -- Paper ledger
- Organization: 1.0 -- Husband-and-wife team
- Customer: 1.0 -- Walk-in only

## Digital Solution

### Step 1: The Basic Trio (~$28/month)

1. **A cloud POS** (Toast Starter / Square for Restaurants -- free tier)
   - One-tap menu entry (barcode scanner + touchscreen supported)
   - Daily revenue report auto-generated (viewable on mobile)
   - Credit card / contactless payment acceptance

2. **List on DoorDash + Uber Eats**: free basic tier
   - Start with 10 best-selling dishes (greatest hits)
   - 3--5 km delivery radius
   - No extra staff needed; the couple keeps cooking

3. **Contactless payment terminal / QR code payment**: free
   - Stick a QR code on the counter
   - Auto-reconciliation with POS
   - Cash share drops from 80% to 20%

### What We Decided NOT to Buy (Explicitly Skipped)
- No loyalty/membership system (not worth it for a single location)
- No inventory management system (all ingredients bought fresh daily from the market next door)
- No branded app/mini-program (walk-in + delivery platforms cover all demand)
- No KDS screen (the kitchen is one person; verbal callouts or a chime are enough)

## Implementation

### Timeline

| Time | Action | Investment | Result |
|------|--------|------------|--------|
| Week 1 | Install POS + learn to use it | $0 + 3 days learning | Order speed improved, no more missed items |
| Week 2 | Go live on DoorDash + Uber Eats | $0 + half-day verification | 7 delivery orders on day one (a surprise!) |
| Weeks 3--4 | Familiarization + tuning | $0 | Delivery orders stabilized at 15--20/day |

### Critical Success Factors
1. **Son's help**: The 20-year-old son, comfortable with smartphones, set up the systems for his parents.
2. **Neighbor already using it**: "The noodle shop next door started last year; they showed us how."
3. **Simplicity is king**: Only 3 tools to learn -- easy to remember, hard to forget.

## Results

### Data After 6 Months

| Metric | Before | After | Change |
|--------|:------:|:-----:|:------:|
| Monthly Revenue | ~$5,500 | ~$7,200 | **+30%** |
| Delivery Revenue | $0 | ~$1,650/month | New channel |
| Daily Reconciliation Time | 30+ min | 2 min | **-93%** |
| Missed/Wrong Orders | 2--3/day | 0 | 100% eliminated |
| Cash Share | 80% | 20% | Change-making time drastically reduced |
| Wife's Closing Time | 10:30 PM | 9:30 PM | Home 1 hour earlier |

### ROI
- Investment: $0 (free-tier products)
- Annualized incremental revenue: ~($7,200 - $5,500) x 12 = $20,400
- ROI: infinite (investment was zero)

## Lessons Learned

### What Went Right
1. **Bought only what was needed.** Did not get upsold into a full-blown suite by a sales rep.
2. **Asked a young person for help.** Digital tools can be intimidating for older generations; having someone guide you makes all the difference.
3. **Test before scaling.** Started with just 10 dishes on delivery; expanded once the process was stable.

### If We Could Do It Again
1. Should have installed the POS from day one -- wasted months on handwritten records.
2. Should have launched delivery earlier -- demand from the neighborhood was much larger than expected.

## Universal Takeaway

> The core principle of digitizing a small street-side eatery: **subtract, don't add**. You need exactly three things: get paid, take delivery orders, see your numbers. Buy nothing extra. Channel the time and energy you save into making better food and taking better care of your guests -- that is the real competitive advantage of a small shop.
