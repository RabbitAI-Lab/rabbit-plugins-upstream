# 03-Investment Decision & Budget Approval

## Triggers
- After ROI analysis is complete, need to form a formal investment recommendation for management approval

---

## The Unique Challenges of Restaurant Digital Investment Decisions

### Why Do Restaurant Owners Hesitate?

| Owner's Concern | Real Reason | Response Strategy |
|----------------|-------------|-------------------|
| "It's too expensive" | Only seeing the upfront cost, not the long-term returns | Place the 3-year TCO and benefits side by side |
| "We don't need something this complex" | Previous failed IT project -- psychological scar tissue | Start with a small pilot (1 location), let the data speak |
| "What if the vendor goes out of business?" | Restaurant SaaS failure rate ~30%/year | Choose financially healthy vendors + no data lock-in |
| "Let's wait until we're bigger" | Think digital is "something you do once you have scale" | Wrong: Digital is the tool to grow and strengthen, not a luxury you buy after you're big |
| "I know a restaurant owner who implemented a system and it didn't help" | Only seeing the input, not the execution difference | Same system, different results: The difference is execution, not the system |

## Step 1: Investment Proposal

### Standard Structure (10-Slide Deck or 5-Page Document)

**Use case**: Investment proposal for CEO / Board of Directors

```
1. Cover + One-Sentence Conclusion ("Recommend investing $XX K to complete XX digital build within X quarters")

2. Problem & Opportunity
   - Current biggest pain points (max 3, data-driven: "Store managers waste 30 min/day on spreadsheets")
   - What competitors are doing ("Competitor XX has used AI scheduling to push labor cost to 16%, we're at 22%")
   - Opportunity cost of NOT digitizing

3. Digital Vision
   - What things look like in 3 years (1 sentence + 1 paragraph)

4. Investment Plan
   - Overall solution overview (architecture diagram + core system list)
   - Phased investment breakdown
   - 3-Year TCO summary table

5. Expected Returns
   - ROI analysis (conservative / baseline / optimistic 3 scenarios)
   - Payback period
   - Non-quantified strategic value

6. Risks & Mitigation
   - Top 5 risks + mitigation measures (be honest, don't oversell)

7. Implementation Roadmap
   - Phase 1: M1-M6 -> What, how much, what results will be visible
   - Phase 2: M7-M18 -> ...
   - Phase 3: M19-M36 -> ...

8. Peer Case Studies
   - 2-3 same-scale / same-format restaurant enterprises' digital investment and results

9. Resource Requirements
   - What decisions are needed from the owner
   - What internal resources are required
   - What external partners are needed

10. Next Steps
    - Specific action plan for the first 30 days after approval
```

## Step 2: Phased Budgeting

### Why Must Budgeting Be Phased?

> "Asking for $1M all at once will terrify the owner. But asking for $100K for a 1-month pilot barely raises an eyebrow."

### Phased Budget Table Template

| Phase | Timeline | Investment ($K) | % of Total | Key Acceptance Criteria |
|-------|----------|:---:|:---:|--------------------------|
| P1-Foundation | M1-M6 | XX | 25-35% | XX system covering XX locations, XX metric reaching XX |
| P2-Enhancement | M7-M18 | XX | 35-45% | Data integrated, XX efficiency improved by XX% |
| P3-Intelligence | M19-M36 | XX | 25-35% | AI scenarios live, ROI positive |
| **Total** | **36 months** | **XXX** | **100%** | |

### Budget Approval Cadence

| Approval Gate | Approval Content | Amount | Decision Maker |
|---------------|------------------|:---:|----------------|
| 1st | Phase 1 Budget | Small | VP Ops + CEO |
| 2nd (after P1 passes) | Phase 2 Budget | Medium | CEO + Board |
| 3rd (after P2 passes) | Phase 3 Budget | Large | Board |

> Core principle: **Use P1 results to justify the P2 budget. Use P2 data to prove the P3 investment.**
> Don't ask for 3 years of funding all at once. Prove this phase's ROI before requesting the next phase's budget.

## Step 4: Executive Communication Guide

### Messaging by Stakeholder

| Role | What They Care About | How to Talk to Them |
|------|---------------------|---------------------|
| CEO / Owner | How much, when it pays back, what are the risks | "Invest $XX K, payback in X months, risks manageable" |
| CFO | Cash flow, TCO, ROI, tax implications | "3-Year TCO = $XX K, conservative scenario ROI = XX%, payback = XX months" |
| COO | Operational impact, location readiness, transition period | "Each location needs XX hours of training, efficiency drops only XX% during XX-week transition" |
| CTO / CIO | Technical feasibility, integration complexity, team capability | "Technology maturity XX, integration interfaces XX, team needs to supplement XX capability" |

### The Golden Rules of Communication

```
1. Lead with the conclusion, then present the evidence
   The owner doesn't want a 1-hour presentation. They want to know: "Should we invest? How much? When will I see results?"

2. Speak in dollars, not technical jargon
   Don't say: "We need to deploy a cloud-native microservices architecture"
   Say: "Invest $90K. Each location saves 1 FTE = $35K/year. 10 locations x $35K = $350K. Payback in ~3 months."

3. Prepare the "elevator pitch"
   If you only have 2 minutes with the CEO in the elevator, what do you say?
   -> "Our store managers spend 40 min/day reconciling accounts. After implementing automated reconciliation,
       each location saves 30 min/day. You can see every location's revenue, costs, and profit in real-time
       on your phone. Project investment: $90K. Payback: 6 months."

4. Proactively address risks, don't avoid them
   What the owner fears most isn't risk -- it's "you deliberately hiding the risks."
   Honestly listing the Top 5 risks + mitigation measures for each actually increases trust.

5. Give the owner an "exit ramp"
   Design the plan as independently executable modules that can be paused or adjusted at any point.
   "Let's test in 5 locations for 2 months. If the data looks good, we'll scale." -- the owner is much more likely to agree.
```

## Common Objections & Responses

| Objection | Response Strategy |
|-----------|-------------------|
| "It's too expensive, we don't have the budget" | "It's not spending $900K all at once. Start with $90K to solve the most painful problem first." |
| "We're too small, not worth it" | "Digital isn't an expense, it's an investment. 10 locations with the right systems can achieve the labor efficiency of 15 locations." |
| "Business is slow right now, bad timing" | "When business is good you have no time. When business is slow you need efficiency even more." |
| "We have internal IT handling this" | "Internal IT can do great work. A consultant brings peer experience and lessons learned from others' mistakes." |
| "Let's wait and see what competitors do" | "By then, competitors already have the cost advantage. First movers crush late movers with efficiency." |
