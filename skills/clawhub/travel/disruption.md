# Disruption — Cancellations, Delays, Lost Bags, Claims

Two jobs, in this order and never mixed: **get the trip working again**, then **collect what is owed**. People who argue about compensation at the desk lose the last seat on the rebooking flight.

Compensation regimes are law and change slowly, but amounts and thresholds are amended — verify the current figures on the regulator's own site before promising a number, and note the date checked.

## First Ten Minutes

1. **Get in two queues at once**: the physical desk and the airline's phone line or app. Whichever answers first wins, and the app often rebooks before the queue moves. A carrier's overseas call centre may answer in one minute while the local one is at ninety.
2. **Take the first acceptable rebooking**, not the perfect one. Availability drops in minutes and a later flight to the right city beats a same-day flight to the wrong one.
3. **Ask for what is owed in care** — meals, and a hotel when the delay crosses a night — and ask before joining the rebooking queue, because care is owed regardless of the cause.
4. **Photograph the departure board** showing the flight and the word cancelled or the delay, and keep every boarding pass and receipt. The evidence disappears within the hour and the claim is filed weeks later.
5. **Do not accept a voucher as a settlement** in the first ten minutes. Accepting a goodwill voucher can extinguish a statutory claim worth several times more.
6. **Tell the downstream providers.** A hotel told at 22:00 that you are delayed will usually hold the room; a no-show will not.

## Which Regime Applies

| You are | Regime | What it gives |
|---|---|---|
| Departing any airport in the EU, any carrier | EU261 | Care, rerouting or refund, and fixed compensation by distance when the cause is not extraordinary |
| Arriving in the EU on an EU carrier | EU261 | Same |
| Departing or arriving UK, per its own rules | UK261 | Mirrors EU261 in sterling |
| On a US domestic itinerary | US DOT rules | **No delay compensation exists.** A refund is owed if the airline cancels or significantly changes and you decline the alternative; involuntary denied boarding is separately compensated |
| On any international flight between treaty states | Montreal Convention | Liability for delay, and for baggage up to roughly 1,288 SDR per passenger |
| On a train, ferry or coach in the EU | Sector-specific EU rules | Reduced ticket price or refund at defined delay thresholds |
| Anywhere else | The carrier's conditions of carriage, plus your insurance | Read the contract; the insurance is often the real recourse |

Two rules that decide most arguments: **the departure airport and the carrier's nationality set the regime**, not your own; and **the ticket price is irrelevant** to statutory compensation — it is set by distance, not by fare.

## EU And UK Air Passenger Rights

The shape, as it has stood since the regulation came into force:

- **Right to care**, regardless of the cause, once a delay passes a threshold that shortens with distance: meals and refreshments, two communications, and hotel plus transfers when it runs overnight. This applies even when the cause is weather or air-traffic control.
- **Right to rerouting or refund** when a flight is cancelled: at the earliest opportunity, at comparable conditions, or a full refund of the unused part.
- **Fixed compensation** for cancellation with insufficient notice, or arrival delayed by 3 hours or more: **€250** for flights up to 1,500 km, **€400** for intra-EU over 1,500 km and other flights 1,500-3,500 km, **€600** above 3,500 km. UK261 mirrors it at **£220 / £350 / £520**.
- **Extraordinary circumstances** — weather, air-traffic control strikes, political instability, security risk — remove the compensation but never the care. A **carrier's own staff strike** and a technical fault are generally not extraordinary, which is where most refused claims are wrongly refused.
- The measure is **arrival delay at the final destination on the booking**, so a short first-leg delay that causes a missed connection can produce a full compensation claim.
- Claim windows run from roughly two to six years by member state. There is no rush; there is a limit.

## Delayed, Damaged Or Lost Bags

- **Report before leaving the airport.** File the Property Irregularity Report at the baggage desk and keep the reference. Leaving without one materially weakens everything that follows.
- **Delayed**: buy the essentials you need and keep the receipts. Reasonable interim expenses are recoverable; the airline's definition of reasonable is narrower than yours, so buy what a reasonable person would.
- **Lost**: most carriers declare a bag lost after 21 days, at which point the Montreal Convention liability applies — roughly 1,288 SDR per passenger, which is the *ceiling* for everything in the bag, not per item. This is why valuables and irreplaceables travel in hand luggage (`travel-day.md`).
- **Damaged**: report within 7 days of receipt; delayed-bag claims within 21 days of delivery. These windows are short and they are the ones that get missed.
- The packing photograph and the tracker location, if there is one, transform the conversation from a dispute into a retrieval.

## Denied Boarding And Downgrades

- **Volunteering** for a bumped flight is a negotiation: the first offer is not the last, ask for cash rather than a voucher, ask for confirmed seats on the next flight rather than standby, and ask for lounge access and meals.
- **Involuntary denied boarding** carries statutory compensation in the EU/UK and the US, and it is separate from the rebooking.
- **Downgrade** entitles a reimbursement of a percentage of the ticket price for that leg under EU rules — it is a refund of the difference in class, not compensation, and it must be claimed.

## Stranded

The order that keeps costs recoverable: care from the carrier first; if the carrier is not providing it, book something reasonable and keep the receipts, because reasonable expenses incurred when care is refused are recoverable; then insurance for what the carrier will not cover.

If the disruption is regional — a strike, a closure, an eruption — the airline queue is the slowest route out. Reroute overland, or from a different airport, and let the refund follow. Money spent avoiding a three-day wait is usually recoverable and always worth it.

## Theft And Lost Documents

1. **Police report first**, in the jurisdiction where it happened, with a reference number. Both the insurer and the consulate require it, and getting one after leaving the country is close to impossible.
2. **Cards blocked** through the numbers on the emergency card, not through a search on a stranger's phone.
3. **Embassy or consulate** for a stolen passport: an emergency travel document is usually issued for a direct route home and can take a day or more. It generally does not carry existing visas, which may have to be reissued (`documents.md`).
4. **Devices**: remote-lock and remote-wipe from another device, change the passwords for anything that was signed in, and treat the authenticator app as compromised if it was on the stolen phone.
5. **Insurance notification within the policy's window**, which is often 24-48 hours for theft.

## Provider Failure

An airline, tour operator or platform ceasing to trade mid-trip is a different problem: the statutory regime dies with the carrier. The recourses, in order of reliability, are the credit-card chargeback (which is the single strongest reason to pay for travel by credit card), any statutory insolvency protection scheme covering package holidays in that country, and travel insurance with a scheduled-airline-failure clause, which most basic policies exclude.

## Filing A Claim That Gets Paid

A claim is a small evidence file, and the failures are administrative:

- Locator, flight numbers, dates, and the **scheduled versus actual arrival time**
- The reason given by the carrier, in writing if possible, and the departure-board photograph
- Every receipt, and every boarding pass
- What was already paid — a voucher, a refund, a rebooking — because claiming twice for the same loss ends the claim

Claim direct with the carrier first: agencies that file for you take 25-40% of an amount most people can recover with one form. Escalate to the national enforcement body when the carrier refuses on grounds that do not hold. Insurance is claimed after the carrier, for the part the carrier does not owe, and both insurers and carriers ask what the other has paid.

**Write the whole episode in the same turn it resolves**: `artifacts/claim-<provider>-<yyyy-mm>.md` with the facts, the amounts, the dates and the outcome, its `## Boxes` line, the claim window in `## Due` while it is open, the updated `status` and refund in `~/Clawic/data/bookings/<year>.md`, and the money in the trip dossier. A claim with no record is refiled from memory a year later and refused. Destinations: `memory-template.md`.
