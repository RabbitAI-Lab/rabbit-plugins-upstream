# Safety — Risk, Scams, Devices, And The Emergency Card

Risk in travel is mostly mundane: road traffic, water, theft, and being somewhere without a plan when something ordinary goes wrong. The dramatic risks people research are rarely the ones that materialise.

## The Emergency Card

One artifact, carried physically and stored digitally, updated before each trip. It exists for the scenario where the phone is gone, so a note on the phone is not a copy of it:

- Insurance provider, policy number, 24-hour line — the number that works from abroad, with the country code
- Card-blocking numbers for every card carried, with country codes
- Embassy or consulate for the destination: address and phone
- Next of kin, and one person at home who has the itinerary
- Blood group, allergies, current medication with generic names, relevant conditions — from `~/Clawic/data/health/profile.md`
- The address of the first night's accommodation, in the local language and script
- Passport last four and where the copies are (`1password:Travel/Passport`, never the number itself)

Template in `memory-template.md`. The card is a one-time build and a two-minute update per trip; it is the highest-value artifact in this skill.

## Advisories And What They Do To Insurance

Government advisories are graded, and the grade has a financial consequence: **most policies void cover for travel against a home-government advisory of the highest levels**, and the change can happen while the traveller is already in the country.

- Read the advisory for the specific **region**, not the country. Advisories are frequently issued for a border strip while the rest of the country is unaffected, and a country-level reading either scares people off or gives false comfort.
- Check the policy's advisory clause **before** the trip, and record what it says in the policy's row in `## Documents`.
- If an advisory is raised mid-trip, the date matters for the claim: note it, and get any decision to stay or leave documented.
- Register with the home country's traveller registration scheme where one exists for genuinely remote or unstable destinations. It costs nothing and it is how consulates find people.

## Actual Risk Ranking

For the ordinary traveller, roughly in order of how often it causes serious harm:

1. **Road traffic** — the largest cause of preventable death and injury abroad by a wide margin. Night driving on unfamiliar rural roads, motorbikes and scooters without a helmet or the right licence class, and unbelted taxis are the specific exposures. Most travel insurance excludes motorbike injury without the correct licence, which means a scooter rental abroad is often uninsured (`health.md`).
2. **Water** — unfamiliar currents, no lifeguard, alcohol, and rip tides at beaches with no signage.
3. **Alcohol and altered judgement**, which multiplies every other item on this list and is present in most incidents involving tourists.
4. **Theft** — common, rarely violent, and almost entirely preventable.
5. **Health events** — usually food, insects and altitude (`health.md`).
6. **Violent crime and terrorism** — the most researched and, for most destinations, statistically the least likely.

The practical consequence: the traveller who researches the political situation for a week and then rides a scooter without a helmet has optimised the wrong variable.

## Theft And The Scam Catalogue

Prevention that works, in order of effect: nothing in a back pocket; a bag worn in front on transport and in crowds; two cards in two places (`money.md`); nothing irreplaceable carried; the day's cash separate from the reserve; and phones not held loosely at the edge of a road.

The recurring scams, which are the same everywhere with local costumes:

| Scam | Shape |
|---|---|
| Taxi meter "broken" | Agree the fare before the door closes, or use a metered or app-based ride |
| The helpful stranger at the ATM | Nobody helps at an ATM. Cover the PIN, decline conversion, walk away with the card in hand |
| Distraction pairs | One creates contact — a spill, a petition, a dropped item, a bird — the other takes the bag |
| The closed attraction | A stranger says the site is shut today and offers an alternative, which is a shop |
| Fake officials | Real police do not inspect wallets in the street. Ask to walk to the station |
| Bar and club bill inflation | Prices appear at the end; the exit is blocked. Do not follow a tout to a second venue |
| Rental damage claim | Photograph and video every vehicle, scooter and jet ski, all sides, before leaving with it |
| Free bracelet, sprig, photograph | Something is placed on you, then payment is demanded |
| Wrong change or a swapped note | Count in front of them, name the note as you hand it over |
| Anything urgent that requires cash now | Urgency is the tell. Nothing legitimate collapses in the next sixty seconds |

## Arrival And Transport Safety

Arrival is the exposed moment: tired, visibly new, carrying everything, no local currency, no data. Decide the airport-to-accommodation route **before landing** and record what worked in the place file. Prefer official ranks, pre-booked transfers or a ride app already installed and set up at home. Confirm the plate before getting in, and share the ride with someone at home when the app supports it.

Ground transport: seatbelt wherever one exists; refuse a motorbike without a helmet; avoid overnight rural road travel where the roads are the risk; and check whether the destination's ferries and buses have a known safety record before treating them as equivalent to home.

## Solo, And Travelling While Visible

Everyone travelling alone benefits from the same protocol; the exposure differs by who is visibly travelling.

- One person at home has the itinerary and the accommodation address, and a check-in rhythm agreed in advance — including what they should do if a check-in is missed.
- Room choice: not ground floor onto a street, door locks verified on entry, and the accommodation's own address known before arriving.
- Arrival before dark on the first day of any new place, which is worth an entire flight-time inconvenience.
- Vagueness is a valid answer to a stranger asking where you are staying or whether you are alone.
- Local norms about dress, unaccompanied women, LGBT status and photography vary from irrelevant to legally dangerous. Research the legal reality of the destination for the specific traveller — this is a legal question in some countries, not a cultural preference — and record it in the place file.

## Devices, Data, And Borders

- **Public Wi-Fi**: anything sensitive goes over a VPN or over mobile data, not over an airport network.
- **Border device inspection** is legal in many countries and there is no universal right to refuse. Travel with a device that is clean by design when crossing into somewhere it matters, sign out of what does not need to be signed in, and know that unlocking is often the price of entry.
- **Two-factor codes**: an authenticator on the same phone as everything else means a stolen phone locks you out of the accounts needed to fix a stolen phone. Backup codes, printed, in the bag that is not the phone.
- **Backups before departure**: the photographs from the trip so far, uploaded whenever there is Wi-Fi. Losing the phone is annoying; losing the only copy of the trip is the part people grieve.
- Location-tagged posting in real time announces an empty home and a current position. Posting on a delay costs nothing.

## Leaving Home Empty

Mail held or collected, a light on a timer, a neighbour or friend with a key and the return date, the heating set so nothing freezes, the water off for a long trip, and anyone who needs to know that the house is empty told deliberately rather than through a public post. The person with the key goes in the shared `~/Clawic/data/contacts/contacts.md` as a pointer, and the alarm code goes nowhere near any file (`memory-template.md`, Secrets).

**After each trip, or whenever a country-specific risk, scam or safe-transfer route is learned**, write it in the same turn into `artifacts/place-<name>.md`, and rebuild `artifacts/emergency-card.md` when any of its numbers changes — both with their `## Boxes` lines. An advisory that affected a decision goes in the trip dossier with its date, because it is what an insurer will ask about.
