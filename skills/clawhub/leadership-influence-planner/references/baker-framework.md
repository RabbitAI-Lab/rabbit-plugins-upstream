# Baker Influence Framework Notes

Source context: MindTools summary of Baker's 4 Strategies of Influence, based on Tim Baker's The New Influencing Toolkit. The framework describes four influence strategies: Investigation, Calculation, Motivation, and Collaboration. These combine Push/Pull style with Logical/Emotional approach.

## Axes

Push: direct, assertive, leader-driven.
Pull: indirect, invitational, participatory.
Logical: facts, rational argument, evidence, tradeoffs.
Emotional: values, identity, story, trust, morale, belonging.

## Quadrants

Investigation: Push + Logical. Use facts, figures, analysis, charts, structured argument, and direct recommendation.

Calculation: Pull + Logical. Help people reason through benefits, costs, incentives, tradeoffs, and consequences.

Motivation: Push + Emotional. Rally people with purpose, vision, standards, urgency, confidence, and a direct call to action.

Collaboration: Pull + Emotional. Build trust, listen actively, share ownership, involve people in the decision or execution plan.

## Selection Heuristic

- If they do not understand or believe the facts, use Investigation.
- If they understand but do not see value, use Calculation.
- If they see value but lack energy or courage, use Motivation.
- If they resist because they feel excluded, disrespected, controlled, or unsafe, use Collaboration.

## Ethical Test

Before recommending an influence plan, check:

- Is the goal legitimate?
- Is the information truthful and complete enough for fair choice?
- Does the other person keep meaningful agency?
- Would the user be comfortable if the influence strategy were visible afterward?

If the answer is no, revise the plan.
