# Visual Direction

Create a specific visual world from the subject, brand materials, audience, and emotional target.

## Direction sheet

Define before production:

- visual premise and three adjectives with observable meaning;
- palette roles: field, surface, signal, type, atmospheric accent;
- typography roles and scale behavior;
- image treatment, crop logic, grain/texture, and material qualities;
- composition rule: centered monument, editorial field, diagonal flow, modular stage, or another deliberate system;
- depth model: flat, layered 2.5D, or true 3D;
- light model and transition logic;
- what the experience explicitly does not look like.

Avoid moodboard collage. Convert references into rules such as “large quiet fields interrupted by one moving specimen,” not copied layouts.

## Anti-generic constraints

Do not default to a dark hero, glowing purple/blue gradient, glass cards, three feature columns, pill buttons, and repeated rounded rectangles. Cards are valid only when the content is genuinely a set of independent comparable objects.

Use decoration sparingly. Prefer one coherent material or spatial metaphor over unrelated effects.

## Responsive art direction

Treat mobile as a different stage, not a scaled desktop. Recompose crops, line breaks, travel distance, pin duration, and interaction. Preserve the premise even when the exact choreography changes.
