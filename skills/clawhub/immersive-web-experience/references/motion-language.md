# Motion Language

## Define a grammar

Assign motion roles:

- orient: establish location or state;
- connect: carry an object or property between scenes;
- reveal: control reading order;
- transform: show a meaningful state change;
- respond: acknowledge input;
- resolve: settle attention and permit action.

## Timing families

Use a small family rather than arbitrary durations. Example starting points, adjusted to content and device:

- micro response: 120–240 ms;
- interface transition: 280–600 ms;
- scene beat: 600–1200 ms;
- cinematic passage: 1200–2400 ms or scroll-linked.

## Easing families

Choose by physical meaning: crisp deceleration for interface response, strong expo/power curves for cinematic reveals, and restrained elastic/spring behavior only for materials that imply it. Avoid one easing everywhere.

## Choreography

Lead with the element that explains the change. Use overlap to imply causality and separation to create emphasis. Staggers should follow reading order, geometry, or propagation—not index by habit.

Prefer motion paths and transforms that preserve spatial logic. Abrupt direction reversals, arbitrary rotations, and simultaneous movement of everything destroy hierarchy.

## Reduced motion translation

Do not merely shorten every duration. Replace spatial travel, scrubbing, parallax, and camera movement with discrete state changes, restrained dissolves, or immediate layout. Preserve content order and state feedback.
