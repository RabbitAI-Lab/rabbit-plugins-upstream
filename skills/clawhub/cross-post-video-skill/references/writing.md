# Writing the text for each platform

One video, three texts. They share the facts and nothing else. Write each from the video itself,
not from one of the other texts.

## Before writing

Know what the video says and shows: read the transcript or script, or ask. Write down in one
sentence what a viewer gets from it. Collect the concrete things in it: names, numbers, places, the
result. Those go in every text; adjectives don't have to.

## Rules for all three

- **The first line stands alone.** Each app shows only the start of the text before "more". Nobody
  publishes the exact cut-off, so don't write to a number: make the first line short and complete.
- **Say what the video is about in plain words.** The text is what each app's search reads.
- **Don't repeat the spoken hook word for word** (when you have the script; if you don't, ask for
  the opening line). The viewer hears it; the text adds to it.
- **Write in the language the video speaks.** A Dutch voiceover gets a Dutch text unless the user
  says otherwise.
- **The text must be true without the video.** "This is why" with nothing after it is not a first
  line; search reads the words, not the picture.
- **Nothing invented.** No numbers, results, quotes or names that aren't in the video or from the user.
- **No filler.** Not "game-changer", "you won't believe", "in today's world", or a wall of emoji.
- **Hashtags name the topic.** `#spacestation`, not `#fyp`, `#viral` or `#foryou`. Instagram's head
  has said hashtags help people find a post by topic and don't increase reach.
- **Links can't be clicked** in any of the three. Say "link on my profile" only when it's true.
- **Handles are per platform.** `@name` on TikTok may be someone else on Instagram. Ask for each
  handle, or leave it out.
- **The ask, if any, is one line** and it is the user's own (follow, comment a word, watch part 2).
- **Credit what isn't the user's.** Licensed or public footage gets its credit line ("Footage:
  NASA.") in every text where it fits, before the hashtags.

## TikTok caption

- One or two short lines, then the hashtags.
- Up to 5 hashtags that name the topic (the TikTok app reports a limit of 5). One good one beats
  three vague ones; none is fine.
- Up to 2,200 characters, but short reads best here.

```
Why does a radio call with the space station last only 10 minutes?
It moves at 17,500 mph, so it's over the horizon before you finish saying hello.
Footage: NASA.
#spacestation #nasa #hamradio
```

## Instagram Reels caption

- A first line that stands alone, a blank line, then 2 to 4 lines on what the viewer gets.
- At most 5 hashtags, at the end. Instagram's limit is 5 since December 2025; the plan stops at 6.
- Up to 2,200 characters and 20 @mentions.

```
A radio call with the International Space Station lasts about 10 minutes.

The station crosses the sky at 17,500 mph. Once it drops below the horizon, the signal is gone
until the next pass. Astronaut Nichole Ayers explains what fits in that window.

Footage: NASA.
#spacestation #nasa #hamradio #astronaut
```

## YouTube Shorts

- **Title** (required, 100 characters at most): the promise of the video in the words someone would
  search. Put the main words first; the end is cut off in the feed. No `<` or `>`.
- **Description:** the first line adds what the title left out (the why, the who, the place).
  Then credits. Counted in bytes (5,000).
- **Tags:** a handful of words and phrases that name the topic, the people and the place. They go
  in the Tags field of YouTube Studio's upload details (look for "Show more"), not in the
  description.
- Up to 3 hashtags in the description show by the title. #Shorts isn't needed.

```
title:        Why a radio call with the space station lasts only 10 minutes
description:  The station moves at 17,500 mph, so it drops below the horizon about 10 minutes
              after it rises, and the call ends with it.
              Footage: NASA. #spacestation #nasa
tags:         space station, ISS, ham radio, NASA, Nichole Ayers
```

## Two accounts on one platform

Give each its own text under its account name, in its own language or for its own audience. Write
the second language natively; don't translate word for word.

## What not to claim, in the text or to the user

No platform has said that a posting order, a gap between platforms, a time of day, or a number of
hashtags increases how many people see a video. Don't write texts around those ideas and don't
promise them.
