# Property Tax Appeals — Evidence, Procedure & Reference

## 1. How property tax actually works
- `tax = assessed value × millage ÷ 1000`. The millage is set by budgets (political); the **assessment** is yours to contest.
- Most jurisdictions assess at a fraction of market value (e.g., 80%) or full value; the **assessment ratio** (assessed ÷ market) is published per-municipality as the equalization rate/director's ratio.
- Mass appraisal: models estimate thousands of properties from rolls, sales, and stale record cards. Errors are systematic, not personal — which is why appeals work.

## 2. Evidence hierarchy (what boards actually rule on)
1. **Record-card factual errors** — wrong sqft/baths/lot/features. Mechanical, near-automatic when documented (measure, photograph, sketch). No market opinion needed.
2. **Uniformity/equalization** — your ratio vs the town average ratio or vs comparable properties' ratios. Equal assessment is a legal duty in most states (e.g., uniformity clauses). Evidence: ratios of 3–5 comparable homes from public assessment rolls + your computed ratio.
3. **Market value (comparable sales)** — closed, arms-length sales: same market area, ≤0.5–1 mi, 6–12 months back, ±20–25% of living area, similar age/style/condition. Adjust for differences or drop outliers; median $/sqft beats mean. Recent purchase price of the subject is admissible and often decisive (check state rules — a few states bar using the sale itself; most allow it).
4. Inadmissible/weak: "taxes went up too much," neighbor's bill without value context, Zestimate alone (boards discount AVMs; use them only to find comps), listing prices, hardship testimony (different docket).

## 3. Procedure (typical; verify locally — names vary by state)
1. **Notice of assessment** arrives (annual or revaluation year). The appeal clock (25–45 days typical) runs from notice date or publication.
2. **Informal review** (many jurisdictions): call/visit the assessor with card errors — a share of appeals die here happily, free.
3. **Formal appeal** filed with the local board of review/assessment appeals: the form, your requested value, evidence attached. Keep stamped proof.
4. **Hearing** (5–15 min): you present; the assessor (a professional) responds; board rules within weeks. Bring 3 copies.
5. Escalation if lost: state tax tribunal / court of common pleas (small claims tier often available pro se up to certain values), then the state board. Cost-benefit check at each rung.

## 5. Where to get the numbers (all public)
- Property record card: assessor's office/website (some states' mass-appraisal portals serve cards online).
- Town average ratio / equalization rate: state department of revenue equalization reports, or computed from the rolls.
- Comparable sales: county recorder/deed search, assessor's sales lists, price-per-sqft from recent sold listings (cross-check with deed records for the closed price).
- Neighbors' assessments: the assessment roll is public — pull 5 similar homes' assessments and compute their ratios.
- Millage: tax bill or the budget ordinance.

## 6. The hearing script (3 minutes)
1. "I'm appealing the [year] assessment of $X on [address]. I request a value of $Y, supported by three items."
2. Factual errors first: "The record card shows 2,100 sqft; measured area is 1,950 (photo, sketch). At the town's $187/sqft that's $28,000 of assessed value that doesn't exist."
3. Uniformity: "My assessment ratio is 1.15 against the town average of 0.92; the five comparable homes on Oakland Ave ratio 0.88–0.97 (table)."
4. Market: "Six closed sales within 0.7 mi in the last 9 months median $172/sqft → $335,000 for my 1,950 sqft."
5. Close: "I request the assessment be set at $335,800. Thank you." (One number. Not "reduce it some.")

## 7. Cost-benefit & the contingency-firm question
- Savings persist until the next reassessment (often years): a $1,500/yr win is $4,500+ over a cycle.
- Your cost: tape measure, public records, an afternoon. Contingency firms charge 25–50% of year-1 (sometimes multi-year) savings for the same public-roll evidence. The kit exists because their packet is reproducible: card + ratios + comps.
- Best single predictor of winning: filing at all. Base rates for well-evidenced appeals commonly run 30–60%+ (higher for card errors).

## 8. When NOT to appeal
1. Equalization shows you below the town ratio — you can trigger an upward correction.
2. Comps support the assessment within ±5% and the card is clean.
3. You just bought above assessment in a state where sale price resets basis — the appeal documents the raise.
4. The requested savings don't justify the hearing time (compute: savings × years-in-cycle vs an afternoon).
5. A revaluation is scheduled within months — your informal call may fix the card anyway; save the formal shot for the new value.

## 9. Annual 30-minute audit (make it a habit)
1. Pull the card; verify sqft/baths/features.
2. Recompute your ratio vs the published town ratio.
3. Spot-check 5 comparable homes' assessments.
4. If any check fails → run this kit. If all pass → stand pat.
