# Rules and fees the guides rely on

Each rule below was checked against the official source listed with it in October 2026. Rules and fees change, and each one applies only where stated. When you tell the user a rule, name the country or platform it applies to and give the link, so they can confirm the current version before acting on it.

If the user's question needs a rule that is not here, say you have not checked it and point them to the authority or platform that sets it.

## United States

**Shipping on time.** Under the FTC Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule, a seller needs a reasonable basis for the shipping time it states, and must ship within 30 days when it states none. If the seller cannot ship on time, it must ask for the customer's consent to the delay. Without consent, it must cancel and refund all money paid for the unshipped item, without being asked. If the new date is 30 days or less, the delay notice may say that no reply counts as consent; if the new date is later or unknown, the order is cancelled automatically at the original promised time plus 30 days unless the customer agrees. Refunds are due within seven working days after cancellation for card payments and most other methods (one billing cycle only where the seller itself extended the credit).
Source: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/business-guide-ftcs-mail-internet-or-telephone-order-merchandise-rule

**"Was" prices.** Under the FTC Guides Against Deceptive Pricing, a former price used in a comparison should be the actual, bona fide price at which the item was openly offered to the public for a reasonably substantial period, in good faith.
Source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-233/section-233.1

**Reviews.** The FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule has been in effect since October 21, 2024. It covers fake reviews and testimonials, buying reviews conditioned on what they say, undisclosed reviews by insiders, company-run review sites presented as independent, suppressing negative reviews through threats or intimidation, and fake social media indicators such as bought followers. Courts can impose civil penalties for knowing violations.
Source: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers

**Children's products.** A product designed or intended primarily for children 12 or younger that is subject to a CPSC safety rule needs a Children's Product Certificate, based on testing by a CPSC-accepted third-party laboratory.
Source: https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Testing-Certification/Childrens-Product-Certificate

**No more duty-free low-value parcels.** Since 12:01 a.m. EDT on August 29, 2025, shipments from every country no longer enter the US duty-free under the $800 de minimis exemption. An executive order of February 20, 2026 continued the suspension, including for postal shipments, and CBP published rules on it in 2026. Duties and fees can apply to every parcel; how they are charged depends on the carrier and the shipping method. This area has changed several times, so tell the user to confirm the current status and to ask the supplier whether a price includes duties.
Sources: https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/07/suspending-duty-free-de-minimis-treatment-for-all-countries/ and https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/02/continuing-the-suspension-of-duty-free-de-minimis-treatment-for-all-countries/

## European Union

**14-day withdrawal.** Under Directive 2011/83/EU, consumers can withdraw from an online purchase within 14 days without giving a reason. For goods, the 14 days start when the consumer, or someone they name other than the carrier, receives the goods; for an order delivered in several parcels, when the last one arrives. If the seller did not inform the consumer of this right, the period is extended by 12 months. The seller must refund all payments, including standard delivery costs, within 14 days of being told of the withdrawal, using the same payment method. Unless the seller offered to collect the goods, it may withhold the refund until it receives them or the consumer shows proof of sending them back. The consumer pays the direct cost of returning the goods only if the seller told them beforehand. The directive lists some exceptions.
Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2011/83/oj

**Withdrawal button, from June 19, 2026.** For contracts made through a website or app, the seller must also offer an online withdrawal function, labelled "withdraw from contract here" or an equally clear wording, shown prominently and available throughout the withdrawal period. It lets the consumer send a withdrawal statement with their name, the order, and where to send the confirmation, then submit it with a button labelled "confirm withdrawal" or similar. The seller must then send an acknowledgement.
Source: Article 11a of Directive 2011/83/EU, added by Directive (EU) 2023/2673, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2023/2673/oj

**Prices include taxes.** Consumers must be shown the total price, including all taxes and additional charges, before they buy.
Source: https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/consumers/shopping/pricing-payments/index_en.htm

**Price reductions.** Any announced price reduction must show the prior price: the lowest price the seller applied during at least the 30 days before the reduction. Member states may set different rules for goods that perish quickly and for products on sale for less than 30 days.
Source: Article 6a of Directive 98/6/EC, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/1998/6/oj

**Product safety (GPSR).** Regulation (EU) 2023/988 applies from December 13, 2024. A product covered by it may not be placed on the EU market unless an economic operator established in the EU is responsible for it. An online offer aimed at EU consumers must clearly show:
- the manufacturer's name or trade mark, and a postal and an electronic address;
- if the manufacturer is not in the EU, the name, postal address, and electronic address of the responsible person in the EU;
- information identifying the product, including a picture, its type, and any other product identifier;
- any warnings or safety information, in a language consumers in that country easily understand.

Source: Articles 16 and 19, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/988/oj

**VAT on low-value imports.** For goods worth up to €150 sold to EU consumers from outside the EU, VAT can be declared and paid at the point of sale through the Import One-Stop Shop (IOSS) instead of at import, and a marketplace that facilitates the sale can be treated as the supplier. How this applies to a dropshipping store is a tax question; suggest a tax adviser.
Source: https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/customs/customs-procedures-import-and-export/customs-operations/customs-formalities-low-value-consignments_en

## United Kingdom

**14-day cancellation.** Consumers can cancel an online order up to 14 days after delivery without giving a reason. If the seller did not tell them about this right, they can cancel for up to 12 months longer.
Source: https://www.gov.uk/online-and-distance-selling-for-businesses

## Shopify

**Refunds keep the card fee.** When an order paid through Shopify Payments is refunded, the credit card fees are not returned. On third-party gateways in countries where Shopify Payments is available, Shopify's transaction fees are not refunded either.
Source: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/orders/refund-cancel-order/refunding-orders

**Third-party gateway fee.** Shopify charges a transaction fee on payments through third-party and alternative gateways, even when Shopify Payments is active. If Shopify Payments is the primary provider, PayPal payments are exempt. The rate depends on location and plan; send the user to Shopify's pricing page for theirs.
Source: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/third-party-providers

**Chargebacks.** Shopify shows the deadline to respond on the order in the admin, usually 7 to 21 days; the card network's review can take up to 75 days after that. The cardholder's bank charges a chargeback fee, which Shopify may refund if the merchant wins, depending on the country. A refund cannot be issued while a chargeback is open; the cardholder must drop it first. For an inquiry that is not yet a chargeback, a full refund prevents the chargeback, but a partial refund does not.
Sources: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/chargebacks and https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/chargebacks/resolve-chargeback

**Payout reserves and holds.** Shopify Payments can hold part or all of the money from transactions for a set period as a reserve, for example when chargebacks or refunds rise, when delivery times are long, or when volume jumps. An account hold stops payouts until its cause, such as missing information or wrong bank details, is resolved.
Sources: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/payouts/reserves and https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/account-holds

**Updating products with a CSV file.** With "Overwrite products with matching handles", the file's values replace the store's:
- A blank cell in a column that is in the file erases the store's value.
- A column left out of the file leaves the store's value unchanged.
- The only required columns for an update are URL handle and Title.
- Changing an option value (such as Option1 value) deletes the variant's ID and creates a new one, which can break apps that rely on variant IDs, such as supplier apps.
- Variant columns such as SKU need the Option1 name and Option1 value columns in the file; without them, the existing variants are deleted and replaced by a default variant.

Column names have changed over time. Work from a file the user exported from their own store and keep its header row as it is.
Source: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/import-export/using-csv

**Cost per item.** Shopify's profit reports include only products and variants that had a cost per item recorded when they were sold.
Source: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/reports-and-analytics/shopify-reports/report-types/default-reports/profit-reports

## PayPal (United States)

**Refunds keep the fee.** When a seller refunds a commercial payment, the fees paid to receive it are not returned.
Source: https://www.paypal.com/us/webapps/mpp/merchant-fees

**Seller Protection for "item not received".** Two of the requirements are shipping to the address on the Transaction Details page and providing valid proof of shipment or delivery. Read the full list on the page before telling the user they are covered.
Source: https://www.paypal.com/us/cshelp/article/what-is-paypal-seller-protection-help156

**Holds for new sellers.** PayPal can hold a new seller's first payments for up to 21 days. When tracking from an approved carrier is added, the hold is released about 24 hours after the carrier confirms delivery to the buyer's address.
Sources: https://www.paypal.com/us/cshelp/article/new-paypal-account-%E2%80%93-payments-on-hold-and-accessing-your-money-quicker-help848 and https://www.paypal.com/us/cshelp/article/how-can-i-release-my-payments-on-hold-help129

## Google Merchant Center

**Titles.** No promotional text; no price, sale, shipping, or delivery information; no capital letters for emphasis.
Source: https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6324415

**Images.** No watermarks, promotional elements or overlays, and no placeholder or generic images that do not show the product.
Source: https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6324350

**Misrepresentation.** Examples Google lists include false business identity or contact information; hiding where products ship from; not disclosing the total price with all taxes and fees, or all shipping costs, including return shipping when returns go to a different address; and starting international return windows at purchase instead of delivery.
Source: https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6150127

## Meta ads

The products in an ad must match those on its landing page.
Source: https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/

## Shipping restrictions

USPS Publication 52 treats lithium batteries, liquids, powders, and magnetized materials as restricted or needing special handling, and international shipments must follow international rules on package contents. Other carriers and countries have their own rules; tell the user to check them for products in these groups.
Source: https://pe.usps.com/text/pub52/pub52c4_001.htm

## Not covered here

These were not confirmed from an official source, so do not state them as facts:
- AliExpress buyer protection periods and dispute rules. Each order page shows its own protection period; tell the user to check it there.
- Any amount above which PayPal requires signature confirmation.
- Fee amounts and rates for Shopify, PayPal, or card chargebacks.
- Changes to EU customs duty on low-value parcels.
