# Depth recipes

Ready stacks for the shots that come up most. Each recipe gives the three planes, the air and light, a camera move for video, and the risk to watch. Adapt the objects to the place; keep the structure.

## 1. Dialogue two-shot at a table

- Foreground: a glass, a candle or a vase on the near corner of the table, out of focus, in the lower corner.
- Subject: the two people at 2.5 to 3 m, angled toward each other, each 3/4 to the lens, sharp.
- Background: the room or view 6 m or more behind, with a light source (a lamp, a window, string lights), softer.
- Air and light: light haze or warm room light behind them, and a rim light on the nearer person's hair.
- Camera: a slow truck along the table, so the foreground glass slides across.
- Risk: the foreground glass fuses with a hand. Keep it at the very edge and keep the hands busy elsewhere.

## 2. Over the shoulder (dirty single)

- Foreground: the listener's shoulder and the back of their head, out of focus, filling the left or right fifth of the frame.
- Subject: the speaker at 2 to 2.5 m, 3/4 to the lens, sharp, speaking.
- Background: the wall, window or doorway behind the speaker, with one practical light, softer.
- Air and light: a backlight separates the speaker's hair from the background.
- Camera: static, or a very small arc. Over-the-shoulder shots usually hold still.
- Risk: the foreground person turns around or grows a second face. Name them ("the back of Damien's head and his charcoal shoulder") and keep them still.

## 3. Single medium close-up

- Foreground: usually none, or a thin soft object at the bottom edge.
- Subject: the face at 1.5 to 2 m on an 85 mm lens, shot from farther back, sharp, eyes clear.
- Background: lights or a window far behind, melted into soft shapes by the long lens. A darker background behind a lit face, or the reverse.
- Air and light: a rim light separates the face. Colour separation helps: a warm face against cool blue lights.
- Camera: a static hold, or a push-in with small amplitude only on the realization beat.
- Risk: the frame drifts to a face edge to edge. Write the distance and lens every time.

## 4. Background reveal with a rack focus

This is the staging-in-depth beat: the lead in the subject plane, the threat or reveal in the background.

- Foreground: optional, a soft object at the edge.
- Subject: the lead at 2 to 3 m, in focus first, not yet seeing what is behind.
- Background: the reveal (a woman in a red dress at the far door, a man at the top of the stairs) 6 to 10 m back, small and soft, already in frame from the start.
- Camera: "the focus shifts from Mia to the woman at the far door" at the beat. Then cut to a closer shot of the reveal; don't travel the camera to it.
- Risk: the far figure walks forward or vanishes. Name them in the shot from the start, with the action "stands still by the far door".

## 5. Outdoor wide

- Foreground: dry grass, thistles, a fence post, rocks or a railing, out of focus, in the lower third.
- Subject: the person or people at 8 to 20 m, small but clear, against an open patch of sky or road.
- Background: hills, a skyline or the sea, much paler and lower in contrast, with the horizon on a third.
- Air and light: atmospheric haze at golden hour, with low sun from the side or behind.
- Camera: a slow truck or crane past the foreground plants.
- Risk: faces at that size drift in AI video. Nobody speaks in this shot; cut closer for the lines.

## 6. Interior through a doorway

A frame within a frame.

- Foreground: the door frame or a wall edge, dark, out of focus, on one or both sides.
- Subject: the people in the next room at 3 to 5 m, lit, sharp.
- Background: that room's far wall or window, with a lamp.
- Air and light: the room beyond is brighter than the dark foreground, so it reads as a lit box.
- Camera: a slow truck along the door frame reveals the room.
- Risk: the door frame cuts through a face. Place the people inside the opening.

## 7. Window light

- Foreground: a dark object near the lens (a chair back, a curtain edge, a plant), silhouetted.
- Subject: the person in the window light at 2 to 3 m, side lit, sharp.
- Background: the bright window or the room behind, overexposed or soft.
- Air and light: dust in the light beam between the window and the subject.
- Camera: a slow pedestal or truck through the beam.
- Risk: haze in front of the face washes it out. Put the beam beside or behind them.

## 8. Night street

- Foreground: rain on a window, a lamp post, a parked car's edge or wet leaves, soft.
- Subject: the person under a street lamp or a sign's glow at 3 m, sharp, rim lit.
- Background: rows of shop lights and car lights receding into soft points, wet asphalt reflecting them.
- Air and light: drizzle or steam from a grate catching the light behind them.
- Camera: a tracking shot or a slow truck past the lamp post.
- Risk: invented readable signs. Keep the lights as soft colour and write any text the viewer needs as a caption.

## 9. Walk-and-talk

- Foreground: objects passing the lens as the camera tracks (columns, pillars, plants, parked cars), out of focus.
- Subject: the two walkers at 2.5 to 3 m, side by side, 3/4 to the lens, sharp.
- Background: the corridor or street receding to a vanishing point, softer.
- Camera: a tracking shot backward at walking speed, with foreground objects sliding through fast.
- Risk: walking legs and a moving camera both drift. Keep the shot short (4 to 6 s) and the walk slow.

## 10. Insert

- Foreground (now the subject): the object at 0.3 to 0.6 m, sharp: the ring box, the key card, the trembling hand.
- Behind it: the person at 2 m, soft, recognizable by colour and shape.
- Background: the room, very soft.
- Camera: static, or a slow push-in. A rack focus from the object to the face makes a strong beat.
- Risk: text on the object is garbled. Angle it away from the lens and caption it.

## 11. Party or crowd

- Foreground: one or two guests' shoulders, glasses raised, out of focus, at the edges.
- Subject: the named pair at 3 m, sharp, the only people facing the lens at 3/4.
- Background: "about thirty guests far behind, small and soft, turned toward the stage", with fairy lights receding.
- Air and light: warm haze from the lights, and a rim on the pair.
- Camera: a slow arc around the pair.
- Risk: duplicate faces and guests staring at the lens. Give the crowd a number, a direction to face and an action.

## 12. Car interior

- Foreground: the steering wheel edge or the dashboard, soft and dark.
- Subject: the driver and passenger at 1 to 1.5 m, using a 35 mm lens, sharp.
- Background: the street through the windows, bright, soft and moving.
- Air and light: passing street lights sweep across their faces.
- Camera: a static mount, with the moving street giving the parallax.
- Risk: a 24 mm lens at that distance distorts faces. Use 35 mm and accept a tighter frame.

## 13. Anime multiplane

- Foreground cel layer: cherry branches, a railing or rain streaks, blurred and darker, sliding fastest in a pan.
- Character layer: the characters, clean line, the strongest colour and contrast.
- Background painting: the town or sky, painted with aerial perspective (paler and bluer with distance), sliding slowest.
- Air: god rays, a light bloom, drifting petals between the layers.
- Camera: a horizontal pan or truck, with each layer moving at its own speed. This is the multiplane look.
- Risk: the model blends the cel layer into the characters. Keep the foreground sparse and at the edges.
