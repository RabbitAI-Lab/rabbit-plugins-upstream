# Stage Rules — Full Reference

## The Governing Frameworks

Three rule systems overlap; the STRICTEST applies:

1. **State/provincial law** — minimums vary (e.g., RF until 1 y + 20 lb in some states, 2 y in
   others; booster until 8 y in some, until 4'9" or 12 y elsewhere). Always check locally.
2. **Seat manufacturer limits** — every seat has RF/FF weight caps, height caps, and often a
   "head within 1 in of shell top" rule. The manual supersedes all general guidance.
3. **Best practice (AAP 2018 / NHTSA)** — rear-facing as long as possible; harness as long as
   possible; booster until the 5-step belt-fit test passes; back seat until 13.

## Stage 1 — Rear-Facing (birth → 2+ years)

- Position: rear-facing, in an infant carrier or convertible installed RF.
- Harness: 5-point, straps **at or BELOW** shoulder level.
- Chest clip at armpit level; pinch test — no slack.
- Recline to the seat's level indicator (newborns need more recline to protect airway).
- Outgrown RF when ANY of: weight cap reached, height cap reached, head within 1 in of shell top.
- Leg crowding is NOT a safety problem — kids fold comfortably; broken legs in a crash are rare
  and far less severe than cervical spine injuries from forward-facing too early.

### Typical RF limits
| Seat class | RF weight max | RF height max |
|---|---|---|
| Infant carrier | 30–35 lb | 32 in |
| Typical convertible | 40 lb | 49 in |
| Extended-RF convertible ("all-in-one") | 45–50 lb | 49 in |

## Stage 2 — Forward-Facing + Harness (~2 → 4+ years, often to 6–8)

- Harness straps **at or ABOVE** shoulders.
- **Top tether required** — reduces head excursion several inches.
- Lower anchors have a combined child+seat weight cap (often 65 lb, printed on the seat); past it,
  switch to seat-belt installation (keep the tether).
- Outgrown the harness when ANY of: harness weight cap (65–90 lb), shoulder slots below shoulders,
  standing-height cap, tops of ears above shell.

## Stage 3 — Booster (~4 → 8–12 years)

- Lap-AND-shoulder belt only; never lap-only (use a vest/harness alternative if needed).
- High-back first (belt geometry + side head support); backless acceptable with vehicle headrest.
- Shoulder belt on the middle of the chest/shoulder — never behind the back or under the arm.
- Gates: 4'9" AND passing all 5 belt-fit steps AND the maturity to sit properly every ride.

## Stage 4 — Adult Belt

- 5-step belt-fit test (all must pass):
  1. Shoulder belt mid-chest/shoulder, not neck/face.
  2. Lap belt low on thighs, not stomach.
  3. Back and bottom flat against seat back.
  4. Knees bend at seat edge with back staying put.
  5. Can stay like this the WHOLE ride.
- Back seat until age 13.

## Special Situations

- **Premature/low-tone infants:** car bed or angle assessments per pediatrician.
- **Multi-kid 3-across:** narrow seats (e.g., "slim" convertibles) — but never compromise stage.
- **Expired seats:** replace at 6–10 years from DOM (printed on label); plastic and webbing degrade.
- **Post-crash:** NHTSA guidance — replace after any moderate/severe crash; many manufacturers
  say replace after ANY crash. Most insurance covers a replacement seat.
- **Second-hand seats:** only if history is fully known (no crashes, all parts, not expired).
- **Winter coats:** none under the harness — buckle, then blanket on top. The coat's puffiness
  creates hidden slack.

## Misuse Statistics (why this matters)

NHTSA estimates ~half of car seats are misused. The most frequent errors at check events:
harness too loose, chest clip too low, wrong strap height for stage, installation too loose,
and — most consequential for staging — premature transition to the next stage.
