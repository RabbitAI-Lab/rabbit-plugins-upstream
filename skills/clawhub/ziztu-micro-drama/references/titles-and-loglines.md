# Titles, loglines, the promise and covers

In an app grid or a feed, the title and the cover are the first episode. They have to sell the fantasy in a glance, in the genre's language, without being a copy of the last hit.

## 1. The logline

One sentence, under 30 words (35 at most), with the status gap visible:

> [Underestimated lead] + [the wound or betrayal] + [the secret advantage] must [goal] against [named power] before [clock].

- "A cleaner the CEO treats like furniture inherits 51% of his company, but only if she marries by Friday, and the only man she can ask is him."
- "Divorced on her anniversary and thrown out pregnant, a quiet wife returns five years later as the surgeon her ex-husband's dying father needs."
- "A wolfless girl rejected at her mating ceremony is taken in by the enemy pack's Alpha, who is the nephew of the man who wants her dead."

If the premise needs two sentences, it is two premises: pick one.

## 2. The promise

The promise is the one fantasy the viewer is buying, written as what they will get to watch:

- "Watch the cleaner he threw money at make him beg to be her husband."
- "Watch a betrayed wife take back every single thing, one step per episode."

Every episode pays a little of the promise; the finale pays all of it. When an episode does not move the promise, it is padding.

## 3. Title patterns

The patterns that recur across hit titles (use the pattern, never the hit's words):

| Pattern | Shape | Example built from the pattern |
|---|---|---|
| The secret in the title | "My [Relation] Is Secretly [Power]" | "My Driver Is Secretly the Heir" |
| The status flip | "The [Low Role] Owns the [Place]" | "The Cleaner Owns the Tower" |
| The warning | "Never [Wrong] a [Hidden Power]" | "Never Fire a Secret Heiress" |
| Too late | "Too Late, [Name/Role] [Leaves/Forsakes] You" | "Too Late, Your Wife Has Signed" |
| After the event | "After the [Divorce/Wedding], [Twist]" | "After the Divorce, She Bought His Company" |
| The command | "[Back Off / Kneel], the [Role] Returns" | "Kneel, the Real Daughter Is Home" |
| The clock | "[Number] Days to [Goal]" / "[Goal] by [Day]" | "Marry Me by Friday" |
| Bound and fated | "Bound by [Thing]" / "Fated to [Forbidden Role]" | "Bound by the Contract Clause" |
| The scandal | "[Shocking Relation] of My [Relation]" | "Pregnant by My Ex's Brother" |
| The double life | "The Double Life of My [Role]" | "The Double Life of My Plain Assistant" |

Rules:
- Put the fantasy word in the title: heiress, billionaire, CEO, Alpha, Luna, revenge, divorce, pregnant, mafia, reborn, queen.
- Short beats long: four to seven words is the sweet spot. Analyses of AI drama channels found most titles overlong; a long title is cut off in the app grid.
- Title case, no hashtags, no emojis in the title itself.
- Give two alternates with every title so the user can test.

## 4. Episode titles

Apps usually label episodes by number, but titles help on social platforms, in the bible and in the grid. Make each one a three to six word hook about that episode's turn, never a spoiler of its cliffhanger: "The Hundred-Dollar Bill", "Majority Owner", "Friday, 11:52 PM", "The Ring on the Wrong Hand".

## 5. The cover brief

Deliver a cover brief per series (and per episode for social posts):

- **Two faces in conflict** or one face mid-reaction, close enough to read on a phone.
- **One concrete object of the conflict** in frame: the bill, the ring, the contract, the test, the badge.
- **Three to five words of text**, the promise or the title, large and high contrast.
- The lead's signature colour, so the cover matches the episodes.
- Avoid the standard "two people glaring with huge text" template when the whole category looks like it: change the angle, the object or the colour so the cover stands out.

## 6. Originality checks

Before you deliver a title and logline:

- Not the title of a known hit, and not a known hit's title with one word changed.
- Not a known hit's character names or exact premise. If the user asks for "a series like X", keep X's emotional engine and change the setting, the secret, the clock and the cast.
- Character names are original, easy to read, and each lead starts with a different letter.
- If you can search, search the exact title in quotes; if a hit already has it, pick an alternate.
