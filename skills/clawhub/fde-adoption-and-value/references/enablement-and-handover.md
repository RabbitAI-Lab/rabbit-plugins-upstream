# User empowerment, support and knowledge transfer

## Target

Enable target users to know when to use, how to judge the results, and when to reject or upgrade without an FDE accompanying them all the way, and enable customer operations and technical teams to support, monitor, and recover the system.

## User Empowerment Plan

| Object | Must master | Training method | Pass evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| frontline users | Applicable scenarios, input, review, rejection, upgrade and feedback | Real task drills | Complete normal and abnormal tasks independently |
| Supervisor/business leader | Scope, quality, exceptions, adoption and decision-making | Scenario review | Able to judge continue, stop and process exceptions |
| Support and Operations | Common faults, user issues, versions and upgrades | Runbook walkthroughs | Complete first-line support without relying on the original FDE |
| Technology & Security | Identity, Data, Logs, Alerts, Rollbacks, and Events | Failure/Incident Walkthroughs | Complete Recovery, Auditing, and Notifications |
| Product/Asset Owner | Feedback, roadmap, configuration, evaluation and retirement | Asset review | Ability to maintain version and usage boundaries |

Training completion cannot be measured solely by “number of participants.” Observe at least standalone completion rates, incorrect operations, manual escalations, support requests, and reuse over time.

## Support models

Explicit L0–L3:

- L0: User built-in instructions, known limitations and self-service checks;
- L1: Customer business or operational support, handling access, usage and FAQs;
- L2: Customer Technology/FDE, handling integration, configuration, data and versions;
- L3: Platform, model, product or security team that handles underlying defects and critical incidents.

Each level describes the channel, service time, response goals, escalation conditions, required accompanying evidence, and responsibility boundaries. Don't think of "Contact FDE" as a permanent support model.

## Knowledge transfer checklist

- Users, scenarios and tasks that are currently applicable and clearly inapplicable;
- Entry, identity, permission application and revocation;
- Normal process, exception, manual confirmation, rejection and upgrade;
- Who owns and updates data, policies, knowledge and configurations;
- Models, skills, tips, evaluation sets and running versions;
- Logs, indicators, alarms, runbooks, rollbacks and event contacts;
- Known limitations, technical debt, residual risks and production gaps;
- Continuous measurement of adoption, value, costs and negative impacts;
- How feedback feeds into the product roadmap and asset maintenance.

## Handover and acceptance

Have representatives not involved in the POC complete:

1. Access and complete a normal task independently;
2. Identify an unsuitable scenario and correctly reject or upgrade it;
3. Locate a simulation failure from the log;
4. Complete the downgrade or recovery drill according to the runbook;
5. Find data, knowledge, configuration and asset owners;
6. Indicate the next version update, evaluation and adoption review time.

If the exercise relies on FDE on-the-spot interpretation, document missing documentation, tools, or training and make it available before scaling up.

## FDE exit conditions

- Customer business and technical leaders have accepted responsibilities;
- No longer dependent on the original FDE for daily use, support, events and changes;
- Residual risk, technical debt and unmet value are documented in writing;
- Adoption and value measurement have owners, data sources and review windows;
- Productization candidates and on-site feedback have entered the 8th round;
- Clarified triggers and channels for re-entering FDE support.

Exiting is not about stopping cooperation, but about avoiding turning a one-time delivery into a permanent manual dependency.
