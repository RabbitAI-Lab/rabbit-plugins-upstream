# Injury Assessment Decision Tree

## Red Flags → Advise Medical Consultation

Any of these = stop running, see a doctor:
- Pain at rest (felt without activity)
- Joint swelling or visible deformity
- Sharp/stabbing pain during running (not dull ache)
- Pain that worsens as the run progresses (not warms up and fades)
- Symptoms persisting > 2 weeks without improvement
- Night pain that disrupts sleep

## Yellow Flags → Reduce Load, Monitor

| Symptom | Likely Issue | Action |
|---------|-------------|--------|
| Lateral knee pain (outside) | ITB syndrome | cut volume 50%, add hip/glute strengthening, foam roll TFL |
| Achilles stiffness in morning | Tendinopathy (early) | eccentric heel drops, avoid speed work, reduce 30% |
| Plantar fascia pain (first steps) | Plantar fasciitis | foot rolling, calf stretch, avoid barefoot, check shoes |
| Shin pain (medial, diffuse) | Medial tibial stress syndrome | reduce impact (softer surface/cross-train), check shoes |
| Anterior knee pain | Patellofemoral pain | strengthen quads/hips, avoid downhill, check cadence |

## Assessment Questions

When the user reports a niggle, ask:
1. Location — exactly where?
2. Onset — sudden or gradual? Specific session?
3. Pattern — warms up and fades, or worsens during run?
4. Rest pain — does it hurt when NOT running?
5. Morning symptoms — stiffness or pain on waking?
6. Recent changes — new shoes, increased volume, new terrain?

## Biomechanical Red Flags from Watch Data

| Change | Threshold | Possible Issue |
|--------|-----------|----------------|
| GCT balance shift | > 2% asymmetry | unilateral fatigue or compensation |
| Cadence drop | > 5 spm from baseline | muscle fatigue, possible strain |
| Ground contact time increase | > 10% from baseline | fatigue-induced form breakdown |
| Vertical oscillation increase | > 15% from baseline | overstriding or fatigue |
