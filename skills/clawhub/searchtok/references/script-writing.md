# script-writing

Use when someone wants a script, a script structure or a story structure to film. The answer shows which structures the niche's outlier posts follow and how to fill one with their own material.

## Goal

Show the script structures behind the niche's outlier posts, as fill-in-the-blank templates with the real posts they come from, so the user can film one with their own material.

## Steps

1. A structure is a fill-in-the-blank template for the whole script. Trends are repeatable structures, so look for the structure under a trend, not its topic.
2. To get the template, read the outlier's transcript, keep its order and format, and blank out the topic details.
3. A script is the hook plus the value plus an ending. The hook can come from a separate outlier, not only the template's post, and has three parts: what's said, what's on screen and what's seen in the first 3 seconds.
4. The value must be specific, clear and actionable, and come from the user's own knowledge or experience. Outside facts get credited.
5. Choose an angle (tutorial, comparison, myth bust or common mistake, do versus don't, single tip, transformation, challenge) and whether to tell it as education or as a story. One idea can become several videos this way.
6. Choose the filming format with the script: sitting and talking to camera is called boring. Named options include smart-versus-dumb back-and-forth, props, voice-over, multitasking, a change of setting, quick shot changes, clone, whiteboard, Q&A, green screen and reaction.
7. Lead with the 3 to 5 top videos for the niche or term, as many as are strong (never pad with weak ones), each with 3 or 4 points on why it worked, from what is seen, said and shown on screen. Visual hooks count as much as text hooks.
8. Add one closing line on a shared pattern only if 3 or more of the top videos show it. Don't build the answer around formats instead of individual videos.
9. Show videos from the US, Europe, Canada, Australia and New Zealand by default. When the user names a market or language, show videos from there.

## When to deviate

- A structure that went viral in another niche can be reused: show it working there first, then adapt it to the user's niche.
- Story scripts: pick one of seven story types (my story, win, loss, lesson, transformation, challenge, big goal with a plan) and find an outlier story of the same type for the structure. A story script can be short: a hook question, a short backstory, a few steps, the result and a closing line.
- For stories, voice-over, quick shot changes, or footage per line work as formats; use the user's past footage if it exists.
- The order is flexible: hook, value or topic can come first.

## By profile type

The user's profile type (app, service, creator, ecommerce, agency) comes from `get_profile`, or from what they tell you.

- creator: use the profile's goal (reach or followers) to pick easy or complex structures.

## Tools

- `search_content` (CLI: `npx searchtok search`): outlier posts in the niche, or in the story type asked for.
- `analyze_post` (CLI: `npx searchtok post`): one post's full transcript, to blank out its template.
- `find_similar` (CLI: `npx searchtok similar`): the same structure in other niches.

## A great answer

From a real searchtok answer to "give me a structure for dog training videos. i want followers, not just views" (16 Oct 2026), one top video and the pattern line. Each point is about that video; the numbers and quotes came straight from searchtok's tools:

> **how to teach any dog leave it**
> - Teaches one command with a clear promise that the method works, a single skill a new owner can try today (2.1M views, 17.8x usual views).
> - Caption frames it as a how-to for any dog, so it fits every new owner.
> - Repeatable as a series (one command per video), which gives viewers a reason to follow.
>
> Pattern: Each one teaches a single skill or fixes a single mistake on a real dog, for a problem new owners face.

## Don'ts

- Don't copy another creator's script word for word: keep the structure and fill it with the user's own information.
- Don't write or improve a hook: quote real ones by reference.
- Don't promise followers or leads from a structure: searchtok only sees views, likes and outlier scores.
- Never show low-view videos, and never say why a video didn't work.
- Leave ads out entirely: never show one or use it as evidence.

## Plain words

- Plain words, short sentences. Lay the template out one line per beat.
