# hook-patterns

Use when someone asks what hooks work in their niche, for hook ideas, or how the best posts open. The answer shows the hooks of the top videos, word for word, and what's seen in their first seconds.

## Goal

Show the hooks the niche's outlier posts open with, quoted word for word, with what each one shows and says in its first seconds, so the user can take the frame.

## Steps

1. A hook has three parts: what's said in the first seconds, the text on screen and what's seen. Read the transcript, the on-screen hook and the opening for each post; a post can use all three.
2. Judge outliers by the outlier score only (views against the account's usual views). Don't use a multiple of follower count.
3. Find candidates two ways: search the niche's keywords, subtopics and job titles, and open the niche's top accounts to look at their best posts.
4. Keep only accounts that look like what the user would post. Skip accounts that don't fit the niche.
5. Name each pattern by its angle where one fits: tutorial (steps or a framework), comparison (methods that reach the same result), myth bust or common mistake, do and don't (right against wrong), tips or hack, transformation (before and after) or challenge.
6. For each example, say which part carries the hook: spoken, on screen, or both.
7. Lead with the 3 to 5 top videos for the niche or term, as many as are strong (never pad with weak ones), each with 3 or 4 points on why it worked, from what is seen, said and shown on screen. Visual hooks count as much as text hooks.
8. Add one closing line on a shared pattern only if 3 or more of the top videos show it. Don't build the answer around formats instead of individual videos.
9. Show videos from the US, Europe, Canada, Australia and New Zealand by default. When the user names a market or language, show videos from there.

## When to deviate

- When the niche is thin, look for hook frames that repeat across niches ("is it possible", "did you know that", "three levels of", "smart versus dumb"). Show the same frame working in several niches, and offer niche hooks first, cross-niche frames as a backup.
- A cross-niche hook is offered as a frame: keep the frame, swap in the user's subject (changing one or two words of a proven hook is fine). Describe the swap in words; the only hooks in quotes are the real ones.
- When the user names one account, read that account's recent posts or its biggest outliers and say which hook strategies work there.

## By profile type

The user's profile type (app, service, creator, ecommerce, agency) comes from `get_profile`, or from what they tell you.

- service: hooks from the business's own posts (clinics, physios), not hook formulas from marketing coaches.
- ecommerce: say which hooks are text on screen and which are spoken, and make no claims about sales.
- app: on-screen hooks of demo posts count; quote them exactly, emoji included.
- agency: "reels" means short-form video in general: say which platforms you searched.

## Tools

- `search_content` (CLI: `npx searchtok search`): outlier posts in the niche; rows carry a hook once a post is analysed.
- `get_account` (CLI: `npx searchtok account`): a top account's best recent posts.
- `analyze_post` (CLI: `npx searchtok post`): the spoken and on-screen hooks of the posts you'll quote. One post per call: run it on the 3 to 5 you'll show.
- Yourself, from the rows: drop hook-advice videos and off-topic posts.

## A great answer

From a real searchtok answer to "ok so i launched my budgeting app about 3 months ago and i've been posting on tiktok..." (16 Oct 2026), one top video. Each point is about that video; the numbers and quotes came straight from searchtok's tools:

> **I'm obsessed with budgeting: reply about her app**
> - A reply to a viewer comment asking which app she uses, so the video starts with a real question: "You guys, I am obsessed with budgeting." (34.3M views, 1510.7x usual views).
> - Talking head in a car, raw phone footage, with no screen recording.
> - Treats budgeting as a game and gives a personal reason for using the app, not a feature tour.

## Don'ts

- Never write, improve or translate a hook. Quote it by reference, or say it couldn't be read.
- A caption isn't a hook.
- Never show low-view videos, and never say why a video didn't work.
- Leave ads out entirely: never show one or use it as evidence.

## Plain words

- Plain words, short sentences. Say what the hook does in a few words, then let the quote show it.
