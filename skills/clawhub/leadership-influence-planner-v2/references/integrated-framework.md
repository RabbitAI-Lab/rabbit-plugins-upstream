# Integrated Framework

Use transactional analysis for pattern diagnosis and Baker's strategies for leadership influence.

## Step 1: Find the Loop

Look for repeated exchanges where the stated topic is not the real driver. Common signs: circular argument, sarcasm, rescue request, rejection of help, gotcha, blame, triangulation, exaggerated apology, helplessness, or audience-seeking.

## Step 2: Identify the Payoff

Ask what each person gets or avoids: attention, rescue, control, righteousness, excuse, superiority, avoidance of intimacy, avoidance of accountability, or belonging.

## Step 3: Use Antithesis

Do not continue the expected role. Change the transaction while staying respectful:

- Return to facts.
- Ask for the real request.
- Refuse judge/rescuer/persecutor roles.
- Set a boundary.
- Invite ownership.
- Pause and reset the meeting.

## Step 4: Choose Baker Mode

- Investigation: use after facts are disputed or clarity is missing.
- Calculation: use after value, incentives, tradeoffs, or risks are unclear.
- Motivation: use after energy, courage, or urgency is missing.
- Collaboration: use after trust, ownership, inclusion, or safety is missing.

## Step 5: Sequence

The strongest leadership moves often have two parts:

1. Transactional reset: stop the old loop.
2. Influence move: make the ethical case or invitation.

Example sequence:

- Reset: "I do not want us to keep proving who caused the problem. Let's name what is true now."
- Influence: "The customer impact is real, and we have two workable paths. Which one can you own by Friday?"
