# Toast Craft — Structure, Roles, and Delivery

## Why a structure at all
A wedding toast is a 3-minute story with two jobs: honor the person you know,
and bless the union you're witnessing. Structure isn't a creativity tax — it's
what lets a nervous speaker remember what comes next. Every section below
exists because its absence is the #1 failure mode observed at real weddings.

## The 5+1 parts (and their budgets)

### 1. Hook (~12% of words, ~20s)
Open INSIDE a moment, not at the podium. "Nine years ago Alex talked me into
a rafting trip that went very wrong" beats "Good evening, for those who don't
know me" — the room decides in the first 10 seconds whether to listen.

Rules:
- No internet jokes. If it could appear on a mug, it's dead on arrival.
- No "I was so nervous about this speech" meta-talk.
- One image, one sentence, then move.

### 2. Who (~10%)
One sentence of credential: relationship + years. "I'm Jake, Alex's brother
and lifelong co-conspirator." Not your resume, not a list of shared jobs.

### 3. Story 1 (~30%) — THE story
The structural center. Arc: scene → complication → payoff. The payoff must
reveal a quality (loyalty, calm, generosity), because a story that only
entertains proves nothing about the person marrying today.

Budget reality: at 135 wpm and 30% of a 3-minute speech you get ~120 words.
That is ONE story told well: "The river flipped us at the rapids. I lost my
paddle and my composure. Alex — who can't swim well — had my lifevest strap
in his fist before I'd finished surfacing. That's who he is."

### 4. Story 2 (~22%)
Either a short second beat OR the pivot to the relationship: how they met,
how the partner changed your person. The pivot is safer and more valuable —
it turns two stories about Alex into a story about Alex-and-Sam.

### 5. Partner (~14-24% by role)
Welcome the partner BY NAME with one observed detail. This section is the
marriage-blessing in miniature; skipping it reads as "I'm only here for my
friend." Parents: this is your biggest section (24%) — you're gaining a child.

Forbidden frames: "we never thought he'd settle down," "she tamed him,"
"finally." Surprise at the marriage = insult to both parties.

### 6. Toast (~12%)
The ask, the wish, the names. "May you keep choosing each other on the bad
days, because we all know there'll be some. To Alex and Sam." End on the
names, never on a punchline — the room needs the landing cue to drink.

## Role budgets (shares of total words)

| Role | hook | who | story1 | story2 | partner | toast |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| best man | .12 | .10 | .30 | .22 | .14 | .12 |
| maid of honor | .10 | .10 | .28 | .22 | .18 | .12 |
| parent | .10 | .06 | .26 | .22 | .24 | .12 |
| friend | .14 | .16 | .28 | .20 | .10 | .12 |
| sibling | .12 | .10 | .28 | .22 | .16 | .12 |

Rationale: parents have the smallest "who" need (the room knows them) and the
largest partner duty (gaining a child). Friends need more credential and less
partner history. Best men have the most story weight — that's the currency of
friendship.

## Timing physics
- Delivery speed under adrenaline: 110-120 wpm, not your conversational 150+.
- Pauses for laughs add ~10-15% (plan for exactly one laugh line per section,
  max). 
- Full formula: minutes ≈ words / 135 for rehearsal, words / 115 for the day.
- Sweet spot: 2.5-3.5 minutes. Under 2 feels careless; over 4 steals time
  from the evening's schedule (and every minute after 3 raises flag risk).

## Delivery-day protocol
1. Print 18pt+ serif, one section per card, cards numbered. Never a phone.
2. Hold your glass in the LEFT hand (right shakes; also: you'll toast before
   you drink, and a cold glass sweats into a phone screen).
3. Feet still, eyes up at sentence ENDS — that's where connection happens.
4. Mic discipline: mouth one hand-width from the mic; if no mic, speak to the
   back wall.
5. If a joke dies: don't explain, don't apologize — next sentence, moving on.
6. The final line is memorized cold. Cards can fail; the ending cannot.

## Repair guide per flag class
- **ex mention** → replace with a "before" state that has no person in it:
  "Alex was in a rough patch the year we became close" (no name, no story).
- **drunk story** → keep the arc, delete the substance: "one memorable night
  involving a karaoke machine and a decision we still debate."
- **divorce joke** → invert to a commitment frame: "Alex researches everything
  for months before buying a screwdriver — and then it's for life."
- **"finally settled"** → swap surprise for witness: "I've watched Alex look
  for this kind of calm for years. Watching him find it was the best part of
  my year."
- **inside joke** → give the room the key or cut: "(you had to be there)"
  translates to "a story I'll tell you at the bar, I promise."
- **money joke** → replace with a generosity detail: "Sam is the person who
  finds out your coffee order and just... starts bringing two."
- **profanity** → one "damn" maximum, only in a quote, only if the couple
  swears more than you do.

## The final gate
Every scanner pass ends with this question, and it outranks any score:
*Would you say this sentence, directly, to the couple's face — with both
grandmothers listening?* If no, cut it. The scanner catches patterns; you
catch tone.
