# Earned-Time Economy

## Concept

Instead of a fixed allowance of screen time (which kids see as an entitlement),
screen time becomes a **currency** that children **earn** through positive
behaviors. This approach is based on behavioral psychology research on
token economies and positive reinforcement.

## How It Works

### Base Allowance
Each child starts the day with a **base allowance** of entertainment minutes
(set by the parent, defaulting to AAP age-based recommendations).

### Earning Bonuses
Children can earn additional entertainment time through:

| Activity | Bonus Minutes | Rationale |
|----------|--------------|-----------|
| Homework completion (per subject) | +15 | Prioritize academics |
| Chore done without being asked | +10 | Build responsibility |
| Reading a physical book (per 15 min) | +5 | Encourage offline reading |
| Good behavior / kindness | +10 | Reinforce character |
| Outdoor play (per 30 min) | +5 | Encourage physical activity |
| Helping a sibling | +10 | Family cooperation |
| Practicing an instrument (per 15 min) | +5 | Skill development |

### Deductions
Time can also be removed as a natural consequence:

| Behavior | Deduction | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|-----------|
| Refusal to stop when time is up | -10 | Respect limits |
| Lying about usage | -15 | Honesty |
| Screen in a screen-free zone | -10 | Follow rules |
| Fighting with sibling over screen | -10 | Conflict resolution |

## Why This Works

### 1. Transparency
Rules are visible and consistent. Kids know exactly what earns time and what
costs it. No arbitrary decisions.

### 2. Agency
Kids choose to earn time rather than having it given or taken away. They
develop self-regulation skills.

### 3. Positive Framing
The system focuses on earning (positive reinforcement) rather than losing
(negative reinforcement). Starting from a base and earning feels different
from starting high and being punished down.

### 4. Real-World Modeling
This mirrors adult life: we earn leisure time by completing responsibilities.
Kids learn time management and delayed gratification.

## Negotiation Protocol

When a child wants more time than they have, the system evaluates the request:

### Auto-Approve Conditions
- Child has >70% compliance score over last 7 days
- Request is for ≤15 extra minutes
- Child has completed today's homework
- Child has logged ≥30 min of educational screen time today

### Conditional Approval
- "You can have 15 more minutes after you finish your homework"
- "You can have 10 more minutes after you set the table"

### Deny Conditions
- Already exceeded by >30 minutes
- Homework not done
- Poor compliance score (<40%)
- Request is unreasonable (>60 min extra)

## Research Basis

- **Token Economy Systems** (Kazdin, 1977; 2008) — well-established in
  behavioral psychology for children
- **Positive Reinforcement** (Skinner) — behaviors followed by rewards
  increase in frequency
- **Self-Determination Theory** (Deci & Ryan) — autonomy, competence, and
  relatedness drive intrinsic motivation
- **American Academy of Pediatrics** — recommends consistent, transparent
  limits with child involvement in rule-setting
