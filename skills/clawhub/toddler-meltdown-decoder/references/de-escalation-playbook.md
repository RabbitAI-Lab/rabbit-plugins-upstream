# Toddler Meltdown Reference

## Why toddlers melt down (the 20-second neuroscience)

The prefrontal cortex — impulse braking, emotional labeling,
delay — is decades from finished. Under stress, a toddler runs
on limbic output. A meltdown is not defiance; it's an alarm
with no brakes. Two consequences drive everything in this kit:

1. **Co-regulation first.** A calm adult nervous system is the
   only brake available. Calm is transmitted through tone,
   posture, slowness — not through content.
2. **Teaching waits.** Post-meltdown, at reconnection, in one
   sentence. Mid-meltdown teaching is noise.

## Cue → class scoring detail

Each class carries cue lists with weights (2 = strong signal,
1 = supporting). Time-of-day priors: pre-meal (16:30-18:30)
boosts HUNGRY; pre-nap/late-afternoon boosts TIRED; post-playdate
/ post-party / post-store boosts OVERSTIMULATED. Onset shape is
a free-text cue: "task-linked" boosts FRUSTRATED; "bid-then-boom"
boosts CONNECTION.

## Class-specific scripts (the part to memorize)

### HUNGRY
- Now: small protein+fat snack (cheese, yogurt, nut butter) —
  not juice/sweets (crash follows). Words: "Food. Let's eat."
- Never: negotiate eating; never make food contingent on calm.
- Prevent: snack at 17:00 SHARP; watch the dinner-late gap.

### TIRED
- Now: reduce demands to zero. "We're going home." Carry, don't
  walk-and-argue. Dim room, slow voice.
- Never: "one more thing"; never fight the nap with activity.
- Prevent: protect the nap window; move bedtime 20 min earlier
  for a week and count meltdowns — the log answers whether it
  worked.

### OVERSTIMULATED
- Now: REMOVE stimulus (leave, dim, quiet). Fewer words. Sit
  nearby, don't touch unless invited. Words: "Quiet now."
- Never: offer choices, ask questions, add entertainment to
  distract. All of it is more input.
- Prevent: decompression buffer after group settings (20 min
  low-stimulation before demands); warn before transitions.

### FRUSTRATED (skill mismatch)
- Now: break the task invisibly ("these blocks are tricky —
  want the bigger base?"). Offer TWO tiny choices to restore
  control: "red cup or blue cup?"
- Never: take over and finish it FOR them (the wound is the
  competence gap; doing it for them confirms it).
- Prevent: scaffold hard steps in advance; choose tools at the
  edge of ability, not beyond.

### SENSORY
- Now: remove/replace the stimulus — sock seams, tag, sound,
  light. Words: "Tag gone. Better?"
- Never: "it's just a sock." The perception is real.
- Prevent: pre-check new clothes (cut tags), pack familiar
  items for transitions; some children need firm pressure
  (tight hug, weighted lap pad) — offer, don't impose.

### CONNECTION
- Now: 5 minutes of FULL attention, phone physically away,
  child-led play. The meltdown stops fast — that speed is the
  signature.
- Never: punish the bid-for-attention delivered as a hit; the
  need is legitimate, the delivery is the limit ("I won't let
  you hit. I'm here NOW.").
- Prevent: daily 10-min "special time" (same time, child-led,
  named) measurably cuts attention-meltdowns.

### PAIN/TEETHING
- Now: check (fingers in mouth, ears, fever); offer cold chew
  thing; appropriate weight-based analgesic per your
  pediatrician's dosing if indicated.
- Never: assume behavior is "just a phase" for 3+ days without
  checking — ear infections present as tantrums.
- Prevent: log night-wakings alongside meltdowns; the
  correlation is your early-warning system.

## Pattern log → prevention pipeline

1. `stats` hour-of-day histogram: one dominant spike = schedule
   fix (snack, nap, transition buffer). This is the highest-yield
   finding in the whole kit.
2. Class frequency: HUNGRY+TIRED dominance = logistics problem,
   not behavior problem. Fix the calendar.
3. Averted-vs-full ratio by intervention: what you did BEFORE
   (`log --averted --intervention`) that prevented it — keep
   doing that.
4. Caregiver comparison: if daycare logs differ from home logs,
   compare environment: noise, transitions, adult ratio.

## The parent check (every triage prints it)

- Are YOU dysregulated right now (voice up, jaw tight, rushing)?
  → Tag out if any adult is available; a 60-second swap is not
  abandonment, it's engineering.
- Alone and maxed out? Floor sit, slow exhale, one sentence:
  "You're safe. I'm here." Your calm IS the intervention.

## What this kit is NOT

- Not a behavioral-diagnosis instrument (red flags route to
  professionals; they diagnose)
- Not a discipline framework (no reward charts, no cry-it-out —
  different tools for different ages/goals)
- Not medical advice: fever + meltdown, head bump, or suspected
  injury → care first, triage never.
