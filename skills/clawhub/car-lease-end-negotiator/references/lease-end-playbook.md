# Lease-End Reference

## Where the numbers live in your contract
| Term | Where | Typical |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase-option price (residual) | Lease contract, "Purchase Option" section | fixed at signing |
| Purchase option fee | Same section | $0-$300 |
| Disposition fee | "End of lease / disposition" | $300-$500 |
| Mileage allowance / rate | "Mileage" terms | 10-15k/yr; $0.15-$0.30/mi |
| Wear guide | Separate booklet/URL "Standards for excessive wear" | defines per-item charges |

## Equity: the thing most lessees give away
Residual was fixed 2-3 years ago; the market moved. Post-2020 used-car spikes left many lessees $2k-$5k above residual. Check equity EVERY lease end:
- Private-party value (KBB/Edmunds private range) → buy-and-sell math.
- Dealer retail → what replacing the car costs if you keep yours.
- If equity > ~$1,000: never simply return. Either buy out and keep (you bought under market) or buy-and-sell through a credit union / third-party buyer service.
- If equity is deeply NEGATIVE (residual way above market — common on leases written at 2022 valuations): return with a clear conscience. That gap is the lessor's forecasting error, not your bill.

## Buyout mechanics (US)
1. Request payoff quote from the captive finance company (Ally, Toyota Financial, GM Financial, etc.) in writing — includes residual + purchase fee + any unpaid amounts + tax handling.
2. Third-party buyouts: many captives restrict assignment to dealers only; credit unions and buyer services (e.g., Carmax-style offers) navigate this — confirm the payoff can be sent to a third party BEFORE the last payment.
3. Taxes: most states charge sales tax on the residual at buyout; trade-in credits may apply if you instead route through a dealer. A handful (e.g., NJ historically) have quirks on lease buyout taxation — one phone call to your DMV beats a $1,200 surprise.
4. Paperwork: title arrives to you (or lienholder); registration transfer same-day if you keep driving it.

## Wear-and-tear war
- The inspection is done by a third party (e.g., dealers' inspection contractors) — their report is an INVOICE DRAFT, not a judgment.
- Categories: excess wear (dents over size threshold, tire tread below spec — usually 4/32", glass chips in driver sightline, missing equipment) vs NORMAL use (minor scuffs under threshold, stone chips, interior wear proportional to miles).
- Playbook: pre-turn-in inspection 30-60 days early → get report → fix cheap items independently with receipts → contest borderline items citing the wear guide → bring printed comparisons to maturity.
- The 4-tire tread charge (~$400-600) usually becomes a 2-tire independent job (~$180-260). Wheel scuffs: mobile alloy repair ~$75-125/wheel vs $150+/wheel adjudicated.

## Mileage economics
- End-of-lease rate: typically $0.20-$0.30/mi.
- Pre-purchase "mile packs" from some captives: ~$0.10-$0.18/mi in blocks — only worth it if you're CERTAIN to return AND certain of the overage.
- Buyout/sale: mileage invisible to the transaction (it affects market price mildly, not a fee).
- 5,000 mi over at $0.25 = $1,250 → check equity before paying this.

## Pull-ahead / loyalty offers
"We'll waive your last 3-4 payments if you re-lease now." Math: waived payments (~$1,000-1,300) vs equity you'd surrender if the car is worth more than residual. With $3k equity, pull-ahead costs you $1,700+ to accept. Run the numbers, decline politely, capture the equity.

## One-page turn-in day checklist
- [ ] Payoff/walk-away numbers in writing (phone recorded or email)
- [ ] Wear report reconciled (fixed items with receipts, contested items documented)
- [ ] Disposition waiver confirmed in writing (or priced into comparison)
- [ ] Mileage computed by THEIR odometer photo vs your allowance
- [ ] No signed anything until numbers match the negotiation card
- [ ] Personal items out, both keys present (missing key = $300-500 charge)
- [ ] Return receipt / condition report copy signed by their rep
