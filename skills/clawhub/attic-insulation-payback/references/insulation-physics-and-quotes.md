# Attic Insulation — Physics, Doctrine & Quote Forensics (reference)

## 1. The heat-loss model
- `U = 1/R` (Btu/h·ft²·°F); seasonal loss `Q = U × A × HDD × 24` (Btu/yr).
- Fuel input = Q ÷ system efficiency (80% furnace, 95% condensing, COP 2.5–4 for heat pumps — use seasonal COP, not brochure).
- Savings = Q(R_now) − Q(R_new). All of the interesting behavior lives in the 1/R curve.

## 2. The diminishing-returns curve (why "double R" lies)
| Upgrade | Heat loss through attic | Change |
|---|---|---|
| R-2 → R-13 | 50% → 7.7% | **−85%** |
| R-13 → R-38 | 7.7% → 2.6% | −66% of remainder |
| R-38 → R-60 | 2.6% → 1.7% | −37% of remainder |
| R-60 → R-80 | 1.7% → 1.25% | −26% of remainder |

The first inches are gold; beyond R-38–49 in most climates you're buying pennies. Cellulose at ~$1.20/ft² installed for R-49 vs R-38 differs by maybe $25/yr on a typical house.

## 3. Measuring existing R honestly
- Depth at ≥5 spots, use the **low** reading (compressed zones dominate loss — heat takes the path of least R, like current through parallel resistors).
- R/inch in service: cellulose 3.2 (settles to ~80% of label over ~10 yr), blown fiberglass 2.9, batts 3.1 (only if uncrushed), rockwool 3.3, open-cell foam 3.6.
- Kitty litter boxes, decking, stored Christmas boxes crush batts → local R debt. The ceiling averages in parallel: `R_eff = A / Σ(A_i/R_i)` — one 10% area at R-5 with the rest at R-40 drags the whole assembly to ~R-26.

## 4. Air sealing — the actual first move
- Fiberglass is an air filter, not an air barrier. Bypass stack effect: warm air exits at top plates, can lights, chases, bath-fan cuts; cold air enters low. Buried bypasses keep wasting energy under new R.
- Targets: can lights (fire-rated covers + foam), top plates and wire holes (firestop foam), chases, dropped soffits, the attic hatch (insulate + weatherstrip — the most-forgotten 10 ft² in the house).
- Cost: $200–600 DIY. Savings often rival the top-up itself, at 1/6 the cost.
- Bath fans must vent outdoors — venting into the attic dumps moisture onto cold sheathing: mold, ruined R, ice dams.

## 5. Quote forensics
| Quote line | Fair range (2026, US) | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| Blown cellulose R-38→R-60, 1000–1500 ft² | $1.80–3.50/ft² | >$4/ft² or "R-60 half your bill" claims |
| Air sealing (included) | $0.30–0.80/ft² or flat $300–800 | Missing entirely = walk-away or add |
| Can-light covers, per | $30–60 | >$100 each |
| Attic hatch insulation | included or <$100 | Charged as line >$250 |
| Baffles/vent chutes | included | Extra charge >$2 each |
| Removal of old insulation (contamination) | $1–2/ft² | Quoted without reason |

Also verify: stated settled thickness on the receipt (not "bags blown" — bags can be blown thin), coverage card left on-site, workers actually sealing before blowing (show up mid-job once).

## 6. Rebates & tax credits (US)
- 25C tax credit: 30% of insulation+air-sealing cost, up to $1,200/yr (2022–2032 era rules; verify current-year limits).
- Utility rebates: often $0.25–0.75/ft² or per-R-ft²; some require pre-approval and a blower-door test.
- Combined, a BORDERLINE contractor job routinely turns into <5-yr payback; stack rebate + fall-season scheduling (contractors are cheapest in Sep–Oct).

Cooling-dominated climates: run the same math with CDD base 75°F and the attic-radiant-gains share; prioritize radiant barrier + ventilation there — conductive R matters less than in HDD country.

## 8. When NOT to add attic R
1. Attic already ≥ R-49 and bills say otherwise → the loss is elsewhere (windows, walls, basement band joist, ductwork in the attic).
2. Quote skips air sealing.
3. Ice dams are the motive: dams are an air-sealing/ventilation failure — added R over bypasses can worsen them by hiding the leak while the roof still warms.
4. Plan to finish the attic or install a roof-mounted solar array with penetrations — sequence projects; insulation first, then penetrations get sealed as installed.
5. Knob-and-tube wiring present under existing insulation — electrician first (fire risk, insurers care).
