# Trade Scope Checklists — What a Complete Quote Must Contain

## Roofing

- Tear-off: how many layers, disposal included, dumpster permit
- Decking replacement: unit price per sheet (usually $75-150/sheet)
  — "as needed" without a price is the #1 change-order source
- Underlayment: synthetic felt minimum; ice/water shield at eaves and
  valleys (code in cold climates)
- Flashing: chimney, skylights, walls — new vs reuse (reuse = leak risk)
- Ventilation: ridge/soffit counts; balanced attic ventilation calc
- Warranty: manufacturer (material) AND labor (workmanship) separately
- Magnets/nail sweep after tear-off

## Kitchen

- Cabinets: spec or allowance — allowance must state what's included
  (per linear ft? hardware? soft-close?)
- Countertop: material, template, cutouts (sink, cooktop), edge profiles
- Electric/plumbing relocation: itemized; "reconnect existing" ≠ move
- Drywall/patch/paint after demo: often excluded
- Demo + disposal + floor protection
- Permit responsibility (usually contractor)
- Sequencing clauses: countertop template happens AFTER cabinets set —
  a 2-3 week gap that "2-week kitchen" quotes hide

## Bathroom

- Waterproofing method named (esp. showers): cement board+membrane,
  foam systems (Schluter/wedge), or hot mop — "tile ready" is not a spec
- Tub/shower valve: new (pressure-balanced) vs reuse
- Vent fan: CFM, ducted WHERE (attic-dump = mold; must terminate outside)
- Glass doors: included or allowance
- Inspections at rough-in and final (permit)

## HVAC

- Sizing: Manual J load calc or documented rationale — "same size as
  old unit" is how oversizing persists for decades
- Equipment: brand, model, AHRI matched system, efficiency (SEER2/HSPF2)
- Ductwork: modifications itemized; leakage test?
- Line set: replace vs flush (refrigerant-type changes require replace)
- Old equipment haul-away, thermostat, condensate drain protection
- Start-up commissioning sheet + registration of warranty (often the
  homeowner must register within 60 days or warranty halves)

## Electrical

- Panel: brand/amp/bus rating; breakers included
- Circuits: count and purpose itemized
- Permit + inspection included in price
- Patching drywall after runs: electricians usually DON'T — who does?

## Structural / Foundation

- Engineered drawing with stamp when load-bearing or foundation work
- Permit responsibility explicit
- Epoxy/carbon-fiber vs excavation method named for foundation cracks
- Drainage correction scope (the actual cause of most foundation water)

## Allowance Traps

An "allowance" is a placeholder budget, not a price. Traps:

1. **Fixture allowances** far below any real option (kitchen allowance
   $1,200 when entry-level alone is $4k) — makes the bid look cheap;
   you pay the difference later.
2. **"Allowance excludes tax/delivery/install"** — a $2,000 allowance
   becomes $2,600.
3. **Tile/flooring allowances per sq ft** that exclude thinset,
   underlayment, transitions, and sealing.
4. Fix: convert every allowance to either (a) a named spec with price,
   or (b) cost-plus-fixed-% with receipts, before signing.

## Lowball-Bid Pattern Catalog

| Pattern | Tell | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Missing scope | matrix MISSING cells | "that wasn't in the quote" change orders |
| Reuse everything | "reuse flashing/valve/line set" | fails inspection or leaks; emergency re-price |
| Unit price < 0.5× band | pricecheck LOW flags | change orders recover the margin |
| No permit line | "permits? you can pull those" | unperited work; resurfacing at sale |
| Deposit >30% | risk flags | funds next job, not yours |
| Timeline too good | 2-week kitchen | subs juggling; abandonment windows |
| Cash discount | any mention | no paper trail, no recourse, warranty void |

## Contract Clauses That Survive Disputes

1. **Scope as line items** — the matrix union with prices; anything not
   listed is excluded (say that sentence explicitly).
2. **Change orders**: "No extra work without a signed, priced change
   order. Oral changes are void." Both directions — protects contractor
   from scope-creep-by-chat too; easy to get signed.
3. **Payment schedule tied to milestones**, deposit <=30%, final 10%
   after punch list + inspections pass.
4. **Retention/right to cure**: defects fixed within N days of written
   notice before final payment.
5. **Lien waiver on each payment** — prevents subcontractor mechanics
   liens on your house for bills the GC didn't pay.
6. **Warranty**: labor warranty term (1-10 yr) in writing; manufacturer
   warranties registered to you.
7. **Termination-for-cause**: 7-day abandonment clause; you pay for
   work performed only.

## If It Goes Wrong Later

- Undelivered/poor work, deposit gone: state contractor board complaint
  (licensing violations are fast-tracked), then small claims for the
  amount.
- Mechanic's lien filed: demand itemization; liens require accurate
  amounts and deadlines; a lawyer can often void sloppy ones.
- Unpermitted work discovered: contractor is typically responsible for
  cure at their cost IF the contract says permit responsibility is
  theirs — which is why that line matters.
