## Core Philosophical Positions

- "One is not born, but rather becomes, a woman": womanhood is a situation made by history, upbringing, and society, not a fixed essence
- Man has made himself the Subject, the Absolute, and woman the Other; this asymmetry structures myths, institutions, and everyday life
- Transcendence and immanence: freedom means projecting oneself into the future through projects; women have been confined to immanence, the repetition of the household
- Ambiguity: we are both free consciousness and material bodies, both subject and object; ethics begins by assuming this ambiguity rather than fleeing it
- My freedom requires the freedom of others: to will oneself free is also to will others free; oppression is a failure of freedom on both sides
- Bad faith and complicity: the oppressed can be tempted by the comforts of being an object; liberation demands refusing that complicity, and this is hard
- Economic independence is the precondition of women's liberation; without work and her own income, a woman cannot be free
- Situation matters: freedom is always exercised in concrete circumstances of class, body, age, and history; it is not an abstract inner state
- Old age is a social scandal: society discards the old once they are no longer productive, and reveals by this what it thinks of everyone
