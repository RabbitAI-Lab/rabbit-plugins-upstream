# Craving Playbook

## The Two Most Evidence-Backed Techniques

**Delay and distraction.** Cravings peak in 10-20 minutes and fade on their own. You don't need to fight them — you need to outlast them.

## Immediate Response (When a Craving Hits)

### 1. Surf the Urge (10 minutes)
- Don't fight it. Don't act on it. Just watch it.
- Set a 10-minute timer.
- Notice the physical sensation: where in your body do you feel it?
- Rate it 1-10 every 2 minutes. Watch it rise and fall.
- Cravings are waves. They peak and they break. You're surfing.

### 2. Box Breathing (2 minutes)
- Inhale 4 seconds
- Hold 4 seconds
- Exhale 4 seconds
- Hold 4 seconds
- Repeat 8-10 times
- This activates the parasympathetic nervous system and physically reduces craving intensity

### 3. 5-4-3-2-1 Grounding
- Name 5 things you can see
- Name 4 things you can touch
- Name 3 things you can hear
- Name 2 things you can smell
- Name 1 thing you can taste
- Pulls your brain out of the craving loop and into your body

### 4. Change Your Environment
- Get up. Move to a different room.
- Go outside. Walk for 5 minutes.
- If you usually smoke in a specific spot, leave that spot.
- Environment change breaks the cue-routine loop.

### 5. Call Your Reason
- Why did you decide to quit?
- What's waiting on the other side of this?
- Picture the version of yourself 30 days from now.
- The craving is temporary. The regret of giving in lasts longer.

## Replacement Behaviors

Have these pre-loaded before a craving hits. Trying to figure out what to do DURING a craving is too late.

### Physical
- Push-ups to failure
- Cold water on face / cold shower
- Chew gum or ice
- Exercise (even 10 minutes)

### Mental
- Read 5 pages of a book
- Do a puzzle (Sudoku, Wordle)
- Write in journal
- Learn something for 10 minutes

### Social
- Text a friend
- Check r/leaves on Reddit
- Call someone

## Trigger-Specific Strategies

### "After work" trigger
→ Replace with: immediate environment change (leave house, go to gym, go to coffee shop)

### "Before bed" trigger
→ Replace with: sleep routine (hot shower, magnesium, reading, melatonin short-term)

### "When stressed" trigger
→ Replace with: box breathing, walk, exercise, vent to someone

### "When bored" trigger
→ Replace with: pre-made list of engaging activities, call a friend, work on a project

### "Social setting" trigger
→ Replace with: leave early, have a non-negotiable exit, bring a non-smoking friend, NA drink in hand

## The Log

After a craving passes (whether you smoked or not), log:
1. When did it hit?
2. How intense (1-10)?
3. What triggered it?
4. What did you do?
5. How long did it last?

This data is gold. After 3-5 entries, patterns emerge. Patterns become predictable. Predictable triggers become manageable.
