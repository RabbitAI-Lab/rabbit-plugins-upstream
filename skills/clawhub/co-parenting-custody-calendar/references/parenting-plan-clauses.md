# Parenting-Plan Clause Library

Model language for the mechanical clauses that make or break a custody
schedule. Adapt to jurisdiction; have a family-law attorney review before
filing.

## 1. Exchange Mechanics

**Standard exchange clause:**
> "Unless otherwise agreed in writing, exchanges occur at [LOCATION] at
> 6:00 PM on the exchange day. When school is in session on an exchange
> day, the exchange occurs at school dismissal and the receiving parent
> collects the child from [SCHOOL]; when school is not in session, the
> receiving parent collects the child from the releasing parent's
> residence."

Why the two-track wording: school exchanges eliminate ~90% of parking-lot
conflict because parents never meet. Non-school days need the residence
fallback or the plan has a hole every summer.

**Late/illness protocol:**
> "A parent delayed more than 15 minutes shall notify the other. If a
> parent is more than 60 minutes late without notice, the exchange
> occurs the following day and the missed time counts against make-up
> time owed."

**Gear clause (the silent friction source):**
> "The child shall travel with their usual bag containing clothing for
> the period, any medications with dosing instructions, school materials,
> and one comfort object. The receiving parent returns the bag at each
> exchange. Repeated failure is a communicable problem under §7."

## 2. Holiday Definitions

Define every holiday by RULE, not by vibe:

> "Holiday time takes precedence over the regular schedule. Holidays
> begin at 6:00 PM the day before the holiday and end at 8:00 AM the
> day after, except: Thanksgiving break begins at school dismissal
> Wednesday; winter break follows the school calendar; summer vacation
> begins the Monday after school ends."

**Alternating priority ladder (draft into plan):**
> "In odd-numbered years, Mother selects first among: Thanksgiving
> break, winter break, spring break. Father selects first from the
> remaining. In even-numbered years, this reverses. Selections are
> exchanged in writing by October 1 and locked by November 1."

**Pinned days:**
> "Mother's Day and Father's Day shall be spent with the named parent
> from 9:00 AM to 7:00 PM regardless of the regular schedule, and this
> time does not count against either parent's overnights."

**Child's birthday:**
> "The child's birthday shall be shared: the parent not scheduled for
> the overnight may take the child for a 2-hour celebration at a time
> agreed 7 days in advance."

## 3. Vacation / Override and Make-Up Time

> "Each parent may take up to [14] days of vacation time with the child
> per year upon 30 days' written notice, and up to 7 days upon 7 days'
> notice, subject to: (a) no single absence from the other parent's
> scheduled time exceeding [7] consecutive days, and (b) make-up time
> of equal length offered in writing within 30 days of the child's
> return and taken within 90 days."

The consecutive-day cap is the clause that protects young children from
a "3-week European tour" override.

**Make-up doctrine (what the script prints):**
1. Override agreed → write it down same day (app message counts).
2. Equal-length block offered in writing within 30 days.
3. Taken within 90 days; unused offers expire but are logged.
4. Third unused offer in 12 months → other parent may invoke §7
   communication protocol and, if unresolved, the dispute ladder.

## 4. Right of First Refusal

> "If the scheduled parent cannot personally care for the child for
> more than [8] hours (overnight: [4] hours), they shall first offer
> the time to the other parent before arranging third-party care."

Set the thresholds honestly: 4 hours is unlivable for working parents;
12+ hours makes the clause decorative. The common livable band is
8 daytime / 4 overnight.

## 5. Communication Protocol

> "Parents shall communicate about the child through [APPS: OurFamilyWizard / TalkingParents / AppClose]. Routine scheduling messages
> shall receive a response within 24 hours. Emergencies are by phone."

The paper trail is the point: these apps produce court-admissible
records, which is why high-conflict co-parenting runs on them.

> "Each parent shall provide the other with itineraries, contact
> numbers, and lodging address for any travel with the child exceeding
> [3] days."

## 6. Schedule Review

> "The parents shall review the schedule annually each [MONTH] and may
> modify by written agreement without court action, provided the
> modification is dated, signed by both, and does not change the
> child's school enrollment."

Review clauses prevent the two failure modes: drift (informal changes
accumulate until nobody knows the real schedule) and fossilization
(the plan no longer fits a 13-year-old it was written for at age 5).

## 7. Dispute Ladder (Short of Court)

1. Written issue notice through the parenting app (48h to respond).
2. One 60-minute mediation session (split cost) within 21 days.
3. Parenting coordinator (binding within scope) if previously appointed.
4. Court motion — attach the calendar log, ICS records, and the unused
   make-up offers: dated records win schedule disputes.

## 8. Red Flags This Skill Will Not Solve

- Any history of violence, threats, or abduction → attorney now.
- Substance-abuse relapse cycles → the schedule is not the intervention.
- One parent unilaterally relocating >X miles → jurisdictional rules
  (UCCJEA in the US) apply before calendar mechanics.

In these cases the tool's only legitimate use is documenting the
existing pattern for counsel.
