# Substitutions — Swapping by Function, With Ratios

Every ingredient in a recipe is doing a job. Substitute the job, not the name. An egg in a brownie is binding and moisture; an egg in a meringue is structure — the same swap works for one and destroys the other.

**Read `~/Clawic/data/health/profile.md` before proposing any swap** (allergies, intolerances, conditions) and the `diet` key in `config.yaml`. An allergen is never substituted silently: name the allergen, name the swap, and mark it `untested` (SKILL.md Output Gates).

## What Is The Ingredient Doing?

| Job | Signals it is the job | Substitution is |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | Flour, egg white, gelatine; the thing that holds the shape | Hardest. Expect a different texture and say so |
| Leavening | Baking powder/soda, yeast, whipped white, steam | Ratio-critical; `vetting.md` bands apply after the swap |
| Binding | Egg, starch, breadcrumb in a meatball | Usually easy |
| Fat / tenderizing | Butter, oil, cream, egg yolk | Watch the water content, below |
| Moisture | Milk, water, purée, syrup | Easy, but changes browning |
| Browning / flavour development | Sugar, dairy solids, protein | Swapping sugar changes colour and crispness, not just sweetness |
| Acid | Buttermilk, yoghurt, vinegar, citrus | Must be kept if baking soda is present, or nothing rises |
| Sweetness | Sugars | Liquid sugars carry water — see below |
| Salt / seasoning | Salt, soy, miso, anchovy | Match by *sodium*, not by spoonful |

## Fixed Ratios Worth Knowing

| Missing | Use | Ratio and caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Baking powder | Baking soda + acid | 5 g powder ≈ 1.25 g soda + 2.5 ml vinegar or lemon, and cut other liquid by that amount. Bake immediately — the reaction starts on contact |
| Baking soda | Baking powder | 3× the powder for the same lift, and it adds salt and its own acid. Never a clean swap |
| Buttermilk (240 ml) | Milk + acid | 240 ml milk + 15 ml lemon juice or vinegar, stand 10 min. Or 180 ml plain yoghurt + 60 ml water |
| Self-raising flour (120 g) | Plain flour | 120 g plain + 6 g baking powder (1½ tsp) + 1 g salt. This is the flour's manufactured specification, deliberately above the 4-5 g per 120 g band of `vetting.md`; a recipe written for self-raising already assumes it, so do not re-check the converted formula against that band |
| Cake flour (120 g) | Plain flour | 105 g plain + 15 g cornflour/cornstarch, sifted twice |
| 1 whole egg (binding) | Flax | 10 g ground flaxseed + 45 ml water, 10 min. Works in brownies and pancakes; fails in anything relying on egg for lift |
| 1 whole egg (moisture) | Purée | 60 g apple sauce or mashed banana; expect a denser crumb and its flavour |
| 1 whole egg (structure, whipped) | Aquafaba | 45 ml chickpea liquid whips like a white; needs cream of tartar and patience |
| Butter (100 g) in a batter | Oil | 80 g oil — butter is ~16-18% water, so cut the fat and add ~18 g liquid. In a pastry, do not: the water is what makes the steam |
| Butter in pastry | Solid vegetable fat | 1:1 by weight, but no water content — add 10-15 ml cold water per 100 g |
| Cream (double, 100 g) | Coconut cream | 1:1 by weight; whips if chilled overnight and the water is drained off |
| Milk | Plant milk | 1:1 by volume. Soy behaves closest in baking (protein content); oat browns fastest; almond is thinnest and weakens custards |
| Sugar (200 g) | Honey | 160 g honey, cut other liquid by 50 ml, drop the oven 15 °C — honey browns much faster |
| Sugar (200 g) | Maple syrup | 150 g, cut liquid 60 ml, same temperature drop |
| Brown sugar (200 g) | White + molasses | 200 g white + 15 g molasses (light) or 30 g (dark) |
| Cornflour (thickening) | Plain flour | 2× the flour for the same thickening, and it must cook 2-3 min to lose the raw taste. Cornflour thickens clear and breaks on long boiling; roux holds |
| Fresh herb | Dried | 1 tsp dried ≈ 1 tbsp fresh (3:1). Dried goes in early, fresh goes in late. Basil, parsley and coriander leaf are poor dried at any ratio |
| Wine (deglazing) | Stock + acid | Equal stock plus 5 ml vinegar per 100 ml. Non-alcoholic wine works; grape juice is too sweet |
| Yeast, instant → active dry | — | 1.25× the instant weight, and hydrate it first in some of the recipe's liquid at 38-43 °C |
| Yeast, instant → fresh | — | 3× the instant weight |
| 1 gelatine leaf (2 g platinum) | Powder | 2 g powdered gelatine, bloomed in 12 ml cold water. Agar is not a swap: it sets firmer, at a higher temperature, and does not melt in the mouth |

## Dietary Conversions

Convert by function, then re-check leavening and liquid — that is where converted recipes fail.

**Gluten-free.** No single flour substitutes for wheat. A blend plus a binder: 40% rice flour, 30% starch (tapioca/potato), 30% a protein flour (sorghum, buckwheat, chickpea), plus 1% xanthan gum for cakes or 2% for breads, by total flour weight. GF batters hydrate slowly — rest them 20-30 min before baking or they read gritty. Coeliac disease is a cross-contact problem, not just an ingredient problem: shared toasters, flour dust, and shared fryer oil all count.

**Vegan.** Egg by function (table above); butter as vegan block for pastry (the water content is closer than oil); milk 1:1; honey → maple or agave with the liquid correction; gelatine → agar at 0.8-1% of the liquid, brought to a boil to activate; Worcestershire, fish sauce, some cheeses and many wines carry animal ingredients that are not obvious from the name — check them, do not assume.

**Dairy-free vs lactose-free.** They are different requests. Lactose intolerance usually tolerates hard aged cheese and yoghurt; a dairy allergy tolerates neither. The health box line should say which (`memory-template.md`).

**Low-sodium.** Salt cannot be replaced by a single ingredient. Cut it 25% at a time and rebuild the gap with acid, umami (mushroom, tomato paste, miso — though miso is itself salty), toasted aromatics, and finishing texture (flaky salt on the surface reads saltier per gram than salt dissolved in the mass). Bread is the exception: salt below 1.5% of flour changes the fermentation, not just the taste.

**Nut allergy.** Sunflower and pumpkin seeds replace nuts by weight in most bakes; sunflower seed reacts with baking soda and turns baked goods green — harmless, alarming, avoidable by using powder. Nut flours (almond meal) are not replaceable by seed flours 1:1 in macarons or frangipane, where the fat content is the structure.

## What Cannot Be Substituted

- Yeast with baking powder, in either direction, in a bread. Different gas, different timescale, different structure.
- The fat in a laminated pastry with a liquid fat. There is nothing to laminate.
- Sugar in a caramel, a meringue, a jam set, or a cure. It is the structural agent, not the sweetener.
- Salt in a cure or a ferment below the safe percentage — that is a food-safety floor, not a flavour choice (`vetting.md`).
- Gluten in bread, with anything, at the same result. A GF bread is a different bread, and pretending otherwise wastes an afternoon.

**Write when a substitution is proposed or made**: as an inline note on the ingredient line plus a dated entry in the recipe's `## Variations`, marked `untested` until a cook clears it (`testing.md`). If the swap was driven by an allergy, intolerance or condition the user has just stated, that fact goes into `~/Clawic/data/health/profile.md` with its date and its trigger form — never into `memory.md`, which would create a second version of a safety fact. A guest's restriction goes into their `Context` in `~/Clawic/data/contacts/contacts.md` (`memory-template.md`).
