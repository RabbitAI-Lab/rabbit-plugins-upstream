# Sourdough Starter — Deep Reference

## 1. What a starter actually is

A stable symbiosis of wild yeasts (mostly *Kazachstania humilis* in wheat
starters) and lactic-acid bacteria (*Lactobacillus sanfranciscensis* and
friends). The yeast leavens; the bacteria acidify (flavor + anti-mold).
The two don't compete — LAB ferment maltose the yeast can't use, and the
yeast consume glucose the LAB leave behind. This is why a healthy starter
smells of yogurt and fruit, not alcohol.

## 2. Feeding ratios — what each does

Ratio notation: **starter : flour : water by weight**.

| Ratio | Effect | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 1:1:1 | very fast peak (×0.65 of standard time), fast acidification | revival cycles, cold kitchens, quick build |
| 1:2:2 | fast, moderate acid | pre-fridge feed |
| 1:5:5 | standard; peak 10-12 h @ 24°C | daily routine |
| 1:8:8+ | slow peak, mild flavor | warm kitchens (>26°C), overnight timing |
| 1:10:10 | very slow, very mild | fixing over-acidity |

The pattern: **more fresh flour per unit starter = slower peak, milder
acid**. Acid accumulates from the old starter fraction, so dilution
directly controls sourness and timing.

## 3. Temperature — the master variable

Peak times for a 1:5:5 wheat feed (approximate, jar-marked reality wins):

| Temp | Peak | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 20°C | 16-20 h | "dead-looking" starter is usually just cold |
| 22°C | 12-15 h | cool kitchen normal |
| 24°C | 10-12 h | the reference point |
| 26°C | 7-9 h | feed twice daily |
| 28-30°C | 5-7 h | proofer territory; 1:8:8 to stretch |

The jar's actual microclimate matters: top of a running fridge ≈ 26-28°C
day and night; a windowsill swings 18-30°C with sun — the worst spot.

## 4. Hydration conversions

Hydration = water weight ÷ flour weight × 100%.

- **100%** — the liquid default (equal flour/water).
- **60-65% (stiff)** — firmer, slower, more acetic acid (sharper tang),
  easier overnight timing. Italian tradition.
- **120-166% (liquid/barm)** — faster, milder, lactic-dominant.

Converting: adding water is instant; removing water is not — instead
discard down to ~20g and feed 2-3 cycles at the target hydration. Each
cycle multiplies the target-consistent fraction; after 3 cycles the old
hydration is a rounding error.

## 5. Diagnosis table (10 clusters)

| Symptom | Cause | First move |
|---|---|---|
| No rise, cold kitchen | temperature | move to 24-26°C spot; 1:1:1 ×2d |
| No rise, day 3-5 of new starter | normal lifecycle | keep feeding; rise arrives day 5-7 |
| Acetone / nail-polish smell | yeast starvation | discard to 10g; 1:5:5 q12h ×2-3d |
| Gray/brown liquid (hooch) | underfed | pour off, feed 1:2:2 — not fatal |
| Alcohol/boozy smell | acid+ethanol accumulation | larger dilute feeds, shorter gaps |
| Rose then collapsed | missed peak | timing problem, not health; dilute more |
| Bubbles but no volume | young or weak network | whole-grain feeds, patience |
| Fuzzy colored spots | MOLD | discard jar, start fresh |
| Pink/orange streaks | bacterial takeover | discard; pineapple-juice restart |
| Sluggish post-fridge | dormancy | 1:1:1 q12h; doubling by cycle 2-3 |

## 6. The float test, honestly

A spoonful of starter floats when its density is low — i.e., when it holds
enough gas. False positives: freshly stirred starter (air pockets) and
starters well past peak (structure still traps old gas). It's a quick
sanity check, never a substitute for the jar mark + expected peak window.

## 7. Vacation and hibernation

- **≤2 weeks**: 1:2:2 feed, 2 h at room temp, fridge. Revive 1:1:1 q12h;
  bake-ready ~48 h.
- **2-6 weeks**: same, coldest fridge zone, expect serious hooch. Revival
  2-3 days.
- **Longer**: dry chips — thin smear on parchment, 24-48 h to glass-hard,
  airtight dark jar. Revival: soak + feed; doubling by day 3-5. Chips
  keep for months to years and are the standard "backup" move.

Starters die from heat (>45°C), mold, or soap residue — rarely from
neglect. The "I killed it" funeral is usually a 48-hour revival away.

## 8. Levain builds for bake day

A levain is an offshoot build timed to peak at dough mixing:

- **1:1:5** (20g starter + 20g flour + 100g water) — peaks ~0.75× the
  standard time; the everyday build.
- **1:2:4 before bed at 20-21°C** — peaks in 10-12 h for a morning bake.
- **Stiff 60% levain** — add 30-50% to peak time; sharper flavor, forgiving
  window.

Rule: use the levain at peak (domed, 2-2.5×, pleasant aroma). Before peak
= weak lift; 3+ h after = acid-heavy and collapsing. Trust the jar mark
over the clock; kitchens swing ±2°C and hours move with them.

## 9. Rye vs wheat

Rye ferments faster (more enzymes and minerals): expect 20-30% shorter
peaks and a slightly different acid profile. A wheat starter fed rye for
two feeds gets a visible vigor boost — the classic revival trick; a
permanently-rye starter runs on shorter cycles. Gluten-free starters are
a different stable entirely (bacteria-dominated, tea-like aromas); treat
advice for wheat as approximate there.

## 10. Safety

Discard on: fuzzy mold (any color), pink/orange pigmentation, or fuzzy
growth on the jar rim. Everything else — hooch, acetone, gray translucence
— is recoverable. Baked bread is acid + heat sanitized; the realistic
hazard from a sour starter is disappointment, not disease.
