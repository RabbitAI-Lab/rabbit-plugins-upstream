# Symptoms — Complaint To Discriminator

One label is never the output (SKILL.md Rule 3). For each complaint: what raises concern, what lowers it, and the single question or observation that separates the candidates.

**Before matching any pattern**, read `~/Clawic/data/health/profile.md` (conditions, medicines, allergies) and the episode log if `## Boxes` indexes one — a symptom that has recurred five times with the same benign outcome is a different problem from a first presentation, and the reverse is also true: a changed pattern in an old symptom is itself a red flag.

## Chest Pain

| Raises concern | Lowers concern |
|---|---|
| Pressure or heaviness, not sharpness; radiation to jaw, neck or left arm; sweating, nausea, breathlessness; brought on by exertion and relieved by rest; over 40 with diabetes, smoking, hypertension or family history | Reproduced exactly by pressing on the chest wall; changes sharply with position or a deep breath alone; brief stabs lasting seconds; long history of identical episodes already investigated |

- Discriminator: **does exertion reproduce it and rest relieve it within minutes?** That pattern is angina until proven otherwise, regardless of age.
- Pleuritic pain plus breathlessness plus a swollen calf, recent flight, surgery, pregnancy or oestrogen contraception → pulmonary embolism; risk scores exist (Wells, PERC) but the referral is clinical.
- Tearing pain radiating to the back with unequal arm pulses → aortic dissection; emergency, and it is the reason not to assume every severe chest pain is cardiac.
- Reflux and cardiac pain overlap so heavily that response to an antacid excludes nothing. Never use it as a test.
- In diabetic and older people, the heart attack presents as fatigue, nausea, or breathlessness with no pain at all (`older-adults.md`).

## Breathlessness

- Timeline decides the shortlist: seconds → pneumothorax, embolism, anaphylaxis, arrhythmia. Hours to days → infection, asthma or COPD flare, heart failure. Weeks to months → anaemia, deconditioning, interstitial disease, thyroid.
- Ask for the **exercise capacity change**, not a severity score: "how many stairs before you stop, and how many last month". It is the only quantity the person can report reliably.
- Orthopnoea (needing extra pillows) and waking at night gasping point to heart failure; leg swelling with it strengthens that.
- Wheeze on breathing out favours asthma or COPD; noisy breathing on breathing in (stridor) is an airway emergency.
- SpO₂ from a fingertip oximeter: ≤91% on room air is an escalation. Cold hands, nail polish and poor perfusion give falsely low readings — warm the hand and re-check before acting, and never use a normal reading to override obvious distress.

## Headache

Red flags (any one → same day or emergency): maximum intensity in under a minute · first severe headache after 50 · headache with fever and neck stiffness · worse lying flat or on waking with vomiting · after a head injury, especially on anticoagulants · with new neurological signs, personality change or seizure · in pregnancy past 20 weeks · in someone immunosuppressed or with cancer.

| Pattern | Fits |
|---|---|
| One-sided, throbbing, 4-72 h, nausea, light and sound sensitivity, wants a dark room | Migraine |
| Band-like pressure both sides, mild-moderate, no nausea, carries on with the day | Tension-type |
| Severe one-sided around the eye, 15-180 min, tearing and nose running, restless and pacing | Cluster |
| Daily headache in someone taking painkillers ≥10-15 days/month | Medication-overuse headache — the treatment is the cause |
| Over 50, scalp tenderness, jaw pain when chewing, visual symptoms | Giant cell arteritis — same-day; untreated it takes sight |

## Abdominal Pain

- Location narrows it: right upper (gallbladder, liver), epigastric (ulcer, pancreas, referred cardiac), right lower (appendix, ovary, ectopic), left lower (diverticular, bowel), suprapubic (bladder, uterus), flank (kidney, stone), diffuse (obstruction, ischaemia, gastroenteritis).
- Discriminator: **does movement make it worse?** Peritoneal pain (appendicitis, perforation) hates movement and coughing; colic (stone, obstruction) makes the person restless and unable to stay still.
- Pain that precedes vomiting suggests a surgical cause; vomiting that precedes pain suggests gastroenteritis.
- In any person capable of pregnancy with lower abdominal pain, ectopic pregnancy is excluded by a test, not by a menstrual history.
- Over 60 with sudden severe pain and a soft abdomen out of proportion to the examination → mesenteric ischaemia; classically missed for hours.

## Back Pain

- **Cauda equina red flags — emergency, same hour**: saddle numbness, new urinary retention or incontinence, faecal incontinence, bilateral leg weakness or sciatica.
- Other red flags: fever with back pain (discitis, abscess), thoracic pain, night pain that wakes them, unexplained weight loss, history of cancer, intravenous drug use, steroids or osteoporosis with a new pain after minor trauma, age under 20 or new onset over 55.
- Without red flags, 90% of acute low back pain settles within six weeks and imaging changes nothing: MRI finds disc bulges in a majority of people with no pain at all, and the scan reliably worsens outcomes by anchoring the person to a structural story.
- What actually helps: stay active within pain, keep working, short-term simple analgesia, movement rather than bed rest beyond a day or two.

## Dizziness And Fainting

- Make them choose the word: **spinning** (vertigo — inner ear, vestibular neuritis, BPPV) · **about to black out** (presyncope — cardiac, blood pressure, anaemia, dehydration) · **unsteady** (neurological, medication, joint) · **fuzzy** (anxiety, hyperventilation, medication).
- Vertigo triggered by rolling over in bed, lasting under a minute → BPPV; continuous for days after a viral illness → vestibular neuritis; with new deafness or neurological signs → same-day assessment.
- **Faint red flags**: happened during exertion, happened while lying down, preceded by palpitations, no warning at all, or a family history of sudden death under 40. Any of these is a cardiac work-up, not a vasovagal faint.
- A vasovagal faint has a prodrome (hot, nauseated, greying vision), a trigger (standing, pain, blood, heat) and a quick full recovery on the floor.

## Fever

- The number matters far less than the person: an adult at 39 °C who is alert, drinking and comfortable is safer than one at 37.8 °C who is confused, breathing fast or mottled.
- Duration rules for adults: fever >5 days, or any fever with a focal severe symptom, warrants review. Fever after travel to a malaria-endemic area within a year is an urgent test, not a wait.
- Antipyretics treat discomfort, not the illness, and do not shorten it. Alternating agents to chase a number is a common and unnecessary source of dosing error.
- Rigors — teeth-chattering, uncontrollable shaking — indicate bacteraemia far more than a high temperature does.
- Infants and children have their own thresholds and a traffic-light system (`children.md`).

## Rash

| Feature | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Does not blanch under pressed glass, with fever | Meningococcal sepsis — emergency now |
| Blisters, skin peeling, or mouth/eye/genital involvement | Severe drug reaction (Stevens-Johnson) — emergency |
| Started within days of a new medicine | Drug reaction until proven otherwise; name the medicine and its start date |
| Spreading redness, hot, tender, with a fever | Cellulitis — same day; mark the border with a pen and time it |
| Target-shaped lesions, or an expanding ring after a tick bite | Erythema multiforme; Lyme — same-week treatment |
| Itchy weals coming and going within hours | Urticaria; with lip or tongue swelling it is anaphylaxis territory |

## Cough And Sore Throat

- Cough duration bands: <3 weeks acute (usually viral) · 3-8 weeks subacute (post-infectious, often lasts and needs no treatment) · >8 weeks chronic (asthma, reflux, post-nasal drip, ACE inhibitor, smoking, and the ones you must not miss — cancer, tuberculosis, interstitial disease).
- Any cough with blood, unexplained weight loss, a smoking history over 40, or persistent hoarseness beyond 3 weeks → urgent review.
- ACE inhibitors cause a dry cough in roughly one in ten users, sometimes months after starting. Check the medicine list before investigating anything else.
- Sore throat: the Centor/McIsaac criteria — fever, absence of cough, tender anterior neck nodes, tonsillar exudate, and age — estimate the chance of streptococcus. 0-1 criteria means no test and no antibiotic; 3 or more justifies testing. Unilateral severe pain with drooling, a muffled voice or trouble opening the mouth is an abscess and is same-day.

## Fatigue

The complaint most often dismissed and most often worth a blood test. Ask three things: is it **sleepiness** (sleep disorder, apnoea), **exhaustion after exertion** (cardiac, respiratory, anaemia), or **lack of drive** (depression, thyroid)?

- Baseline tests a clinician will usually consider: full blood count, ferritin, TSH, HbA1c, kidney and liver function, calcium, coeliac serology, and vitamin D or B12 where the diet or symptoms suggest it (`labs.md`).
- Red flags: fatigue with weight loss, night sweats, lymph node swelling, or that started abruptly on a nameable day.
- Screen for depression and for sleep apnoea (snoring, witnessed pauses, morning headache, BMI) before attributing fatigue to stress (`mental-health.md`, `sleep`).

## Weight Loss And Lumps

- Unintentional loss of **more than 5% of body weight in 6-12 months** is a work-up, not a diet success. Formula: 78 kg → 74 kg unintentionally in six months is 5.1%, and qualifies.
- Lump features that raise concern: hard, fixed to underlying tissue, painless, growing, over 2 cm, in the neck of a smoker, or with skin change over it. Breast, testicular and neck lumps get seen even when they feel benign.
- Night sweats that soak the bedding, plus lymph nodes, plus weight loss is a specific triad — say it as a triad, because each alone is common and the combination is not.

## Urinary

- Cystitis in a non-pregnant woman: burning, frequency, urgency, no fever, no flank pain → often self-limiting; treatment shortens symptoms. In men, in pregnancy, with fever, flank pain, vomiting, or in anyone catheterised, it is an escalation.
- Visible blood in the urine without infection, at any age, is a referral — painless haematuria is the classic presentation of bladder cancer.
- In older people, confusion alone is frequently blamed on a urine infection based on a dipstick; asymptomatic bacteriuria is common and treating it does not fix the confusion (`older-adults.md`).

## Palpitations

- Ask them to **tap the rhythm** on the table: regular fast, irregular chaotic, or single skipped beats. Irregularly irregular suggests atrial fibrillation and needs an ECG within days, because the treatable risk is stroke, not the sensation.
- Red flags: with chest pain, with fainting, during exertion, or a family history of sudden death under 40.
- Isolated skipped beats at rest that vanish with exercise are usually ectopics; still worth one ECG if new and frequent.
- Check the aggravators before the heart: caffeine load, alcohol, decongestants, salbutamol, thyroid medicine, stimulants, anaemia, dehydration.

## Swelling

- **One leg**, calf pain, warmth, more than 3 cm difference in calf circumference → deep vein thrombosis, same-day assessment, especially after travel, surgery, immobility, cancer, pregnancy or oestrogen.
- **Both legs**, worse in the evening, pitting → heart, kidney, liver, or a medicine (amlodipine and other calcium-channel blockers, NSAIDs, gabapentinoids, steroids).
- Face or tongue swelling → allergic or ACE-inhibitor angioedema; the ACE-inhibitor version can begin years into treatment and does not respond to antihistamines.

## Where This Goes

**Once the episode has a shape, write it in the same turn** (`memory-template.md`): a row in `~/Clawic/data/doctor/episodes/<year>.md` — date, complaint, key discriminators found, rung given, outcome once known.
