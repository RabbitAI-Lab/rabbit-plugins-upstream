# Rate Analysis Guide — How to Read Utility Bills & Spot Errors

Understanding your utility bill is the first step to reducing it. This guide breaks down the components of electricity, water, and gas bills, and highlights common billing errors.

---

## 📋 Anatomy of an Electricity Bill

### Key Components
| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Meter reading** | Actual or estimated kWh consumed. Look for "A" (actual) vs "E" (estimated). |
| **Supply charge** | Cost of the electricity itself (per kWh). This is the energy you consumed. |
| **Delivery / Distribution charge** | Cost to transport electricity over wires to your home. Often fixed + variable. |
| **Demand charge** | (Commercial mostly) Peak power draw in kW. Residential rarely has this. |
| **Time-of-use (TOU)** | Different rates for peak, off-peak, and shoulder periods. |
| **Fixed daily charge** | A flat daily fee regardless of usage. Typically $0.30-$1.50/day. |
| **Taxes & fees** | Sales tax, public benefits charge, franchise fee, etc. |

### How to Calculate Your Effective Rate
```
Effective $/kWh = Total Bill Amount ÷ Total kWh
```
This is the true cost of each kWh including all charges. Compare this number across plans and providers.

### Reading the Meter
- **Digital meter**: Read the displayed kWh directly.
- **Dial meter**: Read dials left to right; if pointer is between numbers, use the lower number.
- **Smart meter**: Check your utility's app/portal for real-time data.

**Verify accuracy**: Note the meter reading on the bill date. Next month, subtract previous reading from current. The difference should match the billed usage.

## 📋 Anatomy of a Gas Bill

### Key Components
| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Meter reading** | Volume consumed, usually in m³ or therms or CCF. |
| **Therm conversion** | 1 therm ≈ 29.3 kWh ≈ 100,000 BTU. Gas is often billed in therms. |
| **Supply charge** | Cost of the gas commodity (per therm/m³). |
| **Delivery charge** | Cost to transport gas through pipelines. |
| **Weather normalization** | Some utilities adjust usage for weather (WNA). |
| **Customer charge** | Fixed monthly fee for connection. |

### Gas Unit Conversions
- 1 m³ of natural gas ≈ 10.55 kWh ≈ 0.360 therms
- 1 therm = 100,000 BTU = 29.3 kWh
- 1 CCF (100 cubic feet) ≈ 1.037 therms

## 📋 Anatomy of a Water Bill

### Key Components
| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Meter reading** | Volume consumed, usually in m³, gallons, or CCF. |
| **Tiered rates** | Usage is often priced in tiers — the more you use, the higher the per-unit rate. |
| **Fixed service charge** | Flat monthly/quarterly fee for water connection. |
| **Sewer / Wastewater charge** | Often based on water consumption. |
| **Stormwater fee** | Fixed fee for drainage infrastructure. |
| **Irrigation meter** | Separate meter for outdoor water — may not include sewer charges. |

### Reading a Water Meter
- Most meters read in m³ or gallons.
- Check for a low-flow indicator (small triangle/dial) — if it spins with everything off, you have a leak.
- Use the **2-hour test**: Read meter, don't use water for 2 hours, read again. If it changed, there's a leak.

## 🚩 Common Billing Errors

### 1. Estimated Reading Errors
**Problem**: Utility estimates usage instead of reading the meter. If prior estimates were low, you get a "catch-up" bill that looks like a spike.

**Fix**: Submit your own meter reading. Request a re-bill based on actual readings. Check if your utility offers a "read your own meter" portal.

### 2. Incorrect Meter Assigned
**Problem**: You're being billed for a neighbor's meter, especially in apartments and condos.

**Fix**: Turn off your main breaker / main water valve. Check if the meter stops. If not, call the utility.

### 3. Decimal Point Errors
**Problem**: Meter reading transcribed wrong (e.g., 450 kWh entered as 4,500).

**Fix**: Compare to historical usage. A 10× jump is almost always an error. Request a reading verification.

### 4. Wrong Meter Multiplier
**Problem**: Commercial meters and some residential meters have a multiplier (e.g., ×10). If applied incorrectly, bills can be 10× too high or too low.

**Fix**: Check for "multiplier" or "MF" on the bill. Verify with utility.

### 5. Double Billing
**Problem**: Overlapping billing periods charge the same usage twice.

**Fix**: Check service start/end dates on each bill. Overlapping dates = billing error.

### 6. Rate Plan Mismatch
**Problem**: You're on a default/standard plan when a time-of-use or off-peak plan would be cheaper.

**Fix**: Analyze your usage pattern. If you can shift load to off-peak (EV charging at night, laundry at night), TOU can save 15-30%.

### 7. Phantom Tier 2/3 Charges (Water)
**Problem**: Tiered water rates penalize high usage. A small leak pushes you into Tier 2 or 3, doubling or tripling the marginal rate.

**Fix**: Fix leaks immediately. Monitor usage monthly.

### 8. Sewer Charge Based on Irrigation
**Problem**: Sewer charges are based on total water usage, but irrigation water doesn't go into the sewer.

**Fix**: Install a separate irrigation meter. Request a sewer cap (some utilities average winter usage for summer sewer billing).

### 9. Incorrect Seasonal Rate
**Problem**: Summer/winter rate boundaries are applied incorrectly.

**Fix**: Check the rate period on the bill against the utility's published schedule.

### 10. Fees for Services You Don't Have
**Problem**: Charges for services like "equipment maintenance," "line insurance," or third-party energy supply you never signed up for.

**Fix**: Review every line item. Google unfamiliar charges. Dispute unauthorized ones.

## 🔍 How to Audit Your Own Bill

### Step-by-Step
1. **Gather 12 months** of bills for each utility.
2. **Create a spreadsheet** (or use this tool!) with: date, usage, total cost, $/unit.
3. **Calculate effective rate** = total cost ÷ usage for each month.
4. **Look for anomalies**: Any month where usage or cost is >50% above average.
5. **Compare year-over-year**: Same month, different years. Big differences need investigation.
6. **Check for estimated readings**: If the last 3+ readings were estimated, submit actual readings.
7. **Compare plans**: Use your usage profile to shop competing providers.
8. **Check meter-to-bill consistency**: Read your meter today; compare to last bill's reading.

### Red Flags
- ⚠ Usage spike > 50% with no lifestyle change → check for leaks, meter errors
- ⚠ Effective rate increasing while usage is stable → rate increase, check competing plans
- ⚠ Consecutive estimated readings → submit actual reading
- ⚠ Bill doubled with same usage → tier change, meter multiplier error, or rate change
- ⚠ Charges from third-party suppliers you don't recognize → unauthorized enrollment

## 📐 Rate Comparison Worksheet

When comparing utility plans, calculate the **all-in effective rate**:

```
Effective Rate = (Total Monthly Cost) ÷ (Monthly Usage)
                ↑ includes supply + delivery + fees + taxes
```

| Plan Feature | Check |
|---|---|
| Supply rate ($/kWh or $/therm) | Is it fixed or variable? |
| Fixed monthly fee | $0-$30/month adds up |
| Tiered pricing? | At what usage does Tier 2 kick in? |
| Time-of-use? | Can you shift >50% of usage to off-peak? |
| Contract length | Early termination fees? |
| Renewable/green option | Is it included or extra? |
| Bill credits / incentives | Refer-a-friend, autopay, paperless discounts? |

**Rule of thumb**: If switching plans saves >5% on effective rate, it's worth the paperwork.

## 🌡️ Understanding Degree Days

Heating Degree Days (HDD) and Cooling Degree Days (CDD) normalize energy usage for weather:

- **1 HDD** = a day where the average outdoor temperature is 1°C below 18°C (heating baseline)
- **1 CDD** = a day where the average outdoor temperature is 1°C above 18°C (cooling baseline)

Use degree-day data from your local weather service to compare usage across years with different weather. If your usage went up 20% but degree days went up 25%, your home actually became more efficient.
