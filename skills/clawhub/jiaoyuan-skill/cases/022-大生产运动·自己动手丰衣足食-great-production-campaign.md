# 大生产运动·自己动手丰衣足食 The Great Production Campaign: Do It Yourself, Ample Food and Clothing

## 局势 / The Situation
一九四一年和一九四二年是抗日战争期间根据地最困难的时期。日本侵略军的野蛮进攻加上国民党的包围封锁和停发经费，使根据地财政发生极大困难，一度弄到几乎没有衣穿、没有油吃、没有纸、没有菜。教员在陕甘宁边区高级干部会议上作报告，提出克服经济困难的方针。

**English:**

The years 1941 and 1942 were the most difficult period for the base areas during the War of Resistance Against Japan. The savage attacks of the Japanese invaders, combined with the Kuomintang's encirclement and blockade and its cutoff of funds, brought the base areas' finances to an extreme crisis—at one point there was almost no clothing to wear, no oil to eat, no paper, and no vegetables. Jiaoyuan (the Teacher) delivered a report at a conference of senior cadres of the Shaanxi-Gansu-Ningxia Border Region, setting out a policy for overcoming the economic difficulties.

## 判断 / The Assessment
他提出总方针，反对只在财政收支上打圈子：“发展经济，保障供给，是我们的经济工作和财政工作的总方针。”（《抗日时期的经济问题和财政问题》）他判断财政的根子在经济：“财政困难，只有从切切实实的有效的经济发展上才能解决。”（《抗日时期的经济问题和财政问题》）据此，他主张不靠收缩开支、不靠加重人民负担，而靠领导人民发展生产、机关部队自己动手生产自给来解决问题。

**English:**

He set out the general policy and opposed going around in circles on fiscal revenue and expenditure: "Developing the economy and ensuring supplies is the general policy of our economic and financial work." (《抗日时期的经济问题和财政问题》 "Economic and Financial Problems in the Anti-Japanese War") He judged that the root of finance lies in the economy: "Financial difficulties can be solved only through real and effective economic development." (《抗日时期的经济问题和财政问题》 "Economic and Financial Problems in the Anti-Japanese War") Accordingly, he held that the problem should be solved not by cutting expenditures or by increasing the burdens on the people, but by leading the people to develop production and by having government organs and army units produce their own supplies.

## 行动 / The Action
他号召根据地的机关、学校、部队尽可能地实行生产自给：军队开展大规模生产运动，发展以自给为目标的农工商业；机关学校人员发展同样的自给经济；同时实行精兵简政，达到精简、统一、效能、节约和反对官僚主义五项目的；在帮助人民发展生产的同时注意减轻人民负担、休养民力。

**English:**

He called on the government organs, schools, and army units in the base areas to achieve self-sufficiency through production wherever possible: the army launched a large-scale production campaign, developing agriculture, industry, and commerce with self-sufficiency as the goal; personnel in organs and schools developed a similar self-supporting economy; and at the same time the policy of "better troops and simpler administration" was carried out, aimed at the five goals of streamlining, unity, efficiency, economy, and opposition to bureaucracy. While helping the people develop production, attention was also paid to lightening their burdens and conserving their strength.

## 结果 / The Outcome
在这个方针下，陕甘宁边区和敌后各抗日根据地的生产运动取得巨大成绩，军民胜利渡过了抗日战争的最困难时期，边区军民自己动手达到了丰衣足食，也为后来领导经济建设积累了经验。

**English:**

Under this policy, the production campaigns in the Shaanxi-Gansu-Ningxia Border Region and in the various anti-Japanese base areas behind enemy lines achieved tremendous results; army and people pulled through the most difficult period of the War of Resistance, the border region achieved ample food and clothing through its own efforts, and valuable experience was accumulated for later leadership of economic construction.

## 可迁移点 / Transferable Lessons
- 财政、现金问题不能只在“节流”上打主意，根本出路在“开源”发展——困难时期尤其要先想办法把“生产”做大，而不是一味收缩。
- 靠“自己动手”而不是等靠要：外部资源被卡住时，向内挖潜、自力更生往往是最可靠的路。
- 渡过难关要统筹“取”与“予”：既要解决眼前供给，也要帮扶持对象恢复增长，取之有度、休养民力，才能长期支持。

**English:**

- Financial and cash problems can't be solved only by "cutting expenditure"; the fundamental way out is to "open up new sources" and develop — in hard times especially, think first of enlarging "production" rather than simply contracting.
- Rely on "doing it yourself" rather than waiting, depending, or demanding: when external resources are blocked, digging deep within and standing on your own feet is often the most reliable path.
- To get through a crisis, balance "taking" and "giving": solve the immediate supply problem while also helping the people you depend on recover their growth; take with moderation and conserve their strength, so that support can be sustained over the long term.
