# 重庆谈判·针锋相对 The Chongqing Negotiations: Tit for Tat

## 局势 / The Situation
一九四五年八月日本投降后，蒋介石一面三次电邀教员赴重庆谈判，一面调集军队进攻解放区、准备内战。去不去谈、如何谈，成为关系全局的重大抉择。

**English:**

After Japan's surrender in August 1945, Chiang Kai-shek, on the one hand, three times telegraphed to invite Jiaoyuan (the Teacher) to Chongqing for negotiations, while on the other hand mobilizing troops to attack the liberated areas and preparing for civil war. Whether to go to the talks and how to negotiate became a major decision bearing on the whole situation.

## 判断 / The Assessment
对国民党既要谈判、又要斗争，基本方针是针锋相对、寸土必争。"蒋介石对于人民是寸权必夺，寸利必得。我们呢？我们的方针是针锋相对，寸土必争。"（《抗日战争胜利后的时局和我们的方针》）但针锋相对不能僵化：""针锋相对"，要看形势。有时候不去谈，是针锋相对；有时候去谈，也是针锋相对。"（《关于重庆谈判》）谈判中让步有边界："让步是有限度的，以不伤害人民根本利益为原则。"（《中共中央关于同国民党进行和平谈判的通知》）"绝对不要依靠谈判，绝对不要希望国民党发善心，它是不会发善心的。必须依靠自己手里的力量"（《中共中央关于同国民党进行和平谈判的通知》）。

**English:**

The Kuomintang had to be both negotiated with and struggled against; the basic policy was tit for tat and fighting for every inch of land. "Chiang Kai-shek is trying to wrest every ounce of power and every ounce of gain from the people. And what about us? Our policy is to give him tit for tat and fight for every inch of land" (《抗日战争胜利后的时局和我们的方针》, The Situation and Our Policy After the Victory in the War of Resistance Against Japan). But tit for tat must not be rigid: "'Tit for tat' depends on the situation. Sometimes not going to the talks is tit for tat; sometimes going to the talks is also tit for tat" (《关于重庆谈判》, On the Chongqing Negotiations). Concessions in the negotiations had boundaries: "Concessions are limited, and the principle is that the fundamental interests of the people must not be harmed" (《中共中央关于同国民党进行和平谈判的通知》, Circular of the CPC Central Committee on Peace Negotiations with the Kuomintang). "Never rely on negotiations, and never hope that the Kuomintang will show kindness; it will not. We must rely on the strength in our own hands" (《中共中央关于同国民党进行和平谈判的通知》).

## 行动 / The Action
教员亲赴重庆谈判，坚持针锋相对、寸土必争；为争取和平、击破内战阴谋，作必要让步（让出部分南方解放区、提出军队缩编），但人民的武装一枪一弹都不交出；同时在谈判期间于上党等自卫反击中坚决消灭来犯之敌。

**English:**

Jiaoyuan went in person to Chongqing for the negotiations, upholding tit for tat and fighting for every inch of land; to strive for peace and break the civil-war scheme, necessary concessions were made (giving up part of the southern liberated areas and proposing a reduction of the army), but not a single gun or bullet of the people's armed forces was handed over; at the same time, during the negotiations, the invading enemy was resolutely destroyed in self-defense counter-attacks at Shangdang and elsewhere.

## 结果 / The Outcome
达成《双十协定》，在政治上取得极大主动，击破了"共产党不要和平、不要团结"的谣言；军事上粉碎了国民党对解放区的进攻，为自卫战争赢得了政治和道义基础。

**English:**

The Double Tenth Agreement was reached, gaining great political initiative and shattering the rumor that "the Communist Party does not want peace or unity"; militarily, the Kuomintang's attacks on the liberated areas were smashed, winning the political and moral basis for the self-defense war.

## 可迁移点 / Transferable Lessons
1. 面对强势对手"谈与打"两手并用时，借鉴"针锋相对，寸土必争"：对方进一寸，我争回一寸，核心利益寸步不让。
2. 借鉴"去谈也是针锋相对"：谈判（沟通、合作谈判）本身可以是斗争的一种形式，去谈不是软弱，而是争取主动、占据道义高地。
3. 借鉴"让步有限度、不靠对方发善心"：谈判时明确底线，必要让步可以，但根本利益不动摇，并始终把主动权建立在自己的实力上。

**English:**

1. When facing a powerful opponent who combines negotiation with fighting, borrow "tit for tat and fighting for every inch": for every inch the other side advances, win back an inch; yield not an inch on core interests.
2. Borrow "going to the talks is also tit for tat": negotiation (communication, cooperative negotiation) can itself be a form of struggle; going to the talks is not weakness but seizing the initiative and occupying the moral high ground.
3. Borrow "concessions are limited, and do not rely on the other side's kindness": in negotiation, clarify the bottom line; necessary concessions are acceptable, but fundamental interests must not waver, and always base the initiative on your own strength.
