# 全局与局部 The Whole and the Part

## 触发情境 / Trigger Situations
- 陷入局部得失，赢了眼前，输了整体
- 团队里每个人只顾自己的一亩三分地，没人管整盘棋
- 做决策时只见树木不见森林，抓不住真正关键的那一步

**English:**

- You get mired in local gains and losses: you win the moment and lose the whole.
- Everyone on the team tends only their own plot; no one watches the whole board.
- When deciding, you see only the trees and miss the forest, failing to grasp the truly decisive move.

## 思维路径 / Thinking Path
1. 先跳出来，把整盘棋看清：目标、阶段、各方力量、关键节点
2. 承认全局是"看不见"的，得靠用心想、反复推演，不能只靠眼前直观
3. 分清哪些环节决定全局，哪些只是局部的、可以舍弃的
4. 把资源和注意力优先放在决定全局的那一着上，而不是平均用力
5. 反过来也清楚：全局由局部构成，关键局部一旦失守会拖垮全局
6. 用"全局"这把尺子重新量每个局部：这个局部到底在服务什么全局？
7. 做任何局部决策前先问：这一步对全局是有利还是有害？

**English:**

1. First step back and see the whole board: the goal, the stages, the various forces, the key nodes.
2. Acknowledge that the whole is "invisible"; it must be thought through and re-enacted repeatedly, not grasped by immediate intuition alone.
3. Distinguish which links decide the whole and which are merely local and dispensable.
4. Put resources and attention first on the move that decides the whole, rather than spreading effort evenly.
5. Conversely, be clear that the whole is made of parts; if a key part falls, it drags down the whole.
6. Use "the whole" as a ruler to re-measure every part: what whole, exactly, is this part serving?
7. Before any local decision, ask: does this move help or harm the whole?

## 决策原则 / Decision Principles
- 先看清全局再动局部，不为局部小胜牺牲全局
- 抓"对全局有决定意义的一着"，其余该舍就舍
- 全局观靠刻意训练（想、复盘、画图），不是天赋

**English:**

- See the whole before moving any part; don't sacrifice the whole for small local wins.
- Seize "the move of decisive significance to the whole"; let go of the rest when you should.
- A whole-situation view is built by deliberate practice (thinking, reviewing, mapping), not by talent.

## 适用边界 + 反例提醒 / Applicability Boundaries & Counterexamples
- 适用：战略规划、组织管理、复杂项目、投资组合等需要统筹取舍的决策。
- 反例：全局观不是空谈——只讲全局没有局部执行等于纸上谈兵；也别因强调"全局"而忽视某个关键细节（关键局部本身就是全局的一部分）。

**English:**

- Applies to: strategic planning, organizational management, complex projects, portfolios — decisions that require overall coordination and trade-offs.
- Counterexamples: a whole-situation view is not empty talk — speaking of the whole without local execution is armchair strategy; nor should you neglect a key detail in the name of "the whole" (a key part is itself part of the whole).
