# 实践—认识—再实践 Practice, Knowledge, Practice

## 触发情境 / Trigger Situations
- 学了理论不会用，知识与行动脱节
- 经验固化、方法不更新，重复犯错
- 方案与结果对不上，却不去复盘修正

**English:**

- You've learned theory but can't use it; knowledge and action are disconnected.
- Experience is frozen and methods never updated; the same mistakes repeat.
- The plan and the result don't match, yet you don't review and correct.

## 思维路径 / Thinking Path
1. 从实践（做）中获取第一手经验
2. 把零散的感性经验上升为理性认识，提炼出规律
3. 用提炼出的认识指导新的实践
4. 在实践中检验认识，发现偏差就修正
5. 循环往复，认识与实践螺旋式上升
6. 反对两种偏向：只讲理论不实践（教条主义）、只埋头干不总结（经验主义）

**English:**

1. Gain first-hand experience from practice (doing).
2. Raise scattered perceptual experience into rational understanding, distilling out the laws.
3. Use the distilled understanding to guide new practice.
4. Test the understanding in practice; correct it when deviation appears.
5. Repeat the cycle, with understanding and practice rising in a spiral.
6. Oppose two deviations: speaking only of theory without practice (dogmatism), and working head-down without summing up (empiricism).

## 决策原则 / Decision Principles
- 边做边学边改，用结果检验认知
- 把经验固化为方法，再让方法接受新一轮检验
- 迭代不是随意试错，每次循环都要有提炼

**English:**

- Learn and adjust as you do; test understanding by results.
- Fix experience into method, then put the method through a new round of testing.
- Iteration is not random trial and error; every loop must distill something.

## 适用边界 + 反例提醒 / Applicability Boundaries & Counterexamples
- 适用：可迭代、可反馈、可试错的事务（产品迭代、技能练习、管理改进）。
- 反例：不可逆、无试错机会的重大决策（如法律、医疗、一次性大额投入）需先充分准备再行动，不能靠"先做再改"。

**English:**

- Applies to: matters that can be iterated, fed back, and trial-errored (product iteration, skill practice, management improvement).
- Counterexamples: irreversible, no-retry major decisions (law, medicine, large one-time investments) require thorough preparation before acting; they can't rely on "do first, fix later."

>
> "A correct understanding often requires many repetitions of the process leading from matter to consciousness and then back to matter, that is, from practice to knowledge and then back to practice, before it can be completed." —— Quotations (1965 ed.), Thought and Methods of Work
