# Collections Playbook — Psychology, Scripts, Prevention

## Why Clients Pay Late (and which one you have)

| Type | Behavior | Correct response |
|---|---|---|
| The Forgetter | big company, invoice lost in AP | friendly reminders + phone AP; ask for PO number |
| The Prioritizer | pays whoever squeaks loudest | predictable escalating cadence — be the squeak |
| The Disputer | unhappy but never said so | force the dispute into words NOW; ask what's wrong in writing |
| The Drowning | cash-flow trouble | partial-payment plan; small regular beats promises |
| The Ghost | disappeared | demand letter early; skip to small claims at 45d |
| The Strategic | pays late on purpose (float) | late fees + next-project deposit terms |

First diagnostic question, always: "I may have missed a note — is there
any issue with the work or the invoice?" This splits Disputer from
everyone else in one reply, and its paper trail matters later.

## Scripts Per Step (send log: date, step, channel)

**Day -3 (pre-due):**
> Quick heads-up: invoice INV-1042 ($4,200) is due Friday. Forwarding
> in case it needs routing. [attach]

**Day +1:**
> Invoice INV-1042 was due yesterday and shows unpaid. Could you
> confirm it's in the payment queue and the expected pay date? Happy
> to resend any documentation AP needs.

**Day +7 (fees start):**
> INV-1042 is now 7 days past due. Per our agreement, late charges of
> 10%/yr begin accruing today ($0.xx/day). Current balance: $X.
> If there's a problem processing, a 10-minute call solves most things.

**Day +14:**
> INV-1042 is 14 days past due, balance $X including late charges.
> Unless payment or a written payment plan arrives by [DATE], this
> moves to final notice. I'd rather solve it here.

**Day +30:** formal demand letter (the script's output) — certified
mail + email. 10 business days.

**Day +45 decision:** file small claims / engage collections / write
off + terms hardening. Do one, log it.

**Phone script (the 3-sentence collector call):**
1. "Calling about INV-1042, $4,200, 21 days past due."
2. "What date will payment go out?" (silence — let them answer)
3. "I'll send a confirming email today; if the date slips, the next
   step is [X]." — then send that email. The email after the call is
   the evidence; the call just gets the date.

## Contract Clauses That Make Collection Mechanical

1. **Late fee:** "Invoices unpaid 7 days after the due date accrue a
   late charge of 1.5%/month (18%/yr) or the maximum lawful rate,
   whichever is less."
2. **Suspension:** "Provider may suspend performance while any invoice
   is more than 14 days past due, without penalty, upon written notice."
3. **Deposit + milestone structure:** never 100% at end. 30-50%
   deposit for new clients, milestones under $5k each.
4. **Acceptance window:** "Client has 5 business days to object in
   writing; absent objection, deliverables are accepted and payable."
   (Kills the 60-day-late "actually we're not happy" defense.)
5. **Venue + fees:** "Disputes resolved in [YOUR COUNTY]; prevailing
   party recovers reasonable collection costs and fees." (Makes small
   claims the natural forum — theirs, not yours.)
6. **Kill fee:** "Cancellation after work begins: work performed at
   full rate + 25% of remaining balance."
7. **IP on payment:** "Full IP rights transfer upon final payment."
   (License-terminates-on-nonpayment wording gives real leverage on
   clients still *using* the work.)

## Collections Agency Economics

- Agencies take 25-50% of recovery (contingency), or buy debt at
  5-15 cents on the dollar.
- Below ~$1,000: agency economics rarely make sense — small claims or
  write-off.
- Agencies report to business credit bureaus (the actual leverage for
  ongoing businesses).
- Requirement: clean documentation (your ladder trail).

## Write-Off Notes (US-centric, verify with a tax pro)

- Business bad debt: deductible against ordinary income in the year
  determined worthless (accrual-basis) — needs the documentation trail.
- Log the decision (date, amount, reason). "I gave up quietly" is not
  a tax event; "written off as uncollectible on [date], collections
  attempted per log" is.
- The client may still pay later — income in the year received.

## Preventing Recurrence: Terms That Track Behavior

| Client history | Next engagement terms |
|---|---|
| pays on time ×2 | standard Net-30, no deposit |
| pays <14d late | 25% deposit |
| pays 14-45d late | 50% deposit + Net-15 + late fees enforced |
| paid after ladder/demand | 100% upfront or decline politely |
| ghosted/write-off | decline; if ever re-engaged, prepay only |

The point of the log isn't punishment — it's that your terms price
your actual experience instead of your hopes.

## When It's NOT a Collections Problem

- Client filed bankruptcy → automatic stay; file proof of claim, stop
  direct collection (federal offense to continue).
- You never had a contract + they dispute quality → evidence problem
  first; talk to someone who litigates before filing.
- Government/institutional client → payment cycles are glacial but
  reliable; the fix is in the PO process, not the ladder.
