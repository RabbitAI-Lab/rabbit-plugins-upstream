# Service Foundations and People Skills

## Core Idea
Technical skills get you hired; people skills (EIQ) determine long-term career success. The most successful IT professionals combine deep competence with genuine charisma — and both can be learned.

## Core Frameworks
- **Competence/Charisma Four-Quadrant Model**: Maps professionals on two axes — technical competence and people charisma — revealing four career archetypes.
  - When to use: Self-assessment of career trajectory; identifying personal blind spots.
  - How: Honestly rate yourself on competence and charisma; commit daily to moving toward the upper-right quadrant.
- **Warmth vs. Competence First Impressions (Amy Cuddy)**: When meeting someone, they instantly assess two things: "Can I trust this person?" (warmth) and "Can I respect this person?" (competence).

## Key Concepts
- **EIQ (Emotional Intelligence Quotient)**: People skills; the best predictor of long-term career success (Daniel Goleman).
- **IQ (Intelligence Quotient)**: Raw cognitive ability; gets you the interview, but EIQ gets you hired.
- **Google Hiring Principle**: Technical skills get you interviewed; collaborative skills get you hired.

## Mental Models
- Use the Competence/Charisma model when evaluating your own career growth or advising others.
- Think of technical skills as the "entry ticket" and people skills as the "career multiplier."
- A willingness to learn people skills matters more than innate talent — the same principle applies to technical skills.

## Anti-patterns
- **The Upper-Left Trap**: High competence, low charisma. You may be technically brilliant but become unemployable because people can't tolerate working with you.
- **Overestimating Your Value**: Highly competent people with poor people skills often blame organizations for not recognizing their "genius," when the real issue is interpersonal behavior.
- **Assuming You Can't Teach People Skills**: This is false. The real gate is whether the person is willing to invest time and effort.

## Key Takeaways
1. Both technical skills and people skills are critical — they serve different but complementary roles in career success.
2. IQ gets you the interview; EIQ gets you hired and determines long-term trajectory.
3. The Competence/Charisma model is a practical self-assessment tool — use it honestly.
4. Very few people are so technically skilled that organizations will tolerate bad interpersonal behavior long-term.
5. People skills are teachable — the prerequisite is willingness, not innate talent.

## Connects To
- **Technical competence and career growth** — the hard-skill foundation mapped to the competence axis.
- **Compassion, empathy, listening, and respect** — the people-skill foundations mapped to the charisma axis.
