# Phrase Templates — The Compassionate Geek

Adaptable wording patterns for technical customer service and workplace communication. Load only when exact wording is needed.

**Use rule**: Ground every phrase in confirmed context. Use at most one acknowledgment, then move to a concrete action. Adapt the wording to the sender's voice; a template must not invent feelings, authority, ownership, promises, or timelines.

---

## Empathy Phrases (Authentic)

### When frustration is explicit
- "I can hear how frustrating this has been. I'll focus on the next step we can take now."
- "This has disrupted your work. Here is what I can confirm and what happens next."

### When the experience is outside your knowledge
- "I won't pretend to know exactly how this has felt. I can help by clarifying the next step and following through on it."

### When acknowledging time already spent
- "Thank you for documenting what happened and what you already tried. I'll use that information so we do not repeat the same steps."

---

## Phrases to Avoid — And What to Say Instead

| ❌ Avoid | ✅ Use Instead | Why |
|----------|---------------|-----|
| "I know how you feel" | "I can hear how frustrating this is" | "I know how you feel" shifts focus to you |
| "I'm sorry you're having a problem" (scripted) | Genuine acknowledgment + immediate action | Customers perceive hollow phrases |
| "Calm down" | "Let me help you with this" | "Calm down" escalates; it's dismissive |
| "That's not my department" | "Let me find the right person for you" | Ownership matters even in handoff |
| "You should have..." | "Here's what we can do now" | Blame creates barriers |
| "There's nothing I can do" | "Here's what I *can* do" | Never end on a dead end |
| "Actually, you're wrong about..." | "Let me clarify what's happening" | Don't make the customer feel stupid |
| "Our policy says..." | "Here's what we can work with" | Policy without empathy is a wall |

## De-escalation Phrases

### When the other person is angry
1. **Lower the energy**: use a slower pace and neutral language.
2. **Reflect confirmed impact**: "The outage has blocked your team for two hours."
3. **State bounded ownership**: "I will coordinate the next diagnostic step and update this thread."
4. **Set only confirmed expectations**: "The next update is at 15:00," or "I am confirming the update time now."
5. **Follow through**: deliver the update or proactively revise the commitment before it expires.

### When the other person is venting
- **Give full attention** until the concern is complete.
- **Paraphrase**: "What I'm hearing is..."
- **Confirm**: "Did I capture the main impact, or is something missing?"
- **Pivot to action**: "Here is the next step I can take."

## Respect Phrases

### Being punctual and time-aware
- "I know your time is valuable, so let me get straight to it."
- "I'll keep this brief."

### Acknowledging their expertise
- "You clearly know your system well. What have you already tried?"
- "That's a great observation — that helps narrow down the problem."

### Following through
- "I said I'd get back to you by 3pm. Here's the update."
- If you can't deliver: "I committed to having this done by now. Circumstances changed, and here's what I'm doing about it."

## Listening Cues (Empathic Listening)

### While they speak (not scripted — genuine attention signals)
- "Go on..."
- "And then what happened?"
- "That makes sense."

### After they finish
- **Paraphrase**: "Let me make sure I understand — you're saying [restate their concern]."
- **Confirm**: "Did I miss anything?"
- **Action plan**: "Here's what I'm going to do next..."
