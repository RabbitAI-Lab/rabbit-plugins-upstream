# Technical Competence and Career Growth

## Core Idea
Deep technical competence is the foundation — it must be deep enough to solve problems quickly and permanently, AND deep enough to anticipate and prevent future problems. The level required varies by role, but the ceiling you choose determines your career trajectory.

## Core Frameworks
- **Technical Competence Level Model**: Everyone has a minimum job requirement level (arbitrary "60"). Where you choose to go beyond it determines your career security.
  - Level 60: Bare minimum. At risk from automation/outsourcing.
  - Level 70: Curious learners who exceed requirements. Likely to get promotions.
  - Level 80: Beyond IT — understand business, accounting, marketing, all departments.
  - Level 90+: Insatiably curious across all of IT, voracious readers, lifelong learners.
- **Actions Reveal Desires**: "We get the life we want, whether or not we realize we want it." What you do with your time reveals your true priorities.

## Key Concepts
- **Deep Technical Competence**: Defined differently per role, but always means: solve problems quickly, solve them permanently, design systems that work seamlessly.
- **Perfection vs. Excellence (Vince Lombardi)**: Relentlessly chase perfection to catch excellence in the process.
- **Cross-Domain Curiosity**: The more you know about one system, the more you understand all systems.

## Mental Models
- Use the competence level model when planning career development — set a target level beyond minimum requirements.
- Think of competence as "deep enough to prevent problems, not just fix them."
- Your job description defines the floor; your ambition defines the ceiling.

## Anti-patterns
- **Settling at Minimum**: Performing at level 60 makes you vulnerable to automation and outsourcing.
- **Siloed Thinking**: Limiting curiosity to your narrow specialty limits your value to the organization.
- **Assuming Talent Is Fixed**: Believing you "just aren't good at" something prevents growth. Willingness matters more.

## Key Takeaways
1. Competence is role-dependent but always includes: quick resolution, permanent fixes, and proactive system design.
2. Review your job description periodically to ensure you meet or exceed requirements.
3. The difference between adequate and exceptional is curiosity — not raw intelligence.
4. Build home labs, get certifications, attend conferences, read widely — deliberate practice matters.
5. "We get the life we want" — your actions reveal your true commitment to excellence.

## Connects To
- **Service foundations and people skills** — competence is the hard-skill axis.
- **Compassion practices and time pressure** — competence without compassion can feel effective but cold.
- **Professional respect and dignity** — competence can earn respect, while respectful behavior remains a separate choice.
