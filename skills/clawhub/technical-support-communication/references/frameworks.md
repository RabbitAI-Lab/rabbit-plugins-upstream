## Competence/Charisma Four-Quadrant Model (Ch 1)

Use two independent axes: technical competence and people skills. Name both axes whenever referring to a quadrant; spatial labels alone are ambiguous.

| | Lower People Skills | Higher People Skills |
|---|---|---|
| **Lower Technical Competence** | Limited effectiveness on both axes; choose one observable skill to develop first | Relational strength without enough technical follow-through |
| **Higher Technical Competence** | Strong delivery with collaboration friction | Strong delivery and collaboration — **target** |

Use this model for self-assessment, not labeling other people. Base the assessment on observed behavior and choose one next practice.

## Five Levels of Listening (Ch 5)

The scale runs from lowest focus on the speaker to highest:

1. **Ignoring** — attention is elsewhere.
2. **Pretend** — signals attention while doing something unrelated.
3. **Selective** — attends only to personally interesting parts.
4. **Attentive** — hears the content while evaluating and preparing a response.
5. **Empathic** — focuses on the speaker, then summarizes and confirms before responding.

Use the focus test: *Is attention on the other person's meaning, or on preparing the reply?* Level 5 is the target for emotionally charged or ambiguous interactions.

## Competence Levels (Ch 2)

Everyone has a job-defined minimum technical competence level. The ceiling you choose determines your career trajectory.

| Level | Behavior | Career Impact |
|-------|----------|---------------|
| **60** | Performs at the minimum job-description level | Meets the current floor; exposed when role requirements change |
| **70** | Learns beyond immediate requirements | Expands ownership and growth options |
| **80** | Connects IT with business and adjacent domains | Improves organizational context and cross-functional judgment |
| **90+** | Sustains broad technical curiosity and deep learning | Supports wider technical influence |

**Coaching use**: Compare current behavior with the next level and choose one observable learning habit. Treat the levels as prompts for reflection, not as guarantees of promotion or job security.

## Eight Ways to Show Respect (Ch 6)

### 1. Be Punctual
- "If you're not 15 minutes early, you're late."
- Being late isn't about traffic — it's about not considering the meeting important enough.
- The punctuality test: Would you be late if meeting your hero?

### 2. Compliment Achievements of Others
- Share in others' excitement (positive empathy), even when the topic doesn't interest you.
- Be sincere — people spot phonies easily.

### 3. Be Sincere and Authentic
- Avoid business clichés and automatic apologies.
- Don't fall into insincerity by over-apologizing.

### 4. Do What You Say You'll Do
- If you can't fulfill a commitment, proactively notify affected people ASAP.
- Deliver bad news quickly, not slowly.

### 5. Lose Sarcasm
- Sarcasm is condescending; no place in professional relationships.
- Fine for standup comedians, not for IT support.

### 6. Be Polite
- Say please and thank you.
- Treat people who have nothing to offer you with the same courtesy.
- One measure of character: how you treat people who can't help you.

### 7. Respect Other People's Time
- Don't talk on and on; watch for signs the other person wants to move on.
- Good conversation is more about listening than talking.

### 8. Don't Make Fun of Other People
- Name-calling and mocking are signs of small-mindedness.
- Making fun says "You're different from me, I'm right, you're wrong."

## "Each of Us Represents All of Us" (Ch 6)

Your individual behavior shapes how people perceive your entire profession. If one IT person is rude, the customer thinks all IT people are rude. If one is helpful, it reflects positively on the whole group.

## IQ vs. EIQ (Ch 1)

| Metric | What It Is | What It Predicts |
|--------|------------|------------------|
| **IQ** | Raw cognitive ability | Gets you the interview |
| **EIQ** | Emotional intelligence (people skills) | Gets you hired; predicts long-term career success |

**Google Principle**: Technical skills get you interviewed; collaborative skills get you hired.

## The Human Moment (Ch 4, Hallowell 1999)

A moment when two people fully connect:
- Turn off devices (iPad, phone, etc.)
- Give full attention to the other person
- Do it in person when possible

Technology makes it possible to work without human contact. But human contact is what builds trust and healthy connections.

## The Good Samaritan Experiment (Ch 3, Princeton 1973)

Students told to deliver a speech across campus encountered a slumped "victim" actor in a doorway. Key finding: **Students in the greatest hurry were the least likely to stop and help.**

Half the students were assigned to speak about the Good Samaritan parable. In this experiment, assigned time pressure was more strongly associated with stopping behavior than the speech topic. Use it as a reminder that hurry can narrow attention and reduce compassionate action.

## Five Compassion Practices (Ch 3)

1. **Express Gratitude** — Focus on gifts you've received (big or small)
2. **Look for Commonality** — "Like me, this person seeks happiness, avoids suffering, is still learning"
3. **Look for Ways to Help** — Small acts: feeding a parking meter, holding a door, picking up trash
4. **Practice Kindness** — Especially toward people you don't like or who mistreat you
5. **Slow Down** — The single most actionable way to become more compassionate

## Don't Believe Everything You Think (Ch 4, Tom Kida)

Your beliefs and judgments are not always accurate. Challenge your own prejudices by seeking understanding from people whose beliefs differ from yours. "Seek first to understand, then to be understood" (Covey).

## Viktor Frankl on Dignity (Ch 6)

From *Man's Search for Meaning*: The true measure of an individual is their ability to maintain a sense of dignity in all circumstances — even when treated disrespectfully by others.

## Vince Lombardi on Excellence (Ch 2)

"We are going to relentlessly chase perfection, knowing full well we will not catch it, because nothing is perfect. But we are going to relentlessly chase it, because in the process we will catch excellence."
