# Opening Patterns

Use these patterns to turn a scene idea into "opening impact" rather than a plot summary.

## Core Formula

1. Start after the quiet part has already broken.
2. Make the reader accept one fact, then contradict it immediately.
3. Place the protagonist inside the contradiction instead of observing it from a distance.
4. End before the explanation, leaving a concrete question.

## Pattern Library

### Death Warning / Impossible Event

- Beat 1: A normal late-night action, call, message, door knock, medical report, or dream.
- Beat 2: A precise impossible statement, often about death, disappearance, betrayal, or identity.
- Beat 3: The source of the warning should be impossible, present elsewhere, dead, asleep, or already missing.
- Best for: suspense, supernatural romance, thriller, rebirth, infinite-loop stories.

### Betrayal Plus Inner Reversal

- Beat 1: The love interest or authority figure says something cruel.
- Beat 2: The protagonist hears, sees, or discovers a hidden contradiction.
- Beat 3: The cruelty is revealed as forced, fake, system-driven, protective, or emotionally opposite.
- Best for: romance, system stories, wife-chasing, contract marriage, transmigration.

### Folk Suspense / Taboo Object

- Beat 1: A practical rural or family request appears ordinary.
- Beat 2: A taboo object or ritual enters the scene, such as a coffin, memorial tablet, wedding dress, old photo, locked room, or ancestral rule.
- Beat 3: Someone asks to undo the request, hide the object, or break the rule for a reason they refuse to explain.
- Best for: folk horror, family secrets, mystery, revenge, period village stories.

### Old Love Reunion / Hidden Sacrifice

- Beat 1: The protagonist appears to have made a heartless choice in the past.
- Beat 2: The ex-lover now has status, money, a new partner, or public approval.
- Beat 3: A scar, illness, debt, promise, hidden child, or missing memory shows the breakup may have been protective.
- Best for: angst romance, reunion, billionaire romance, regret, second-chance stories.

### Identity Misread / Power Reversal

- Beat 1: The protagonist is treated as replaceable or low-status.
- Beat 2: The opposing side acts confidently because they think they know the truth.
- Beat 3: A document, witness, inherited object, private mark, old promise, or real identity flips the balance.
- Best for: revenge, family conflict, workplace drama, palace intrigue, fake heiress stories.

## Quality Checklist

- The opening begins with an event, not an explanation.
- The protagonist has something to lose within the first paragraph.
- The contradiction is visible, not hidden in future setup.
- The hook can be understood without a full synopsis.
- Each of the five options changes the narrative engine, not just the wording.
