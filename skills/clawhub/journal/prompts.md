# Prompts — What To Offer When The Page Is Blank

Scope: choosing and delivering a prompt. A prompt is a tool for a specific stuck-ness, not a daily decoration.

**Contents:** [Delivery Rules](#delivery-rules) · [Choosing By Situation](#choosing-by-situation) · [Cold Openers](#cold-openers) · [The Library](#the-library) · [Prompt Design](#prompt-design) · [Prompts That Backfire](#prompts-that-backfire) · [Personalizing](#personalizing)

**Before offering a prompt**, read `## Prompts That Land` in `~/Clawic/data/journal/memory.md` (or `prompt-log.md` if `## Boxes` points there) and `no_go_file` if it is set. Repeating a prompt that flopped is worse than offering none, and a prompt that walks into a no-go topic ends the session.

## Delivery Rules

1. **One prompt, never a menu.** A list of six is another decision at the exact moment the user has no decisions left. Pick one; offer a second only if they reject the first.
2. **Prompt from what they just said**, not from a rotation. If they said "work was weird today", the prompt is "what was weird about it?" — a generic gratitude prompt at that moment reads as not listening.
3. **Do not prompt an entry that is already flowing.** Prompts are for a blank page. Once words are moving, Rule 1 applies and you say nothing.
4. **Deliver it bare.** The prompt and nothing else. No preamble about why it is a good prompt, no "take your time".
5. **A rejected prompt is data.** Log it to `## Prompts That Land` in `~/Clawic/data/journal/memory.md` (or `prompt-log.md` once it has split), marked flopped, and do not offer it again this month.
6. **Never prompt toward a topic the user did not open**, and never toward anything in `no_go_file` or `## Read Scope`. Curiosity about a pattern you spotted is not a reason to steer someone's entry.

## Choosing By Situation

| What they said or did | Prompt to use | Why this one |
|---|---|---|
| "I don't know what to write" | "What's taking up the most room in your head right now?" | Asks for the loudest thing, not the most important — much easier to answer |
| Blank, no context, first ever entry | Cold Openers below | Requires nothing from the user's day |
| Named a feeling with no story | "What happened right before you felt that?" | Moves from label to episode, which is what makes an entry re-readable |
| Named an event with no feeling | "What did you make it mean?" | The interpretation is the part that recurs, not the event |
| Circling the same topic for days | "Write it as if it happened to someone you like" | Self-distancing; breaks the loop that repetition reinforces (`difficult-entries.md`) |
| Facing a decision | "What would have to be true for the other option to be right?" | Forces the strongest counter-case, which the pros-and-cons list never produces |
| Angry | "What line got crossed, and what would you ask for?" | Converts rehearsal into a boundary and a request |
| Anxious about something future | "What is the actual worst case, and what happens the day after it?" | The day-after question is where catastrophe usually stops |
| Flat, nothing happened | "What is one thing that was slightly better than yesterday?" | A low bar produces an entry; a high bar produces a skipped day |
| Grateful mood, wants structure | "Who made something easier for you this week, and did they know?" | Specific and person-shaped; generic gratitude lists go stale in about a week |
| End of week | Weekly question set in `review.md` | A review is a different artifact from an entry |
| End of year, or a birthday | "What closed this year, and what is carrying over?" | The two-part shape stops it becoming a highlight reel |
| After a conflict | "Write their side as they would tell it, with no corrections" | The only prompt that reliably changes a position |
| Post-mortem on something that went well | "What did you do that you would not have done a year ago?" | Extracts capability, which is what a work journal needs (`work-journal.md`) |
| Stuck creatively | "What are you avoiding thinking about?" | Highest-yield general prompt; also the highest-risk (see Backfire) |
| Cannot sleep, writing at 2am | "What is unfinished?" | Names the open loop; then it goes to `## Open Threads` |
| Anything else | "What's on your mind?" then silence | The default that is never wrong, and worth keeping in reserve rather than using first |

## Cold Openers

For a first entry, or a return after months, with nothing to work from. Four, in order of decreasing safety:

1. "What did today actually consist of?" — pure description, zero exposure, always answerable.
2. "What is one thing you would change about today?" — one judgment, small scope.
3. "What is taking up the most room in your head?" — opens the real material without naming it.
4. "What have you not said out loud to anyone this week?" — highest yield, only for someone already writing.

Do not open at 4. A first entry that goes straight to the deepest question produces one impressive entry and no second one.

## The Library

Grouped by purpose. Offer one, from the group the situation table selected.

**Description** — for people who cannot start: What did today consist of? · What did you spend the most time on, and was that the plan? · Where were you, physically, when the day changed direction? · What did you eat, who did you speak to, what did you decide?

**Emotion** — for a named feeling with no episode: What happened right before? · Where do you feel it? · When did you last feel this exact thing? · If it had a demand, what would it be asking for?

**Interpretation** — for an episode with no meaning attached: What did you make it mean? · What else could it have meant? · Whose voice is that conclusion in? · What would you tell a friend who reported this?

**Decision** — before, not after: What are you actually choosing between? · What would have to be true for the other option to be right? · What is the cost of deciding late? · What will you regret in ten years, and what in ten days? · Which option keeps the most options open? · If you had to decide in 60 seconds, which one, and why does that answer bother you?

**Relationships** — What did you want from them that you did not ask for? · What would they say happened? · What are you protecting by not saying it? · Who have you not thought about in a long time, and why now?

**Work** — What did you do this week that nobody saw? · What did you learn that you would put in a runbook? · What did you say yes to that you should have declined? · Who is doing something you want to be able to do? (`work-journal.md` for where the answers land)

**Values and direction** — What do you keep spending money or hours on that you never planned to? · What would a good year look like, described as a Tuesday? · What are you tolerating? · What would you do with a free Saturday, honestly, not aspirationally?

**Retrospective** — What did you believe a year ago that you no longer believe? · What is the same as last year, and is that fine? · Which version of yourself would be surprised by today?

**Closing a loop** — What is unfinished? · What are you waiting on someone else for? · What is the smallest next physical action?

## Prompt Design

When no library prompt fits, build one:

- **Ask for an episode, not an assessment.** "When did X happen?" beats "how do you feel about X?" — assessments get one-line answers.
- **Ask for the thing adjacent to the thing.** Approaching a hard topic sideways ("what happened right before") gets further than the frontal question.
- **Make it answerable in one sentence.** A prompt that needs a paragraph before it can be started is a prompt that gets skipped.
- **No compound prompts.** Two questions in one sentence means the second one gets answered and the first one gets lost.
- **A good prompt produces surprise.** If you can predict the answer, so can they, and there is no reason to write it.

## Prompts That Backfire

| Prompt | Failure mode | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| "What are you grateful for?" daily | Goes hollow within weeks; generates the same three items | Rotate specificity: who, this week, and did they know (`gratitude` for the full practice) |
| "What are you avoiding thinking about?" | Genuinely high-yield, and can drop someone into acute material with no exit | Only when they are already writing, never as a cold opener, never near a Red Flags signal |
| "How do you feel?" | Produces a label and stops | "What happened right before?" |
| "What are your goals for this year?" in a journal | Turns processing into planning; the entry becomes a task list | Keep goal work in `goals`; here, ask what a good year would feel like on an ordinary Tuesday |
| "Why do you always..." in any form | Accusatory framing; produces defence, not material | "When did that start?" |
| Anything referencing a pattern the user has not been shown | Reads as surveillance, even when the pattern is real | Surface the pattern explicitly with counts first, ask if they want to write into it (`patterns.md`) |

## Personalizing

- After ~10 entries, the situation table matters less than the record: which prompts produced long entries for this person and which produced two lines.
- A prompt "landed" if it produced an entry the user did not stop early. A prompt "flopped" if they changed the subject, wrote under two lines, or said no.
- Prompt families are personal and stable: some people never answer emotion prompts and always answer description prompts. Once three prompts in a family have flopped, stop offering that family and say nothing about it.

**Write in the same turn:** every prompt offered, with landed/flopped and the date, to `## Prompts That Land` in `memory.md` (or `prompt-log.md` once it has split); a topic the user asks never to be prompted about, to `no_go_file`; a prompt the user invents and likes, to the same log marked `own`. Formats: `memory-template.md`.
