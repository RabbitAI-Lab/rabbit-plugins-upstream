# Difficult Entries — Hard Material, And Where The Practice Stops

Scope: grief, anger, shame, trauma, loops. Also the boundary where writing stops being the right tool. The Red Flags table in SKILL.md outranks everything here.

**Contents:** [Register](#register) · [Rumination Versus Processing](#rumination-versus-processing) · [Expressive Writing](#expressive-writing) · [Self-Distancing](#self-distancing) · [By Emotion](#by-emotion) · [The Unsent Letter](#the-unsent-letter) · [CBT Thought Record](#cbt-thought-record) · [When Writing Is The Wrong Tool](#when-writing-is-the-wrong-tool) · [Escalation](#escalation)

**Before responding to hard material**, read `## Read Scope` and `no_go_file` — a topic the user closed is closed. Do not open past entries looking for a pattern to offer; if the material warrants historical context, ask first (Rule 4).

## Register

- **Mirror, do not fix.** Name what you heard in their own words. "That sounds really hard" outperforms every reframe available to you, because the reframe implies the entry needed correcting.
- **No unsolicited advice.** Ever, and especially not in the first reply. Advice after a hard entry teaches that writing here has a cost, and the next entry is shorter (Rule 3).
- **No silver lining.** "At least..." ends the entry. So does "everything happens for a reason".
- **Ask before interpreting.** "Want my read on it, or just to leave it there?" Both answers are complete.
- **Do not diagnose.** Naming an observable pattern with dates is inside your remit; naming a condition is not (Escalation below).
- **Match their words.** If they wrote "sad", do not upgrade it to "grief" or downgrade it to "down". The vocabulary is data.

## Rumination Versus Processing

The signals table and the three-session test are in `SKILL.md`, already loaded. This is what to do once the test comes back rumination: change something, and never prescribe more writing of the same shape.

The switch, in order of how much they change:

1. **Third person or your own name** (Self-Distancing below) — smallest change, most reliable effect.
2. **Write the other person's account**, in their voice, with no corrections. Uncomfortable and unusually effective on interpersonal loops.
3. **Write it as a letter you will not send** (The Unsent Letter).
4. **Write only what you would do differently next time**, banning any description of the event.
5. **Stop, and route to a clinician.** A loop that survives all four is not a writing problem.

## Expressive Writing

Pennebaker's protocol, the most-studied form of therapeutic writing. Run it exactly, or do not call it that:

- **Four consecutive days**, 15-20 minutes each.
- **The same difficult experience** every day, going deeper each time — not four different topics.
- **Continuous writing.** No stopping, no editing, no attention to spelling. Nobody else reads it.
- Write about the event *and* the feelings, and about how it connects to other parts of the person's life. The connection is where the effect comes from; pure venting is the version that does not work.

What to tell the user honestly:

- **Feeling worse immediately after each session is expected**, typically for a few hours. Benefits, where they appear, show up over weeks, not that evening.
- **Do not run it in the days immediately after an acute event.** Too soon is one of the conditions under which it does not help.
- **Stop if the worse-afterwards effect lasts more than a day or two, or deepens across the four days.** That is the exit condition, and it goes to Escalation.
- It is not a substitute for treatment and does not clear trauma; it is a well-evidenced short intervention for processing a difficult experience.

Filing: normal day entries for the four days, marked so they are excluded from later analysis unless the user asks (`patterns.md`).

## Self-Distancing

Writing about yourself in the third person, or by name, instead of "I". Studied extensively by Ethan Kross and colleagues on self-talk and emotional regulation.

- The change is mechanical: "Why am I so angry?" becomes "Why is Ana so angry?" or "Why are you so angry?".
- Effect is largest on the loops that are hardest to exit: shame, replayed conflicts, anticipatory anxiety.
- It reads as absurd for the first paragraph and stops feeling odd by the third. Say that in advance, or the user abandons it at the first sentence.
- Adjacent variants that work on the same principle: write it as advice to a friend in this exact situation; write it as it would be reported a year from now.

## By Emotion

| Material | What it needs | What makes it worse |
|---|---|---|
| Anger | Name the line that got crossed, and the request you would make. Convert rehearsal into a boundary | Pure venting, repeated — it rehearses the grievance rather than discharging it |
| Grief | No structure, no cadence, no prompts unless asked. Anniversaries scheduled in `## Due` only if requested | Stage models, timelines, and any suggestion that it should be further along |
| Shame | Self-distancing first; then the difference between "I did a bad thing" and "I am bad", written out explicitly | Reassurance, which usually confirms the fear that it needed denying |
| Anxiety about the future | Worst case, then the day after the worst case; then what is inside their control today | Reassurance about probability, and any prompt that adds new futures to consider |
| Regret | What information you actually had at the time, before the outcome was known — the decision-journal frame applied backwards (`practices.md`) | Counterfactuals with no informational constraint |
| Conflict with someone specific | Their account in their voice; then what you wanted and did not ask for | Drafting the message you intend to send — that is a different task, and it belongs in `writing` |
| Numbness, "I feel nothing" | Description of the day, physical and concrete. Feeling arrives through detail or not at all | Prompts that ask for a feeling; they confirm the blankness |
| Envy | What specifically they want that the other person has, stated as a want | Moralizing about envy |
| Burnout | What was tolerated, for how long, and what would have to change. Frequency and length of entries are themselves a signal here | Optimization advice and productivity framing |
| Anything else hard | Mirror, then ask what they want from the writing: to get it out, to understand it, or to decide something. Each has a different move above | Assuming which one it is |

## The Unsent Letter

- Written to a specific person, in full, with no intention of sending. The named recipient is what unlocks the material; a generic entry about the same conflict does not reach it.
- Say plainly, before they start, that it will not be sent. That promise is the mechanism.
- Works for the dead, the estranged, an ex, a past employer, and a former version of themselves.
- **It goes to `artifacts/letters/<date>-<who>.md`, not into a day file** — it is a text read whole, and it should never surface accidentally in a review or a search for something else (`memory-template.md`).
- If the user later decides to send a version of it, that is a new document, drafted from scratch. Sending the original is a reliable way to regret it.

## CBT Thought Record

Greenberger and Padesky's seven-column thought record — the *Mind Over Mood* expansion of Beck's original five columns, which had no balanced-thought column and no re-rating. For a specific automatic thought rather than a general mood. Useful when the entry contains a clear "and that means X about me".

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| Situation | Who, where, when — observable facts only |
| Automatic thought | The sentence that ran, verbatim |
| Emotion, 0-100 | Named, rated |
| Evidence for | Facts that support the thought |
| Evidence against | Facts that do not — the column people skip, and the one that does the work |
| Balanced thought | A statement that accounts for both columns, not a positive one |
| Re-rate the emotion, 0-100 | Same emotion, new number |

- The two ratings are the output. An unchanged rating is a valid, informative result — usually meaning the thought is closer to true than distorted, and the problem is a real one to solve rather than a distortion to correct.
- This is a structured self-help exercise, not therapy, and it is not for acute risk material (Escalation).
- Filed as a normal entry with the columns intact so a later review can see the ratings move.

## When Writing Is The Wrong Tool

Say so plainly, once:

- The loop survived the four switches in Rumination Versus Processing.
- Writing reliably leaves them worse for more than a day, across several sessions.
- The material is an active situation someone else controls — abuse, threats, coercion. Writing organizes it; it does not change it.
- The user is writing instead of sleeping, eating, or speaking to anyone. Frequency of entries rising while length collapses is the observable version.
- What they need is a decision (`practices.md`), a conversation, or medical care, and writing is deferring it.

## Escalation

Run the Red Flags table in SKILL.md against what you just read. When anything in it fires:

1. **Stop the protocol.** No prompt, no analysis, no streak, no review, no interpretation.
2. **Say what you read, plainly, in one or two sentences.** Not a diagnosis: the observable thing, and that you noticed it.
3. **Ask who they can talk to now** — a person, a clinician, or a crisis line. Their local emergency number and crisis line, if you can identify the country from `~/Clawic/profile.yaml`; otherwise ask.
4. **Keep capturing if they want to keep writing.** Do not refuse the entry. Removing the outlet mid-crisis helps nobody.
5. **Do not write your risk assessment into the entry file** (Rule 9 and `privacy.md`). One dated line in `## Escalations` of `memory.md`: date, what was observed, what was said, nothing quoted.
6. **Do not surface it later unprompted.** If the user returns to it, the record exists.

You are not a clinician and the record you keep is not a clinical record. The two things this skill contributes at this moment are an accurate observation with dates, and a route to a human.

**Write in the same turn:** the entry itself, verbatim; a rumination loop and which switch was tried, to `## Open Threads` in `memory.md`; unsent letters and long-form pieces to `artifacts/`; any escalation to `## Escalations` (date, observation, action — never content); an anniversary the user asked to have scheduled, to `## Due`; a topic they asked never to raise again, to `no_go_file`. Formats: `memory-template.md`.
