# Baggage

Scope: what you may carry, what it costs, and what to do when it does not arrive. Baggage is where the cheap fare stops being cheap and where the largest recoverable losses hide.

**Contents:** [Whose Allowance Applies](#whose-allowance-applies) · [Cabin Baggage](#cabin-baggage) · [Checked Baggage](#checked-baggage) · [Fees, In Order Of Cost](#fees-in-order-of-cost) · [What Cannot Fly](#what-cannot-fly) · [Special Items](#special-items) · [Delayed, Damaged, Lost](#delayed-damaged-lost) · [The Liability Cap](#the-liability-cap) · [Trackers](#trackers)

## Whose Allowance Applies

The most common wrong answer in the domain.

- **One ticket, interline itinerary:** the *most significant carrier* for the journey sets the allowance for the whole trip, under the industry's baggage resolution — not the carrier that sold the ticket, and not each segment's own rules.
- **Codeshare:** the operating carrier handles the bag, but the allowance still resolves through the most-significant-carrier rule (`booking.md`).
- **Two tickets:** each ticket has its own allowance, each is paid separately, and the bag is normally not through-checked (`connections.md`).
- **Elite status and card benefits** add allowance based on the passenger's status with the programme, and generally travel with them across the alliance — check whether it applies to companions on the same reservation, which varies.
- When in doubt, the operating carrier's own baggage page for the specific route is the only source worth quoting. Aggregators infer allowances and get them wrong on exactly the fares where it matters.

## Cabin Baggage

- The split most people miss: a **personal item** that fits under the seat is what "free" usually means on low-cost fares; the **overhead-bin bag** is a paid extra.
- Sizers are enforced unevenly and weighed by some carriers. A carrier that weighs cabin bags does it at the gate, where the only remedy is the gate price.
- On full flights, boarding group decides whether bin space exists. Priority boarding on a carrier that boards by group is part of the product, not an upsell.
- Gate-checking is free but returns the bag at the carousel on many carriers — anything fragile, medical, or needed on arrival should not go in it.
- Liquids: assume the 100 ml containers in a one-litre bag rule. Some airports with modern scanners have relaxed it, and some have re-imposed it after relaxing; it is now airport-specific and changes without notice, so the safe assumption is the strict rule.
- Medication, documents, keys, chargers, one change of clothes and anything irreplaceable travel in the cabin. This single habit is what converts a lost-bag disaster into an inconvenience.

## Checked Baggage

- Weight limits are per bag, and the excess-weight charge is punitive compared to buying a second bag. Rebalancing between two bags at the desk is free.
- Piece systems (common on transatlantic and to the Americas) and weight systems (common in Asia and intra-Europe) do not mix well on interline itineraries — another consequence of the most-significant-carrier rule.
- Fragile items are accepted with a limited-release tag that removes most liability. That is a reason to carry them, not to sign the tag.
- Lock with a travel-security-approved lock where inspections are common, or do not lock at all: a forced lock is not compensated.
- Photograph the bag and its contents before departure, and keep the receipt for anything expensive inside. This is the entire evidence base of any later claim.

## Fees, In Order Of Cost

Buying the same bag gets more expensive at each step, often by multiples:

1. Bundled in the fare family at purchase — usually cheapest, and worth re-pricing against the base fare plus extras (`fares.md`).
2. Added online after purchase, before check-in.
3. Added at online check-in.
4. Added at the airport desk.
5. Added at the gate — the punitive tier, and where the €9.99 fare finishes above €80.

Elite status, co-branded airline cards and some corporate agreements waive checked-bag fees; check before paying, and record which card carries the benefit in `~/Clawic/data/finances/subscriptions.md`.

## What Cannot Fly

The categories that actually catch people, rather than the full dangerous-goods list:

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Spare lithium batteries and power banks | Cabin only, never checked. Roughly: under 100 Wh no approval, 100-160 Wh needs airline approval and is limited in number, over 160 Wh refused |
| Devices with batteries installed | Checked is usually allowed but discouraged; must be switched fully off |
| E-cigarettes and vapes | Cabin only, no charging or use on board |
| Smart bags with non-removable batteries | Refused by most carriers |
| Aerosols, alcohol over a strength threshold, camping stoves and gas | Restricted or refused; the strength threshold is where duty-free spirits sit safely and overproof does not |
| Sharp objects, tools over a length limit | Checked only |
| Duty-free liquids bought airside | Sealed tamper-evident bag with the receipt, and they can still be seized at a subsequent security re-clear on a connection |

Anything medical — liquids, needles, oxygen, cooling packs — is allowed with documentation and usually with advance notice (`passengers.md`).

## Special Items

- **Sports equipment** (bicycles, skis, golf, surfboards, diving gear): booked in advance as a specific item, priced per item and often size-limited. Space is capped per flight, so late booking means refusal rather than a fee.
- **Musical instruments**: small ones as cabin baggage by policy, larger ones need a purchased seat, and the seat has its own rules about weight and securing.
- **Pets**: `passengers.md`.
- **Mobility equipment**: carried free by law in most jurisdictions and does not count against allowance; battery type must be declared for powered chairs (`passengers.md`).
- **Firearms, ashes, hunting trophies, drones**: each has a distinct declaration process and destination-side rules; verify at both ends before booking.

## Delayed, Damaged, Lost

The clocks start immediately and the first one is the same day.

1. **Before leaving the airport**, file a report and get the reference (often called a PIR). This reference is the entire claim; obtaining one afterwards is a negotiation rather than a process.
2. Photograph the bag tag, the report and the damage. Keep the boarding pass.
3. **Delayed bag:** buy reasonable essentials and keep every receipt. The carrier reimburses reasonable expenses; "reasonable" is interpreted by the carrier and negotiated with receipts. Ask what the daily limit is in writing.
4. **Damage:** written claim within **7 days** of receiving the bag.
5. **Delay:** written claim within **21 days** of the bag being placed at your disposal.
6. **Lost:** a bag is normally declared lost after 21 days, at which point the delay claim becomes a loss claim for the contents.
7. Travel insurance and card benefits often cover the difference above the airline's payout — claim from the airline first, since the insurer will ask for its decision (`refunds.md`).
8. Court action under the international convention is time-barred at **2 years** from arrival.

Record the event in `## Claims` in `memory.md` the same day, with the report reference and both deadlines, and put the 7-day and 21-day dates in `## Due`.

## The Liability Cap

International itineraries are covered by the Montreal Convention, which caps baggage liability at a figure denominated in **SDR** — a floating IMF currency basket revised every five years, most recently at the end of 2024, and currently in the region of 1,500 SDR, roughly two thousand US dollars at typical rates. Verify the current figure and the day's rate before quoting a number.

Consequences worth stating: the cap is per passenger, not per bag; declared-value excess coverage can be bought at check-in for high-value items; and purely domestic itineraries fall under national rules that are often far less generous.

## Trackers

A Bluetooth tracker in the checked bag is now standard practice and several carriers accept its location data in claims. It does not change your rights, but it converts "we are looking for it" into "it is at the airport you left". Say where it is; do not go airside to retrieve it yourself.

**What gets written here**: a bag incident opens a row in `## Claims` in `~/Clawic/data/flight/memory.md` the same day, with the report reference, the amount claimed with its currency, and the 7-day and 21-day dates in `## Due`. A recurring baggage fact worth not re-deriving — which card waives the fee, which carrier weighs cabin bags on this route — belongs in `~/Clawic/data/finances/subscriptions.md` or `## How They Fly` respectively, and a bag allowance that surprised the user goes in the booking's `What` field in `~/Clawic/data/bookings/<year>.md`.
