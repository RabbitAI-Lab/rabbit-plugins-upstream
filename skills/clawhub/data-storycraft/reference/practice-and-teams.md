# Practice and teams

## Iterating

- Sketch on paper before opening a tool. Paper imposes no tool constraints and creates less attachment.
- Iterate like an optometrist. Duplicate the chart, change one thing, keep the better version, and repeat.
- Budget real time for the communication step. It is the only part of the analysis the audience sees, and tool defaults do not know the story.

## Recurring reports

Do not overhaul everything at once. Turn the existing report into an appendix and add a few story pages up front that pull out what matters and what action follows.

## Style

Learn one tool deeply. Keep a library of good examples, and articulate why the bad ones fail. A personal or brand style is fine as long as it makes the information easier to read.

## Team capability

| Approach | Practice |
|---|---|
| Upskill everyone | A reading group on the method, workshops on the team's own examples, weekly makeovers of weak charts, a norm of sharing work in progress, a contest that builds a gallery of model examples |
| Internal experts | Invest in one or two interested people as in-house consultants |
| Outsource | High-stakes one-offs, learning from the consultant during the work |
| Combined | The most successful teams train everyone, support internal experts and bring in outside help as needed |

Frame feedback using the six steps: context, visual, clutter, focus, design, story.
