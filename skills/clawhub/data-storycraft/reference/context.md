# Context

## Who, what, how

| Question | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Who is the audience? | Name one. A decision maker beats "internal and external stakeholders", and different audiences may need different communications. If credibility is not yet established, show more of the process and evidence. |
| What should they know or do? | Make a recommendation. Whoever did the analysis knows the data best, and even a wrong recommendation starts the right conversation. When a direct recommendation is inappropriate, propose next steps for the audience to react to. Set the tone: celebrating, alarming, serious or light. |
| How will it be delivered? | Live, the presenter carries the detail, so slides stay sparse. Sent, the page must answer likely questions on its own. One deck serving both tends to serve neither; case-studies.md has the fix. |

Verbs that make the ask concrete: approve, decide, fund, change, adopt, prioritize, stop, start, investigate.

## Questions for the requester

When someone else commissioned the work, these surface context they assume is known:

1. What background is essential?
2. Who is the audience or decision maker, and what do we know about them?
3. What biases might make them receptive or resistant?
4. What data strengthens the case, and is it familiar or new to them?
5. What risks weaken the case and should be addressed up front?
6. What does a successful outcome look like?
7. With one sentence or thirty seconds, what would you say?

Questions 6 and 7 are usually the most revealing.

## 3-minute story and Big Idea

The 3-minute story is what you would say with only three minutes. It survives an agenda cut and frees the presenter from the slides. The Big Idea (a concept from Nancy Duarte's *Resonate*) compresses it into one complete sentence with a point of view and the stakes. A topic ("Q3 results") or a statement without stakes ("sales changed") is not a Big Idea yet.

```
Audience:  Budget committee that funds school programs
Ask:       Approve budget to continue the summer science program
Mechanism: Live presentation, deck circulated afterwards
Big Idea:  The pilot summer science program improved how students feel
           about science, so we recommend continuing it; please approve
           the budget.
```

## Storyboarding

Plan the sequence before building anything, with sticky notes or, for an agent, a plain list of slide headlines. A list is cheap to reorder and creates no attachment to built slides. For expensive deliverables, agree the storyboard with the requester first.
