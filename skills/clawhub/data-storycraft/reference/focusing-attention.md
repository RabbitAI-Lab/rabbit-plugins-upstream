# Focusing attention

## Preattentive attributes

The eye registers these before conscious thought: orientation, shape, line length, line width, size, curvature, added marks, enclosure, hue, intensity, spatial position and motion (list adapted from Stephen Few). Use them to direct attention and to build a hierarchy that delivers the gist within the first few seconds. Layer several (large, colored, bold) on the single most important element.

Encode quantities only with attributes people read as magnitude: length and position, and less precisely width, size and intensity. Hue and shape are categorical.

Short-term memory holds about four chunks, which is why direct labels beat a legend once there are more than a few series.

## Emphasis nuances

- Labels on every point clutter. Labels on the two or three points that matter say "look here".
- In a live setting, reuse one chart several times and highlight a different part each time, from macro to micro. The audience learns the chart once.
- Highlighting hides whatever is not highlighted. That is right when explaining and wrong when exploring.
- Relative size signals relative importance. Give items of equal importance equal size, and check that a layout's proportions were not set by whichever data arrived first.

## Color

| Principle | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Base | Grey gives more contrast than black as a base. Rainbow encodings destroy preattentive value; for ordered values, use saturation of one hue. |
| Consistency | Do not change colors or chart types for novelty. Change color only to signal a real change of topic or tone. |
| Colorblind readers | About 8% of men and 0.5% of women, mostly red-green. Never rely on red vs green alone; reinforce with bold, saturation or +/- signs. |
| Tone | Muted palettes, or a black accent with uppercase titles, read as clinical. Bright palettes suit light topics only. Check cultural connotations for international audiences. |
| Brand | Use one or two brand colors as accents and keep the rest grey. If the brand color lacks contrast, use black or another accent that does not clash. |

## Position

Work with reading direction, not against it: no flows that start at the bottom right, and no positive values on the left of a negative-to-positive scale.

## Eyes-drawn test

Look away, look back, and note where the eye lands first. Better, ask someone to narrate their path through the visual. If the first landing point is not the message, adjust the emphasis.
