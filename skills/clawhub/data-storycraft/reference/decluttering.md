# Decluttering

Every element costs mental effort, and perceived effort decides whether people engage at all: a visual that looks hard gets skipped. Maximize the share of ink that shows data.

## Gestalt principles

| Principle | What people perceive | Application |
|---|---|---|
| Proximity | Close objects form a group | Spacing steers reading across rows or down columns; put labels next to their data |
| Similarity | Same color, shape or size forms a group | Shade alternate rows instead of drawing borders; write labels in their series color |
| Enclosure | Enclosed objects form a group, and light shading is enough | Shade a forecast period or an event window |
| Closure | The eye completes incomplete shapes | Chart borders and background fills are unnecessary |
| Continuity | The eye follows the smoothest path | Drop the y-axis line; aligned white space still anchors the bars |
| Connection | Connected objects form a strong group | Lines join points over time; line weight and darkness set the hierarchy |

## Alignment and white space

- Centered text creates no clean edge. Align elements so they form clean vertical and horizontal lines.
- Rotated text reads markedly slower (the book cites about 50% slower at 45° and about three times slower at 90°). Abbreviate labels so they stay horizontal.
- Treat white space like a pause in a speech. Keep margins empty, never add data because space is left over, and consider putting one key message alone on a page.

## Contrast

The more things differ, the less any one stands out: a hawk is easy to spot among pigeons and hard to spot among many kinds of birds. Make the important thing the only thing that is different. "Our business" in blue among grey competitors works; six competitors in six colors and shapes does not.
