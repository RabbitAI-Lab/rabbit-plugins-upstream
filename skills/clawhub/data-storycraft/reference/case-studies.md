# Case studies

## Dark backgrounds

Prefer white: dark backgrounds feel heavy and pull the eye away from the data. If a brand template forces dark, invert the logic. Black becomes the baseline, grey recedes, white stands out most, and bright colors pop, even yellow, which is unusable on white. Expect a bolder, heavier tone.

## Live and sent versions of one chart

A full chart on screen lets the audience race ahead and stop listening. Live, show the empty axes and explain them, then reveal or highlight one segment per point while narrating, and end on the complete picture. For the sent version, use one annotated chart in which text boxes beside the data carry each point.

In slide software, stack the builds on one slide with the annotated version on top, so that is what prints. Use only appear and disappear transitions; fly-ins and fades are the animation equivalent of 3D. This works only if the presenter knows the story without reading the slides.

## Telling several stories from one chart

- Sort by the metric you want the audience to focus on, with the focus segment first (leftmost, against the baseline). Put negative values on the left and positive on the right.
- Fix the order once and move the highlight between stories. Re-sorting forces the audience to relearn the chart.
- For a sent version, combine the highlights into one dense visual with color-matched callout boxes. It is fine to read and too dense to present.

## Tangled line charts

| Strategy | When |
|---|---|
| Emphasize one line at a time, cycling in a live setting | Each series has its own point to make |
| Stacked panels sharing the x-axis | The trend within each category matters most |
| Side-by-side panels sharing the y-axis | Comparing levels across categories matters most |
| Small multiples with one series highlighted per panel, the rest grey | Full context matters but focus is needed; better on paper than live |
| Show less data | Not every category or year is needed |

Panels must share identical axis ranges, or the comparisons mislead.

## Pie replacements

| Alternative | Best when |
|---|---|
| The numbers as text | One headline shift is the whole message |
| Simple bars, before vs after per category | Comparing two states, with text boxes beside the bars and only two colors |
| 100% stacked horizontal bar | Part-to-whole matters; the aligned ends let readers compare the negative and positive sides |
| Slopegraph | The rate of change per category matters; because category order follows the values, avoid it when a fixed order is required |
