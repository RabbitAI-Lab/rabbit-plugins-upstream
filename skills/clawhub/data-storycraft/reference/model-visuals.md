# Model visuals

Recipes for common charts. Adapt them; what matters is that every choice is deliberate.

## Progress vs goal (line)

- The current series is the accent: thicker, with a marker and value label on the latest point.
- The goal is a thin grey reference line with a grey label.
- The prior period is a thinner line in a lighter tint of the same hue, related but not competing.
- Label every Nth tick when the trend matters more than individual days.

## Actual vs forecast (line)

- Actual is a solid line; forecast is a thinner dotted line, which reads as less certain.
- Shade the forecast period lightly and write ACTUAL and FORECAST in caps under the x-axis.
- Bold the last actual value, which anchors the forecast.
- Put markers only on points that annotations reference, and write annotation dates in the accent color.
- Label the forecast values; keep a grey axis for the history.

## Composition over time (100% stacked bars)

- Stack categories in scale order, for example Miss, Meet, Exceed from bottom to top.
- The focus series sits on the baseline in the accent color with white value labels; the others take close shades of grey.
- Right-align the takeaway text beside the bar it describes, and footnote the absolute totals that 100% stacks hide.

## Gains and losses (positive and negative stacked bars)

- The base series starts at the axis. Reductions go below zero in a lighter tint of the base hue; additions go above in a positive hue.
- Draw a gap or shortfall as an outline only, so it reads as empty space.
- Put series labels at the right edge in their series colors, with the biggest, boldest text on the number that matters.

## Ranked priorities (horizontal stacked bars)

- First, second and third priority are the darkest to lightest shades of one hue, and rows are sorted by total.
- Accent the top items across label, total and bar.
- Keep in-bar labels small, muted and left-aligned, and drop the x-axis when values are labeled.

## Typography

- The title is dark grey, which keeps pure black available as an accent.
- Use one text size, changing it only deliberately: a larger title, a slightly larger rotated axis title, a smaller footnote.
