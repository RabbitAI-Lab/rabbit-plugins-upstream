# Writing for TikTok

Rules of thumb from what works for creators, not TikTok policy. TikTok publishes no weights for
any of this; judge by the account's own results (`log.py`).

## The hook

One promise, said three times in the first second or two: in the picture, on screen, in words.

- **First frame:** start on the most telling moment. Cut the walk-in, the "hey guys", the logo.
  WoopSocial adds the cover image as the first frame, so the cover is taken from the first second.
- **On-screen text:** about 10 words, readable in one glance, the main search phrase in the
  first words. Keep it in the upper middle (`video.py hook` does), away from the buttons on the
  right and the caption at the bottom. Show it for 2 to 3 seconds.
- **Spoken line or first caption line:** the same promise in plain words.

Patterns that hold up (they need a real payoff in the video):

- **The surprising number:** "Only 2.5% of Earth's water is fresh."
- **The correction:** "Your water bottle isn't the problem. Here's what is."
- **The result first:** "This is what 30 days of journaling did to my sleep." (Only the user's
  real result.)
- **The list promise:** "3 things I'd never put in a carry-on." (Then exactly 3.)
- **The question people search:** "How much fresh water is left?"

Never: a hook the video doesn't keep, fake urgency, "wait for it", clickbait about people.

Endings: stop on the payoff or loop back to the first frame. No "follow for more" outro card
longer than a second; it is where people swipe away.

## The middle

- One idea per video. If it needs two, it's two videos.
- Cut every pause longer than a breath. A jump cut is fine on TikTok.
- Text on screen follows the voice; don't put a paragraph on screen.
- If the video makes a claim with a number, say or show the source.

## The ending

One ask that fits: follow for part 2 (only if there is one), comment a word ("comment WATER and
I'll answer the top questions"; only if the user will), save it for later, send it to someone
specific. Not all four.

## Captions

- First words: the search phrase and the hook. TikTok shows about one line over the video.
- A question or reveal post: the first line repeats the question, never the answer. It shows
  over slide 1 before anyone swipes, so an answer in it spoils the swipe.
- One or two lines of context, then the ask. Up to 5 hashtags that name the topic
  (#freshwater, #earthscience), never #fyp, #viral or #foryou.
- Credits for anything that isn't the user's (data sources, music the user licensed).
- No long dashes, no "In today's world", no emoji walls. Read it out loud.

## Photo posts

- **Slide 1 is the hook**, in the renderer's classic or highlight style, on the strongest photo
  or a plain colour.
- **One idea per slide**, 1 to 3 short lines, swipe-worthy order: build to the best one.
- **The payoff** gets its own slide: one big number or word, little else.
- **Last slide:** one ask, or the payoff.
- 4 to 8 slides is typical; 35 is the limit; 1 works for a single striking image.
- A real photo behind each slide beats plain colour; keep text clear of faces and the subject.
- `autoAddMusic` lets TikTok add recommended music; for a chosen sound, post it as an inbox
  draft and add the sound in the app.

## Several accounts

Each account gets a real version: another hook, another cut or order, another language written
for that country (in Spain "grifo", in Mexico "llave"; decimal commas; metric units; accents
kept). The same file on several accounts is reused material to TikTok. Two accounts in the same
language must not get the same post with new words: give each its own idea or angle.

## Honesty

Every claim is something the user did, saw or can show. No invented results, views, followers,
testimonials or quotes. Health, money or legal topics: no promised outcomes, say who it's not for.
Paid work is disclosed as Paid partnership; posts promoting the user's own business as
Promotional content.
