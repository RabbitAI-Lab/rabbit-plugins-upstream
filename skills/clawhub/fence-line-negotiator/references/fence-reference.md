# Fence Line Negotiator — Reference

## State doctrine map (verify current statute — this is a practical band, not legal citation)

**Cost-sharing / partition fence family** (both owners owe equitable shares of a *boundary* fence, usually after written notice): CA (Civ. Code 841 — 30-day written notice with cost breakdown REQUIRED before shares arise), OR, WA, NV, ID, MT, plus many states whose rural "line fence" statutes (WI, MI, OH, IA, MN, IN...) assign shares, sometimes via elected township **fence viewers**.

**Agreement-first family** (no automatic residential cost-sharing; contribution only by agreement or where a fence is deemed a common necessity in rural contexts): TX, FL, GA, AL, MS, SC, UT, CO, WY...

Key nuance: in sharing states the duty attaches to the **boundary** fence, not to any fence; in agreement states a fence you built is yours alone even on the line (with boundary-fence obligations like mutual assent possibly attaching). When stakes are real, read your state's fence statute (every state has one, usually 1-3 pages) and any county/city fence ordinance.

## Height & permit norms

- Back/side yards: 6 ft is the near-universal cap without a variance (7-8 ft sometimes allowed, permit required).
- Front yards: commonly 3.5-4 ft (open-fence rules in some zones — check BEFORE buying pickets).
- Corner lots: "sight triangle" setbacks can forbid solid fences near the corner.
- Finished-face rule: many codes require the finished side to face OUT (neighbors/street) — increasingly codified precisely because of the good-side fight.
- Pool barriers: separate code track (usually 4 ft min, self-latching gate, gap limits) — never assume the yard fence qualifies.

## Survey vs assumption

County GIS/plat lines are approximations (often feet off). A licensed boundary survey ($500-1,500 residential) is the only evidence that wins rung 3. When a fence is within 1 ft of the line, survey BEFORE: building, replacing posts in place, or signing any boundary agreement.

## Boundary maintenance agreements (the one-page fix)

Contents: names, legal descriptions, fence location relative to the line, cost split formula (current replacement and future repairs), maintenance duties, signature + notary, then RECORD with the county recorder. Recorded agreements run with the land — binding on future owners of both lots — which is the entire point: it outlives both of you.

## Adverse possession quick table (why the line matters)

| State type | Typical period | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Short (CA w/ tax payment, TX 10 yr cultivar) | 5-10 yr | mowing/fencing over line can mature |
| Mid (FL, CO...) | 7-18 yr | color-of-title variations |
| Long/strict | 20+ yr | plus NE never (oral only) |

Defeats: written permission (kills "hostile" element — a dated letter "you may keep using that strip" resets hostility), recorded boundary agreement, or periodic reassertion. A 2-ft strip of a $300k lot is $3,000-10,000 of land — more than most fences.

## Damage & insurance notes

- Homeowner's policies cover fences (other structures, ~10% of dwelling coverage) for covered perils (wind, fallen tree, vandalism) minus deductible — for a $2,000 fence with a $1,500 deductible, usually not worth claiming (and the claim counts against you).
- Neighbor's healthy tree falls → generally YOUR loss (act of god) if on your fence in agreement states; shared fences in sharing states split. Dead/leaning tree documented as neglected → tree owner's negligence, they pay.
- Photograph dead trees on neighboring land NOW, date-stamped, if you foresee failure — the before-photo is the negligence case.

## Mediation & escalation costs (reality check)

- Neighbor conversation: free, resolves most cases
- Surveyor: $500-1,500, resolves line questions definitively
- Mediation: $0-300 (community services) to $500-1,500 (private), non-binding but effective
- Attorney demand letter: $250-500
- Fence litigation: $5,000-50,000+ and 6-24 months — ALWAYS more than the fence
- Small claims (for a damaged fence under the cap, e.g., $5-10k): filing $30-100, no lawyer needed, judge reads your photos and letters — which is why rungs 2-4 exist: they manufacture the exhibit list

## Neighbor psychology (short)

- Lead with the shared-asset frame ("OUR fence is failing"), not the bill.
- Bring printed quotes to the first conversation — numbers de-escalate, adjectives escalate.
- Offer structure: "I'll handle the contractor logistics if you cover your half" removes work, not just money.
- The person who moves first on paper (letter, agreement) usually sets the terms — be that person, but write like a judge is reading.
