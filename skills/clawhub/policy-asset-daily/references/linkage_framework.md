# Policy-to-Asset Transmission Framework

This document describes the standard transmission model used to analyze and visualize
the linkage between political policy events and asset market movements.

## Core Transmission Chain

The fundamental logic follows a multi-stage transmission from policy signal to market
reaction:

```
Political Policy / State Media Signal
        │
        ▼
Policy Implementation / Regulatory Action
        │
        ├──► Energy Sector (Oil, Gas, New Energy)
        │         │
        │         ▼
        │    Commodity Prices (Input costs)
        │
        ├──► Commodity / FX Market
        │         │
        │         ▼
        │    Gold, Copper, DXY, USD/CNY
        │
        ├──► Monetary / Fiscal Policy
        │         │
        │         ▼
        │    Liquidity, Interest Rates
        │
        └──► Capital Markets
                  │
                  ▼
             A-shares / HK / US Stocks
                  │
                  ▼
           Sector Rotation
```

## Five-Domain Transmission Details

### 1. Political Policy → All Assets (Source Layer)

Political policy is the **source layer** — it initiates transmission chains that flow
to all other domains. Key signals:

- **State Council meetings**: Signal sector-focused stimulus or regulation
- **Politburo meetings**: Signal macro-level policy direction shifts
- **Xinhua/People's Daily editorials**: Signal policy trial balloons or forthcoming
  changes (especially "评论员文章" or "社论")
- **CCTV News Network lead stories**: Signal priority topics for government attention

**Transmission logic**: Policy announcement → Market expectation formation →
Position adjustment → Price movement

### 2. International Macro → Commodities, FX, Stocks

International macro events transmit through:

- **Central bank policy** (Fed, ECB): → Interest rate differentials → Capital flows →
  FX rates → Stock valuations
- **Geopolitical events**: → Risk sentiment → Safe-haven assets (gold, USD) →
  Equity risk premium
- **Trade policy**: → Supply chain expectations → Sector-specific impacts

### 3. Energy → Commodities, Stocks, FX

Energy is both a policy target and an input cost:

- **Oil price spikes** → → Cost-push inflation expectations → Central bank response →
  Bond yields, equity multiples
- **Energy policy** (carbon goals, subsidies) → → New energy sector valuations →
  Renewable vs. traditional energy rotation
- **Natural gas supply** → → Industrial input costs → Manufacturing margins

### 4. Commodities / FX → Stocks, Bonds

- **Gold**: Risk sentiment barometer; rising gold = risk-off, benefiting defensive sectors
- **Copper**: "Doctor Copper" — economic growth barometer; rising copper = growth optimism
- **USD/CNY**: Directly impacts export/import sectors, A-share foreign capital flows
- **DXY**: Global liquidity proxy; strong USD pressures emerging market equities

### 5. Capital Markets (Receiver Layer)

Capital markets are the **receiver layer** — they reflect the cumulative impact of all
upstream policy and macro signals:

- **A-shares**: Most sensitive to domestic policy, state media signals, northbound flows
- **Hong Kong stocks**: Bridge between China policy and international capital
- **US stocks**: Sensitive to Fed policy, global risk sentiment

## Diagram Construction Guidelines

When building the linkage diagram for the daily report:

1. **Identify the primary signal source** for the day (which policy event is driving)
2. **Trace each branch** to the affected asset class
3. **Note the transmission mechanism** (liquidity, sentiment, cost, valuation)
4. **Mark direction** with arrows (→)
5. **Highlight active chains** for the day's specific events

## Scenario Reasoning Framework

Use the following structure for 2-3 scenario observations:

```
IF [source] signals [topic] [frequency/tone],
THEN [policy type] may be forthcoming,
WHICH WOULD benefit [sector/asset class].
```

### Common Patterns to Watch:

1. **State media trial balloon**: If Xinhua/People's Daily publishes 2+ articles on a
   topic within a week → policy may follow within 1-3 months

2. **Regulatory silence then action**: If a regulator stops commenting on an issue that
   was previously discussed → major action may be imminent

3. **Cross-domain convergence**: If political, energy, and monetary signals all point
   to the same direction → high-confidence sector trade

4. **International-domestic divergence**: If international macro (e.g., Fed hawkish)
   diverges from domestic policy (e.g., PBOC dovish) → watch for FX pressure and
   capital flow reversals
