# Faceless YouTube: channel, ideas, script and film with AI

This is a method for starting and running a faceless YouTube channel with AI. You pick a niche, study one working video's structure without copying it, write a chapter-by-chapter script with a strong hook, keep one look and one narrator, write the title and thumbnail, set the altered or synthetic content label, and check the video before you upload. Then you make the film in an AI YouTube video maker.

"YouTube automation with AI" can mean two things. One is a pipeline that turns an idea into a script, pictures, a voice and a finished video, with you deciding every step that matters. The other is mass-produced, repeated or reused content with nothing original in it. YouTube's monetization policies cover reused and inauthentic content, and the second kind can be refused or removed. This skill is the first kind. Read the policy page once before you start: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392.

You are the producer and the writer. By the end you hand over a niche, a list of video ideas, a full script outline, a title and thumbnail brief, a publish pack and a checklist. The method does not promise views or income, and neither do you.

## Step 1. Pick a niche

A niche is a subject you can make 30 videos about without running dry, with facts you can check and pictures you can make without a face. Good faceless niches share three traits:

1. **Sourceable.** Every claim can be checked: history, science, true stories, myths, business stories, geography, explainers, documentaries.
2. **Drawable.** Each scene can be shown with a picture that moves: a place, a machine, a map, a person in costume, an animation. Not a talking head.
3. **Repeatable.** The same structure works for the next video.

Test a niche by listing 10 faceless YouTube video ideas. Each idea needs a subject, a time and a place that you can state in two sentences. If you cannot list 10, the niche is too narrow. If every idea needs a different kind of picture, it is too wide.

Idea patterns that work across niches (more in `references/niches-and-ideas.md`):

- The day it happened: one event, hour by hour.
- The rise and fall of one thing: a company, a city, an empire, an invention.
- How it works: one machine, system or process, explained in stages.
- The mystery: an object or event with no agreed answer.
- Then and now: a place at its peak and the same place today.
- Comparison: two things side by side, in a fixed order.
- The last days: the final stretch before an ending.

Pick the niche, name the channel's promise in one line ("We explain how forgotten inventions worked"), and list the first 10 ideas. Check each fact exists in more than one reliable source.

## Step 2. Study one working video, do not copy it

Find one video in the niche that holds viewers and write down how it is built. Not what it says. Record:

- **Length** and the number of chapters.
- **Opener type** (see step 3) and what the first 30 seconds promise.
- **Order of reveal:** what comes first, what is held until late.
- **Pace:** how often the picture changes, how often a new fact lands.
- **Re-hooks:** the lines at the end of chapters that make you stay.
- **Title and thumbnail promise,** and whether the video keeps it.
- **What the comments ask for:** gaps you can fill.

Then write your own video from your own research. Use different sources, a different angle, your own sentences and your own pictures. Rules:

1. Never copy a script, a narration, a thumbnail or footage that someone else made.
2. Never re-upload someone else's video with small changes.
3. A video must add something of its own: a fresh angle, better organisation, new research, a clearer explanation.
4. The same structure is fine. The same words are not.

Read YouTube's policy on reused and inauthentic content before you publish: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392. If the Ziztu maker shows an originality score against the source, use it as one more check, not as permission.

## Step 3. Write the script, chapter by chapter

Use `assets/templates/video-outline.md`. A script is a list of chapters. Each chapter answers one question and ends by raising the next.

### The outline

1. **Promise.** The title in one sentence, and what the viewer will know at the end.
2. **Hook (chapter 0).** The first 30 seconds. See the openers below.
3. **Chapters in order.** Plain chronological: what happened, then what happened next. For each chapter write:
   - The question it answers.
   - The turn: the fact that changes what the viewer thought.
   - The pictures: what the viewer sees (place, person, object, map, date).
   - The re-hook: the last line, which opens the next chapter.
4. **The ending.** Keep the final event for the last chapter. Close the loop the hook opened.
5. **The outro.** One line on what to watch next. No long goodbye.

Planning figures: narration runs about 150 spoken words a minute. A 10 minute video holds roughly 1,500 words. Check by reading the script aloud with a timer.

### Pace and picture

- A new picture for each new sentence or idea. Never hold one picture while the narrator moves on.
- Show the thing the narrator names: the place, the object, the date, the person.
- Put numbers, dates and names on screen as text, never inside AI-drawn pictures.
- Say a place and a time again when the scene changes.

### The opener: the first two sentences carry the subject, the time and the place

A good opener puts the viewer inside a concrete moment. By the end of the second sentence they know what the video is about, when it happened and where. Never begin with an announcement.

Pick one opener type per video. Take the one the story already has:

| Type | How it works |
|---|---|
| Cold open | Drop into the most dramatic moment of the story. Name the place and the year within two sentences. |
| One number | Start with one number, say what it counts, then compare it to something human. |
| Then and now | The place at its peak, the same place today, and the question that links them. |
| A real question | A specific question with stakes, answered by the film. |
| Object with a mystery | Describe a thing, say what is strange about it, say who could explain it. |
| Ending first | State the outcome the title already promises, then start at the beginning. Never spoil a mystery. |

Bad starts: "This is a story about ...", "In this video ...", "Today we ...", "Have you ever wondered ...", "Imagine a world ...", "Welcome back", "Hey guys", "Let's dive in".

Self-check: read the first three sentences aloud. Can you see the first sentence? After sentence two, can a stranger say what the video is about, when and where? Are the stakes clear by sentence three? Is every fact checked? If any answer is no, rewrite from the moment, not from the topic.

Rules for every sentence: plain words, 18 words or fewer, no filler, every fact true. If a date or number is uncertain, check it or cut it. Do not invent a quote.

## Step 4. Keep the facts straight

- Write down the source for each claim next to the sentence.
- Use at least two reliable sources for any date, number or quote.
- Label guesses and disputed claims as such in the script ("historians disagree on ...").
- Do not show or invent real people in a harmful or false way.
- Re-read the finished script for anything that sounds sure but is not.

## Step 5. One look and one narrator

A channel is recognised by its look and its voice. Decide both once and keep them for every video.

Write a channel sheet (one page):

- **Look:** one visual style for all videos (for example painted, 3D, cinematic, cartoon). Same colours, same lighting, same camera feel.
- **Narrator:** one voice, one pace, one tone. Keep sentence length and warmth steady.
- **Chapter cards:** how chapters are shown, if at all.
- **Music mood:** one bed under the voice, quieter than the voice.
- **Cast:** a fixed look for any recurring person, in under 25 words, repeated word for word in every scene they are in.

Test the look on one chapter before you make the whole film. A look that works for a battle can fail for a boardroom. Change the look, not the voice, if the test fails.

## Step 6. Title and thumbnail

### Title

- Specific: a subject, a place or a time in the first words.
- Promise one thing and deliver it in the video.
- One curiosity gap, not three.
- No ALL CAPS, no lies, no "you won't believe".
- Write five titles. Read each aloud. Pick the one a stranger would understand at a glance.
- Keep the search words people type ("history of ...", "how ... works") in plain language.

### Thumbnail

- One focal subject, large, with strong contrast against the background.
- 1 to 4 words of text. They add something the title does not. They do not repeat it.
- Readable on a phone at a very small size.
- Keep clear of the corner where the timestamp sits.
- Match the look of the video so the click is honest.
- Make three variants and pick one. Do not claim any variant will win.

Brief the picture the same way you brief a scene: subject, setting, light, one accent colour, the words. Details in `references/titles-and-thumbnails.md`.

## Step 7. The publish pack

Prepare these before upload:

- **Title** and two alternates.
- **Thumbnail,** with the text.
- **Description:** the first two lines say what the video is about; then a chapter list with timestamps (start at 0:00, at least three chapters); then sources.
- **Chapters,** with names that say what happens, not "Part 1".
- **Tags:** a short list of the real topic words.
- **Pinned comment:** one question that starts a conversation, or the source list.
- **Subtitles:** an SRT file for the narration. Check it against the audio.

## Step 8. The altered or synthetic content label

YouTube asks creators to say when a video has realistic content that is altered or synthetic. Read the page and answer honestly each time you upload: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491.

A good rule: if a viewer could take a scene, a person, a voice or an event for real when it is not, label it. A clearly stylised cartoon, painting or animation is a different case from a photorealistic scene of an event that never happened. The page has the current rules. When in doubt, label. Do not use AI to put words in a real person's mouth.

## Step 9. Check list before uploading

1. The niche, the promise and the title match what the video delivers.
2. The first two sentences give the subject, the time and the place.
3. The stakes are clear within 15 seconds.
4. The video is in plain chronological order, with a re-hook at the end of each chapter.
5. Every fact and number has a source.
6. No sentence is copied from another video, article or script.
7. One look and one narrator from first frame to last.
8. A new picture for each new idea. Text is on screen, not inside pictures.
9. The thumbnail has one focal subject and 1 to 4 words that do not repeat the title.
10. The description has chapters and sources. The SRT matches the audio.
11. The altered or synthetic content label is answered honestly.
12. The video adds something of its own. You would watch it if a stranger made it.

Do not upload a video that fails 2, 5, 6 or 11.
