# Titles and thumbnails

## Titles

A title is a promise. The video must keep it.

1. **Lead with the subject.** A person, place, object or event in the first words.
2. **One idea.** One promise, not a list of three.
3. **One gap.** The title raises one question the video answers.
4. **Plain words.** No filler: "amazing", "insane", "shocking".
5. **Honest.** Do not promise a reveal the video does not deliver.
6. **Searchable.** Include the words people type for this topic.

### Patterns

- "[Subject]: What Really Happened in [Place], [Year]"
- "How [Thing] Works"
- "The Rise and Fall of [Thing]"
- "Why [Surprising Outcome] Happened"
- "The Mystery of [Object]"
- "[Place] Then and Now"

### Process

1. Write five titles.
2. Read each aloud.
3. Remove every word that does not add meaning.
4. Choose the one a stranger understands at a glance.
5. Keep two alternates in the publish pack.

## Thumbnails

A thumbnail is a picture and a few words that make one promise.

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Focal subject | One, large, with strong contrast against the background |
| Words | 1 to 4. They add to the title and do not repeat it |
| Colour | One accent colour against a calm base |
| Light | Clear separation between subject and background: rim light, glow or colour split |
| Size test | Still readable when small, on a phone |
| Safe zone | Keep text and faces clear of the corner where the timestamp sits |
| Honesty | Show something the video really contains |

### Archetypes

- A face or figure with an emotion and a stake word.
- A map with the place highlighted.
- Then and now, split in two.
- A large number with a thing attached.
- An iconic object with a device: circle, arrow, label.

### Brief for a thumbnail

Write it like a scene: **subject**, **setting**, **light**, **accent colour**, **words**, **what it must not show**. Make three variants. Pick one by squinting at it: the subject should be the biggest, brightest mass.

### Match the look

Use the video's own look so the click feels honest. A painted film gets a painted thumbnail. Do not use a photoreal thumbnail for a cartoon film.
