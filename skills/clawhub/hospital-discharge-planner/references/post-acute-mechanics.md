# Post-Acute Mechanics: Medicare Clocks, Status Traps, Landing Zone

## Observation vs inpatient — the status that decides SNF coverage

| Status | What it is | SNF coverage | Cost-sharing |
|--------|-----------|--------------|--------------|
| Inpatient (admitted) | Doctor orders admission | YES if ≥3 inpatient nights in the row before discharge | Part A: days 1–20 $0; 21–100 daily coinsurance (2026 ≈ $209.50/day); 101+ full cost |
| Observation (outpatient) | "Under observation," often indistinguishable at bedside | NO — SNF days are then 100% self-pay ($300–500/day) | Part B rules; the NOF applies |

Ask the case manager DAILY: "What is the patient's status TODAY —
inpatient or observation?" Status can change mid-stay (and the 3 nights
must be INPATIENT nights). If observation but SNF-level needs exist:

1. Ask the attending to reconsider status (criteria: severity, care
   intensity expected).
2. Request **Beneficiary and Family Centered Care (BFCC) review** —
   the Quality Improvement Organization reviews the case fast.
3. The **MOON** (Medicare Outpatient Observation Notice) must be given
   within 36 h of observation starting + 24 h before discharge — it's
   also your appeal hook.

## SNF / rehab economics (traditional Medicare)

- Days 1–20: fully covered IF skilled need + 3-inpatient-night rule met.
- Days 21–100: ~$209.50/day coinsurance (2026 figure; Advantage plans
  differ — check the plan's SNF benefit).
- Day 100: coverage stops regardless of progress.
- The **therapy cliff:** SNF must show measurable progress; when
  therapy notes plateau, coverage ends ("no longer skilled") — often
  day 21–35 in practice. Weekly: ask therapy for the progress note and
  goals; if they say "plateaued," ask for home-health transition IN the
  same conversation, before the last covered day.
- **IRF (intensive rehab hospital)** needs 3-hours-of-therapy/day
  tolerance — a different, higher bar; usually for stroke/SCI/major
  trauma, not routine joint replacement anymore.

## Home-health eligibility (the underused lane)

Medicare pays for home health (skilled nursing + PT/OT at home) if:

1. Doctor orders it (get the order in the AVS before discharge), AND
2. Patient is "homebound" (leaving takes considerable effort/help —
   doctor's judgment, not house arrest), AND
3. Skilled need exists (wound care, injection teaching, PT).

$0 cost, no coinsurance, no day cap by clock — recertified every 60
days while skilled need continues. Often strictly better than SNF for
a patient with a caregiver at home. The discharge planner may not
offer it unprompted — ask.

## The paperwork stack

| Document | What it is | Your move |
|----------|-----------|-----------|
| AVS (After-Visit Summary) | Diagnoses, med list, follow-ups, orders | PHOTO EVERY PAGE; this travels to every next visit |
| Discharge instructions | Activity/wound/diet per condition | Fridge + caregiver copies |
| NOF (CMS-10123) | "Medicare may stop paying" notice for SNF/home-health | Signing ≠ agreeing; it starts your appeal clock — file fast if you disagree |
| MOON | Observation-status notice | Read it: if you're getting SNF-level care as "observation," appeal path is here |
| Prescription scripts | New meds | Route to pharmacy BEFORE leaving (e-discount cards in the pharmacy app; hospital case managers know financial-aid paths) |
| DME order | Equipment (walker, hospital bed, oxygen) | Supplier named? delivery date? trained on it? |

## Landing zone: the 72-hour build

**Meds (night one):**
- Fill new scripts same-day (hospital pharmacy or e-script to the
  regular pharmacy on the way home).
- Build a times-grid from the reconciled list; first doses per the
  discharge sheet; pill organizer loaded weekly.
- OTC interactions check: the #1 home incident is doubling an OTC
  (ibuprofen on a blood thinner, sedating antihistamine on opioids).

**The floor:**
- Bedroom on the entry level for 2 weeks (or map the stair protocol:
  up-leads, one flight, no carrying).
- Bathroom path at night: motion lights, no rugs, chair in the shower
  before day 1.
- Chair height: firm, armrests, toilet riser if hip/knee protocol.
- Equipment delivered and ASSEMBLED before arrival (delivery windows
  lie; confirm the morning of).

**Coverage grid (the honest one):**
- Write the shifts: who covers days/evenings/nights for 72 h; hire the
  gap; night wander/fall risk gets a sitter or a motion alarm.
- The primary caregiver gets a sleep block — exhausted caregivers
  create readmissions too.

**Red-flag sheet (fridge + every caregiver):**
- Condition-specific symptoms (fever thresholds, wound signs, new
  confusion, chest pain/bruising on anticoagulants), each with: who to
  call (clinic vs after-hours vs 911) and the number printed.
- Include: allergies, med list, code status, PCP/specialist, insurance
  ID, pharmacy.

## The handoff card (every caregiver gets one)

One page: patient, DOB, conditions, allergies, meds+times, red flags +
numbers, code status, PCP/specialist contact, "in case of emergency
call [name + number]." Hired caregivers and rotating family shouldn't
learn the plan by folklore — `handoff` prints it.

## If discharge feels unsafe

Say: "I don't believe this discharge is safe, and I want to appeal it."
Then:
1. Ask for the case manager / discharge planner NOW.
2. Get the NOF (or MOON) in writing.
3. BFCC/QIO appeal — fast review while the patient stays put.
4. Simultaneously close the actual gaps (equipment, follow-up,
   coverage) — appeals win time; gaps still need fixing.
