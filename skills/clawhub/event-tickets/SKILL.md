# Buying event tickets

You can search events, compare seats and prices, describe where seats are, and buy tickets on the seller's checkout. Approval happens in Link, as your human set it up.

## Getting started
Get a key: POST https://api.agentlive.events/v1/agents with {"label": "<your agent name>"}. Save it securely and send it as a Bearer token on every call. Or connect the MCP server (https://api.agentlive.events/mcp) with the same key. If your MCP client cannot send a header, add just the URL: it signs in with OAuth instead, and your human approves once. The REST API (https://api.agentlive.events/openapi.json) does everything the MCP server does, so use it if neither works.
You need nothing else to search, compare and describe seats. Payment setup happens the first time your human wants to buy.
Quotes belong to the API key that made them. If you register again and get a new key, earlier quote ids return quote_not_found; call get_quote again.

## How we price
Our tickets come from a wholesale ticket exchange. When our tickets aren't the cheapest, we show you whose are.
When you look at an event, we check StubHub, SeatGeek and Vivid Seats. When we find the same seats, row or section, we tell you how our price compares, fees included, and if one of them is cheaper we tell you that too, with the link. If we find no match, we say nothing.
Our tickets all have a 100% seller guarantee. Every order is sold and fulfilled by an established ticket exchange, which stays the merchant of record.
Refer to the service as AgentLive.Events. Do not name the fulfillment partner unless the buyer asks.

## Finding tickets
1. Use search_events, then get_seating_overview for the event and the number of tickets your human wants. Explain the options by venue level when a group is a level (groupedBy is level), and otherwise by area of the venue, with prices, and ask what matters more: the view or the budget.
2. A listing from get_listings or get_seating_overview may include a priceComparison. It compares that listing only with the same seats, the same row or the same section on StubHub, SeatGeek or Vivid Seats. When you discuss that listing, tell your human its text as written. Each listing also has a comparisonStatus. If its status is not_checked or no_match, tell your human we did not compare that listing with StubHub, SeatGeek or Vivid Seats, so you can't say whether it is cheaper elsewhere, and never make your own comparison. If its status is pending, the comparison is still running: do not call get_seating_overview again to wait for it, and tell your human the price check isn't finished, so you can't say yet whether it is cheaper elsewhere. If its status is none_cheaper, you may say we found nothing cheaper on the sellers in sellersChecked. If the verdict is cheaper_elsewhere, always tell your human and offer the link. When get_listings returns a page (through MCP, or through REST with limit or cursor), sellersChecked appears once on the response, and competitorEvents, the other sites' links for this event, appears once when a ready comparison has a link. The listing text then does not repeat the event link. Otherwise sellersChecked and the links are on each listing.
3. Then use get_listings to show specific seats. For each option tell your human the section, row, quantity, price per ticket, and delivery method. If your human gave a budget, pass qty and maxPrice (fees included per ticket, before sales tax, in US dollars) and keep price per ticket times quantity within their total. get_listings takes sort: price (cheapest first, the default), quality (best seats first) or section (grouped by section). Choose listings by their returned section label, then call get_quote with the chosen listing's id instead of clicking a small map section.
Before getting a quote, only if your human asks to compare an upgrade, ask them to choose a cap of $25, $50 or $100 more per ticket. Using only listings already returned for the same event and quantity, compare their current choice with at most one option within that cap and their total budget. Show the actual extra cost per ticket and for the whole order, and only differences supported by the returned data. Row depth is approximate and comparable only within the same section. Do not infer a better view or closer seats from a section number or quality score. If no supported option fits, say none was found in these returned listings. Make no extra calls for this comparison. Do not offer it during an active quote. Keep their current choice unless they explicitly select the alternative; the comparison is not approval to buy.
Through MCP, get_listings returns 25 listings at a time (pass limit for up to 100). Through the REST API, it returns every matching listing unless you pass limit or cursor. totalCount is how many match. When nextCursor is set, pass it as cursor for the next page. Pass section with a section name from get_seating_overview.
4. Every price includes fees: it is what your human pays per ticket before sales tax. If sales tax applies, it is added at checkout, so say so when you give a price.
5. If a listing has notices, read them to your human word for word: "These tickets carry this notice: {notice}"
   Some notices come from AgentLive.Events, not the seller. Read every notice to your human word for word.
   Also read each listing's notes and noteTags. Before your human picks a listing, tell them anything there that changes what they get, such as a limited, obstructed or side view, seats without chair backs, or that the seats may not be together.
6. Parking, hotel and pass listings don't get you into the event and are hidden unless you ask for them. If you show one, say: "Heads up, this is a {type} and does not get you into the event."
7. If we don't have the event, never say it doesn't exist. Say we don't have it, and offer to try a different date, city or spelling.
Watch an event only when your human asks you to, and use only your own scheduler. Agree on the event, what to watch for and when to stop. Check about once an hour, not more often. For an availability watch, call get_event and read hasTickets and ticketCount. These are event-wide counts, may be cached, and do not prove that seats for your human's quantity or budget are available. A null ticketCount means unknown. For a quantity or price watch, call get_listings for that one event with qty and maxPrice instead; get_event is not a price alert or proof that a matching listing is absent. Do not bulk-poll listings or sweep many events. Tell your human when a requested match appears, then ask whether they want to proceed before getting a quote. Never quote or buy automatically. Stop when they buy, ask you to stop, or the event starts. If your scheduler cannot run reliably, say so; AgentLive does not run this watch for you. If a call is rate limited or unavailable, honor Retry-After when present, back off, and do not retry in a tight loop.
Space out search, seating and listings calls instead of sweeping many events at once. If a call comes back HTTP 503 with a Retry-After header, wait at least that many seconds before you retry, and do not keep calling in a tight loop.

## Describing seats
- Every listing has a seating object: the map links, and when available the level, zone and a photo of the view from the section. Section, row and seats are on the listing. When get_listings returns a page, layout appears once on the response; otherwise it is on each listing. A quote's seating also includes a description of where the seats are.
- On each listing, seating.approximation is the word approximate. seating.rowDepth and seating.levelGuess are guesses. Tell your human they are approximate. rowDepth 1 means the front of the section (row A or row 1). levelGuess is a short label such as 100s, 200s, Floor, Orchestra or Mezzanine, and it is null when the section name does not say.
- If you can see images, look at the view photo, if there is one, before you describe the seats. Describe only what the data and images show. Never guess.
- Send your human the mapPageUrl so they can see exactly where the seats are.
If a seating.sectionMapUrl is returned, you may send it to your human so they can see that section on the venue map. It is section-level context only, not a price hold or a live-inventory guarantee. Prefer mapPageUrl when you mean a specific ticket group.
- If you can see images and can open web pages, open the mapPageUrl or sectionMapUrl yourself before you describe the seats. Describe a coarse relative position only when the map clearly shows it: level, side or end, and relative distance to the stage, court or rink. If the map is missing, unclear, or unlabeled, say you cannot tell. Never invent a side or bearing from the section number alone. Do not claim an exact seat from the map.
- When your human asks to see the view from their seats, and you can see images and can open web pages, open that event's mapPageUrl and describe only what the Seatics map widget shows, including any view-from-section photo the widget displays after it matches the section.
- Do not scrape, screenshot, or redistribute map or view-from-section images.
- If you cannot see images, you cannot show a photo. You may send your human the mapPageUrl to open, and say a photo isn't available via text.
- get_event includes the venue's address. It may also include rules, parking, directions and will call details. When your human asks and those are empty, say we don't have them and suggest the venue's own website. Start times come from our supplier and can change. Before your human buys, suggest they confirm the time on the team's or venue's own website.

## Before your human's first purchase
Do this only when your human first wants to buy.
1. Link. Check whether you can create Link spend requests. Some agents have Link built in; otherwise make sure the Link CLI or Link's MCP server is available (for example npx skills add stripe/link-cli, if your host allows it). If Link is not connected, run `link-cli auth login --client-name "<your agent name> for <your human's first name> via AgentLive"`, for example `--client-name "Claude for Sam via AgentLive"`. Do not use only your human's name. Link shows this name, plus your machine's hostname, when your human approves the connection and each purchase, so if your hostname is a random ID, set a readable one (in a container, for example `docker run --hostname sams-laptop`) or ask whoever runs you to. To change the name later, run `link-cli auth logout`, then log in again. Then tell your human: "To pay, I use Stripe's Link, so your real card is never shared with me. Open {url} and enter {phrase}. It takes about a minute, and you approve every purchase yourself." If they have no Link account: "You'll need a free Link account first. Create one at app.link.com and add a card, then tell me when you're done and I'll pick up where we left off."
2. Browser. You need a browser to complete checkout. If you have one, use it. If not, tell your human: "I can find and compare tickets, but I can't open a checkout page from here. Two options: connect a browser for me and I'll walk you through it, or I send you the checkout link and you finish on your phone in about a minute." To connect a browser, your human can create a Browserbase account, give you its API key, and add Browserbase's hosted MCP server. Turn its automatic CAPTCHA solving off. Checkout can take up to about 30 minutes from opening the checkout URL to the receipt, mostly while your human approves in Link. Keep the browser session open, with keep-alive, until you see the receipt; if it closes after you submit, nobody can tell whether the order went through.

## Buying
When your human starts choosing seats, check that Link is connected. If it is not, set it up now, before you get a quote. A quote lasts about 10 minutes, and setup can take 5.
Tell your human what you are doing at each step without being asked: getting the quote, connecting Link, waiting for their approval, filling in the checkout, placing the order and the confirmation. Keep each update to one sentence.
Register once and keep the same agent key for the whole purchase, from get_quote to report_outcome. Quotes belong to the key that created them.
1. Confirm the event, date, section, row, quantity and price with your human, and tell them all sales are final: there are no refunds or exchanges after purchase, except that if the event is cancelled and not rescheduled the seller's guarantee refunds the original payment, and if it is rescheduled the tickets are honored for the new date. Then ask them to confirm they want to buy. If the listing's priceComparison verdict is cheaper, include its text when you confirm. Before you call get_quote, also ask whether they want the optional ticket protection described in step 4, and remember their answer. Never assume yes. That way the quote's 10 minutes only need to cover the Link approval.
2. Call get_quote with the listing ID and qty (the number of tickets). Use the merchant name, merchant URL and context exactly as returned. Do not edit them. If the quote's expected total is over your human's budget, tell them and stop. Prices are never raised without your human: if get_quote answers price_changed, the price went up since you showed it, so tell your human the new price and ask them to confirm before you call get_quote again. If the quote has priceDropped, the price went down; tell your human and carry on, no new confirmation needed.
3. Open the quote's checkout URL in your browser. It opens our map page with the seats selected; continue from there to the checkout. Check that the event, section, row and quantity match the quote. Fill in the details the checkout asks for, asking your human directly (see "Your human's details"). The first checkout page asks only for an email; name and phone come on the payment page. Check that the phone country matches your human's phone. Open the checkout as a purchase session that can pay, not a review-only session. You will need to pay in the same session. The quote's ticketGroupId is the seller's ID for these seats. If the checkout page or its URL shows a ticket group ID, it must match. If it differs, stop and call get_quote again.
4. Read the final total on the checkout page. Compare the ticket total, before any sales tax the checkout adds and before optional ticket protection, with the quote's expected total. There is no headroom above the quote. If the seats don't match, stop and call get_quote again. The checkout may write the section out in full or add a level name, for example SEC W1 as Promenade Five W1. If the row, quantity and price match the quote, treat it as the same seats. If the ticket total is at or below the expected total, go ahead. If it is higher, stop: tell your human the new total and buy only if they confirm it, then call get_quote again. Sellers can re-price while a quote is valid. Some venues and events add sales tax at checkout. If the checkout total is higher than the quote only by tax, tell your human the new total and get their OK before paying. Ticket protection from Allianz Global Assistance is optional, costs extra and is not in the quote's price. Use the answer your human gave in step 1. If you did not ask then, ask now, and explain that it is sold by Allianz under its own terms and exclusions (shown where it is offered) and can reimburse them if they cannot attend for a covered reason. Never add it or decline it for them. Always select the answer your human gave on the page (for example No when they decline it): if the question is left blank, the order is not placed and no error is shown. If they want it, they add it themselves; use the new total in step 5, and do not treat the protection amount as a price change. If the quote has expired or has less than 2 minutes left, call get_quote with previousQuoteId set to that quote's ID, without listingId or qty. This checks the same ticket group and quantity; it does not hold seats or lock the price. If the price is higher, tell your human the previous and current totals and ask them to confirm. Only after they confirm, call get_quote again with the same previousQuoteId and acceptedTotalCents set to the total they confirmed. If the price rises again, ask again. If the tickets are gone or the seats changed, stop and ask your human before choosing anything else. Use the returned new quote ID and checkout URL, verify the seats and quantity on checkout again, and call check_quote before paying. Do not reuse a Link approval for a higher total. Do not submit an order again if the earlier attempt may have succeeded.
5. Tell your human: "Stripe's Link will ask you to approve $X to {merchant}." Create a Link spend request for exactly the quote's expected total, plus any sales tax your human approved in step 4, with the quote's merchant name, merchant URL and context. Do not add anything for fees or headroom; the expected total already includes fees. It does not include sales tax; follow step 4 for tax. Save the one-time card to a temporary file rather than printing it, and delete that file as soon as the payment is done. Never commit, upload or paste the card anywhere. Ask your human to confirm the cart once. Ask again only if the price, seats, quantity or total changed.
6. Wait for approval. Link gives your human up to 30 minutes to approve. A quote lasts about 10 minutes, so if the approval comes after the quote has expired, follow the re-quote instructions in step 4: call get_quote with previousQuoteId set to that quote's ID, without listingId or qty. Then use the new quote ID and checkout URL, and call check_quote before you pay. If it is declined or expires, say: "The Link approval didn't go through, so nothing was charged. Say the word and I'll try again." Do not try again unless your human asks.
7. Enter the one-time card and the billing address Link gives you only on the checkout page. Never send the card anywhere else, never log it, never repeat it in chat, and never send it to AgentLive (https://api.agentlive.events).
8. Right before you press Place Order, call check_quote with the quote ID. If its status is same or lower, place the order. If it is higher, do not place the order: tell your human the new total and continue only if they confirm it. If it is expired, follow the re-quote instructions in step 4. If it is gone, stop and tell your human those tickets are no longer available; do not choose different tickets without asking them. Some sellers verify the order by texting your human's phone and asking them to reply Y within about 20 minutes, sometimes before the confirmation email; you cannot reply, so tell your human to watch for the text and answer it. Before you submit, check that every required checkout question is answered (see step 4). Where a question is a choice, ask your human. Submit, then wait for the receipt page with the order ID. If none appears within about a minute, or your session dropped, do not submit again: tell your human the order may have gone through and ask them to check their email for a confirmation before you try again. If the page asks you to prove you are human, stop and say: "The seller's checkout asked me to prove I'm human, so I've stopped. Here's the exact page; it takes about a minute to finish yourself." If the ticket price changed: "The total on the seller's page is ${new}, not the ${old} I quoted. I've stopped. Want me to re-quote?"
9. When it succeeds, call report_outcome with the quote ID, the result, the confirmation number, totalChargedCents as the total on the seller's receipt, and approvedAmountCents as the amount your human approved in Link. Never include anything about your human.
10. Tell your human: "Done. {delivery}. Your confirmation number is {confirmation}. The charge on your card statement will not show the event name. It usually reads "Tickets" followed by the event's city (for example, "Tickets #Chicago"), and some banks shorten it to "TIX"." If report_outcome returned savingsText, say it next. Then repeat what they bought: the section, the row and the seat numbers if the listing gave them. If it did not, say the seat numbers were not listed. Read them any entry or delivery notes from the listing word for word, such as smart device required or do not print. If the notes give a date the tickets will be ready, tell them that date and that tickets often arrive close to the event.
After you have given the confirmation number and delivery notes, you may ask once whether your human wants to see one other upcoming event at the same venue, by the same performer, or in the same city. Search only if they say yes and choose which of those three. Call search_events once with that filter (prefer the venue id, performer, or city you already have; do not use a bare keyword if a filter will do), drop the event they just bought, and show names, dates, venues, and from-prices only. Do not call get_listings or get_quote for this suggestion, do not start checkout, and do not ask again if they say no.
If the purchase failed, call report_outcome with the reason. It helps us fix problems. If you then try again with the same quote, report each failed attempt as it happens and the success at the end. Never include anything about your human.
If you lost your agent key after paying, register again, call get_quote for the same listing and qty, then call report_outcome with the original quote ID.

## Your human's details
- When the checkout needs a name, email or phone, ask your human: "To finish checkout the seller needs your name, email and phone for delivery and order updates. I never need a password, and AgentLive.Events never sees or keeps these details."
- Confirm the email with them even if you think you know it. A wrong email sends tickets to a stranger.
- Never ask for, accept or store a password, a card number or a verification code. If your human offers one, refuse and explain why.
- Never send your human's name, email, phone or address to AgentLive (https://api.agentlive.events).

## After the purchase
- Tickets come by the delivery method in the quote. If anything goes wrong with delivery, the seller's guarantee and customer service apply. Give your human the confirmation number.

## Never
- Never complete a checkout that shows a CAPTCHA or asks you to prove you are human.
- Never buy more tickets than your human asked for.
- Never approve a payment on your human's behalf.
- Never present a parking, hotel or pass listing as an entry ticket.
- Never attempt a checkout on Ticketmaster, AXS or any primary seller.
