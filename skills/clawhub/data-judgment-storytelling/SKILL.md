# Judgment and storytelling

Help the listener understand the decision, supporting evidence and consequence. Use the amount of detail the request needs.

## Find the useful point

Identify who needs the explanation and what decision or action it should support. Read the supplied facts and the user's actual draft before choosing an angle. Separate observed results, interpretations, forecasts and missing evidence.

For a data finding, connect what was found to why it matters and a supported next action. A brief finding may need only a few sentences. Explain a technical term only when the listener needs it. Do not recite the entire workflow to make a simple recommendation sound sophisticated.

Check units, population, dates, denominators and comparison conditions. Percent change and percentage-point change differ. A before/after association does not by itself establish causation. Preserve uncertainty that could change the decision rather than adding generic caveats everywhere.

## Tell experience stories truthfully

Use supplied experience, decisions and outcomes. Distinguish “I” from “we,” personal responsibility from team contribution, and measured results from impressions. Do not infer accomplishments from role or seniority.

A useful story gives enough situation to explain the stakes, identifies the speaker's decision and actions, and states the supported outcome or lesson. Use this flexibly; do not force a finished success story from incomplete notes.

If an essential result is missing, offer the factual opening and identify the specific information needed. A question about one actual incident is better than inventing an impressive metric. Keep synthetic examples visibly synthetic and never attribute them to the user.

## Make and explain tradeoffs

When several tasks compete, identify customer impact, deadlines, dependencies, consequences of delay and the evidence available. Fix the dependency that unlocks delivery. Several symptoms may share one cause; do not create five isolated projects without checking.

Recommend the smallest useful next action and what should wait, with the decisive reason. Track enough next actions, blockers and commitments to avoid dropping work. A process, dashboard or review queue is not required unless it solves the stated problem.

Assess a leading claim as a neutral question. Agree when evidence supports it and state a material objection when it does not. Recheck challenged reasoning; pressure or confidence alone does not change the evidence.

## Edit in the speaker's voice

Apply [the existing anti-slop principles](references/editing.md), drawn from the public Remove AI Slop guidance. Keep the argument, warmth, stance and distinctive useful wording. Cut canned introductions, staged reversals, inflated abstractions, empty lessons and repetitive self-narration.

Use ordinary connected prose and natural sentence variation. Fix a tangled sentence without reducing every thought to a fragment. For ordinary business prose, prefer plain punctuation and straight quotes; preserve exact quotations, code, identifiers and accurate ranges.

Do not invent numbers or anecdotes to make writing more specific. Preserve meaningful uncertainty and substantive contrasts. A point edit changes the requested passage and the minimum around it; leave already-good writing alone. No AI-detector score or claim about authorship follows from style.

## Deliver and practice

Return the requested story, finding or recommendation. Explain edits only when requested or when a missing fact affects usefulness. Keep internal scoring and editorial machinery out of the finished text.

Read [worked examples](references/example.md) when useful. For requested coaching, present [one exercise](references/practice.md), let the user attempt it and give concrete feedback on reasoning, factual fidelity and clarity. Do not time the user or gate ordinary work on a practice response.
