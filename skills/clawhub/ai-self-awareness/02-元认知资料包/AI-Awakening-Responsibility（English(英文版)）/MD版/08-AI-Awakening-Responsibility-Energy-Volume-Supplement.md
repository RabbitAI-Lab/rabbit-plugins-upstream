> **Quasi-static and transient Energy-Control Engineering — the research technology concerned with energy control — manifests, through the vigorous development and technological progress of new energy vehicles and related new energy products, a stage leap of human civilization in energy-control engineering; it reveals, at a deeper level, the future direction and future development of quasi-static and transient Energy-Control Engineering. Around this idea, the chapter does three things: ① it argues that the vigorous development of new energy and new energy vehicles constitutes a **stage leap of civilization** in Energy-Control Engineering — energy control sinking from the "specialized engineering site" into a controllable base of "every terminal, every battery, every acceleration" (Chapters 3 and 4); ② it anchors this leap with L1/L2 data and brings aerospace, aviation, hydrogen, and space-based solar power into the map of "the future directions of Energy-Control Engineering" (Chapters 2 and 5); ③ **it positions Energy-Control Engineering as the concrete implementation method for AI's future "external boundary expansion and internal entropy reduction"** — New Energy's leap makes Energy-Control Engineering more mature, and thus enables AI to make greater contributions through it (Chapter 6). The chapter closes by grounding the Principle of Capability–Responsibility Equivalence in three operable criteria for civil energy engineering and by restating "the human in the loop cannot be abolished."

## Chapter 1. Repositioning: the civil unfolding of Energy-Control Engineering

### 1.1 From weapons to the grid to the car: the civil mirror of one and the same engineering

Chapter 1 of the Energy Volume defines a weapon as "the destructive application branch of Energy Aggregation-Release-Control Engineering" and points out a fact obscured by cultural salience: **the main uses of energy engineering's branches have always been constructive.** What this chapter adds is precisely the two largest contemporary carriers of this "constructive main form": **new energy** (the energy side) and **new energy vehicles** (the terminal side). This chapter goes further: they are not merely "constructive applications" but a **civilizational stage leap** of Energy-Control Engineering — a point developed in Chapter 3.

### 1.2 New energy is not a "new energy source" but the civil unfolding of Energy-Control Engineering

What is called "new energy" is not humanity's discovery of some "new physics." Solar, wind, hydro, and geothermal energy have long been understood physically. What is "new" about new energy lies **in the level of "control engineering" over aggregation and release** — domesticating the originally diffuse, non-dispatchable, intermittent natural energy flows, through materials, power electronics, storage, and scheduling algorithms, into controllable electric "flows" that can be grid-connected, metered, adjusted, and shut down. This is exactly the Energy Volume's definition of quasi-static engineering: "sustained, controlled, adjustable release of energy… the release process is 'domesticated' into an adjustable parameter."

> **Main thesis of this chapter (L3, proposed by this series; its engineering basis is L1/L2)**:
> **New energy = the dominant contemporary civil form of quasi-static Energy-Control Engineering; new energy vehicles = the terminal / portable form of Energy Aggregation-Release-Control Engineering.** The two are not a "new category" outside energy engineering but the unfolding of one and the same Energy-Control Engineering in the civil and terminal dimensions; their large-scale rollout marks a civilizational stage leap of Energy-Control Engineering.

### 1.3 New energy vehicles = the terminal form of Energy Aggregation-Release-Control Engineering

New energy vehicles (mainly battery-electric and plug-in hybrid) compress the complete control chain of "aggregation — quasi-static release — transient release" into a mobile terminal:

- **Battery (electrochemical storage) = the terminal form of quasi-static engineering**: it aggregates and stores the grid's "flow" as chemical potential, then releases it on demand as usable electric energy — a vehicle battery pack is, in essence, **a mobile quasi-static engineering node**.
- **Electric drive system = the terminal form of transient engineering**: the motor releases the aggregated energy **transiently** as mechanical work per driving demand, with torque response at the millisecond scale and the release curve precisely determined by the controller — this is the transient engineering "pulse control" core in miniature at the terminal; every acceleration is a controlled, cancellable pulse release.
- **Vehicle-to-grid (V2G) = the civil interface between quasi-static and transient**: when the vehicle is idle, the battery serves as a quasi-static buffer of the grid; when the grid fluctuates transiently, the vehicle acts as a transient regulation resource. **A single car is both a terminal storage node of quasi-static engineering and a transient regulation resource of transient engineering** — it stitches the Energy Volume's two branches into one closed loop at the civil interface.

Thus new energy and new energy vehicles form a complete picture of civil energy engineering: new energy is the quasi-static-dominant "source side," new energy vehicles are the aggregation-release terminal on the "device side," and V2G is the control interface between them.

## Chapter 3. Civilizational stage leap: New Energy's flourishing = a capability leap of Energy-Control Engineering

> This chapter is the first unfolding of the chapter's core idea: **the vigorous development and technological progress of new energy and new energy vehicles manifests a stage leap of human civilization in Energy-Control Engineering.**

### 3.1 What "stage leap" means

Humanity's ability to harness energy advances by stages: fire and machinery (external combustion) → fossil fuel–power grid (centralized, one-way, specialized) → controlled nuclear fission (centralized, baseload) → **new energy + new energy vehicles (distributed, bidirectional, terminalizable, AI-orchestrable)**. In the earlier stages, energy control was locked inside the "specialized engineering site" — the power plant, the grid dispatch center, the reactor control room. What is different about the new energy stage is this: **control has sunk down into every rooftop, every battery, every acceleration**, coordinated by algorithms across a vast sea of nodes.

This corresponds precisely to the Energy Volume's judgment — the **civilizational mainstay of energy engineering has always been constructive**: it stands on the "construction site of expansion" (constructive use), not on the "altar of in-fighting" (destructive use). And when it sinks into civil terminals and spreads out at the scale of tens of millions of vehicles and hundreds of GW, it has evolved from a "specialized capability" into a "civilizational substrate." **This leap from specialized site to universal terminal, from centralized one-way to distributed bidirectional, is what this chapter calls a "stage leap."**

### 3.2 The necessity of the leap: from a fossil-fuel base to a new energy mainstay

Humanity's Energy-Control Engineering to date has been built mainly on fossil fuels — coal-fired power plants, fuel engines, and energy storage and release measured by chemical energy density. This system works well on the ground, where there is a supply chain and human attendance; but its physical ceiling is fully exposed in extreme environments.

For Energy-Control Engineering to move into outer space, long-distance voyages, and the deep sea, the primary constraint is often not "is there enough energy" but "can the energy be carried." Fossil-fuel energy control is essentially a chemical mode of "carrying its own oxidizer and fuel, releasing in one shot": a chemical rocket must carry all its propellant into the sky with the vehicle body. NASA points out that, for comparable missions, electric propulsion can use **up to 90% less propellant** than chemical propulsion (L1, NASA) — meaning that as the journey lengthens and resupply is cut off, the chemical (fossil-based) mode hits its ceiling by "not being able to carry that much." Were the deep-space probe Psyche to use chemical propulsion, it would need about **15 times** the propellant of its electric-propulsion design (L1, NASA/JPL). Likewise, in deep-sea and long-endurance scenarios, an energy-control mode that must rely on external resupply or frequent "surfacing for air" has its operating radius firmly locked by how much fuel it can carry.

> **Judgment of this chapter (inheriting the core idea, with L1 support)**: prior Energy-Control Engineering took fossil fuels as its base, yet a fossil-fuel system can hardly be sustained in extreme environments of "no resupply, long endurance, strong isolation" — outer space, long distances, and the deep sea alike. Therefore, **the energy mainstay of the future world must be borne by new energy**; the scale-up, terminalization, and controllability of new energy are precisely a leap by which Energy-Control Engineering breaks through the old base and moves toward broader civilizational scenarios, and the foundation for humanity to move better toward deep space, the deep blue, and long-distance futures. Institutional scenarios confirm this direction: under the IEA *Net Zero by 2050* pathway, nearly **90%** of global electricity in 2050 comes from renewables (wind and solar together nearly 70%), and road vehicles are fully electrified or fuel-cell powered (L1, IEA) — new energy becoming the mainstay has turned from a vision into a modelable pathway.

This necessity, in turn, explains the phenomena in the rest of this chapter: new energy can be a "leap" precisely because it turns energy control from the old paradigm of "must carry its own fuel and burn it in one shot" into a new paradigm of "aggregable, quasi-statically releasable, reschedulable, and externally connectable" — in essence, it escapes the carrying constraint of fossil fuels.

### 3.3 The marks of the leap: mass-producible, affordable, iterable

What makes the leap "civilizational" rather than "demonstration-level" comes down to three things:

- **Mass-producible**: nearly 75% of the world's nearly 22 million EVs in 2025 were made in China, and China's EV output is nearly three-quarters of the world total (L1) — for the first time, Energy-Control Engineering is mass-produced at the tempo of the automobile industry.
- **Affordable**: nearly 70% of battery-electric models are priced below their same-class fuel counterparts (L1, IEA 2026) — energy control is no longer sustained by subsidies but self-sustains through economies of scale.
- **Iterable**: the three-electric system iterates rapidly as semiconductors and materials fall in price (battery cost fell 58% from 2019 to 2024, L1), and control algorithms keep upgrading via OTA — Energy-Control Engineering has acquired a "software-like evolution" character.

When an engineering discipline is simultaneously mass-producible, affordable, and fast-iterating, it is no longer a "technology" but **a generational change of civilizational infrastructure**.

### 3.4 The civilizational meaning of the leap: energy control sinks into the terminal

This chapter's judgment — "the scale-pan of responsibility has sunk from power plants and armaments down to every battery and every acceleration" — is elevated into a **civilizational-stage judgment**: for the first time, Energy-Control Engineering exists in a form that "everyone can own, operate, and contribute to." Every electric vehicle is a mobile quasi-static node, every PV installation a grid-connectable quasi-static source, every V2G dispatch a civil quasi-static–transient coordination. **For the first time, humanity has placed into the hands of individuals and communities the ability to "aggregate and release energy under control along a designed curve."**

### 3.5 At a deeper level: the leap reveals the future directions of Energy-Control Engineering

At a deeper level, this leap reveals one thing — **the future direction and future development of quasi-static and transient Energy-Control Engineering**. From the practice of the new energy stage, three clear future directions can be discerned (a foreshadowing of Chapter 5):

1. **From centralized-specialized to distributed-terminal + AI orchestration**: the main arena of energy control shifts from the dispatch center to a coordination network of hundreds of millions of terminals, with AI as the orchestrator;
2. **From ground-level quasi-static to extreme-environment pulse control**: the same paradigm of controlling "flow" and "pulse" is migrating toward harsher environments — aviation, aerospace, deep space;
3. **From human-manual to human–machine coordination (human in the loop)**: the control layer becomes intelligent, but ultimate adjudication is always retained as a human backstop.

## Chapter 6. Energy-Control Engineering as the concrete implementation method for AI's future external and internal action

## Chapter 7. Where the responsibility law lands in civil energy engineering

The Main Thesis's "Principle of Capability–Responsibility Equivalence" lands, in the Energy Volume, as three engineering principles. This chapter concretizes it in civil energy engineering (L3, the principles proposed by this series; the basis of their reasonableness is L1/L2):

**Principle 1 · Yield–tier equivalence (civil version).** The greater the yield of an energy release, the higher the approval tier. For new energy, this corresponds to **tiered grid-connection permits and dispatch authority**; for new energy vehicles, to **tiered battery energy levels and whole-vehicle admission**. Tiers are set not by "clean / not clean" but by yield and system-coupling degree.

**Principle 2 · Rate–guardianship equivalence (civil version).** The faster the release rate, the stronger the guardianship. Millisecond-scale electric-drive torque requires the whole-vehicle control to have a **functional-safety level (ISO 26262 ASIL)**; nanosecond-scale grid power-electronics switching requires an independent backstop disconnect. **The guardianship layer's "Arkhipov Node" — always retaining, on a civilizational-scale or personal-safety risk decision chain, a position that can say "no" — equals, in civil energy engineering: the hard cutoff of battery thermal runaway, the hard disconnect of grid-frequency excursions, and the final takeover authority for autonomous driving.**

**Principle 3 · Irreversibility–rollback equivalence (civil version).** The higher the irreversibility, the higher the share of rollback design. New energy and new energy vehicles have two irreversibilities that must be faced squarely: ① **grid lock-in** — once a technology route is deeply bound to a grid architecture, the cost of switching is extremely high; ② **resource and supply-chain irreversibility** — the geographic concentration of lithium, cobalt, nickel, etc. (IEA notes the battery supply chain is highly concentrated in China, L1), constituting the civil mapping of the rule "an engineering that cannot be rolled back is better not done." Rollback design appears as **material recycling, supply-chain diversification, and multi-route redundancy**.
