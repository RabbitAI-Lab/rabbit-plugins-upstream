# Ergo Desk Calibrator — Reference

## Why desks hurt people
The "standard" 75 cm desk fits people around 181 cm. Global average
adult height is well below that; most women and many men typing at 75 cm
hold shoulders shrugged or wrists extended for hours. Add a laptop
(hinge forces screen-low + keyboard-high simultaneously) and a monitor
bought by diagonal size rather than placement math, and the 3 PM
neck/wrist ache is a geometry certainty, not bad luck.

## The anthropometric model
Fractions of stature H (erect seated posture, population averages;
individual variation ±3-4 cm — treat outputs as starting points, then
comfort-check):

| Landmark | Fraction | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Popliteal (seat) height | 0.26 H | +2-3 cm shoe heel allowance included |
| Elbow above seat | 0.155 H | defines keyboard plane |
| Desk height | 0.415 H | seat + elbow |
| Seated eye height | 0.674 H | from floor, erect |
| Standing elbow | 0.63 H | standing desk height |
| Standing eye | 0.936 H | |
| Viewing distance | 0.38 H | clamp 50-85 cm (target) |

Worked: H=170 → seat 44.2, desk 70.6, eye 114.6 (44.0 above desk).
H=185 → desk 76.8 — above the standard 75, which is why tall people
hunch at fixed desks.

## Monitor geometry
16:9 panel height ≈ 0.49 × diagonal. Examples (bottom edge on 5 cm
stand, person H=175, eye line 45.5 cm above desk):
- 24": panel h 29.9 → top 34.9 → 10.6 BELOW eye line → riser ~5 cm ideal.
- 27": panel h 33.5 → top 38.5 → 7 ABOVE eye line → lower stand or
  distance 75-80 cm.
- 32": panel h 39.7 → top 44.7 → 19 above eye line → this size needs
  distance 85+ cm and generally a lower/thinner desk bezel setup.
Ultrawides: top edge matters more; use monitor arm with 20+ cm vertical
range.

Distance rule of thumb: diagonal in inches × ~2.5-3 cm, never < 50 cm.
Bigger screens pushed back also fix "too tall" — the eye-line check is
about the TOP edge only; centers naturally sit below.

## The laptop problem, precisely
Built-in screen top edge sits ~18-25 cm above the desk (hinge geometry)
vs the 30-45 cm eye line — a 20-30° neck flexion. External keyboard
with laptop-flat reduces wrist angle but keeps neck flexion; riser
without external keyboard fixes neck but wrecks wrists. The ONLY
one-device solution doesn't exist: riser + external input devices.
Cost of fixing: a $10 riser (or books) + $15 keyboard/mouse. Compare
with physiotherapy co-pays.

## Symptom → cause ranking heuristics
Rankings in `symptom` are priors from ergonomic literature orderings
(neck flexion and desk-too-high dominate their pain classes), not
diagnoses. Use as a checklist to eliminate the 80% mechanical causes
before seeking clinical help. Red flags that skip straight to a doctor:
numbness/tingling into fingers lasting > days, grip strength loss,
night pain, radiating arm pain.

## Standing desks
- Height = standing elbow (0.63 H) ± a couple cm for taste.
- Full-day standing is its own injury path (veins, feet, low back):
  the honest protocol is alternation — 15-30 min standing per hour,
  anti-fatigue mat, one-foot rail (changes pelvic tilt).
- Transition schedule for new users: week 1 — 1-2 h/day standing total,
  add 30-45 min/day weekly, cap around 3-4 h/day standing.

## 20-20-20 and breaks
Every 20 minutes, look at something ~6 m away for ~20 seconds (relaxes
accommodation); every 30-60 min, stand/walk 5 minutes (discs rehydrate
only in movement; static loading is the mechanism behind sit-all-day
back pain). Micro-breaks beat perfect posture: variability of position
is protective; the best posture is the next posture.

## Children / teens
Same fractions apply, but kids grow — recalibrate every growth spurt
(foot dangling and monitor-above-eyes are the usual failures). Screen
distance guidance for kids is commonly stated as more conservative
(aim toward the far end of the range).

## What this tool is not
- Not medical advice; persistent or radiating symptoms → clinician.
- Not a substitute for a professional ergonomic assessment for
  disability accommodation or injury claims.
- Fractions are population averages; individual limb proportions vary —
  the comfort tests (shoulder shrug, arm's-length, wrist neutral) are
  the ground truth, the numbers are the starting point.
