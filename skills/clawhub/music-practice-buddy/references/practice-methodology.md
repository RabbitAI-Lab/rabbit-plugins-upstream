# Evidence-Based Practice Methodology

This document covers the practice techniques recommended by Music Practice Buddy, grounded in music education research and expert performance studies.

## The Science of Deliberate Practice

### K. Anders Ericsson's Research

Ericsson's landmark 1993 study of expert musicians found that what separates elite performers is not just total practice hours, but the **quality** of practice. Key findings:

- **Deliberate practice** is more predictive of expertise than total practice time
- Deliberate practice has four characteristics:
  1. **Specific goals** for each session (not "practice for an hour")
  2. **Immediate feedback** on performance
  3. **Focused repetition** of weak areas (not playing through start to finish)
  4. **Progressive difficulty** — starting easy and building up

Music Practice Buddy implements these principles by:
- Identifying your **specific weakness** (timing, pitch, tempo, or dynamics)
- Providing **objective feedback** (scores and measurements)
- Recommending **targeted exercises** for the weak area
- Building **progressive plans** (slow → fast, simple → complex)

## Practice Techniques by Weakness Area

### Timing Issues (Low Timing Score)

#### Technique 1: Metronome at 60% Speed

**When**: Timing score < 70

**How**: Set the metronome to 60% of the target tempo. Play the piece perfectly at this speed. If you make any timing error, restart from the beginning.

**Why**: Slowing down reveals timing habits you can't hear at full speed. You rush because your fingers anticipate — at 60% speed, you must place each note deliberately.

**Duration**: 10 minutes per session

#### Technique 2: Speed Bursts

**When**: Can play cleanly at 60% but not at 100%

**How**: Practice short sections (2–4 measures) at 110% of target speed. Then return to 100% — it will feel easier.

**Why**: Playing faster than target "reprograms" your motor memory. When you return to target speed, your hands have surplus capacity.

**Duration**: 5 minutes per difficult section

#### Technique 3: Rhythmic Displacement

**When**: Rhythmic patterns feel mechanical or inconsistent

**How**: Practice the passage with the rhythm reversed — long notes become short, short become long. Then return to the original.

**Why**: This forces you to understand the rhythm intellectually, not just feel it. It breaks automatic muscle memory that may be slightly wrong.

**Duration**: 5 minutes per rhythmic passage

### Pitch Issues (Low Pitch Score)

#### Technique 1: Drone Practice

**When**: Pitch score < 75

**How**: Play a sustained drone (using an app or a sympathetic string) on the tonic. Practice your piece against the drone, matching every note to its interval relationship with the tonic.

**Why**: The constant reference pitch trains your ear to hear intervals accurately. You develop an internal sense of tuning rather than relying on visual cues (frets, keys).

**Duration**: 10–15 minutes

#### Technique 2: Slow Pitch Matching

**When**: Specific notes are consistently out of tune

**How**: Play the problematic note. Hold it. Slide up and down until you find the center of the pitch (where it resonates most). Memorize the physical sensation.

**Why**: Intonation problems are often physical technique issues (finger pressure, bow angle, breath support), not ear issues. This exercise connects the correct pitch to the correct physical action.

**Duration**: 2–3 minutes per problem note

#### Technique 3: Record and Compare

**When**: Self-assessment is unreliable

**How**: Record yourself. Play it back with a tuner running. Note which notes are flat/sharp. Practice those notes specifically.

**Why**: What feels in tune while playing is often out of tune. The recording removes the sympathetic resonance inside your skull that makes your own pitch hard to judge.

**Duration**: Full recording + 10 minutes targeted practice

### Tempo Issues (Low Tempo Score)

#### Technique 1: Metronome with Recording

**When**: Tempo score < 70

**How**: Set the metronome to your target BPM. Record yourself playing with it. Then play the recording back — can you hear the metronome clearly throughout?

**Why**: If the metronome disappears during certain passages, you're playing over it — rushing. If it gets louder, you're dragging behind.

**Duration**: 15 minutes

#### Technique 2: Tempo Mapping

**When**: Tempo drifts inconsistently

**How**: Practice the piece in sections. Play each section with the metronome separately. Identify which sections you naturally speed up or slow down. Mark these in your score.

**Why**: Drift is usually caused by specific passages (easy = fast, hard = slow). Identifying them lets you focus your attention there.

**Duration**: 15 minutes

#### Technique 3: Gradual Tempo Increase

**When**: Can play at slow tempo but not at target

**How**: Start at 60% of target. When you can play 3 repetitions perfectly, increase by 5 BPM. Repeat until you reach target tempo.

**Why**: This builds technique incrementally. Jumping from slow to fast causes technique breakdown. 5 BPM increments are small enough to adapt to.

**Duration**: 15–20 minutes

### Dynamic Issues (Low Dynamics Score)

#### Technique 1: Dynamic Extremes

**When**: Dynamic range is too narrow (< 6 dB)

**How**: Practice the piece at two dynamic extremes: as softly as physically possible (pianississimo), then as loudly as possible (fortississimo). Then find the middle.

**Why**: Most players live in a narrow dynamic range because they never test the extremes. Exploring the full range makes the middle feel controlled and easy.

**Duration**: 10 minutes

#### Technique 2: Crescendo/Decrescendo Practice

**When**: Volume changes are inconsistent

**How**: Practice long notes with gradual crescendos (8 counts, pp to ff) and decrescendos. Focus on smooth, even changes.

**Why**: Dynamic control is about proportion — increasing/decreasing by the right amount at the right rate. This builds the physical control for smooth changes.

**Duration**: 5–10 minutes

#### Technique 3: Accent Patterns

**When**: Some notes consistently too loud/soft

**How**: Practice scales or passages with varied accent patterns. Accent beats 1 and 3. Then 2 and 4. Then every note. Then no accents.

**Why**: This builds dynamic awareness and control on every note, not just the downbeat. It prevents the "autopilot" accenting that flattens dynamics.

**Duration**: 10 minutes

## Practice Session Structure

A well-structured 30-minute practice session:

```
  5 min  — Warm-up (scales, long tones, technique exercises)
  3 min  — Review of previous session's weak spot
 12 min  — Targeted work on current weakness (from Music Practice Buddy)
  8 min  — Repertoire practice (piece you're learning)
  2 min  — Cool-down (easy playing, relaxation)
```

## The Progress Spiral

Effective practice follows a spiral:

1. **Assess** — Record and analyze
2. **Identify** — Find the weakest area
3. **Target** — Apply specific exercise for that weakness
4. **Practice** — Focused work for 10–15 minutes
5. **Re-assess** — Record again, measure improvement
6. **Adjust** — If improved, move to next weakness. If not, try a different exercise.

Music Practice Buddy automates steps 1, 2, and 5, letting you focus on the actual practice.
