# Insurance Claim Compass — Reference

## The problem in numbers
- Burst pipes, kitchen fires, and break-ins produce hundreds of thousands
  of residential claims per year in the US alone; the median contents
  claim is a few thousand dollars, and payout variance on comparable
  losses is driven overwhelmingly by documentation quality.
- Adjusters handle 100+ claims at once. Yours is a file. Files with
  photos, receipts, and a written notice move faster and settle higher
  than phone calls and memory.
- Most policyholders never appeal a low offer. Of those who do (with an
  itemized demand + independent estimate), a large share see increases —
  the first offer prices in your expected passivity.

## Loss-type playbooks (extended)

### Water
Failure modes: supply-line burst (washer/fridge), drain backup (verify
backup coverage!), slow leaks (often excluded as seepage), appliance
hose pop. Critical: the failed part IS the evidence — keep the burst
hose. Secondary damage (mold) has its own coverage limits and timing
rules; report promptly because late-reported mold is a classic denial.
If the source is a drain/sewer backup, say so accurately — a
mischaracterized cause is treated as misrepresentation.

### Theft
Police report number first — claims without it stall. Serial numbers
from boxes, accounts (Find My, Google account device lists), or old
photos. Card statements substitute for lost receipts. Jewelry/cash have
special sub-limits (often $200 cash, $1500 jewelry unless scheduled) —
the tool's inventory flags high-value items needing an appraisal.

### Wind/hail
Hail bruises asphalt shingles (look for dimples + granules in gutters).
Get the exact storm date from NOAA/NWS records; insurers check it
against radar. Roof age matters: adjusters depreciate roofs heavily;
have your closing documents or prior-roof invoice ready. Tarping is
covered emergency mitigation — keep receipts.

### Fire
Total-loss inventory is the hard part: rebuild from memory + old photos
of rooms + card statements + online accounts (Amazon order history is
an accepted inventory source). Loss-of-use (ALE) covers lodging/meals —
track every receipt. Smoke odor remediation is claimable even where
structure looks fine.

### Auto
Never admit fault at scene (liability determination is the insurers'
job). Photos before vehicles move, if safe. Injury symptoms can appear
days later — see a doctor when they do, and note the date. Rental
coverage limits are per-day; check before picking a large vehicle.

### Liability (guest injury)
Same-day notice matters most here: liability defense duty can be
impaired by late notice. No statements about fault. Photograph the
hazard before fixing it; the "after" state proves nothing.

## ACV vs RCV — worked examples
Policy with RCV endorsement, furniture class (10-yr life):
- Sofa $2000, age 4 → retained 40% → ACV $1200; holdback $800 recovered
  after replacement receipts.
- TV $1200 (electronics, 5-yr), age 5 → 100% depreciated, salvage floor
  applies → ACV $120, holdback $1080. Electronics depreciate brutally —
  this is where claimants leave the most money.

Depreciation is negotiable: request the adjuster's itemized depreciation
sheet; "functional age" arguments (a well-maintained 8-year-old fridge)
move numbers.

## Appeal ladder in depth
1. **Itemization demand.** Insurers must explain deductions; unexplained
   line items frequently dissolve. 10-business-day deadline in writing.
2. **Independent estimate.** A contractor's written estimate on
   letterhead beats your spreadsheet. Reconcile scope differences —
   maybe the adjuster missed a room.
3. **Appraisal clause.** In most US property policies: each side
   appoints an appraiser, the two pick an umpire, agreement of any two
   binds the AMOUNT (not coverage). Costs split. Right tool when the
   dispute is valuation, not coverage.
4. **DOI complaint.** Free, creates a tracked record, assigned to a
   consumer analyst, insurer must respond (commonly within 2-4 weeks).
   Insurers resolution-rate on DOI complaints is very high.
5. **Public adjuster / attorney.** PA fee typically ~10% of recovery;
   worth it on five-figure losses, not on $800 disputes.

## Denial reasons and counters
| Denial reason | Counter |
|---|---|
| "Gradual leak / wear" | Expert letter re sudden & accidental; failed-part photos |
| "No proof of ownership" | Card statements, photos of item in home, account history |
| "Late notice" | Show first written notice date; prejudice required in many states |
| "Flood exclusion" | Verify source characterization (backup rider vs surface flood) |
| "Betterment" deduction | Argue like-for-like replacement, not upgrade |

## Deadlines to verify locally
- Proof-of-loss deadlines (often 60 days–1 yr; policy-specific)
- Suit-limitation clauses (commonly 1–2 yr from loss)
- State DOI complaint process (US) / national insurance regulator (EU: e.g.
  national complaints bodies; UK: Financial Ombudsman Service)
The tool prints "verify" flags — these rights are real but dates vary.

## Safety note
Life safety first, always. In active fire/flood/injury situations the
first calls are emergency services; claim mechanics start after.

## Not legal advice
This skill structures documentation and negotiation. Policies and laws
differ; read your declarations page; consult professionals for large
disputes.
