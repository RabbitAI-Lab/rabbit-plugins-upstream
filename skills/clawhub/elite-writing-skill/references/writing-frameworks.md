# Writing Frameworks Library

> Proven structural frameworks from world-class writers, copywriters, and content strategists.

---

## 1. AIDA (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action) 🎯

**Best for**: Sales pages, ads, landing pages, email marketing

**Structure:**
```
ATTENTION — Hook the reader immediately
  → Bold statement, surprising fact, provocative question

INTEREST — Build curiosity and relevance
  → Expand on the hook. Why should they care?
  → Connect to their pain point or aspiration

DESIRE — Make them WANT the solution
  → Benefits, not features
  → Paint the picture of the transformed state
  → Social proof, testimonials, case studies

ACTION — Tell them exactly what to do
  → One clear CTA
  → Remove friction
  → Add urgency/scarcity if appropriate
```

**Example (Landing Page):**
> **Attention**: "Your website is leaking money."
> **Interest**: "Every day, visitors leave your site without buying — not because they don't want your product, but because your copy doesn't convert."
> **Desire**: "Imagine waking up to sales notifications. Your copy working 24/7. Your revenue growing while you sleep. That's what happens when every word is engineered to convert."
> **Action**: "Get your conversion audit now — free for the next 48 hours."

## 2. PAS (Problem, Agitate, Solution) 🔥

**Best for**: Pain-point marketing, problem-aware audiences, email sequences

**Structure:**
```
PROBLEM — Identify the pain point vividly
  → "You know that feeling when..."
  → Make it specific and relatable

AGITATE — Amplify the pain
  → What happens if they don't fix this?
  → Emotional and logical consequences
  → "And it gets worse..."

SOLUTION — Present your offering as the relief
  → Not just features — transformation
  → How it specifically solves the agitated problem
  → Proof that it works
```

**Example (Email):**
> **Problem**: "Writing content that converts feels impossible."
> **Agitate**: "You spend hours crafting posts that get 3 likes. Your competitors publish mediocre content and get hundreds of shares. Every blank screen is revenue lost. And the worst part? You KNOW your product is better — you just can't communicate it."
> **Solution**: "The Elite Writing Skill changes everything. In 30 days, you'll write copy that converts at 3x your current rate. Here's how..."

## 3. The 4 P's (Picture, Promise, Prove, Push) 🖼️

**Best for**: Long-form sales pages, product launches, webinars

**Structure:**
```
PICTURE — Paint the dream scenario
  → Where they want to be
  → Sensory details, emotional payoff

PROMISE — Make a bold, specific claim
  → "In 30 days, you'll..."
  → Specific, measurable, time-bound

PROVE — Back it up with evidence
  → Case studies, testimonials, data
  → Before/after comparisons
  → Expert endorsements

PUSH — Create urgency to act
  → Limited time, limited spots, price increase
  → Risk reversal (guarantee)
  → Clear CTA with reason to act NOW
```

## 4. StoryBrand Framework 📖

**Best for**: Brand storytelling, about pages, brand messaging

**Structure:**
```
1. HERO — The customer (NOT the brand)
2. PROBLEM — External, internal, and philosophical problems
3. GUIDE — The brand as the mentor (empathy + authority)
4. PLAN — The process to solve the problem
5. CALL TO ACTION — Direct and transitional CTAs
6. SUCCESS — What life looks like after
7. FAILURE — What happens if they don't act
```

**Key Insight**: The customer is Luke Skywalker. Your brand is Yoda.

## 5. SCQA (Situation, Complication, Question, Answer) 📊

**Best for**: Business proposals, consulting reports, executive summaries

**Structure:**
```
SITUATION — Establish context everyone agrees on
  → "As you know, our Q3 revenue grew 15%..."

COMPLICATION — Introduce the problem or opportunity
  → "However, customer acquisition costs have doubled..."

QUESTION — Frame the strategic question
  → "How can we maintain growth while reducing CAC?"

ANSWER — Present your recommendation
  → Clear, actionable, supported by analysis
```

## 6. BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front) 📌

**Best for**: Business emails, military/technical communication, executive briefs

**Structure:**
```
BOTTOM LINE — The main point in the first sentence
  → "We need to delay the launch by 2 weeks."

BACKGROUND — Context (brief)
  → "The reason is..."

DETAILS — Supporting information
  → "Specifically..."

ACTION REQUIRED — What they need to do
  → "Please approve the revised timeline by EOD."
```

**Rule**: The reader should know the main point after reading the first sentence.

## 7. The Feature-Benefit-Outcome-Emotion Ladder 🪜

**Best for**: Product descriptions, SaaS copy, e-commerce

**Structure:**
```
FEATURE → What it is
  → "Our platform has real-time analytics"

BENEFIT → What it does for them
  → "So you can see exactly how users interact with your product"

OUTCOME → What they achieve
  → "Which means you can optimize features that drive revenue"

EMOTION → How they feel
  → "Finally, making data-driven decisions without the guesswork"
```

**Always climb to emotion. Features don't sell. Feelings do.**

## 8. The Hero's Journey (Monomyth) ⚔️

**Best for**: Brand origin stories, case studies, keynote speeches, fiction

**Structure:**
```
1. ORDINARY WORLD — Status quo
2. CALL TO ADVENTURE — The challenge/opportunity
3. REFUSAL — Hesitation, fear
4. MEETING THE MENTOR — Guidance appears
5. CROSSING THE THRESHOLD — Commitment
6. TESTS, ALLIES, ENEMIES — The struggle
7. APPROACH TO THE INMOST CAVE — Facing the biggest challenge
8. ORDEAL — The crisis moment
9. REWARD — The breakthrough
10. THE ROAD BACK — Integration
11. RESURRECTION — Final test
12. RETURN WITH THE ELIXIR — Transformation complete
```

## 9. The Inverted Pyramid 📰

**Best for**: News articles, press releases, blog posts, reports

**Structure:**
```
TOP (Most Important):
  → Who, What, When, Where, Why
  → If reader stops here, they have the essentials

MIDDLE (Important Details):
  → Background, context, quotes
  → Supporting evidence

BOTTOM (Least Important):
  → Additional resources
  → Related information
  → Contact details
```

---

## 10. The Before-After-Bridge 🌉

**Best for**: Case studies, testimonials, transformation stories

**Structure:**
```
BEFORE — The painful present
  → "Sarah was working 80-hour weeks and barely breaking even..."

AFTER — The transformed future
  → "Now she runs a 7-figure business working 30 hours a week..."

BRIDGE — How they got there
  → "Here's exactly what changed..."
```

## 11. The PASTOR Framework ⛪

**Best for**: Long-form sales letters, video sales letters

**Structure:**
```
P — PERSON, PROBLEM, PAIN
  → Identify with the reader's struggle

A — AMPLIFY
  → Make the problem feel urgent and costly

S — STORY, STRUGGLE, SOLUTION
  → Share a transformation story

T — TESTIMONY, TRANSFORMATION
  → Social proof and results

O — OFFER
  → Present the solution clearly

R — RESPONSE
  → Call to action with urgency
```

---

## 12. The 5-Point Email Structure ✉️

**Best for**: Cold emails, sales emails, newsletters

**Structure:**
```
1. SUBJECT LINE — Curiosity or benefit-driven
2. OPENING LINE — Personal, relevant, NOT "I hope this finds you well"
3. VALUE PROPOSITION — What's in it for them?
4. PROOF/SOCIAL PROOF — Brief credibility builder
5. CTA — One specific, low-friction ask
```
