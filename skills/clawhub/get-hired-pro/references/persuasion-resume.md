## Applying the research to resumes

Reuse the general argument principles below for resume positioning, evidence selection, and explanation. This source-linked synthesis was developed for a cover-letter revision. Its historical examples and company-first letter sequence document that case; they do not require a company-motivation paragraph in a resume, a particular employer first, or a fixed section or bullet formula. Keep each company's current facts in the shared private dossier.

- Choose the role-relevant organizing idea before the headline, section order, or most quotable achievement. A profile should explain the contribution before presenting technical details or metrics.
- Show the full relevant capability through complementary evidence. Ownership, decisions, people development, operational delivery, and hands-on building can establish different reasons to interview; the target role determines which matter.
- Use numbers to make relevant scale or results clearer. Keep strong metrics when they help, but do not let one measurable result shrink the account of a substantial role. Specific unquantified work can carry equal or greater relevance.
- Give each section a useful job. An optional impact section should surface evidence rather than repeat the summary and employment bullets. Remove it when direct experience makes the argument better.
- Check what stands out on the finished page. Follow the applicant's chosen structure, including functional or hybrid arrangements. In a reverse-chronological work history, keep the roles in their true order. Preserve dates and role attribution in every format, and improve page flow so a forced break does not hide a central role. Clearly attributed highlights can surface older evidence when needed.

The principles are editorial applications informed by general persuasion and selection research.
