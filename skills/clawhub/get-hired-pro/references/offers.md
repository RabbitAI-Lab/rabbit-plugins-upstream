# Research pay and negotiate the offer

Use the applicant's target work, market, experience and priorities. Research a salary target before applications; use the same findings when an offer arrives. A direct salary or offer request can start here. Reuse known answers and ask only for a missing detail that changes the recommendation.

## Set a salary target

Once the target role and market are known, research current pay for comparable work. Use the current verified date, employer pay disclosures and credible salary data for that country or region. Match the duties, level, industry, location or remote-pay policy, and employee or contractor arrangement. Read the underlying sources rather than treating search snippets or a model's recalled salary as a benchmark.

Put the comparable pay range, recommended target, short reason and dated source links in the reply. Keep detailed comparisons in the research record. The Claw finds and interprets the data; the applicant supplies their goals and experience. If sources disagree, explain the relevant difference and choose the evidence that fits this work. Stop when the evidence supports a useful recommendation; no source quota or repeated search is needed.

State the currency and whether pay is hourly, monthly or annual. Separate base pay from bonuses, equity and other benefits. Show any conversion assumptions. Record both the period the data covers and the date checked.

Base the recommendation on comparable work, relevant experience and stated priorities. Keep the recommended target, employer's posted range and actual offer clear.

Save the sources, comparisons and recommendation in the user's existing search/research records. Reuse them for comparable roles and later offer discussions. Refresh when the role, market or material pay evidence changes.

If live research is unavailable or suitable figures cannot be found, say what is missing once and continue useful work.

## Compare an offer

Start from the actual terms and the applicant's goals. Ask for missing terms that affect the decision or response. Reuse the pay research and update material differences.

## Compare what matters

Confirm the role, location, currency, response deadline and the terms actually supplied. Compare base pay, guaranteed and variable compensation, benefits, working hours, travel/commute, flexibility, responsibilities and development opportunities where relevant.

Distinguish recurring pay from one-time payments and guaranteed compensation from conditional bonuses or equity. Keep currencies and periods comparable. Explain the meaningful tradeoffs using the applicant's priorities instead of imposing a salary-only ranking.

Compare the offer with the researched target and relevant current market evidence. Retain source, date, currency and geography, distinguishing the employer's posted range, the actual offer and a market estimate. Unavailable market data does not prevent useful discussion of the supplied offer.

State the recommendation directly, then identify unresolved terms that could change it. Keep calculations focused on the decision.

## Make a useful request

Use the applicant's priorities and actual alternatives to recommend a negotiation approach.

Choose an ambitious opening request supported by the role, the applicant's contribution and comparable pay. Explain the recommended number to the applicant. Do not calculate it by automatically adding a percentage to the offer or splitting the difference. A strong posted range can change an earlier target; a low initial offer does not define the applicant's value.

When an employer asks for expectations early, use known market evidence and available budget information. If the range or responsibilities are unclear, ask about them before anchoring unnecessarily low. If an answer is required, prepare a supported figure or range for the actual field.

If proposing a range, make both endpoints acceptable as an opening proposal. A modest range from a defensible ask upward can be useful when supported.

Identify the terms worth negotiating together. If salary is genuinely fixed, examine useful flexibility in signing pay, leave, hours, remote work, start date or a documented review, following the applicant's priorities. Keep recurring compensation distinct from one-time concessions. Consider alternative packages only when the applicant would actually value them comparably.

Draft the selected next response in direct professional language suited to this employer and relationship. Express genuine interest, make the specific request and connect it briefly to relevant work or market evidence. Add acceptance, decline or alternative responses only when requested.

Follow the applicant's voice and confident-advocacy instructions. When an important term is missing, prepare the question needed to clarify it. Show what information would change the recommendation.

Prepare the response for the chosen next step. Record the deadline, employer reply and next action.

## Respond to the employer

Use the actual reply to update the comparison and recommend the next move. Identify what changed and what remains unresolved. Avoid automatic concessions or repeated requests after a clear final position. Assess whether another request advances the applicant's priorities enough to justify its effect on the decision, relationship and deadline.

When useful, prepare a concise reply to a refusal, revised package or request for justification. Treat a promised future review as a review, not a guaranteed raise. Before acceptance, compare the written terms with what was discussed and flag an important difference.

## Save the decision context

Keep the offer, material terms, response drafts and the applicant's priorities in their own project. Update the application history from actual events.
