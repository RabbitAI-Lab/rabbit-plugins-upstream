# Find the work the person wants to do

Start with the roles or kinds of work the person wants. Reuse their résumé, full career reference, skills matrix and saved preferences. Ask for their target only when it is missing. Compare the actual duties with their strengths and transferable experience before choosing search terms.

Find jobs first, then use the five-round application workflow for selected openings. A user who already has a posting can skip discovery. One Claw can do both workflows; additional bots are optional.

## Establish the target and practical preferences

Read the existing answers first. Ask concise questions about missing preferences that would materially change the search: location, where they can work, remote/hybrid/on-site arrangements, employment type, compensation preferences, travel, role interests and explicit exclusions. Ask which are nonnegotiable and which are flexible; do not turn a preference into a hard stop yourself. Do not import another applicant's requirements or make the user repeat settled answers.

If their target and recorded experience do not connect, explain the concrete mismatch and ask about relevant work they may have left out. Offer better-fitting roles alongside their chosen direction. Save what they accept or reject. Keep searching their chosen direction when they choose it; do not silently substitute a different career. A missing title match or career change is not proof of a mismatch.

Agree the useful initial batch or search scope before prolonged work. An exploratory user can start with broad role suggestions and then choose a direction. If they have no résumé/reference, use [guided discovery](skills-matrix.md) to establish enough experience to search; do not require an exhaustive career inventory first. Suggest missing related capabilities for the user to accept/correct/reject and save those answers before treating suggestions as established facts.

## Research pay before choosing applications

Once the target work and market are known, use [pay research](offers.md#set-a-salary-target) to find comparable pay and recommend a salary target. Reuse valid findings from existing records. Save the researched range and recommendation with the search records. Continue the shortlist with available information when a source is unavailable.

## Search from capabilities and the work

Within the agreed direction, identify suitable types of roles from the full career record: responsibilities owned, practical skills/tools, independent work, leadership, delivery, writing, research, commercial work and other accepted capabilities. Include adjacent titles and industries when the actual work fits. Do not let an impressive but unrelated accomplishment dominate the search, and do not overlook substantial implementation or independent work because it lacks a familiar employer/title.

Use existing web search, fetch and browser tools to find real current openings. Search with relevant activities, deliverables and tools as well as title variants. Read the actual posting and relevant public company material; a search snippet or taxonomy suggestion is not a reviewed vacancy. Prefer the employer's careers page or its linked ATS listing. Aggregators can identify leads, but follow through to the original when available. Career and skills directories can suggest useful search terms.

For each promising opening, establish what the employer does, what this hire would spend time doing, and the useful contribution the person could make. Use the JD's substantial clues and relevant company context. Save source URLs, date checked, requisition ID and posting date when available. Check the posting's current status and remote/location terms rather than assuming a search result is still open or globally remote. Mark inaccessible or unresolved details plainly.

## Judge relevance without shrinking the person

Read the duties against the person's full reference, not just a keyword overlap. Explain the useful work they could own and identify the closest relevant experience supporting it. Where they have automation capability, consider how it improves that role's work. Do not assign automation experience to a person who has not supplied or accepted it.

Honor the user's explicit nonnegotiable filters. For a supplied posting that conflicts with one, ask whether to proceed before investing in preparation; the posting alone does not override their saved requirement. A title, seniority label, salary level, stated years, degree wishlist, missing exact tool or unfamiliar industry is not by itself a reason to discard a role. Assess the actual work and meaningful transfer from what the person has done. Explain practical conditions that need a decision while keeping the focus on what the applicant can do. Do not add percentage-match thresholds, a claim-verification stage or a new eligibility judge.

Rank the opportunities with brief reasons. Prefer roles where the applicant's useful contribution is easy to explain through relevant experience; unfamiliar titles may still be strong opportunities. Keep sensible exploratory opportunities visible when they fit the user's goals. Missing salary or an inaccessible detail can be reported without inventing certainty or blocking all progress.

## Return a useful, reusable shortlist

Show the user:

| Information | What to include |
|---|---|
| Employer and role | Actual title, company and requisition ID when available |
| Work and company context | What the person would do and the relevant work/problems the employer needs help with |
| Why this person | What they could contribute and brief closely relevant experience from their reference |
| Practical terms | Stated location/remote scope, employment type, pay/currency and other user-important terms; identify unknowns plainly |
| Original listing | Direct employer/ATS URL, date checked and observed status |
| Next step | Recommended priority and any concrete question the user needs answered |

Save the shortlist, useful search directions, posting text and applicable user preferences in the user's project directory. Reuse existing job lists/trackers when suitable. Deduplicate by employer/requisition/original URL; respect the user's existing application-history preferences. Update changed findings incrementally and retain useful prior work.

When the user chooses a role or asks to prepare a batch, pass the job description, source URLs and company material into round 1. That round researches the selected employer's work without reverse-engineering it around the candidate. Rounds 2–5 then use the full current career reference and feedback. Reuse applicable collected sources, but do not copy one employer's argument into another application.

Deliver the agreed shortlist and stop at the agreed scope. If live search is unavailable, say so and offer to evaluate supplied postings.
