# LinkedIn and recruiter outreach

Use the applicant's career goals, full experience, voice and current preferences. Start from supplied profile text or existing records. Ask for only the missing sections or target information needed.

## Research what recruiters in this field are looking for

Before drafting LinkedIn copy, research the titles, skills and experience employers seek in the applicant's field and location. Reuse relevant current employer research and readable job postings; find what is missing with the existing search and website-reading tools. Consult current first-party LinkedIn guidance when a platform-specific choice needs it. The Claw collects the examples.

Look for the role titles, skills, responsibilities and accomplishments that employers in this field seek. Compare that language with the applicant's full career and chosen direction. Use recognizable terms where their existing experience supports them, with readable descriptions of the actual work.

Save a short account of the useful terms and dated sources alongside existing career/profile records. Research only what can improve the requested copy, then draft it. Reuse the findings until the target or material hiring language changes. For a bio request, deliver the bio.

Keep the chosen industry/role scope broad enough for the applicant's goals rather than copying one vacancy's wording. Preserve their voice and disclosure preferences.

If live research is unavailable, explain that briefly outside the bio and use the available career details. Deliver the useful copy instead of assigning research to the applicant.

## Make the profile support their direction

Revise the requested headline, About, experience, skills or other sections. Make relevant capabilities and accomplishments understandable to the intended audience. Keep the applicant's preferred chronology, disclosures and distinctive voice.

A public profile may need to serve several target roles. Follow that chosen scope rather than rebuilding it around one opening. Use relevant terms naturally; do not stuff keywords or copy an entire cover letter into the profile.

Provide complete replacement sections ready to paste.

## Write a message worth receiving

Apply the saved persuasion and hiring-behavior research required by SKILL.md, reusing it when already in context. A direct recruiter-message request uses this method without running the full application workflow. Choose the relevant contribution before wording the introduction. Give the recipient a specific reason to consider the applicant and support it with the most useful experience for this message. Adapt the detail to the channel; keep the language natural rather than compressing a cover-letter template.

Use the same governing hiring argument and current career/preferences. Where the applicant has automation capability, show its relevance through the work they would help improve and the strongest concise support. Choose the message's immediate purpose: introduce a relevant candidate, clarify a condition that determines whether to proceed, or follow up. End with a clear next step suited to that purpose. Preserve real applicant requirements; do not automatically append a separate eligibility question to an introduction or suppress it when it is the reason for contacting the recruiter. Stop once the evidence and next step are clear; avoid another generic fit summary.

When the user wants to message a recruiter, research the recruiter or agency, employer and relevant openings using the available web tools. Reuse current employer and industry research. For a general introduction, focus on the applicant's chosen field and target roles.

Use the findings to explain why the applicant is contacting this recruiter and which relevant experience to mention. Draft the requested LinkedIn message or email with a clear next step, such as discussing a named opening or suitable roles.

Identify the recipient, purpose, relationship and relevant role from the available material. Give a specific reason for contacting them and a useful next step. Present the applicant's relevant contribution directly.

Match the message to its channel and available length. Give the applicant finished text with any genuinely missing detail identified separately, rather than a generic fill-in script. Preserve their chosen contact and disclosure preferences.

Reuse contact history before suggesting another message. Respect replies, rejected approaches, holds and prior follow-ups. For LinkedIn, return ready-to-send text for manual use. For another supported channel, follow [application management](application-management.md) to send and track the requested message.

Save useful profile wording, preferences and contact outcomes in the user's project.
