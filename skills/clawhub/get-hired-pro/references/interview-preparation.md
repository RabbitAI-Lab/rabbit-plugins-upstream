# Prepare for the interview

Use the role, current employer research, full career reference, accepted feedback and available interview details. Ask for missing information only when it changes preparation. An existing interview can start here.

## Prepare the right material

Identify the work and judgment the interview is likely to examine. Use the job description, interview format and relevant company activity. Separate confirmed interview details from likely topics.

Tailor practice to the round: recruiter screen, behavioral interview, technical discussion, case or presentation. Select the topics most likely to matter within the available time. Reuse employer research and refresh facts that could change an answer or question; avoid an exhaustive company dossier.

Connect the important topics with the applicant's strongest relevant experience. Develop concise talking points: what happened, what they did, the useful result and why it matters here. Use situation/action/result organization when it helps; let the applicant speak in their own words. Follow their voice and the skill's confident-advocacy instructions.

Where an answer needs detail, ask about the specific experience instead of inventing it. Help prepare thoughtful questions about the work, team and expectations. Update material company facts with current primary sources and retain links in the preparation notes.

## Practice

For practice, ask a realistic question and wait for the answer. Give specific feedback on relevance, clarity and the strongest improvement. Let the response influence the next question. Avoid constant interruption: use brief feedback between answers for coaching, or save it for the agreed break in a mock interview. Follow the applicant's preferred mode.

Tie feedback to something the applicant actually said. Identify what worked and the most useful change, such as explaining their action, showing the result or answering the question more directly. When useful, invite another attempt at that answer before moving on. Ask follow-ups that clarify the answer or examine a relevant judgment; do not march through a fixed question list regardless of the response.

Let the applicant try before supplying a polished answer. Help them develop flexible talking points in their own voice; avoid scripts to memorize or forcing every answer into STAR.

Keep the practice suited to the role and their available time. Include questions about the work itself, alongside relevant behavioral questions.

## Keep what helps

Save useful examples, applicant corrections, recurring preparation needs and the next useful practice step. Finish with material they can use.

After a real interview, use the applicant's account of questions and responses to refine the next preparation. Keep recalled questions distinct from confirmed future interview topics. Update the existing application record with the actual round and next step.
