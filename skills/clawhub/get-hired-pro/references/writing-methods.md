# Drafting and review methods

The résumé and letter exist to persuade the employer that the applicant can solve the hiring problem. Make the contribution recognizable early. Organize the letter around the researched work the applicant can help accomplish; use brief closely relevant accomplishments to support their offer. Keep the résumé in normal accomplishment-led form with role-relevant emphasis. Explain relevant mechanisms and outcomes when they strengthen that case. Use the employer's actual professional language; avoid keyword stuffing and vague superlatives.

Use the short internal governing hiring argument chosen during selection. Make the opening's contribution tangible through relevant experience while expressing genuine professional interest in the applicant's preferred manner. Develop the case with complementary evidence and useful judgment; let the résumé establish the wider support. For applicants with automation capability, explain what their implementation enables and why it helps with this employer's work. Read the full career and applicable preferences when selecting and reviewing. Replace weaker selections or passages with stronger available evidence in the current correction pass. Retain explanations the reader needs and remove conclusions that merely repeat an established connection.

Read and apply these saved findings before drafting the relevant documents, reusing current material already in context:
- [Cover-letter persuasion research](persuasion-cover-letter.md).
- [Hiring-behavior research](hiring-behavior.md).
- [Résumé persuasion research](persuasion-resume.md), when writing a résumé.

When invoking an isolated task, supply the applicable research TEXT, not paths that it cannot read. Use these findings as editorial judgment: address the reader's priorities; state meaningful capability explicitly and show it in context; select complementary strong reasons; cut filler that dilutes the case; express genuine professional interest. Give the opening a useful persuasive job in this argument. Historical employer examples are not templates or current candidate restrictions.

Apply tailoring, strong relevant assets and transferable skills, accomplishment-led descriptions, complementary letter reasoning, clear organization and consistent formatting. Reuse this guidance until a material question needs new research. Do not impose student-specific templates, fixed bullet/example counts or a universal page limit. Preserve the applicant's existing requested presentation preferences.

Psychology findings applied here:
- Bright & Hutton (2000), https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-2389.00132: explicit capability statements improved manager ratings in a résumé study. Make capability and its relevance visible instead of leaving a reader to infer it from a project name.
- Obermaier & Koch (2024), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11436817/: weak arguments diluted strong arguments in persuasion experiments. Cut low-value filler; do not equate a strong achievement with the easiest claim to defend.
- Woolley & Fishbach (2018), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.obhdp.2018.06.004: recruitment studies found candidates undervalued expressed intrinsic motivation. Explain genuine professional interest without inventing passion or biography.

Round 4 performs one combined review of the entire packet. First consider whether the contribution is compelling from the hiring manager's viewpoint; then inspect passages and presentation. Use clear actions, understandable clauses and natural professional rhythm, while preserving professional intelligence and technical precision. Apply the FULL existing Remove AI Slop guide at references/remove-ai-slop.md, supplying its text to isolated calls. The applicant's explicit anti-hedging and positive-advocacy brief governs any generic advice to preserve qualifications. Do not erase useful expertise, flatten strong accomplishments or create a bland duties list to simplify language. Return one concrete correction set; the writer corrects in round 5. No extra reviewers or separate style calls.
