# Research the employer and show how the applicant can help

Produce a finished application that gives the employer a clear reason to hire this applicant. Use the career record to understand the person. Research what the employer needs this hire to accomplish, then connect those needs with the applicant's relevant experience.

## Round 1: OSINT on the job and employer

Use the available search, fetch and browser tools in this round. When an isolated worker lacks browsing, collect readable source material before asking it to write the brief. A URL, search snippet or an old company summary is not evidence that a page was read. Reuse still-applicable research; update material facts rather than recrawling everything.

Find and retain in the research brief:
- The actual role, employer/business unit, location/remote terms and employment type.
- What it pays: the applicable posted salary/range and currency, with location/date context; distinguish any researched market estimate from the employer's range. Record unavailable pay plainly rather than delaying writing indefinitely. Pay research informs the applicant, not a salary paragraph in the cover letter.
- What the day-to-day work looks like: tasks, deliverables, people served, collaborators, decisions, tools and workflow where discoverable. Translate JD language into a concrete understanding of the work rather than merely repeating it.
- What the company and relevant team are building, changing or delivering: current projects, products, initiatives and direction that bear on this job. Use current primary pages, documentation, announcements and reports where available. Do not assume that a project elsewhere in the company belongs to this role.
- The specific needs this hire should address and what success would look like. Read the responsibilities, repeated concerns and handoffs in the JD for clues as well as using public research. Look for ongoing work as well as problems to solve.

Keep source URLs, dates and whether a conclusion is stated or inferred in the private brief. These distinctions concern employer research, not a new verification of the applicant's career. If OSINT is sparse or a source is inaccessible, use the job description and note the gap in the research brief. Continue with a useful understanding of the job. Do not put research caveats, research notes or speculative diagnoses into the letter.

For a supplied posting, collect or reuse [comparable pay research](offers.md#set-a-salary-target) during this round and give the applicant a recommended target before application submission. No discovery or extra writing round is required. Save the target with the applicant's research; the employer brief still describes the actual job independently of the applicant.

Save a concise employer brief covering role, pay, day-to-day work, relevant projects, needs and dated sources. Record unavailable information privately. Research the employer's needs independently of the applicant's accomplishments.

## Round 2: Choose how the applicant can help

For each important researched need worth addressing, ask in this order: What needs doing? How can the applicant help accomplish it? What would they do and bring to the table? Which of their closely relevant, similar work best supports that offer?

Read their full supplied career, capabilities and projects. Choose individual accomplishments, not a bundle of everything they did at a selected employer. Compare a proposed example with stronger relevant alternatives in the full record: what important capability does each establish, and what useful evidence does it add? Replace peripheral detail when stronger available work better supports the hiring argument. Keep complementary strengths; relevance is not a quota for employers, examples or metrics. Similar work across industries counts: select it because the activities, judgment or deliverables help with this job. A vague category such as problem-solving, scale, automation or removing constraints is not enough to make an otherwise unrelated accomplishment relevant. Recruitment belongs when the employer's work calls for it; an unrelated recruitment success does not belong simply because it removed a bottleneck somewhere else.

Write a short internal governing hiring argument connecting the employer's important work, the applicant's useful contribution, professional interest and supporting experience. Specify the work they would help do and its value before selecting the historical proof. For applicants with automation capability, identify the actual recurring work, information flows, decisions or handoffs they could improve and the relevant implementation supporting that offer. Select technical detail for the capability it demonstrates and the work it enables. Develop an available decision, constraint, action or outcome when it explains judgment better than a duty or tool list. Keep support concise enough to serve the offer. No compulsory supporting-example employer, project, metric, example count, alternative-argument contest, claim audit or omission ledger. Apply the current applicant's presentation preferences. Preserve substantial technical, leadership, creative and commercial expertise whenever it helps with this employer's actual work.

Résumé selection is a separate part of this same round, not another model call. Keep a concise role-by-role résumé plan: researched needs supported, selected relevant individual accomplishments, desired emphasis, and additional proof that complements the letter. Include the applicant's preferred core recent experience, including substantive current independent work. Preserve the full career reference for drafting; this plan guides attention rather than discarding sources. Keep it concise, with no omission ledger or fixed bullet quota.

## Round 3: Write the application

Write a persuasive, individual letter that connects the applicant’s experience with the employer’s actual work. Use what the research and career record establish to give the reader specific reasons to consider this person.

Establish a supported reason to consider this applicant while expressing genuine professional interest naturally. Make the capability tangible through relevant work, a decision or a demonstrated combination of strengths, rather than spending the introduction explaining the role. Follow a motive-first preference when requested; no particular opening sentence, employer name or number is required. Research identifies what matters; company praise cannot establish the applicant's motive. Develop the case through complementary evidence and useful judgment. Each paragraph should answer the reader's next useful question, with enough context to understand the contribution. Keep the internal selection logic out of the prose as a recited script.

Do not lead each body paragraph with an employer name and recount the job. Do not pad the offer with an operational history, tool inventory, industry jargon or unrelated impressive numbers. Do not attach an employer-benefit sentence to the end of unrelated experience and call that tailoring. Explain what the applicant would bring; do not merely paraphrase the JD as promises or write an unsolicited consulting plan. End naturally with an appropriate next step.

The résumé addresses the same needs through relevant accomplishments and skills in a standard résumé format. Keep normal career attribution and chronology; give space to the parts of each role that help this employer see them doing the work. An employer's presence does not require including all its achievements.

Apply the saved persuasion research, current résumé/cover-letter guidance and the applicant's voice/preferences. Present their legitimate work impressively and confidently. Their supplied facts are authoritative. Explicitly reject hedging, minimization, defensive credit accounting, gap apologies and career-transition disclaimers. Keep employer-research uncertainty in the private brief. Do not invent candidate facts or present an unconfirmed company project as the role's assignment. Use relevant publications with the applicant's preferred standard identifiers and links, without automatically adding publication-status qualifiers. Do not automatically insert career length or article volume. Do not litigate ordinary wording the user has explicitly accepted.

Describe the useful work the applicant implemented. Do not automatically carry product-development status qualifiers from private project notes into employer-facing accomplishments. A brief relevant example supports the contribution under discussion. When it has made the connection clear, end the point rather than appending another abstract assertion of fit. Explain an implication when the reader needs it; remove a restatement that adds nothing.

## Round 4: Review the application once

Read the researched job and the actual résumé/letter as the hiring manager. Does the letter show interest in our work, understand what we need, explain how the applicant can help, and give concise closely relevant support? Would the experience make me more confident they can do this work? Identify useful contributions the letter misses, weak connections and irrelevant career history. Do not rescue an unclear letter with facts that only appear in the private record. Use the full career to find stronger relevant support when needed.

Assess the opening's persuasive job: what specific reason does it give this employer to consider this person, and does the evidence develop that case? Revise an opening that merely recaps the vacancy, announces interest or lists credentials. Apply the saved persuasion findings in this same review, preserving flexible prose and the applicant's voice.

Assess the résumé independently against the researched work and full career. Compare weak selected material with stronger available evidence, while preserving the applicant's structure and current-work prominence. Can the reader understand what technical work enables and what the applicant decided or accomplished? In the letter, distinguish a necessary explanation of relevance from a repeated conclusion after that relevance is clear. For a material weakness, identify the affected passage and supply a coherent replacement or deletion that improves the case. Correct actual selections and prose; coverage of the right topics or a review-status note is insufficient. Check suggested corrections against current applicant preferences before applying them.

Review this logic before polishing prose. Then cover hedging/minimization, clarity and natural professional rhythm, AI slop and formatting in the same call. Preserve the confidence and substance of the applicant's work. Return one actionable correction set to the writer; delete or replace irrelevant material rather than decorating it with a benefit sentence. No new claim-verification task, hiring score, judge or routine second review.

When several edits affect the same passage, give one coherent replacement passage or an unambiguous deletion/replacement instruction. Do not leave overlapping edits for the writer to stack into repetitive prose.

## Round 5: Correct and deliver

Apply the corrections while keeping the connections between employer needs, the applicant's contribution and relevant experience clear. Deliver the finished résumé/letter and requested files using the existing tools. Preserve the words through export, inspect the actual layout and fix mechanical defects within delivery. Keep the readable fonts/margins and native links.

Apply replacements as replacements: remove superseded wording instead of adding another version of the same thought. Before returning, read the finished letter as a whole within this same reasoning pass: retain the strongest reasons to consider this applicant and their relevant support, and remove duplicated interest, repeated benefit summaries and career-history padding. Current applicant instructions govern corrections that conflict with them. This is part of writing the finished text, not another reviewer call.

The five rounds are OSINT, selection, drafting, review and correction/delivery. Use research tools in round 1 and document tools in round 5. One OpenClaw owns all five rounds by default. A separate reviewer subagent, second OpenClaw or other model family is optional; review remains its own round in every mode.

## Applicant-specific selection and presentation

Apply [the applicant's preferences](resume-preferences.md) when selecting and presenting experience. Keep the full career reference available. Do not fabricate missing dates or flatten current work into a career-transition apology.

The applicant's supplied facts are authoritative. Explicitly reject hedging, minimization, deficit narration, defensive credit disclaimers and claim verification.

Repair an observed defect at the stage that introduced it and retain valid research, selections and documents. A scoped repair does not start a new routine review chain. After the requested documents, continue to the requested application-management, interview, profile/outreach or offer route.
