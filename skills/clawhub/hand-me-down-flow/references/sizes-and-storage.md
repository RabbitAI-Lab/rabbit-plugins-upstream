## 2. Condition grading (the A–D language)

| Grade | Meaning | Storage verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A | new, with tags | store — top value |
| B | gentle wear, no stains/defects | store |
| C | visible wear, minor stains | store only if chain short |
| D | play clothes / stained / worn | do NOT store; donate/dispose now |

Grading at intake takes 10 seconds per item with the pile sorted; from
memory months later it is fiction.

## 3. Storage physics (why bins die)

- **Attic:** summer peaks 50–65 °C. Elastic (spandex waistbands),
  laminated bibs, rain-coat membranes, EVA foam (shoe soles, mats), and
  diaper-era plastics embrittle or delaminate in 1–3 seasons. Also moth
  and wasp habitat.
- **Basement/crawl:** RH > 65% → mildew on cotton in weeks; musty smell
  never washes out fully.
- **Garage:** temperature cycling + rodents; car-exhaust proximity for
  anything breathable.
- **Ideal:** interior closet, sealed clear bins (see-through = no
  archaeology), items washed (clean fabric resists moths; food stains
  attract them), no dryer sheets or mothballs in contact with fabric —
  cedar blocks if wool.
- Wool (baby sweaters): clean + sealed + cedar; moth larvae eat keratin,
  they find dirty wool fastest.
- Battery items (swing, bouncer): remove batteries before storage —
  one leaking AA destroys a battery tray.

## 4. Chain math

Kid gap decides everything:

- Gap ≤ 2.5 yr: hand-me-downs flow well; most B-grade clothes survive;
  buy the older kid's key items (coat, boots, dress clothes) one
  quality notch up.
- Gap 3–4 yr: seasonal outerwear mostly survives; everyday clothes
  style-shift; rubber (boots) rots.
- Gap ≥ 5 yr: storage losses + style + safety-standard drift (car seats
  EXPIRE — see below) kill most of the chain. Store only A-grade
  classics and hard goods.

**Car seats expire** (plastic fatigue + standard drift): typically
6–10 yr from manufacture date stamped on the shell. NEVER store-and-
reuse past expiry, never use a seat with unknown crash history.

## 5. Season planning rule

Buy/store ahead by matching the **fit window** to the calendar, not the
label: a 4T snowsuit fits a median 44–52-month-old — if the younger kid
hits 44 months in March, the snowsuit needs to survive one more winter
in storage; if they hit it in November, pull it now. The script's match
pass does exactly this check per item per kid.

## 6. Transfer etiquette between families

- Send a manifest (list + sizes + grades) — a parent receiving a graded
  bag can plan; a parent receiving a mystery bag usually donates it.
- Mark returns: items borrowed (special-occasion, expensive outerwear)
  get a "return to" note in the registry.
- Reciprocity ledger isn't petty — it's what keeps the circuit alive.
  Note what came in from whom; send their next kid's sizes back.
- Never pass: recalled items, expired car seats, cribs older than the
  current drop-side ban, bike helmets after a crash (EPS is done).

## 7. Donation routing

- Best value per item: friends/family chain > consignment (B/A grade,
  brand names) > thrift (C+) > textile recycling bins (D, anything
  unwearable — they're shredded, not resold).
- Consignment math: ~30–50% of sale price to you, but only A/B brand
  items clear; basics (onesies, leggings) are worth more as gifts to
  the next family in the chain.
