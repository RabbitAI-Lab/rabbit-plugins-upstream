# EU AI Act Legislative Timeline

## Current Timeline (as of July 2026)

### Key Dates

| Date | Milestone | Status | Impact |
|------|-----------|--------|--------|
| **2 February 2025** | Prohibited practices enforcement | ✅ In force | Article 5 prohibitions apply (social scoring, real-time biometric ID in public spaces, emotional recognition in workplace) |
| **August 2026** | Article 4 AI literacy obligation | ⏳ Imminent | Providers and deployers must ensure staff have sufficient AI literacy. **NOT extended** by Digital Omnibus. |
| **2 December 2027** | Annex III high-risk compliance | ⏳ Extended | Was 2 August 2026. Extended by Digital Omnibus package (2026). HR/recruitment AI systems must meet full high-risk requirements. |
| **August 2028** | Annex I product safety AI | ⏳ Future | AI systems that are safety components of regulated products (machinery, medical devices, toys, etc.) |

### What Changed — Digital Omnibus Package (2026)

The European Commission's Digital Omnibus package modified the EU AI Act in several key ways:

1. **High-risk deadline extended:** Annex III systems (HR, recruitment, law enforcement, etc.) now have until **2 December 2027** instead of 2 August 2026.

2. **Technical documentation simplified:** Article 11 requirements scoped down for some categories. Reduced documentation burden for SMEs and startups.

3. **Fundamental rights impact assessments scoped down:** Narrower set of organisations required to conduct FRIAs. Fewer deployers caught by the obligation.

4. **Article 4 literacy NOT moved:** The August 2026 deadline for AI literacy training was not extended. Organisations should be preparing now.

### UK Divergence — Data (Use and Access) Act 2025

The UK updated its automated decision-making framework:

| Aspect | EU GDPR (Article 22) | UK DUAA 2025 (Articles 22A–22D) |
|--------|----------------------|----------------------------------|
| **Model** | Restrictive — prohibition on solely automated decisions with legal/significant effects | Permissive + safeguards — allows automated decisions with appropriate safeguards |
| **Effective** | Since 2018 | 5 February 2026 |
| **Consent** | Explicit consent required for solely automated decisions | Consent or legitimate interests (broader) |
| **Safeguards** | Right to human intervention, express view, contest | Safeguards required but more flexible (notice, ability to object, human review) |
| **Material scope** | Decisions producing legal or similarly significant effects | Decisions producing legal or similarly significant effects (similar) |
| **Key difference** | Treated as a restriction | Treated as a regulated activity |

**For UK/Ireland cross-border work:** Assess under both frameworks. A system compliant in the UK under DUAA may not be compliant in Ireland under GDPR Article 22, and vice versa.

### Northern Ireland

The EU proposed extending the AI Act to Northern Ireland under the Windsor Framework. This has **not been agreed** as of July 2026. Monitor for updates — if agreed, NI would be subject to EU AI Act requirements while Great Britain follows the UK's own approach.

### What This Means for HR Directors

**Do now (before August 2026):**
- Article 4 AI literacy: Train staff on AI capabilities, limitations, and risks
- Identify all AI systems in use (inventory)
- Begin DPIA for high-risk systems
- Review Article 5 prohibited practices — cease any prohibited uses

**Prepare by December 2027 (Annex III high-risk):**
- Full risk management system (Art. 9)
- Data governance framework (Art. 10)
- Technical documentation (Art. 11) — note: simplified by Omnibus
- Record-keeping/logging (Art. 12)
- Transparency measures (Art. 13)
- Human oversight mechanisms (Art. 14)
- Accuracy and robustness testing (Art. 15)
- Conformity assessment (Art. 43-48)

**Positioning note:** The extended deadline is an opportunity, not a reason to delay. "Use this time wisely" — organisations that prepare early will have a competitive advantage and avoid last-minute scramble.
