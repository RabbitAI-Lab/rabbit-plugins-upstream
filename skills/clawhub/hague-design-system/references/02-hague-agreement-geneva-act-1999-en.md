> **Clause hierarchy**: This Act is the **treaty text** of the Hague Agreement 1999 Geneva Act, containing **Articles 1–34 only** (no "bis" articles such as Article 8bis or 15bis). Withdrawal, refusal, refusal periods, changes and renewals and other procedural rules are found in the **Common Regulations under the Geneva Act (Rule)** and the **Administrative Instructions**. ## INTRODUCTORY PROVISIONS

### Article 1 — Abbreviated Expressions
- "the Hague Agreement" means the Hague Agreement Concerning the International Deposit of Industrial Designs, henceforth renamed the Hague Agreement Concerning the International Registration of Industrial Designs
- "this Act" means the Hague Agreement as established by the present Act
- "Regulations" means the Regulations under this Act
- "prescribed" means prescribed in the Regulations
- "Paris Convention" means the Paris Convention for the Protection of Industrial Property
- "international registration" / "international application" / "International Register" / "applicant" / "holder" / "intergovernmental organization" / "Contracting Party" / "applicant's Contracting Party" / "Office" / "Examining Office" / "designation" / "designated Contracting Party" / "designated Office" defined at (i)–(xxix)
- "1934 Act" / "1960 Act" / "1961 Additional Act" / "Complementary Act of 1967" / "Union" / "Assembly" / "Organization" / "Director General" / "International Bureau" / "instrument of ratification" defined accordingly

### Article 2 — Applicability of Other Protection Accorded by Laws of Contracting Parties and by Certain International Treaties
- (1) The provisions of this Act shall not affect the application of any wider protection that may be accorded by the law of a Contracting Party, nor in any way affect the protection given to works of art and applied works of art by international copyright treaties and conventions, or the protection given to industrial designs under the TRIPS Agreement
- (2) Each Contracting Party shall comply with the provisions of the Paris Convention relating to industrial designs

### Article 3 — Entitlement to File an International Application
Nationals of a State that is a Contracting Party, or of a State member of an intergovernmental organization that is a Contracting Party; and persons having a domicile, habitual residence, or a real and effective industrial or commercial establishment in the territory of a Contracting Party — shall have the right to file an international application.

### Article 4 — Procedure for Filing the International Application
- (1)(a) The international application may, at the applicant's option, be filed directly with the International Bureau or through the Office of the applicant's Contracting Party
- (1)(b) Any Contracting Party may notify the Director General, by a declaration, that international applications may not be filed through its Office
- (2) The Office of any Contracting Party may require the applicant to pay a transmittal fee for any international application filed through it, for its own benefit

### Article 5 — Contents of the International Application
- (1) The international application shall be in the prescribed language or one of the prescribed languages, and shall contain or be accompanied by: a request for international registration; prescribed data concerning the applicant; reproduction(s) or specimen(s) of the industrial design(s); an indication of the products; an indication of the designated Contracting Party or Parties; the prescribed fees; any other prescribed particulars
- (2) An Examining Office Contracting Party may declare that the application must contain (creator identity / brief description of the characteristics / claim)
- (3) May contain other particulars prescribed by the Regulations
- (4) One international application may contain two or more industrial designs
- (5) May contain a request for deferment of publication
- China declaration (Art.5(2)(b)(ii)): requires a brief description of the characteristics of the industrial design
- China declaration (Rule R9(3)(a)): requires specific views of the industrial design

### Article 6 — Priority
- (1)(a) May include a declaration of priority under Paris Convention Article 4
- (1)(b) The Regulations may permit the declaration to be made after filing
- (2) The international application shall, from the date of filing, rank as a regular national application within the meaning of Paris Convention Article 4
- Priority period: 6 months from the date of the first application

### Article 7 — Designation Fees
- (1) The prescribed fees shall include, for each designated Contracting Party, a designation fee
- (2) An Examining Office Contracting Party or an intergovernmental organization Contracting Party may declare that the prescribed designation fee is replaced by an individual designation fee
- (3) Designation fees are transmitted by the International Bureau to the Contracting Parties concerned
- China declaration (Art.7(2)): individual designation fee for designation of China (first period 497 CHF/design; first renewal 922 CHF; second renewal 1820 CHF)

### Article 8 — Correction of Irregularities
- (1) Where the International Bureau finds the application does not comply, it shall invite the applicant to correct within a prescribed time limit
- (2)(a) Failure to correct → the international application shall be deemed abandoned
- (2)(b) Irregularity relating to Article 5(2) or special requirement not corrected → deemed not to include the designation of that Contracting Party
- Note: The Chinese version does not specify "fixed 3 months, non-extendable"; that time limit is set by Common Regulations Rule 14(1)(a) (three months from the date of the notification; not extendable; two months for payment of at least the basic fee, Rule 14(1)(b)). Cite at Regulation level.

### Article 9 — Filing Date of the International Application
- (1) Filed directly with the International Bureau → filing date is the date of receipt
- (2) Filed through an Office → filing date determined as prescribed
- (3) Where an irregularity delays the filing date → filing date is the date of receipt of the correction

### Article 10 — International Registration, Date of the International Registration, Publication and Confidential Copies of the International Registration
- (1) The International Bureau shall register immediately upon receipt of the application or correction
- (2) Date of international registration is generally the filing date; for Art.5(2) irregularities, the later of the correction date or filing date
- (3) The international registration is published by the International Bureau and constitutes sufficient publicity in all Contracting Parties; copies sent to designated Offices
- (4) Confidential until publication
- (5) The International Bureau sends confidential copies to designated Offices; Offices may use them only for examination, not disclose

### Article 11 — Deferment of Publication
- (1) A Contracting Party whose law limits or does not allow deferment must declare so
- (2) Deferment period as prescribed or as declared (shortest declared period)
- (3) If law does not allow deferment, the request or designation is disregarded
- (4) The holder may request earlier publication or grant third-party access
- (5) Renunciation or limitation → corresponding non-publication
- (6) On expiry of deferment, publication on payment of fee; failure to pay or supply reproductions → cancellation
- China has not shortened the deferment period; designation of China allows deferment up to 30 months from filing

### Article 12 — Refusal
- (1) The designated Office may refuse effect, in whole or part, for non-compliance with protection conditions, but not on grounds of form/content requirements not permitted by the Act and Regulations
- (2) Refusal must be notified in writing to the International Bureau within the prescribed period, stating all grounds
- (3) The International Bureau transmits a copy to the holder; holder entitled to review, reconsideration or appeal
- (4) A refusal may be withdrawn, in whole or part, at any time
- China declaration (Rule R18(1)(b)): refusal notification period extended to 12 months from publication

### Article 13 — Special Requirements Concerning Unity of Design
- (1) A Contracting Party may declare unity-of-design requirements, without affecting the right to include multiple designs under Art.5(4)
- (2) Such declaration allows the Office to refuse until compliance
- (3) Divisional processing may incur a further fee
- China declaration (Rule R13): one application limited to one industrial design (similar designs must designate the basic design)

### Article 14 — Effects of the International Registration
- (1) From the registration date, at least equivalent to a regular national application in each designated Contracting Party
- (2) If not refused (or refusal withdrawn), equivalent to grant of protection
- (3) An Examining Office Contracting Party may declare that a designation is without effect where it is the applicant's Contracting Party ("self-designation prohibition")
- China has **not** made such a declaration under Art.14(3)(a) → Chinese applicants may self-designate China (though not economical in practice)

### Article 15 — Invalidation
- (1) No invalidation without timely opportunity for the holder to defend
- (2) The Office must notify the International Bureau of any invalidation

### Article 16 — Recording of Changes and Other Matters Concerning International Registrations
- (1) The International Bureau records: change of ownership, change of name/address, appointment of representative, renunciation, limitation, invalidation, etc.
- (2) Recording has the same effect in each Contracting Party as recording in the Office's register (subject to declaration)
- (3) Recording may be subject to a fee
- (4) The International Bureau publishes and sends notifications
- China declaration (Art.16(2)): effect of change of rights in China requires submission of particulars/documents prescribed by the declaration

### Article 17 — Initial Term and Renewal of the International Registration and Duration of Protection
- (1) Initial term: 5 years from the date of international registration
- (2) Renewable for periods of 5 years
- (3) Duration of protection at least 15 years from the date of international registration; if law provides more, that applies; each Contracting Party must declare its maximum
- (4) Renewal may be for fewer Contracting Parties or designs
- (5) Renewal recorded, published and notified
- China declaration (Art.17(3)(c)): Chinese law provides a maximum protection period of 15 years (from the date of international registration)

### Article 18 — Information Concerning Published International Registrations
- (1) On payment, extracts or information may be obtained
- (2) Extracts by the International Bureau are exempt from legalization

## CHAPTER II — ADMINISTRATIVE PROVISIONS

### Article 19 — Common Office of Several States
- (1) Several States may notify a common Office to replace their respective Offices; their unified territory treated as one Contracting Party
- (2) Timing of notification: on participation or upon unification
- (3) Effective date: on participation or three months after notification

### Article 20 — Membership of the Hague Union
Each Contracting Party shall be a member of the same Union as the States party to the 1934 Act or the 1960 Act.

### Article 21 — Assembly
- (1) Composition (same Assembly as bound by Complementary Act of 1967 Article 2; one representative per Contracting Party; non-member observers)
- (2) Tasks (maintain/develop the Union, amend Regulations, budget, etc.)
- (3) Quorum (majority of voting States; fallback written voting)
- (4) Voting (consensus preferred; one vote per State, IGO votes for its members)
- (5) Majority (two-thirds, except as provided)
- (6) Sessions (ordinary every two years, extraordinary by quarter or Director General)
- (7) Adopts its own rules of procedure

### Article 22 — International Bureau
- (1) Performs international registration and administrative tasks
- (2) Director General is chief executive
- (3) Convenes other meetings
- (4) Director General attends without vote, ex officio secretary
- (5) Prepares revision conferences
- (6) Performs other tasks

### Article 23 — Finances
- (1) Union has a budget including common expenditure apportionment
- (2) Coordination with other Union budgets
- (3) Sources (fees, service fees, sales, gifts, miscellaneous)
- (4) Fees set by Assembly; if budget not adopted, previous year's applies
- (5) Working capital fund
- (6) Host State advance
- (7) Audit of accounts

### Article 24 — Regulations
- (1) Govern implementing details
- (2) Certain provisions amendable only by unanimity or four-fifths majority
- (3) In case of conflict, the Act prevails over the Regulations

## CHAPTER III — REVISION AND AMENDMENT

### Article 25 — Revision of This Act
- (1) May be revised by a conference of Contracting Parties
- (2) Articles 22, 23, 26 may be amended by revision conference or by the Assembly

### Article 26 — Amendment of Certain Articles by the Assembly
- (1) Proposals by Contracting Party or Director General, circulated six months in advance
- (2) Amendment requires three-fourths majority; Article 21 or this Article requires four-fifths
- (3) Binding on Contracting Parties upon entry into force; specific amendments not accepted have no effect on the non-accepting Party

## CHAPTER IV — FINAL PROVISIONS

### Article 27 — Becoming Party to This Act
- (1) Any State member of the Organization, or eligible intergovernmental organization, may sign
- (2) Deposit of instrument of ratification or accession
- (3) Effective date generally the deposit date; exceptions for common Office or conditional declarations

### Article 28 — Effective Date of Ratifications and Accessions
- (1) Only instruments with an effective date are considered
- (2) Entry into force three months after six States deposit, at least three meeting the application-volume condition
- (3) Bound from entry-into-force date, or three months after deposit for later depositors

### Article 29 — Prohibition of Reservations
No reservations to this Act are permitted.

### Article 30 — Declarations Made by Contracting Parties
- (1) Declarations under specified Articles may be made on deposit or later
- (2) Common-Office States' declarations need corresponding declarations in force
- (3) Declarations may be withdrawn at any time

### Article 31 — Applicability of the 1934 and 1960 Acts
- (1) Between Parties to both this Act and an earlier Act, only this Act applies (except prior registrations)
- (2) Relations with States party only to the 1934 or 1960 Act continue under that earlier Act

### Article 32 — Denunciation of This Act
- (1) Notify the Director General of denunciation
- (2) Effective one year later; does not affect pending applications or existing registrations

### Article 33 — Languages of This Act; Signature
- (1) Original in English, Arabic, Chinese, French, Russian and Spanish, all equally authentic; other official texts may be established
- (2) Open for signature at headquarters for one year after adoption

### Article 34 — Depositary
The Director General shall be the Depositary of this Act.

## Agreed Statements by the Diplomatic Conference Regarding the Geneva Act and the Regulations under the Geneva Act

**1.** When adopting Article 12(4), Article 14(2)(b) and Rule 18(4), the Diplomatic Conference understood that a withdrawal of refusal by an Office that has communicated a notification of refusal may take the form of a statement to the effect that the Office concerned has decided to accept the effects of the international registration in respect of the industrial designs, or some of the industrial designs, to which the notification of refusal related. It was also understood that an Office may, within the period allowed for communicating a notification of refusal, send a statement to the effect that it has decided to accept the effects of the international registration even where it has not communicated such a notification of refusal.

**2.** When adopting Article 10, the Diplomatic Conference understood that nothing in this Article precludes access to the international application or the international registration by the applicant or the holder or a person having the consent of the applicant or the holder.

---
**Note**: Withdrawal of an international application/registration is a matter of the Regulations / Administrative Instructions level, not of the treaty text (Articles 1–34); the relevant provisions are scattered (e.g. Rule 16(2) withdrawal of a designation in the context of deferred publication, Rule 18(4) withdrawal of a refusal, Rule 21 recording of changes) rather than concentrated in a single Rule. **The Hague System does not provide for a conversion (transformation) of an international registration into a national application** — Rule 21 governs the recording of changes (change of ownership, change of name/address, renunciation, limitation, appointment of a representative, indications concerning the creator), not conversion; conversion/transformation is a mechanism of Article 9bis of the Madrid Protocol and must not be attributed to the Hague System.
