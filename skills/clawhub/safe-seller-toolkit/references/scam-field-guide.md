# Scam Field Guide — 9 Families, Anatomy and Counters

## 1. Verification-code phishing (+40) — THE number hijack
**Script:** "I need to verify you're real — I sent a 6-digit code to your
phone, read it back to me." / "Google Voice is sending you a code to confirm
the listing."

**Mechanics:** they're initiating a Google Voice (or similar) signup using
YOUR number. The code arrives to you; they need you to read it back. Once
armed, your number is verified for their account and they run further scams
under your identity.

**Counter:** never share codes with anyone, for any reason. A real buyer
never needs a code that arrives on YOUR phone. Report the account.

## 2. Overpayment / 'accidental' extra (+35)
**Script:** "I sent $500 for the $350 item — oops! Just refund me the $150
difference and keep $20 for your trouble."

**Mechanics:** the 'payment' is a fake screenshot, stolen card charge, or
reversible transfer. Your refund of $150 is real money leaving your account.
The original never existed or gets clawed back.

**Counter:** never refund anything. If a payment arrives for the wrong
amount, tell them to cancel it on their end and re-send the right one. Then
watch it never happen.

## 3. Off-platform payment (+30)
**Script:** "Let's skip fees — Zelle / Venmo friends-and-family / Cash App /
Apple Pay / gift cards / Bitcoin."

**Mechanics:** platform payment systems exist precisely to protect both
sides. Off-platform peer transfers have zero buyer/seller protection and are
explicitly against marketplace rules (account ban risk). Gift cards and
crypto are irreversible and untraceable — the preferred rail of every scam.

**Counter:** on-platform payment or cash-in-hand, full stop. "Friends and
family" specifically waives even PayPal's own protections.

## 4. Off-platform shipping / courier pickup (+30)
**Script:** "I'm out of town — my shipper/mover/agent will collect it. I'll
email a prepaid label." / "Email me at ... / text me at ..."

**Mechics:** the label is fake (your package vanishes or 'delivers' nowhere);
the payment confirmation email is forged. Moving you to email/SMS escapes
platform moderation and evidence trails.

**Counter:** you ship only via labels YOU buy, after payment shows completed
in the app you logged into yourself. No agents, no movers, no email deals.

## 5. Too-good offer (+30)
**Script:** "I'll pay $500 for your $350 listing, above asking, today."

**Mechanics:** the overpayment family's opener. Nobody pays above asking on
used goods; there is no rational honest reason to.

**Counter:** block. If curious, reply "asking price, cash in person" — the
scammer vanishes, an honest oddball accepts.

## 6. Escrow / agent theater (+20)
**Script:** "My assistant/broker/PA will handle payment on behalf of my
client." / "Use this escrow service."

**Mechanics:** fake escrow sites and 'assistant' wiring create a layer of
theater between you and a payment that never clears. Common in rental and
car scams, increasingly in marketplace high-value deals.

**Counter:** you deal with the buyer, in person or on-platform, or not at
all. Real escrow is chosen by neither party unilaterally.

## 7. Switch / swap bait (+18)
**Script:** "I'll bring mine and we'll swap plus I add cash." / At the
meetup: hands you a box 'with the phone inside', takes yours, returns later
complaining theirs was broken.

**Mechanics:** the physical swap — your genuine item exchanged for a dead
clone, or the 'let me see it' walk-away.

**Counter:** serial numbers photographed and verified at the meetup, item
never leaves your sight, money counted before the item changes hands.

## 8. Pressure / urgency (+15)
**Script:** "ASAP" / "today only" / "I need it before my flight" / "don't
tell my wife."

**Mechanics:** urgency short-circuits the exact judgment this skill exists
to slow down. Secrecy adds isolation.

**Counter:** a legitimate buyer accepts "give me an hour." Any deal that
can't survive an hour of thinking isn't a deal.

## 9. Price-blind buyer (+12)
**Script:** "What's the lowest?" with zero questions about condition,
history, or accessories.

**Mechanics:** usually a lowball reseller (annoying, survivable) or the
setup for a switch/'not as described' scam (they'll claim damage later).

**Counter:** fine to engage once, firmly: "Price is $X, cash, public
meetup." No questions after that = walk.

## If you already got hit
1. **Codes shared:** secure the account the code was for immediately
   (Google Voice: reclaim your number; carrier: add a port-out PIN).
2. **Money sent:** contact your bank/transfer app within minutes — Zelle
   and wire reversals are rare but time-critical. File a police report for
   the paper trail.
3. **Item shipped on a fake label:** contact the carrier with your proof
   pack photos; open a platform dispute; report the account.
4. **Everywhere:** report the profile on the platform, and to
   reportfraud.ftc.gov (US) / Action Fraud (UK) / your national equivalent.
5. **Identity exposure:** if you gave address + schedule, vary your routine
   for a few weeks; stolen-listing fraud rings do recon via marketplace.

## Meetup escalation ladder (by deal value)
- **under $100:** daylight, busy public place, cash, friend knows where
  you are.
- **$100-299:** police-station exchange zone preferred; count cash twice;
  verify serials.
- **$300-999:** bank lobby; verify IMEI (*#06#) for phones at a carrier
  store; deposit cash before parting.
- **$1000+:** inside the bank, teller-verified funds or cashier's check
  drawn in person; never accept a cashier's check from a stranger outside
  a bank (forged ones fool everyone).
