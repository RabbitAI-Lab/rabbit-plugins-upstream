# Cleaning Chemistry & Poisoning Reference

## 1. Why this database exists

US poison centers log ~2 million human exposure cases per year; cleaning
substances are consistently among the top categories, with thousands of
cases involving chlorine/chloramine gas from mixed cleaners. The UK NHS
and EU poison centres report the same pattern. The classic injury paths:

1. Toilet bowl cleaner (acid) → then bleach → chlorine gas in the face.
2. "Glass cleaner + bleach makes a stronger cleaner" → chloramine gas.
3. One drain cleaner doesn't work → pour in a different one → violent
   heat/boiling from acid + caustic in the pipe.
4. Mixing in a spray bottle "to save time" → pressure + gas in hand.

All four are preventable with knowledge that fits on an index card —
which is what this skill is.

## 2. The core reactions

### Bleach + ammonia → chloramine (NH₂Cl)
Household bleach is 3–6 % sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl) in water. Ammonia
sources: glass cleaners, multi-surface cleaners, some dish soaps (amine
surfactants), urine (which is why bleaching a poorly flushed urinal
smells sharp).

```
NaOCl + NH3 → NH2Cl + NaOH        (monochloramine)
further:   NH2Cl + NH3 etc. → hydrazine traces under strong excess ammonia
```

Chloramine is less acutely potent than chlorine but **longer-lived in
air**, causing delayed-onset respiratory irritation hours later. Pool
"chlorine smell" is actually chloramines.

### Bleach + acid → chlorine (Cl₂)
```
NaOCl + 2 HCl → Cl2↑ + NaCl + H2O
```
Acid sources: toilet bowl cleaners (5–10 % HCl), limescale/descalers
(sulfamic, citric), vinegar (5 % acetic — weaker but real), rust
removers (oxalic, phosphoric, occasionally HF).

Chlorine is a WWI chemical weapon — at household quantities it's a
medical emergency, not a war crime, but the gas is the same molecule:
heavier than air, pools in the bowl/room low zones, attacks mucous
membranes on contact. Water contact hydrolyzes it to HCl + HOCl, which
is why eyes/throat burn first.

### Hydrogen peroxide + vinegar → peracetic acid
Slow reaction, accelerated by heat/metal. Peracetic acid is a potent
disinfectant (used industrially) and a significant respiratory/eye
irritant at vapor levels. The "natural cleaning" community spreads this
mix; poison centers counter it. Peroxide + bleach releases oxygen and
heat — in a capped bottle, a small pressure bomb.

### Acid + caustic → violent neutralization
Drain-cleaner scenarios. H₂SO₄ drain cleaners + NaOH drain cleaners:
neutralization enthalpy boils the water present; gas + hot caustic can
erupt from the drain. Corrosive burns from splatter are the injury.

### Bleach + isopropyl alcohol
Can form chlorinated acetones/ethers — chloroform-adjacent chemistry. At
cleaning concentrations the risk is mostly irritation/dizziness in
enclosed spaces, but it has no cleaning benefit. Don't.

## 3. Product → class mapping (why labels matter)

Formulations vary by brand and market. Rules of thumb encoded here are
deliberately conservative:

- Any product with "bleach" or "hypochlorite" in the ingredients →
  **bleach** class.
- Any product declaring ammonia, quaternary ammonium + acid co-surfactant
  systems, or amines → treat as **ammonia** class with bleach.
- Acids appear as hydrochloric, sulfamic, citric, acetic, oxalic,
  phosphoric, "limescale", "descaling", "rust" → **acid** class.
- Caustics: sodium hydroxide, potassium hydroxide, "caustic soda" →
  **caustic** class.
- Peroxide sources: hydrogen peroxide, sodium percarbonate (in
  "oxy" laundry boosters and dishwasher tabs!) → **peroxide** class.
  Note: dishwasher tabs + acid descaler in the same closed machine is a
  real o2-gas pressure scenario.

**If you can't classify it, treat it as incompatible with everything.**

## 4. Safe sequencing (when two products are both needed)

The universal protocol:

1. Apply product A per label; let it dwell its time.
2. **Rinse/flush thoroughly**: surfaces with running water; toilets
   flushed twice; drains flushed with plenty of hot water.
3. Wait 15–30 min with active ventilation (fan, open window).
4. Apply product B. Never let pooled residues of both exist together.
5. Tools (brushes, sponges) get rinsed between products — a bleach-soaked
   sponge wiped into an acid-cleaned bowl is the same reaction.

## 5. Symptom progression & first aid

Gas exposure timeline (typical):

| Time | Symptom set |
|---|---|
| Seconds–minutes | Sharp odor, eye/throat burning, cough |
| Minutes–hours | Chest tightness, wheeze, breathlessness |
| 1–24 h (chloramine) | Delayed cough/bronchial irritation — can worsen |

First aid sequence:

1. **Remove everyone from the space** — including pets (gases pool low).
2. Ventilate from outside where possible; don't lean into the room.
3. Fresh air, rest, observation for 30+ min minimum.
4. Flushing: eyes or skin contact → clean water 15+ min.
5. Escalation triggers → call emergency services: any breathing
   difficulty, persistent cough, chest pain, symptoms worsening after
   leaving the exposure, pre-existing asthma/COPD reacting.
6. Poison control (US 1-800-222-1222) for guidance when unsure — free,
   24/7, staffed by toxicology professionals.

**Do NOT induce vomiting after swallowing cleaners.** Call poison control
with the bottle in hand.

## 6. Storage & habits that prevent the whole category

- Bleach and acids in **separate cupboards** (leaks mix in the tray).
- Keep products in original containers; label anything decanted.
- Never store cleaners above eye level or where a fall could mix them.
- One drain chemistry per clog; mechanical tools (plunger, snake) have
  no chemistry to conflict.
- "Natural" ≠ safe: vinegar is an acid; essential oils dissolve into
  lungs; peroxide is an oxidizer. The hazard classes don't care about
  marketing.
