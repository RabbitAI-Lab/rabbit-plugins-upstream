# POC Contract Field Manual

## 1. Opening positioning

Explain that POC is a joint experiment to reduce investment uncertainty. It is not a borderless free customization, nor is it to prove that the supplier will definitely succeed.

## 2. Target level

-Business goals: why it’s worth changing;
- Decision goals: what to approve, adjust, or discontinue after the POC is over;
- Justification goal: what evidence supports the decision;
- Delivery Objectives: What needs to be built in order to obtain evidence.

Delivery goals should not be used as a substitute for business and decision-making goals.

## 3. Indicator Negotiation

For each indicator, ask: where does the baseline come from, who endorses it, why the threshold is reasonable, what sample, who scores, what to do after passing, and what to do after failing.

Prioritize when there are too many metrics: core business results, user tasks, technical feasibility, unacceptable risks and costs.

## 4. hard gate

Suitable for hard gate: sensitive leaks, unauthorized actions, key policy errors, regulatory violations, and irreversible data damage.

Not suitable as a hard gate: slight wording preference, non-core format, individual user subjective preference.

## 5. Range compression

Constraints in this order: one role, one trigger, one workflow, one data domain, one environment, limited samples, and limited time.

If it is still too large, priority is given to verifying the maximum uncertainty and not pursuing end-to-end appearance integrity.

## 6. Insufficient customer resources

| Missing | Processing |
|---|---|
| No real users | Suspended user value conclusion |
| No baseline | Pre-collection, no commitment to improvement rate |
| No data | Use synthesis/Mock, limit conclusions |
| No technical leader | No commitment to real integration |
| No Decision Maker | Contract Keeping Draft |
| No security involved | High-risk data and actions prohibited |

## 7. Sales pressure

Business meetings or contract renewal nodes can become time constraints, but the evidence cannot be modified. Separate presentation content and POC conclusions.

## 8. Scope Change Order

Records: Request, rationale, support criteria, incremental cost/risk, replacement scope, approver, version, and effective time.

## 9. Governance rhythm

- Kick-off meeting: freezing contract;
- Every 2–3 days: Evidence, blocking and scope review;
- Major risk: instant upgrade;
- Closing meeting: decision-making based on standards one by one;
- Review: learning and handover to the next step.

## 10. Common disputes

"Do it first and then set the targets": reject; at least freeze the core hard access and decision-making issues.

"POC does not require security": Deny; can simplify engineering but cannot bypass critical risks.

“The customer wants to see a few more features”: Ask for mapping proof criterions or enter the candidate pool.

"Approaching the threshold is considered as passing": According to the freezing rule; it can form "re-verification after adjustment", and the history cannot be changed.

"Mock works well": Only prove the conclusion other than the mocked part.

## 11. Signature and Confirmation

Legal signatures are not necessarily required, but key business, user, technical and risk owners must leave traceability confirmation. The draft bid has not been confirmed.

## 12. End of contract

Valid outputs include Pass, Partial Pass, Fail, Stop, and Unable to judge. Failure to judge usually means insufficient experimental design or customer input, and responsibilities and re-entry conditions need to be explained.
