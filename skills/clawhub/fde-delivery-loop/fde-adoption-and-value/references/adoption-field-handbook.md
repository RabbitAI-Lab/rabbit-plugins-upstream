# Adoption and Value Field Manual

## Use event design

Adoption events must represent the completion of a real task, such as "generate and review a valid draft," rather than a login, click, or page opening.

## Commonly used adoption indicators

-Number of eligible users;
- Get access ratio;
- First time success rate and time;
- Weekly/monthly reuse;
-Core mission coverage;
- Ongoing use and loss;
-Per capita/task frequency;
- Active use and requested use;
- Manual bypass and replacement tools;
- Support requests and recovery times.

## Common task indicators

- Completion rate;
- Median and P90 times;
- First pass rate;
- Rework/upgrade;
- Manual intervention;
- Errors and severity;
- The evidence is complete;
- User modification ratio;
- Downstream acceptance rate.

## Business indicator candidates

- Service: SLA, resolution rate, satisfaction, queue and upgrades;
- Sales: lead time, conversion, cycle time and compliance;
- Operations: throughput, cost, quality and exceptions;
- Risks: incidents, missed detections, audits and losses;
- R&D: cycle, defects, recovery and reuse;
- Knowledge work: search, first draft, review and decision time.

## Negative indicators

- False confidence;
- Over-reliance;
- Skill degradation;
- Transfer of work to others;
- Privacy or security incidents;
- Unfair results;
- support burden;
- Cost and delay fluctuations;
- User circumvention controls.

## User segmentation

By role, task frequency, experience, region, shift, risk, data access and job complexity. Average adoption rates can mask critical group failures.

## Use interviews

- What task did the first success occur in;
- Why it didn’t work the last time;
- Which results must be reviewed;
- Which step increases the burden;
- How to recover after an error;
-Whether you would recommend colleagues;
- What are the real impacts of cancellation;
- Which tasks clearly should not be used.

## Observation method

Incorporate journals, task results, interviews, supporting notes, and field observations. Self-reported use is often higher than true behavior.

## Baseline quality

Confirm the same group of people, the same task, the same window and the same metric definition. Use unit task indicators when business volume changes.

## Attribution strength

From weak to strong: subjective feedback, before-and-after comparison, similar group control, batch launch, randomization or quasi-experiment. Choose based on decision-making risks. Complex experiments are not required but limitations must be stated.

## ROI Negotiation

Time savings do not automatically equal layoffs or cash benefits. Need to explain what the release time is used for, whether it improves capacity/quality, and whether it is truly realized.

## Check before expansion

- POC key risks have been fixed;
- Target groups and tasks are similar;
- Permissions, training and support are scalable;
- Affordable cost and capacity;
- There are monitoring, feedback and decommissioning paths;
- owners accept responsibility for long-term operations.

## Stop signal

Low long-term use, value only appears when running with you, high error costs, user avoidance, complete costs are not established, risks are unacceptable, or there is no long-term Owner.

## Value communication

Provide business results, evidence, and investment options to executives; provide task improvements and limitations to users; provide quality, cost, risk, and operations to technology; and provide metric definition, cost, and attribution boundaries to finance.
