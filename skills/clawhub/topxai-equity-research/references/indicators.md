# Indicator and data-source library

> Use together with `references/framework.md`. Every indicator carries a **Cause / Process / Outcome** tag (framework section 1.1):
> **Cause** = a precondition that determines the future · Process = a mechanism that is running now and leads disclosure · _Outcome_ = already happened and already priced.
>
> **Test for the Nature column (restate it every time before you fill the column):**
> - **Cause** = a precondition or structural constraint that is **already locked in and cannot be undone in the short run**
>   (capacity under construction, long-term contracts already signed, demographic structure, rule effective dates).
>   Test: it will **not** reverse next quarter because conditions change.
> - **Process** = a mechanism that is running now and **leads the disclosure of the outcome**, but **can reverse**
>   (bank credit-standards surveys, capacity utilisation, inventories and shipments, disclosed policy intent).
>   Test: it reflects the current state, and the next release may turn around.
> - **Outcome** = a result that has already happened (net income, CPI, GDP, export value, share price).
>   **Fast release does not make something a cause.** A low-lag outcome (a ten-day export report, a nowcast) is still an outcome:
>   use it only to shorten the time to confirmation, never as a premise of the thesis.
>
> **Warning: verify before use.** Every source in this library was checked for existence, but dataset names,
> release frequency and definitions can change. **The first time you use a source, open it and confirm the current definition and coverage period**,
> and write the actual access date in the report's source list. If a source disagrees with this library, fix this library.
>
> **General rules for retrieving data by script:**
> - **sec.gov / data.sec.gov**: the SEC fair-access policy requires a declared User-Agent in the form "Organisation name email address"
>   and at most 5 requests per second; a browser-style user agent is rejected with 403. Set the environment variable `SEC_USER_AGENT="Name email"`.
>   For SEC data use `scripts/sec.py` (`facts`, `filings`, `feed`); it caches the raw JSON on disk.
> - **FRED**: the no-key CSV `https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=<ID>` (add `&cosd=YYYY-MM-DD` to set the start date).
>   Send an honest, plain user agent. If the CSV endpoint is unreachable, switch to the JSON API
>   `https://api.stlouisfed.org/fred/series/observations?series_id=<ID>&api_key=<KEY>&file_type=json&observation_start=YYYY-MM-DD`
>   (free key: fredaccount.stlouisfed.org; keep it in an environment variable such as `FRED_API_KEY`, and never write it in a report or a source list;
>   without a key the API returns 400 "api_key is not set"; add `&vintage_dates=YYYY-MM-DD` to retrieve a historical vintage).
>   Every FRED series in this library (written "FRED: <ID>", "FRED series …" and similar) is retrieved by one of these two routes.
> - **Unofficial endpoints**: do not scrape the private web APIs of aggregator sites, and never spoof a browser user agent to reach one.
>   Where this library names a third-party aggregator, read its public pages by hand, tag the number [C] and cross-check it against the official source.
> - **Evidence grades**: exchange and regulator figures (FINRA, exchange quotes and short interest, OCC, SEC filings) are [A];
>   third-party aggregations (institutional holdings and analyst summaries on aggregator sites, Yahoo, stockanalysis and similar) are [C];
>   quantities you compute from raw data (implied volatility, delta-hedge shares and similar) are [C], and you show the formula.
>
> A source marked "paid" need not be forced. Writing "this data needs a paid source and the report does not use it"
> is far more honest than using a substitute number of unknown origin.

## L0 First principles

_Demographic cycle, productivity, relations of production and distribution, institutions and international rules, development-stage positioning._

| Indicator | Nature | Source | Frequency | Key caution |
|---|:--:|---|---|---|
| Total and old-age dependency ratio ((ages 0-14 plus 65 and over) / ages 15-64) | **Cause** | UN DESA, *World Population Prospects 2024 Revision*, Demographic Indicators data files (still the latest edition as of August 2026; the 2024 edition is the 28th); for cross-country comparison use World Bank WDI indicators SP.POP.DPND / SP.POP.DPND.OL | A revised edition every 2 years, with annual series and projections to 2100 | The structural constraint with the longest lag and the best predictability in L0: the working-age population of 20 years from now has already been born. Read the turning-point year, not the level: China's total dependency ratio bottomed around 2010 and Japan's around 1990, and in both cases the shift in asset prices and growth model followed closely. Use it as a long-cycle anchor; do not use it to explain quarterly swings. |
| Working-age (15-64) population, absolute level and annual growth | **Cause** | UN WPP 2024 (single-year age data); national sources: China National Bureau of Statistics (NBS), *China Statistical Yearbook* population-by-age tables; U.S. Census Bureau Population Estimates & National Population Projections; Statistics Bureau of Japan, Population Estimates (Jinko Suikei) | UN every 2 years; national bureaus annual (Japan publishes monthly provisional estimates) | The first term of potential growth. When the working-age population turns from growth to decline, input-driven growth must end; after that GDP growth has to come from TFP and capital deepening. Definitions differ by country (some use 15-64, some 16-64, and some move the lower bound with compulsory-schooling years), so align the age band before comparing countries. |
| Total fertility rate (TFR) and annual births | **Cause** | UN WPP 2024; national registers: U.S. CDC/NCHS *National Vital Statistics System - Births*; Japan Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, Vital Statistics (Jinko Dotai Tokei); China NBS annual population statistical communique | UN every 2 years; national vital statistics annual (the US also has quarterly provisional estimates) | Births are the only labour-supply variable known 18-20 years ahead, and the demand variable for education, maternity and infant goods and housing known 3-6 years ahead. The UN medium variant embeds a prior that TFR returns toward replacement level, which is systematically optimistic for East Asian countries with ultra-low fertility; read the low variant and domestic registration data alongside it. |
| Public pension and health spending as a share of GDP, and long-run projections | **Cause** | OECD *Social Expenditure Database (SOCX)* and *Pensions at a Glance*; European Commission DG ECFIN *The Ageing Report* long-term fiscal projections; IMF *Fiscal Monitor* | SOCX annual; Pensions at a Glance every 2 years; Ageing Report every 3 years; Fiscal Monitor twice a year (April/October) | The link that turns the dependency ratio into a fiscal constraint. It determines the future tax path, the supply curve of sovereign debt and the ceiling on "can fiscal policy stimulate again". Treat it as a precondition of L1 policy space, not as a welfare topic. |
| R&D intensity (GERD/GDP) and business share of R&D | **Cause** | OECD *Main Science and Technology Indicators (MSTI)*; UNESCO Institute for Statistics (UIS) R&D dataset; China NBS / Ministry of Science and Technology *National S&T Expenditure Statistical Bulletin* | MSTI twice a year; UIS annual; China annual (around September of the following year) | TFP is a residual, an outcome; R&D input is the cause you can observe in advance. Read the trend and composition of intensity (share of basic research, share funded by firms): an economy with a persistently low basic-research share loses its catch-up dividend near the technology frontier. |
| Total factor productivity (TFP) level and growth | _Outcome_ | Penn World Table 11.0 (Groningen Growth and Development Centre, released October 2025, 185 countries 1950-2023, variables rtfpna (TFP at constant national prices) / ctfp (TFP level at current PPPs, USA=1) / cwtfp (welfare-relevant TFP level)); OECD Productivity Statistics, "Multifactor productivity"; U.S. BLS *Total Factor Productivity — Major Sectors* | PWT a major release every 2-3 years; OECD annual; BLS annual (preliminary quarterly productivity figures are published separately) | Must be tagged Outcome. TFP is the Solow residual, the "measure of our ignorance" left after the factor contributions are subtracted from GDP growth, and it mixes in capacity utilisation, markups, factor misallocation and measurement error. Use it for after-the-fact attribution (was growth driven by inputs or by efficiency); never use it as a leading indicator. |
| Capital stock per worker and the contribution of capital deepening | Process | Penn World Table 11.0 (cn capital stock, ck capital services); OECD Productivity Database capital-service decomposition; national-accounts gross fixed capital formation series | Annual | Answers "is this round of investment closing a gap or just piling up capacity". A capital-output ratio (K/Y) that keeps rising while TFP does not move is the classic signal of diminishing returns and a common shape in the late debt cycle. |
| Labour income share (compensation of employees as a share of GDP) | Process | ILOSTAT *SDG Labour Market Indicators (ILOSDG)*, indicator 10.4.1 "Labour income share as a percent of GDP" (93 countries/areas, from 2004); U.S. BLS Productivity and Costs, non-farm business sector labour share (FRED: PRS85006173); China NBS *China Statistical Yearbook* flow-of-funds (physical transactions) table, compensation of employees | ILOSTAT annual; BLS quarterly; China annual with a lag of about 2 years | The core quantifiable variable of the "relations of production and distribution" layer: it sets the ceiling on consumption in aggregate demand, the durability of corporate margins and the direction of the political cycle. A long decline in labour share with debt substituting for income is the most typical fragile structure of consumption-led economies. |
| Income and wealth concentration (Top 1% / Top 10% share) | _Outcome_ | World Inequality Database (WID.world) income and wealth share series; U.S. Federal Reserve *Distributional Financial Accounts (DFA)* and *Survey of Consumer Finances (SCF)*; OECD *Income Distribution Database (IDD)*; World Bank *Poverty and Inequality Platform (PIP)* | WID annual; DFA quarterly; SCF every 3 years; IDD annual (countries report unevenly) | The distribution of wealth determines the marginal propensity to consume out of the same monetary or fiscal stimulus, so it is a structural parameter of the L2 multiplier. DFA's quarterly frequency is rare high-frequency distributional data and lets you observe the asymmetric pass-through of asset-price gains into aggregate demand. |
| Quality of national governance (rule of law / regulatory quality / control of corruption) | **Cause** | World Bank *Worldwide Governance Indicators (WGI)*, six dimensions; World Bank *Business Ready (B-READY)* (successor to Doing Business, which was discontinued in 2021); for contract-enforcement and property-rights questions use the World Justice Project *Rule of Law Index* | WGI annual; B-READY annual (countries added in batches); WJP annual | Institutions are a precondition for capital returns to be collected, and they determine how large a risk premium is required at a given return. These are perception-based aggregates: use them for cross-country ranking and multi-year trends, not for causal readings of small year-to-year changes. |
| Stock of trade and investment rule barriers (tariffs, non-tariff measures, export controls, investment screening) | **Cause** | WTO *Integrated Trade Intelligence Portal (I-TIP)* and *World Tariff Profiles*; UNCTAD TRAINS non-tariff measures database; Global Trade Alert (GTA, run by the St. Gallen Endowment for Prosperity through Trade, from 2021-01-01) intervention database; U.S. Commerce BIS Entity List (EAR Part 744 Supplement No. 4, announced in the Federal Register); U.S. Treasury CFIUS *Annual Report to Congress* | WTO annual; GTA near real time (rolling entry); Entity List announced on every amendment; CFIUS report annual | The rule base of supply-chain restructuring, industrial policy and "securitisation". Because GTA is near real time and covers hidden measures such as subsidies, procurement and export restrictions, it is the fastest public source for the stock of protectionism; the tariff schedule itself is the most lagging layer. |
| Position in the international monetary system (reserve-currency composition and payment share) | _Outcome_ | IMF *Currency Composition of Official Foreign Exchange Reserves (COFER)*; SWIFT *RMB Tracker* monthly report; IMF *Coordinated Portfolio Investment Survey (CPIS)* | COFER quarterly (about 1 quarter lag); RMB Tracker monthly; CPIS semi-annual | Tagged Outcome: reserve composition changes very slowly and reflects choices made over the past decade, and any one quarter's move sits within the noise of exchange-rate valuation effects (adjust for exchange rates before reading shares). Its value is to check the magnitude of the "de-dollarisation" narrative, not to forecast. |
| National debt by sector as a share of GDP (government / non-financial corporate / household) | Process | BIS *Credit to the non-financial sector* (BIS Data Portal, long series, by sector and lender type); IMF *Global Debt Database* | BIS quarterly; IMF GDD annual | The main coordinate for locating the debt cycle. Look at three things: the slope of the total, the transfer of leverage between sectors (corporate to government is the typical post-crisis pattern), and the gap between debt growth and nominal GDP growth (which decides whether leverage is digested naturally or snowballs). |
| Debt service ratio (DSR: private non-financial sector principal and interest payments as a share of income) | **Cause** | BIS *Debt service ratios for the private non-financial sector* (about 32 countries in aggregate, with household/corporate sub-sectors for some) | Quarterly | A stronger warning than the leverage ratio: it combines the interest-rate level and the debt stock into a cash-flow constraint. One to two years after DSR breaks above the upper edge of its historical range, credit usually tightens and investment drops sharply. One of the few stock-type indicators in this layer that genuinely leads. |
| Development-stage positioning (GDP per capita (PPP), urbanisation rate, three-sector output and employment structure) | _Outcome_ | World Bank *World Development Indicators (WDI)*; World Bank Country and Lending Groups income thresholds (Atlas-method GNI per capita, updated every 1 July); UN DESA *World Urbanization Prospects*; ICP purchasing-power-parity benchmark rounds (2011/2017/2021) | WDI annual (several updates a year); income groups every July; WUP a new edition every few years | Locates "where on the S-curve this economy sits", which decides whether to apply a catch-up model or a frontier model: catch-up growth comes from capital deepening and structural change, frontier growth only from TFP. Once urbanisation passes 60-70%, the demand function for property and infrastructure shifts as a whole; this structural break has been confirmed in China, Japan and Korea. |

## L1 Capital and liquidity

_Central-bank balance sheets, policy rates and term premium, credit creation, cross-border capital and exchange rates, risk appetite._

| Indicator | Nature | Source | Frequency | Key caution |
|---|:--:|---|---|---|
| Federal Reserve balance-sheet size and composition (SOMA holdings, duration, MBS runoff) | Process | Federal Reserve *H.4.1 Factors Affecting Reserve Balances of Depository Institutions and Condition Statement of Federal Reserve Banks*; New York Fed *System Open Market Account (SOMA) Holdings* detail and annual SOMA report | H.4.1 every Thursday (Wednesday is the reference date); SOMA holdings weekly, maturity distribution detail weekly | Reading total assets is the most elementary approach. What matters is the composition of the runoff (Treasuries maturing without reinvestment versus passive MBS paydown) and the caps, because that determines the effect on duration supply (term premium), not only on the quantity of reserves. |
| Net-liquidity trio: reserve balances + ON RRP usage + Treasury General Account (TGA) | Process | H.4.1 (reserves, TGA, reverse repo liabilities); New York Fed *Repo and Reverse Repo Operations* daily usage; U.S. Treasury *Daily Treasury Statement* daily TGA balance | Reserves weekly, ON RRP daily, TGA daily | The redistribution on the liability side is the liquidity the market actually feels. While ON RRP was being drained during QT, reserves barely fell; once it was exhausted the same pace of balance-sheet reduction drained reserves directly. Watching total assets alone reads these two phases backwards. TGA rebuilding during heavy issuance is likewise a drain event. |
| Reserve-abundance stress indicators: SRF usage, EFFR-IORB spread, SOFR percentiles and SOFR-IORB | Process | New York Fed *Reference Rates* (EFFR, SOFR and its 1st/25th/75th/99th percentiles, daily); New York Fed Standing Repo Facility operation results; OFR *Short-term Funding Monitor* | Daily | A real-time thermometer of the slide from "abundant" to "ample/scarce" reserves. Before the September 2019 repo crisis, the high SOFR percentiles and the EFFR-IORB spread drifted first. If month-end/quarter-end spikes start to become routine, that is mechanical evidence of a liquidity turning point. |
| Major central-bank balance sheets (ECB / Bank of Japan / People's Bank of China) | Process | ECB *Weekly financial statement* and ECB Data Portal (BSI statistics); Bank of Japan "Business Accounts every ten days" (Eigyo Maijun Hokoku) and Accounts of the Bank of Japan (Nihon Ginko Kanjo); People's Bank of China (PBoC) *Balance Sheet of the Monetary Authority* and *Sources and Uses of Credit Funds of Financial Institutions (RMB)* | ECB weekly; BoJ every ten days (end of each ten-day period); PBoC monthly | Global liquidity is the combined force of four balance sheets, to be read in a common currency. The PBoC is special because it expands mainly through structural tools such as relending, MLF and outright reverse repo, not asset purchases, so "balance-sheet expansion" and "easier credit" are not the same thing in China; cross-check against total social financing. |
| Market-implied policy-rate path (fed funds futures / OIS curve) | _Outcome_ | CME *FedWatch Tool* (based on settlement prices of 30-day fed funds futures); CME futures settlement data; for the euro area, the €STR OIS curve (ECB Data Portal / exchange data) | Daily (continuous intraday) | It leads actual policy moves, but state that it is risk-neutral pricing, contains term and convexity premia, and has had a poor record at turning points (2021 inflation, the repeated repricing of 2023-2024 cut expectations). Its proper use is to quantify "what the market has already priced"; its research value lies entirely in the expectation gap, not in the path itself. Classification note: the market-implied path is the day's settlement price and the whole path can reprice within a week, so it fails the "does not reverse next quarter" test and is an Outcome. Use it only to quantify what is priced and the expectation gap; never use it as a Cause in the load-bearing chain. |
| FOMC Summary of Economic Projections (SEP) and dot plot | Process | Federal Reserve Board *Summary of Economic Projections* (released with the March/June/September/December meetings, includes the median longer-run neutral rate); FOMC Statement and Minutes (three weeks after the meeting) | Four times a year (SEP); statement at every meeting; minutes lag 3 weeks | The central bank's official self-disclosure of its reaction function. The useful reading is not the median but the dispersion of the dots and the drift of the median longer-run neutral rate: the latter is the only public trace of how the committee's view of r* is changing. Classification note: this is a self-disclosure of policy intent, not a commitment; handle it under `references/macro.md` pitfall 3, "do not treat policy intent as policy effect". |
| Treasury term premium | Process | New York Fed *Treasury Term Premia* (Adrian-Crump-Moench, ACM model, daily and month-end series; official page newyorkfed.org/research/data_indicators/term-premia-tabs; for scripts fetch newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/data_indicators/ACMTermPremium.xls); Federal Reserve Board three-factor Kim-Wright term premium (FRED: THREEFYTP10) | ACM updated daily; Kim-Wright daily | Splits long yields into the "expected policy path" and "compensation for holding duration". It is the only quantitative way to tell whether a rise in yields reflects better growth or a fiscal/supply risk premium. State that it is a model estimate: ACM and Kim-Wright can differ by 50-100bp at the same date. |
| Real rates, breakeven inflation and curve slope | _Outcome_ | Federal Reserve Board TIPS yield curve and D'Amico-Kim-Wei (DKW) decomposition; FRED series DFII10 (10-year TIPS real yield), T10YIE (10-year breakeven), T5YIFR (5y5y forward breakeven), T10Y3M / T10Y2Y (slopes) | Daily | The real rate is the foundation of every asset's discount rate and a direct measure of how tight policy is (use it with r*). As a recession-leading signal, the curve slope was contaminated by term-premium compression in the QE era, and 2022-2024 saw the longest "inversion without recession" window on record; use it as one condition, not as a timing tool. Classification note: TIPS yields, breakevens and slopes are market prices and, by the rule that market prices are Outcomes, are tagged Outcome. In a report they serve as E1/E4-type "market-implied" comparison evidence, not as Cause or Process in the load-bearing chain. |
| Natural rate r* estimates and the policy-stance gap | Process | New York Fed *Measuring the Natural Rate of Interest* (Laubach-Williams and Holston-Laubach-Williams model estimates; 2026 release dates are 20 March, 29 May, 27 August and 27 November); Richmond Fed Lubik-Matthes r* | Four scheduled releases a year | Turns "are rates high" into a falsifiable claim: the real policy stance is the real rate minus r*. r* itself is unobservable and is revised as a whole when the sample lengthens, so use the trend and range, not one release's point estimate. |
| Bank credit standards (loan officer surveys) | Process | Federal Reserve *Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey on Bank Lending Practices (SLOOS)*; ECB *Bank Lending Survey (BLS)*; Bank of Japan *Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey on Bank Lending Practices at Large Japanese Banks*; PBoC *Bankers' Questionnaire Survey* loan-approval index | All quarterly (SLOOS is usually released about a week after the FOMC meeting) | Among the strongest public leading indicators in L1: tighter credit standards usually lead the contraction of actual credit balances by 2-4 quarters and corporate defaults by longer. It measures "willingness to supply", so read it separately from the demand-side items (loan demand); when the two diverge, demand has collapsed rather than banks becoming reluctant. Classification note: the survey reflects behaviour that banks are already tightening or loosening this quarter and can reverse next quarter, so it is a mechanism, not a locked-in condition. |
| Bank credit balances and composition | Process | Federal Reserve *H.8 Assets and Liabilities of Commercial Banks in the United States* (weekly; commercial and industrial, real estate, consumer loans); ECB Data Portal MFI loan statistics (BSI); PBoC *Sources and Uses of Credit Funds of Financial Institutions (RMB)* and *Financial Statistics Report* | H.8 weekly (Friday); ECB monthly; PBoC monthly | The central account of credit creation. Weekly H.8 swings often come from accounting causes such as mergers, reclassifications and asset transfers (for example during the 2023 bank events), so always check the Fed's Notes on the Data before interpreting direction. |
| Total credit creation (including non-bank channels) | Process | BIS *Credit to the non-financial sector* (two bases: "bank credit" and "all sources"); PBoC *Total Social Financing stock/flow statistical reports* (data sources include the National Financial Regulatory Administration, CSRC, China Central Depository & Clearing and NAFMII); Federal Reserve *Z.1 Financial Accounts of the United States* credit-market-instruments tables | BIS quarterly; China total social financing monthly (usually mid next month); Z.1 quarterly (about 10 weeks lag) | Upgrades "how much did banks lend" to "how much money did the real economy actually get". For China, use total social financing rather than RMB loans alone, because substitution among government bonds, entrusted/trust loans and direct finance often moves the two in opposite directions. |
| Credit-to-GDP gap | Process | BIS *Credit-to-GDP gaps* (the difference between the credit-to-GDP ratio and its one-sided HP trend; the common reference point of the Basel III countercyclical capital buffer) | Quarterly | The most formal credit-overheating warning in the Basel framework, and Schularick-Taylor-type empirics also support credit growth as the most robust leading variable for crises. Flaw: the end-point problem, since the latest gap is revised as a whole when later data arrive, so the real-time value cannot be the only basis. Classification note: all inputs are stocks that have already happened, the lead is a statistical association and not a physical precedence, and there are end-point revisions. |
| Corporate credit supply: bond issuance, maturity wall and non-bank/private credit | Process | SIFMA *US Corporate Bond Statistics* (issuance, outstanding, trading); Federal Reserve *Z.1 Financial Accounts* non-bank sector tables; FSB *Global Monitoring Report on Non-Bank Financial Intermediation*; SEC *Private Funds Statistics* (Form PF aggregates); Federal Reserve *Financial Stability Report* | SIFMA monthly; Z.1 quarterly; FSB annual; SEC Private Funds Statistics quarterly (long lag); FSR twice a year | The locus of crisis has moved from regulated banks to non-banks. The maturity wall (the amount maturing in the next 12-24 months) combined with the current rate level gives the timetable of the "refinancing shock" directly; it is the most direct way to turn Minsky's three-stage financing taxonomy into observable data. |
| Cross-border capital flows and international investment position | Process | IMF *Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Statistics (BOP/IIP)*; U.S. Treasury *Treasury International Capital (TIC)* monthly and annual survey; IMF CPIS; China State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) "Bank-Client Cross-border Receipts and Payments" and "Bank Foreign Exchange Settlement and Sales" monthly | IMF BOP quarterly; TIC monthly (about 6 weeks lag, with large revisions); CPIS semi-annual; SAFE monthly | TIC attributes holdings by custodian country, not by beneficial owner; holdings booked in Belgium, Luxembourg or the Cayman Islands do not represent local money. Before concluding "country X is selling Treasuries", look through the custody location and remove valuation effects. On the Chinese basis, the gap between FX settlement/sales and cross-border receipts/payments is a high-frequency proxy for willingness to convert export proceeds and for carry-trade unwinds. |
| Global dollar credit and global liquidity indicators | Process | BIS *Global liquidity indicators* (USD credit to non-US non-bank borrowers); BIS *Locational banking statistics*; BIS *Triennial Central Bank Survey* FX market turnover | GLI and LBS quarterly; Triennial every 3 years (April survey, first release in September) | The dollar is the liability side of the global balance sheet. When growth in offshore dollar credit turns negative, it often precedes funding crises in emerging markets; this is the data handle on Rey's "global financial cycle". |
| Exchange rates: nominal/real effective exchange rates and the broad dollar index | _Outcome_ | BIS *Effective exchange rate indices* (NEER/REER, broad and narrow baskets); Federal Reserve *H.10 Foreign Exchange Rates* and the Nominal/Real Broad U.S. Dollar Index; IMF IFS | BIS monthly (a daily NEER is also available); H.10 weekly (daily rates) | The exchange rate is the price-clearing result of all L1 variables, hence Outcome. Its research value is to use REER deviation from the long-run mean as a valuation gauge, and the dollar index as the master gate of global financial conditions: dollar strength is a balance-sheet shock to emerging markets, not only a trade shock. |
| Credit spreads (investment-grade / high-yield option-adjusted spread) | _Outcome_ | ICE BofA index OAS, publicly available on FRED: BAMLH0A0HYM2 (US High Yield OAS), BAMLC0A0CM (US Corporate OAS), BAMLH0A3HYC (CCC and below); euro-area counterpart BAMLHE00EHYIOAS (Warning: because of ICE licensing, FRED keeps only about the last 3 years of the BAML* series; longer history needs an ICE licence) | Daily | The measure of risk appetite closest to fundamentals. Tighter spreads do not necessarily mean better risk appetite; they can come from changes in index duration and rating mix (CCC weight, energy share), so read the spread's historical percentile together with the index composition. The CCC-minus-BB spread (the quality spread) turns before the total spread. Classification note: spreads are prices, hence Outcome; "turns earlier" only shortens the time to confirmation. The Process in the load-bearing chain lands on quantity indicators such as credit issuance and default rates. |
| Volatility and tail pricing (VIX / SKEW / rate volatility) | _Outcome_ | Cboe *VIX Index* and *SKEW Index* history (free daily data on Cboe's site; for scripts fetch cdn.cboe.com/api/global/us_indices/daily_prices/VIX_History.csv and SKEW_History.csv, preferred over FRED reposts); FRED: VIXCLS; for rate volatility use CME option implied volatility or OFR monitoring tools (the ICE BofA MOVE index is commercially licensed, not a free public source) | Daily | Strictly an Outcome: VIX is the model-free implied price of known risks in 30-day options and contains no unknown information. Low VIX often appears at the moment of highest leverage (reflexivity between volatility and leverage), and reading low volatility as "safe" is exactly the Minsky-style misjudgement. The divergence between SKEW and VIX is more informative than the VIX level. |

| Indicator | Nature | Source | Frequency | Key caution |
|---|:--:|---|---|---|
| Concentration of a step: CR5 / HHI | Process | Computed from TrendForce / Omdia / Yole / IDC share data (paid); or build an approximation from export country shares by HS code from China Customs (General Administration of Customs) and UN Comtrade | Quarterly / annual | Concentration is only a necessary condition for a bottleneck. _Gross-margin gaps and the flow of bargaining power._
