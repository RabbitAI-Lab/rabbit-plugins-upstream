# Read It. Reframe It. Own It

The dividing line in building a knowledge system is **goal characterization**:
first clarify your learning goal, then identify a key framework around that goal
to serve as the knowledge tree's trunk. Without this step, organizing knowledge
can easily become copy-and-paste.

For example, an author describes “three key mistakes in sales.” Drawing those
three mistakes as branches of a mind map merely copies the author's knowledge.
To build a knowledge tree, first consider the key pieces of sales, such as
value, trust, and competition; then absorb the methods, mistakes, and other
ideas you encounter into the corresponding pieces according to their roles.

This is **letting the classics serve your own understanding**: organize what
you learn around your own goal and key framework, so new knowledge becomes
pieces of that framework and gradually forms a genuine knowledge system.
