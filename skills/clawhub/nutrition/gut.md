# Gut — Fiber, Microbiome, and the Symptoms That Come With Them

Most "my gut hates healthy food" reports are a ramp problem, a fermentable-carbohydrate problem, or a fluid problem — three fixable things. This file covers the ramp protocol, the common symptom routes, and what the microbiome interventions actually deliver.

**Before changing fiber or starting an elimination**, read `## Reactions` in `~/Clawic/data/nutrition/memory.md` (or `reactions.md` if `## Boxes` points there) and the conditions in `~/Clawic/data/health/profile.md`. A prior IBD or celiac diagnosis changes every recommendation here, and a reaction history stops the same food being re-trialed a third time.

## The Fiber Ramp

The protocol, in full, because this is where most attempts fail:

1. **Establish the baseline.** Estimate current intake over 3 days. Guessing the starting point makes the increment meaningless.
2. **+5 g per week, no more.** From 15 g to 28 g is roughly a 3-week ramp, not a Monday decision.
3. **Fluid rises with it.** Bulking fiber without fluid produces harder stools, which is the exact opposite of the intent, and is the most common self-inflicted failure.
4. **One new source at a time.** Legumes, then whole grains, then a fermentable source. When something causes symptoms, the culprit is identifiable.
5. **Two symptom-free weeks before the next step.** Gas during the first 1-2 weeks of a legume increase is expected microbial adaptation, not intolerance — say so in advance, or the attempt ends on day four.
6. **Split across meals.** 30 g at dinner behaves differently from 10 g at each of three meals.
7. **If symptoms persist past 3-4 weeks at a level**, it is not adaptation: drop back one step and change the type before changing the amount (`diet-quality.md` has the type table).

Ramping is also the answer for the reverse case: someone leaving a low-fiber phase (post-illness, post-low-FODMAP, post-keto) restarts at their tolerated level, not at the target.

## Symptom Routes

| Symptom | First suspects | First move |
|---|---|---|
| Gas and bloating starting after a diet improvement | Fermentable fiber ramped too fast | Back off one step, hold two weeks, resume (`## Reactions` gets the dates) |
| Bloating with no diet change, ongoing months | FODMAP sensitivity, IBS, lactose, SIBO, celiac | Celiac screen before any gluten removal — removing gluten first destroys the test |
| Bloating within 1-2 hours of dairy | Lactose intolerance | Trial with hard cheese and yogurt kept in — most people tolerate them (`restrictions.md`) |
| Loose stools with sugar-free products | Sugar alcohols (sorbitol, mannitol, xylitol, maltitol) above ~10-20 g | Read labels for -itol endings; often the whole answer |
| Constipation on a "healthy" diet | Insoluble fiber up, fluid flat | Fluid first, then magnesium-rich food, then kiwi or psyllium |
| Alternating constipation and diarrhea, with pain | IBS pattern | Structured low-FODMAP with reintroduction, ideally clinician-supervised |
| Reflux worse after a change | Meal size, timing, fat, and alcohol before food identity | Portion and timing before elimination |
| Weight loss, blood, nocturnal symptoms, fever, or new symptoms over 50 | Not a fiber question | Stop and route to a clinician (`safety.md`) |
| Anything else | Log food, amount, symptom, and onset for two weeks before theorizing | `## Reactions` |

## FODMAPs

Fermentable oligosaccharides, disaccharides, monosaccharides, and polyols — short-chain carbohydrates that draw water into the gut and ferment quickly. Developed at Monash University for IBS.

- **It is a three-phase diagnostic protocol, not a diet**: strict restriction 2-6 weeks, then structured reintroduction one group at a time, then a personalized long-term diet that is as broad as tolerance allows.
- Phase 1 is the part people find online and the only part they run. Staying in restriction indefinitely narrows the diet, starves the microbiome of fermentable substrate, and cuts fiber, calcium, and B vitamins.
- Reintroduction is the phase with the value: most people tolerate several groups and only react to one or two, and the reintroduction is what tells them which.
- **Screen for celiac before restricting wheat**, because the restriction invalidates the test.
- Cross-check every phase against the health profile: a low-FODMAP diet on top of an existing exclusion can leave very little.
- Each phase and its dates go into `artifacts/fodmap-protocol.md` — three phases across two months is exactly the thing that gets lost mid-way.

## Probiotics, Prebiotics, Fermented Food

| Intervention | What the evidence supports | Practical use |
|---|---|---|
| Probiotics | Strain-specific and indication-specific; the strongest cases are antibiotic-associated diarrhea and some IBS symptoms with particular strains. Effects generally do not persist after stopping | Name the strain and the indication or skip it. A generic "gut health" product has no target and no endpoint |
| Prebiotics (inulin, GOS, resistant starch) | Feed existing bacteria; measurable microbiome shifts | Ramp them like any fermentable fiber; they are the most gas-producing category |
| Fermented foods (yogurt, kefir, sauerkraut, kimchi, miso) | A small controlled trial (Wastyk 2021, Stanford) found increased microbiota diversity and lowered inflammatory markers on a high-fermented-food arm, where a high-fiber arm did not show the same effect over the same period | The most food-shaped intervention here, and cheap. Pasteurized versions on the shelf contain no live cultures |
| Synbiotics and "biome" test kits | Consumer microbiome sequencing does not yet produce validated individual recommendations | Decline to interpret; spend the money on food variety |

Fiber variety beats any capsule for microbiome goals, and it delivers nutrients at the same time. That is the default recommendation here.

## Constipation

Work in this order, stopping when it resolves:

1. **Fluid** — the cheapest and most often missing (`water`).
2. **Soluble and bulking fiber** — psyllium is the best-tolerated single addition; add it with fluid, at a ramped dose.
3. **Kiwi, prunes, or figs** — two kiwifruit daily has trial support for stool frequency in constipation, and it is food.
4. **Magnesium-rich foods**, and magnesium citrate or oxide where a clinician has approved a laxative effect — oxide is poorly absorbed precisely because it draws water in.
5. **Movement and routine** — the non-food half, and the half that usually gets skipped.
6. **Review medications** — opioids, iron supplements, some antidepressants, and calcium carbonate all constipate. Iron is the one this skill often introduced itself: switching to alternate-day dosing usually resolves it (`supplements.md`).

Red flags that leave this list immediately: blood, unintended weight loss, a new persistent change in bowel habit over 50, or a family history of colorectal cancer (`safety.md`).

## Diarrhea and Loose Stools

- Sugar alcohols, excess fructose, caffeine, and alcohol before anything exotic.
- Lactose, if dairy correlates and symptoms arrive within a couple of hours.
- Fat malabsorption signs — pale, floating, foul-smelling stools — are a clinician question and point at pancreatic insufficiency or celiac (`conditions.md`).
- Persistent diarrhea costs potassium, magnesium, zinc, and fluid, and the replacement is part of the answer (`water` for the fluid and electrolyte side).
- Soluble fiber (oats, psyllium) firms stools; insoluble bran worsens them. Getting this backwards is the most common self-treatment error.

## Bloating Without a Diagnosis

A two-week structured observation beats a guess:

- Log food, amount, timing, and symptom onset in `## Reactions` — onset time is what separates lactose (~1-2 h) from a fermentable load (~4-8 h).
- Note the non-food variables in the same rows: meal size, eating speed, carbonation, stress, sleep, and cycle phase, all of which move bloating independently.
- After two weeks, the pattern either names a candidate — which then gets a single-food elimination and reintroduction (`restrictions.md`) — or it does not, which is itself the finding and points at portion, pace, and IBS-type patterns rather than a culprit food.
- Do not run three eliminations at once. Simultaneous removals produce an answer that cannot be attributed and a diet that cannot be rebuilt.

## Nutrient Costs of Gut Diets

Every gut-motivated restriction removes nutrients, and naming the replacement is part of the recommendation:

| Restriction | Removes | Replace with |
|---|---|---|
| Low-FODMAP (phase 1) | Fiber, calcium (if dairy out), prebiotic substrate, some B vitamins | Low-FODMAP fiber sources (oats, kiwi, firm tofu, quinoa), lactose-free dairy or fortified alternatives |
| Dairy-free | Calcium, iodine, B2, B12, protein | Calcium-set tofu, fortified plant milk **checked for calcium and B12**, canned fish with bones (`restrictions.md`) |
| Gluten-free | Fiber, folate, iron, B vitamins — most gluten-free flours are unfortified | Oats where tolerated and certified, buckwheat, quinoa, legumes (`patterns.md`) |
| Low-fiber (post-flare, post-surgery) | Fiber, magnesium, potassium, prebiotic substrate | Time-limited by design: hold the ramp plan for when the clinician clears it |

**Write in the same turn**: every food-symptom observation as a row in `## Reactions` with the food, amount, symptom, and onset; the ramp's current level and start date in `## Nutrient Status` under fiber; the reintroduction or next-step date in `## Due`; and any multi-phase protocol (FODMAP, elimination) in `artifacts/` with its `## Boxes` line (`memory-template.md`). Onset times and dates are the whole dataset here — an undated symptom memory proves nothing in week six.
