# Diet Quality — What Makes a Diet Good Beyond the Numbers

Two people can hit identical calories and macros and eat completely different diets. This file is the other axis: fiber, processing, the sodium-to-potassium ratio, added sugar, fat quality, and plant variety. Each is measurable, each has a target, and each has one swap that moves it most.

**Before scoring anything**, read `## Usual Foods` and `## Nutrient Status` in `~/Clawic/data/nutrition/memory.md`, plus the latest rollups in `intake/<year>.md` if `## Boxes` points there. Quality is a trend, and a single day is not one. Skip this file entirely if a Red Flags signal is present — a quality score is the wrong object to hand someone with a restrictive pattern (`safety.md`).

## The Six Measures

| Measure | Target | Typical Western intake | The one swap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fiber | 14 g per 1000 kcal (~25 g F / 38 g M) | ~15 g/day | Legumes at one meal a day: +8-10 g in a single move |
| Ultra-processed share of energy | Lower is better; there is no threshold to defend | ~50%+ of energy in the US and UK | Replace the highest-volume single UPF item, not all of them |
| Sodium : potassium | Potassium above sodium in mg | Inverted — sodium high, potassium ~half the AI | Add a potassium-dense food before removing salt |
| Added sugar | <10% of energy (WHO), <5% for further benefit | ~13% of energy in US adults | Sugar-sweetened drinks first; they carry no satiety |
| Saturated fat | <10% of energy (US DGA); replace with unsaturated, not with refined carbohydrate | ~11-12% | The replacement is the intervention, not the removal |
| Distinct plants per week | 20-30 is a widely used practical target for microbiome diversity | Most people count under 15 | Herbs, spices, nuts, and seeds count and are free wins |

Report two or three of these, never all six at once. A person given six deficits fixes none.

## Fiber

Not one nutrient. The type decides the effect, and "eat more fiber" fails because the wrong type was added for the goal.

| Type | Found in | Does | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soluble, viscous (beta-glucan, pectin, psyllium) | Oats, barley, apples, citrus, psyllium | Forms a gel; lowers LDL cholesterol and slows glucose absorption | Cholesterol, glycemic response, loose stools |
| Insoluble (cellulose, bran) | Wheat bran, vegetable skins, nuts | Adds bulk and speeds transit | Constipation — with fluid, or it makes it worse |
| Fermentable (resistant starch, inulin, GOS) | Cooked-and-cooled potato and rice, legumes, onion, garlic, chicory | Feeds the microbiome, produces short-chain fatty acids | Microbiome goals — and the source of gas during a ramp |
| Non-fermentable bulking | Psyllium mostly | Bulks without much gas | People who bloat on everything else (`gut.md`) |

- Target: 14 g per 1000 kcal (US DRI). At 2000 kcal that is 28 g.
- Ramp at +5 g/week with fluid alongside (SKILL.md Rule 8). Two weeks of tolerance is the gate before the next step.
- The gap-closing foods, per serving: legumes 200 g cooked ~8-15 g · chia 30 g ~10 g · raspberries 100 g ~6.5 g · oats 40 g dry ~4 g · whole-wheat bread 2 slices ~4-5 g.
- Fiber supplements are the fallback, not the plan: they add one type and none of the nutrients the food carries.

## Ultra-Processed Share

The NOVA framework's group 4: formulations of substances extracted from foods plus additives, industrially assembled — not simply "processed". Canned beans, frozen vegetables, and plain yogurt are minimally processed; the packaged bread, the flavored yogurt drink, and the reconstituted meat product are not.

- The strongest single piece of evidence is a controlled feeding trial (Hall 2019, n=20, 2-week crossover, matched for presented calories, sugar, fat, sodium, and fiber): the ultra-processed arm ate roughly 500 kcal/day more and gained weight. It is small, and it is the cleanest causal signal the field has.
- Observational associations with cardiometabolic outcomes are consistent but confounded, and the critique that UPF is a proxy for sugar, salt, and fat is fair (SKILL.md, Where Experts Disagree).
- Practical use: **share of energy, and the top three contributors by volume**. "Cut ultra-processed food" is unusable; "your two daily sweetened drinks and the packaged breakfast cereal are ~40% of it" is a plan.
- The realistic target is displacement, not elimination: one category replaced at a time, keeping the convenience the item provided. A UPF item removed with nothing convenient replacing it comes back within a fortnight.

## Sodium and Potassium

- Sodium: US CDRR 2300 mg/day; WHO and EFSA use ~2000 mg. Roughly 70% of intake comes from processed and restaurant food, not from the shaker — which is why "I don't add salt" is compatible with a high intake.
- Potassium: AI 3400 mg men / 2600 mg women. Most people sit far below it, and this is the half that gets ignored.
- The ratio is the target: potassium above sodium in mg. Adding potassium is easier and better tolerated than subtracting sodium, and it moves the ratio from the direction people actually enjoy.
- Potassium-dense per serving: white beans 200 g ~1000 mg · baked potato with skin ~900 mg · tomato paste 50 g ~550 mg · yogurt 170 g ~250 mg · banana ~420 mg.
- Label reading: EU labels give **salt**, US labels give **sodium**. Salt g × 400 = sodium mg (`labels.md`).
- **Inverted in CKD, and in some heart-failure and medication contexts**: potassium restriction, not encouragement, and potassium-chloride salt substitutes are hazardous with ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and potassium-sparing diuretics (`conditions.md`, `interactions.md`).

## Added Sugar

- WHO: below 10% of energy, with further benefit below 5%. On 2000 kcal, 10% is 50 g and 5% is 25 g.
- Added sugar is labelled separately in the US and is inferred from the ingredient list in the EU. Fruit sugar in whole fruit is not the target and never has been.
- Sugar-sweetened drinks are the priority because they deliver energy without satiety and are usually a single habit rather than a diet-wide pattern.
- Sugar goes by many names in an ingredient list — syrups, concentrates, anything ending in -ose, malt, molasses. Several appear separately so none reaches the top of the list, which is itself the signal (`labels.md`).
- Sugar alcohols and non-nutritive sweeteners: useful for displacing sugar-sweetened drinks; sugar alcohols above ~10-20 g cause GI symptoms and are a frequent hidden cause of bloating (`gut.md`).

## Fat Quality

- The intervention is **replacement**, not reduction: saturated fat replaced by polyunsaturated fat lowers cardiovascular risk; replaced by refined carbohydrate, it does not. Any advice to cut saturated fat states what takes its place.
- Trans fat from partial hydrogenation is now restricted or banned in many jurisdictions; residual sources are labelled and can still be rounded to zero per serving under some rules.
- Omega-3 EPA and DHA: two portions of oily fish a week, algal oil where fish is not eaten. ALA is not a substitute (`micronutrients.md`).
- Practical swaps: olive oil for butter in cooking; nuts and seeds as the snack; oily fish twice a week; fatty cuts and processed meat reduced rather than lean protein increased in the abstract.
- Low-fat diets sabotage fat-soluble vitamin and carotenoid absorption (`absorption.md`) — a real cost of the fat-phobic version of this advice.

## Plant Variety

- 20-30 distinct plants a week is a practical microbiome-diversity target rather than a validated requirement. Say which it is.
- Everything botanical counts once per week: herbs, spices, nuts, seeds, whole grains, legumes, coffee, cocoa. This is what makes the target achievable rather than an expense.
- Variety is the cheapest lever available in this file: swapping between three legumes and four grains costs nothing and delivers different fibers, polyphenols, and minerals.
- Diversity also spreads the risk of a single-source shortfall — the person eating the same four vegetables has one gap list, not four.

## Running a Quality Review

The `review_cadence` cycle, in order:

1. Pick the two measures furthest from target — never more.
2. State the current number, the target, and the gap in the same sentence.
3. Name one swap per measure, with the serving size and what it delivers.
4. Check the swap against `~/Clawic/data/health/profile.md`: allergies, conditions, medications, and CKD-style inversions.
5. Set the next review in `## Due`, and say what would count as progress.

Do not produce a composite score. A single number invites optimization of the number, hides which measure moved, and is the format most likely to feed a restrictive pattern.

**Write in the same turn**: the rollup row into `~/Clawic/data/nutrition/intake/<year>.md` with days logged, fiber average, and the nutrients short and over; any food newly in rotation into `## Usual Foods`; the next review into `## Due` (`memory-template.md`). Week-over-week is the only view that makes a quality claim credible, and it requires the rows to exist.
