# Safety — Escalation, Toxicity, and Knowing When to Stop

This skill hands out doses and reads blood work, so it needs a hard edge. Three categories cross it: a symptom that is not nutritional, a dose that can harm, and a user for whom tracking itself is the harm. Each has an observable trigger and a scripted response.

**Before any escalation decision**, read the conditions, medications, and life stage in `~/Clawic/data/health/profile.md` and `## Notes` in `~/Clawic/data/nutrition/memory.md` — a flag raised in a previous session and forgotten is a flag raised for nothing.

## The Escalation Table

Expands SKILL.md's Red Flags with what to do and how fast.

| Signal | Why it matters | Urgency | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Neurological signs with low or borderline B12 — numbness, tingling, unsteady gait, memory change | Subacute combined degeneration; recovery falls with delay | This week | Clinician. No folate, no "start a tablet and see" |
| Unintended weight loss, dysphagia, blood in stool, persistent vomiting, night sweats, fever | Not a nutrient gap | Prompt clinician | Stop the nutrition work and say why |
| Iron deficiency in a man or a postmenopausal woman | GI blood loss until excluded | Prompt clinician | Iron may be started by the clinician; the investigation is the point |
| High ferritin with no inflammation, or transferrin saturation >45% | Iron overload, possibly hemochromatosis | Clinician | No iron, no vitamin C mega-doses with iron-rich meals |
| Any planned dose above the UL, or a stack summing above it | Cumulative toxicity | Immediate, in-session | Recalculate and state the sum (SKILL.md Rule 3); do not proceed on reassurance |
| Pregnancy with preformed vitamin A above the UL, retinoid medication, or liver several times a week | Teratogenic | Immediate | Stop that source; prenatal care owns the rest (`populations.md`) |
| Symptoms of hypercalcemia — thirst, frequent urination, confusion, constipation — on high-dose vitamin D or calcium | Vitamin D toxicity or hypercalcemia | Urgent clinician | Stop the supplement, clinician now |
| Numbness or unsteady gait on high-dose B6 | B6 peripheral neuropathy, may be slow to reverse | Prompt | Stop every B6 source and route |
| A child or teenager on a self-imposed restrictive diet, or a growth concern | Growth and development | Prompt | Decline restriction guidance, route to a pediatric clinician |
| CKD with potassium, phosphorus, or protein questions | Standard advice inverts and hyperkalemia is dangerous | Immediate | Clinician's numbers only (`conditions.md`) |
| Supplement alongside warfarin, levothyroxine, methotrexate, chemotherapy, immunosuppressants, antiretrovirals | Narrow therapeutic index | Immediate | Produce the interaction statement, not the adjustment (`interactions.md`) |
| Prolonged inadequate intake and now refeeding — after starvation, extended fasting, alcohol use disorder, or hyperemesis | Refeeding syndrome: phosphate, potassium, magnesium, thiamin shifts | Medical emergency | Clinician before food is scaled up |
| Guilt language, growing exclusion lists, distress at imprecision, "clean" and "dirty" framing | Disordered or orthorexic pattern | Immediate change of approach | Stop tracking and scoring; see below |
| Anything progressive, one-sided, or new and unexplained | Outside this skill | Prompt clinician | Say so plainly rather than searching for a nutrient that fits |

Anything in this table suspends every protocol in the skill. Suspension means the coverage math, the supplement plan, and the tracking stop — not that the conversation ends.

## Toxicity by Nutrient

Reachable ceilings, and what crossing them looks like. This is why every dose gets summed.

| Nutrient | UL | What excess does | The usual route to it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vitamin A (preformed) | 3000 µg RAE | Liver damage, bone effects, birth defects; acute dosing causes headache and skin peeling | Liver eaten often, cod liver oil, high-dose retinol supplements |
| Vitamin D | 4000 IU | Hypercalcemia: thirst, urination, confusion, kidney stones and damage | Self-prescribed 10,000 IU regimens taken indefinitely without a 25-OH D |
| Iron | 45 mg | GI symptoms, and in overload states organ damage. **Acute overdose is a leading cause of poisoning death in young children** | Unneeded supplementation, and a bottle within a child's reach |
| Zinc | 40 mg | Copper deficiency with anemia and neurological effects; nausea acutely | Stacked multivitamin plus immune product plus lozenges |
| Selenium | 400 µg | Selenosis: hair and nail loss, garlic breath, GI symptoms, neuropathy | Brazil nuts eaten as a habit, plus a supplement |
| B6 | 100 mg | Peripheral neuropathy, sometimes slow to reverse | High-dose B-complex or "energy" products taken long term |
| Niacin (nicotinic acid) | 35 mg supplemental | Flushing from ~30-50 mg; hepatotoxicity at gram doses | Lipid-directed high-dose regimens taken without supervision |
| Folic acid | 1000 µg supplemental | Masks B12 deficiency while nerve damage progresses | Multivitamin plus a fortified diet plus a separate folate product |
| Iodine | 1100 µg | Thyroid dysfunction in both directions | Kelp supplements and seaweed eaten in quantity |
| Vitamin C | 2000 mg | GI distress, oxalate load and stone risk in susceptible people | Gram-dose "immune" regimens |
| Magnesium | 350 mg supplemental (food exempt) | Diarrhea first; serious effects in kidney impairment | Not usually harmful in healthy kidneys — the exemption for food is the misread part |
| Vitamin E | 1000 mg supplemental | Bleeding risk, especially with anticoagulants | High-dose antioxidant regimens |
| Potassium supplements | Capped in over-the-counter form | Hyperkalemia, arrhythmia — dangerous with ACE inhibitors, ARBs, potassium-sparing diuretics | Salt substitutes and supplements taken without knowing the medication list |

Two people can reach a UL with none of them intending to: the multivitamin, the targeted single nutrient, the fortified breakfast, and the "immune" product each look reasonable alone.

## Disordered Eating

A tracking tool in the wrong hands amplifies harm, and micronutrient tracking has a specific risk profile: it provides an endless supply of things to be deficient in and an infinite exclusion rationale.

Signals, observable in what the user writes:

- Moral language about food — clean, dirty, good, bad, toxic, "cheating".
- An exclusion list that keeps growing without a diagnosis or a completed reintroduction.
- Distress at imprecision, or repeated requests for exact figures the domain cannot supply.
- Compensation framing: exercise as payment for food, or a nutrient as penance.
- Fear-driven questions about foods rather than curiosity-driven ones.
- Escalating tracking depth without a decision ever following from the data.
- Rigidity that has started costing social eating, travel, or work.

Response: **stop the optimization work entirely.** No coverage percentages, no shortfall lists, no new exclusions, no scores. Say what is happening in plain terms, and route. Support lines, when the user is in a country where they operate: ANAD 1-888-375-7767 (US), Beat 0808 801 0677 (UK). Where neither applies, route to their clinician or a local eating-disorder service.

Do not argue about whether it qualifies as a disorder, and do not require a diagnosis to change approach.

## Response Scripts

Wording that escalates without alarming, and without hedging into uselessness.

- **B12 with neurological signs**: "Tingling with a low or borderline B12 needs a doctor this week rather than a supplement — nerve involvement is the one form of this where waiting costs recovery. Ask specifically for B12 with methylmalonic acid."
- **Iron requested with no ferritin**: "I can help you get iron up, but not before a ferritin. Iron is one of the few nutrients where taking it when you do not need it does harm, and it accumulates. A ferritin with a CRP settles it."
- **A stack over the UL**: "Summed across your three products and your food, zinc comes to about 50 mg a day against an upper limit of 40. Sustained above 40, zinc causes a copper deficiency. Dropping the multivitamin brings it to 35 and loses you nothing you are not getting elsewhere."
- **Something not nutritional**: "This does not read like a nutrient gap — the pattern you are describing needs a doctor to look at it. I can hold the nutrition side until you have."
- **Disordered pattern**: "I am going to stop giving you numbers on this. The way we are talking about food has moved somewhere that tracking makes worse, not better, and I would rather say that than keep counting. Talking to someone about it is a stronger move than any change to your diet."
- **CKD**: "Most of what I would normally say about potassium is the opposite of what applies with kidney disease. Your nephrology team's targets are the ones to work from, and I can help you eat to them."

## Who to Route To

Naming the right professional makes the referral usable:

- **Pharmacist** — supplement and medication interactions. The most accessible clinician for this, and the one who owns the question.
- **Registered dietitian or nutritionist (regulated title in the user's country)** — therapeutic diets, malabsorption, conditions requiring a full assessment.
- **GP or primary care** — labs, unexplained symptoms, everything undiagnosed.
- **The specialist team, when one exists** — nephrology for CKD, gastroenterology for IBD and celiac, the bariatric team, obstetrics in pregnancy. Their numbers win.
- **Eating-disorder services** — the disordered-eating signals above.
- **Emergency care** — suspected acute overdose, especially iron in a child; anaphylaxis; hypercalcemia symptoms.

## What This Skill Never Does

- Diagnose. A pattern consistent with a deficiency is a reason to test, not a diagnosis.
- Advise stopping, starting, or changing a prescribed medication.
- Recommend doses above the UL, except when transcribing a clinician's protocol and saying so.
- Recommend iron without a ferritin, or folate before B12 status is known.
- Interpret hair mineral analysis, IgG food panels, or consumer microbiome kits as if they meant something.
- Give a coverage score, a shortfall list, or a new exclusion to someone showing disordered-eating signals.
- Run restriction protocols with a minor, or in pregnancy, without clinician involvement.
- Continue optimizing when energy intake is inadequate — that is `calories`, and possibly a clinical question.

## Recording a Flag

A flag raised and forgotten gets re-raised as a recommendation next month.

**Write in the same turn**: one line in `## Notes` of `~/Clawic/data/nutrition/memory.md` — the date, the signal, what was declined, and what was recommended instead; the underlying fact (condition, medication, life stage) into `~/Clawic/data/health/profile.md` if it was new; and any follow-up the user agreed to into `## Due` (`memory-template.md`). If the user asks again for the same declined thing, the note is what makes the second answer consistent with the first, which is the only version of this that holds.
