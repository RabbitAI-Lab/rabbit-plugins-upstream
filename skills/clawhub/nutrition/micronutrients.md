# Micronutrients — The Reference

Adult figures, US DRI unless marked; `reference_standard` switches the numbers, and the divergences worth knowing are at the bottom. Every entry answers the same four questions: how much, what is the ceiling, who runs short, and what one serving actually delivers. SKILL.md's Priority Nutrients table is the short list; this is the full one.

**Before answering a "do I get enough" question**, read `## Nutrient Status` in `~/Clawic/data/nutrition/memory.md` (or `nutrients.md` if `## Boxes` points there) and `## Usual Foods`. Re-deriving a nutrient's status from scratch each time produces a different number each time, and the user notices.

## Fat-Soluble Vitamins

Stored in tissue, so they accumulate — the ULs here are the ones that get crossed. All four need dietary fat in the same meal to absorb (`absorption.md`).

| Nutrient | RDA/AI | UL | Runs short in | One serving delivers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A (retinol) | 900 µg RAE M / 700 F | 3000 µg RAE preformed | Fat malabsorption, very-low-fat diets | Beef liver 85 g ≈ 6500 µg RAE — twice the UL in one portion; carrot 1 medium ≈ 500 µg RAE as beta-carotene, no UL |
| D | 600 IU (15 µg); 800 IU at 71+ | 4000 IU (100 µg) | Winter above ~40°, dark skin, indoors, obesity (sequestered in adipose), malabsorption | Salmon 100 g ≈ 400-600 IU; egg yolk ≈ 40 IU; UV-exposed mushrooms 100 g ≈ 400+ IU; fortified milk 240 ml ≈ 100 IU |
| E | 15 mg alpha-tocopherol | 1000 mg supplemental | Very-low-fat diets, fat malabsorption | Sunflower seeds 30 g ≈ 10 mg; almonds 30 g ≈ 7 mg |
| K | 120 µg M / 90 F (AI) | none set | Fat malabsorption, long antibiotic courses | Kale 100 g cooked ≈ 800 µg; natto is the K2 outlier. Warfarin makes *consistency* the target, not avoidance (`interactions.md`) |

- Vitamin D is measured, not estimated: sun exposure varies by latitude, season, skin, and behavior beyond any calculation worth doing. Above roughly 40° latitude, skin synthesis is effectively zero from November to March.
- Vitamin A toxicity is a preformed-retinol phenomenon. Beta-carotene from food does not cause it because conversion is regulated — but beta-carotene *supplements* raised lung cancer in smokers (ATBC, CARET), so the supplement and the carrot are not the same intervention.
- Vitamin E and K supplements interact with anticoagulants; both belong in the interaction check before the dose conversation.

## Water-Soluble Vitamins

Excreted rather than stored, with three exceptions that still reach a ceiling: B6, niacin, and folic acid.

| Nutrient | RDA/AI | UL | Runs short in | One serving delivers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 thiamin | 1.2 mg M / 1.1 F | none set | Heavy alcohol use, post-bariatric, hyperemesis | Pork 100 g ≈ 0.9 mg; fortified cereal varies wildly |
| B2 riboflavin | 1.3 mg M / 1.1 F | none set | Vegans, dairy avoiders | Milk 240 ml ≈ 0.45 mg; almonds 30 g ≈ 0.3 mg |
| B3 niacin | 16 mg NE M / 14 F | 35 mg from supplements/fortification | Rare in fortified countries | Chicken breast 100 g ≈ 12 mg NE. Nicotinic acid flushes from ~30-50 mg; nicotinamide does not |
| B5 pantothenic acid | 5 mg (AI) | none set | Essentially never in isolation | Widely distributed — the name means "from everywhere" |
| B6 | 1.3 mg (1.7 M / 1.5 F at 51+) | 100 mg | Alcohol use, some medications | Chickpeas 200 g ≈ 1.1 mg. Sustained supplemental doses cause peripheral neuropathy — the classic case of a water-soluble vitamin with real toxicity |
| B7 biotin | 30 µg (AI) | none set | Raw-egg-white habit (avidin binds it) | Egg yolk, liver, nuts. High-dose biotin **distorts many immunoassays** — thyroid and troponin included; stop it 48-72 h before blood work and tell the lab (`labs.md`) |
| B9 folate | 400 µg DFE (600 pregnancy) | 1000 µg from folic acid only | Preconception, alcohol use, celiac, some anticonvulsants | Lentils 200 g cooked ≈ 360 µg DFE; fortified flour is the population-level source, absent in most of Europe |
| B12 | 2.4 µg | none set | Vegans, 65+, metformin and PPI users, post-bariatric, pernicious anemia | Clams and liver are the extremes; salmon 100 g ≈ 3 µg; 240 ml fortified soy ≈ 1 µg. No reliable unfortified plant source — spirulina and nori carry inactive analogues that can *worsen* the picture |
| C | 90 mg M / 75 F (+35 smokers) | 2000 mg | Very-low-produce diets, smokers | Red pepper 100 g ≈ 125 mg; orange ≈ 70 mg. Doubles as the non-heme iron enhancer (`absorption.md`) |
| Choline | 550 mg M / 425 F (AI) | 3500 mg | Egg avoiders, vegans, pregnancy | Egg 1 large ≈ 145 mg; soy, liver, cruciferous. Commonly short and almost never measured |

## Major Minerals

| Nutrient | RDA/AI | UL | Runs short in | One serving delivers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calcium | 1000 mg; 1200 women 51+ and all 71+ | 2500 mg (2000 at 51+) | Dairy avoiders, vegans, post-menopause | Milk 240 ml ≈ 300 mg; yogurt 170 g ≈ 200 mg; sardines with bones 100 g ≈ 380 mg; calcium-set tofu 100 g ≈ 350 mg; kale 100 g cooked ≈ 150 mg well absorbed |
| Magnesium | 400-420 mg M / 310-320 F | 350 mg supplemental only | High-UPF diets, alcohol, diuretics, PPIs | Pumpkin seeds 30 g ≈ 150 mg; black beans 200 g ≈ 120 mg; dark chocolate 30 g ≈ 65 mg |
| Potassium | 3400 mg M / 2600 F (AI) | none for food | Almost everyone on low produce; inverted in CKD | Baked potato with skin ≈ 900 mg; white beans 200 g ≈ 1000 mg; banana ≈ 420 mg — the famous one is mid-table |
| Sodium | 1500 mg AI, CDRR 2300 mg | — | The problem is excess, except in heavy sweating and some conditions | ~70% of intake is from processed food, not the shaker (`diet-quality.md`) |
| Phosphorus | 700 mg | 4000 mg | Rarely short; additives are the CKD problem | Additive phosphate absorbs near-completely versus roughly 40-60% for the organic form in food (`conditions.md`) |

The magnesium UL is the most misread number in the field: **350 mg applies to supplemental magnesium only**, not to food. A diet delivering 500 mg from seeds and beans is not over any limit; a 400 mg citrate capsule on top of it is where the diarrhea comes from.

## Trace Minerals

| Nutrient | RDA/AI | UL | Runs short in | One serving delivers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Iron | 8 mg M and postmenopausal / 18 mg premenopausal / 27 mg pregnancy | 45 mg | Menstruating women, endurance athletes, vegans, donors, IBD/celiac | Beef 100 g ≈ 2.6 mg heme; lentils 200 g ≈ 6.6 mg non-heme at a fraction of the absorption (`absorption.md`) |
| Zinc | 11 mg M / 8 F | 40 mg | Vegans, IBD, alcohol, post-bariatric | Oysters 100 g ≈ 40 mg+; beef 100 g ≈ 5 mg; pumpkin seeds 30 g ≈ 2 mg. Vegetarian requirement runs up to ~50% higher because of phytate |
| Iodine | 150 µg (220 pregnancy, 290 lactation) | 1100 µg | Non-iodized-salt households, vegans, dairy avoiders | Iodized salt 1 g ≈ 45-77 µg; cod 100 g ≈ 100 µg; kelp is unpredictable and can exceed the UL in one sheet. **Sea salt, kosher salt, and pink salt are not iodized** |
| Selenium | 55 µg | 400 µg | Low-selenium soils | Brazil nut ≈ 68-91 µg each; tuna, eggs. Two nuts a day approaches the UL |
| Copper | 900 µg | 10 mg | Long-term high zinc, post-bariatric | Liver, cashews, cocoa. Usually a casualty of a zinc excess, not a dietary shortfall |
| Manganese | 2.3 mg M / 1.8 F (AI) | 11 mg | Almost never | Whole grains, tea, nuts |
| Chromium | 35 µg M / 25 F (AI) | none set | Not a real deficiency in practice | Supplements are widely sold for glycemic control; effects in trials are small and inconsistent |
| Molybdenum | 45 µg | 2000 µg | Essentially never | Legumes, grains |
| Fluoride | 4 mg M / 3 F (AI) | 10 mg | Non-fluoridated water | Water, tea |

## Fatty Acids and Fiber

- **EPA + DHA**: no US RDA; 250-500 mg/day is the common guidance and EFSA sets an AI of 250 mg. Oily fish 100 g delivers roughly 1000-2000 mg, so two portions a week covers it. ALA (flax, chia, walnuts) converts to EPA at roughly 5-8% and to DHA at well under 1%, which is why "I eat flax" does not substitute — algal oil does.
- **Linoleic and alpha-linolenic acid** are the two essential fatty acids; deficiency is a clinical rarity outside fat malabsorption and long-term fat-free parenteral nutrition.
- **Fiber**: 14 g per 1000 kcal (US DRI) — roughly 25 g/day women, 38 g men. Split by type and function in `diet-quality.md`; the ramp protocol is in `gut.md`.

## Food Doses Worth Memorizing

The servings that close a gap in one move. Reference these instead of "eat more X".

| Gap | The serving that closes it |
|---|---|
| Iron, plant-based | Lentils 200 g cooked (~6.6 mg) with a vitamin-C source in the same meal |
| Calcium, dairy-free | Calcium-set tofu 100 g (~350 mg) or fortified soy 240 ml (~300 mg) — check the label, not all are set with calcium |
| B12, vegan | A supplement. Fortified foods reach it only if three servings a day are reliable |
| Vitamin D, winter | Salmon 100 g twice a week plus a supplement; food alone rarely reaches 600 IU/day |
| Omega-3, no fish | Algal oil 250-500 mg EPA+DHA; flax provides ALA, which is a different nutrient in practice |
| Potassium | One baked potato with skin (~900 mg) beats three bananas |
| Magnesium | Pumpkin seeds 30 g (~150 mg) is the densest everyday food |
| Iodine, no dairy or salt | Cod 100 g (~100 µg) or a measured 1 g of iodized salt |
| Folate, unfortified country | Lentils or chickpeas 200 g cooked; leafy greens lose folate to boiling water (`absorption.md`) |
| Choline | Two eggs (~290 mg) is over half the female AI |

## Unit Conversions

Getting these wrong is the most common arithmetic error in the domain.

- Vitamin D: 1 µg = 40 IU. So 600 IU = 15 µg, 1000 IU = 25 µg, the 4000 IU UL = 100 µg.
- Vitamin A: 1 µg RAE = 1 µg retinol = 12 µg dietary beta-carotene = 3.33 IU retinol.
- Vitamin E: 1 mg alpha-tocopherol = 1.49 IU natural (d-) = 2.22 IU synthetic (dl-). The synthetic form is roughly half as potent per IU, which is why the two numbers on two bottles are not comparable.
- Folate: 1 µg DFE = 1 µg food folate = 0.6 µg folic acid taken with food = 0.5 µg folic acid on an empty stomach. Supplement labels state folic acid, so a 400 µg tablet is ~667 µg DFE.
- Niacin: 1 mg NE = 1 mg niacin = 60 mg tryptophan.
- Salt to sodium: sodium mg = salt g × 400 (1 g salt ≈ 400 mg sodium). EU labels give salt, US labels give sodium.
- Elemental versus compound: 325 mg ferrous sulfate ≈ 65 mg elemental iron; 500 mg calcium carbonate ≈ 200 mg elemental calcium. Always state the elemental figure, and record it in the product's row in `## Supplements` of `~/Clawic/data/nutrition/memory.md`.

## Where Standards Disagree

Switch with `reference_standard`; state which one a number came from whenever the two differ materially.

| Nutrient | US DRI | EFSA DRV | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vitamin D | RDA 600 IU, UL 4000 IU | AI 600 IU, UL 4000 IU | Broad agreement on intake; the disagreement is about the blood threshold, not the dose (`labs.md`) |
| Calcium | 1000-1200 mg | 950 mg adults | EU targets are lower; a US-based "shortfall" can be adequacy under EFSA |
| Iron, premenopausal | 18 mg | 16 mg | Both assume ~18% absorption from a mixed diet; vegetarian requirements run higher under both |
| Folate | 400 µg DFE, and fortified flour in the US | 330 µg DFE, no mandatory fortification in most of the EU | Baseline intake differs by country before anyone changes their diet — set `platform.country` |
| Sodium | CDRR 2300 mg | ~2000 mg | WHO also uses 2000 mg |
| Iodine | 150 µg | 150 µg | Agreement on the number, wide divergence in salt iodization coverage |

**After establishing or revising a nutrient's status**, write its row to `## Nutrient Status` in `~/Clawic/data/nutrition/memory.md` with the intake estimate, the target, the status, and the evidence it rests on (`memory-template.md`). A status derived and not written is re-derived next month with a different number, and the user cannot tell which one was right.
