# Bike Maintenance Engineering Reference

## 1. Why chains die and take friends with them

A chain "stretches" as bushings and plates wear — pitch grows from 12.7 mm. Measured over 12 links (12 in ≈ 304.8 mm):
- 0.5% wear = early, disc-only territory; 12.7 mm pitch ×12 = 305.3 mm vs 304.8. Racing/maybe replace.
- 0.75% = **the replacement point** for derailleurs systems (Shimano/SRAM guidance).
- 1.0% = cassette's teeth have worn to match; replace both.
- Gauge substitutes: ruler (1/16 in over 12 in ≈ 0.5%), or the "lift-at-chainring" test (chain lifts clear of ring teeth at 3 o'clock = very worn).

Worn chain profile grinds cassette valleys wide; new chain rollers then sit high and skip under torque. There is no cheap undo.

## 3. Service cadences (base — multiplier-adjusted)

**Every ride (30 sec):** tire pinch-pressure check; quick lever squeeze; wheel quick-releases thru-axles seated.

**Weekly (5 min at your km; biweekly if <50 km/wk):** chain lube (wipe FIRST), wipe rims/rotors (never oily rag on rotors), tire inspection for cuts/glass, brake pad glance, lights charge.

**Monthly (20 min):** chain wear measurement; wheel true by eye/spin; cable stretch re-index (barrel adjuster ¼ turns); bolt check (stem, seat, rack, bottle — NOT crank/pedal direction-blind); headset play test (front brake on, rock bike).

**Quarterly (1 hr):** drivetrain deep clean (degrease chain+cassette+jockeys, dry, lube); tire pressure seating + tubeless sealant top-up; pad removal inspection; housing end-cap look; hub bearing play test.

**Yearly / 5,000 km:** cables+housing replacement; BB check (creak under load / side play); headset re-grease; wheel bearings; hydraulic bleed (or when spongy); suspension lower-leg service if applicable (50 hr); torqued reassembly of everything you own tools for.

**Post-incident:** after any crash, transport, or dropped bike: rotor true, DER hanger alignment, bar/stem torque, wheel true.

## 4. Diagnostic decision trees (condensed)

### Skip under load
1. Chain wear gauge. ≥0.75% → replace chain; then test.
2. If skip persists in same 2–3 cogs with new chain → cassette.
3. Stiff link check: backpedal slowly watching DER cage jump → flex the tight link.
4. B-gap / h-limit: only if skips at cassette extremes.

### Mystery creak (rank by frequency)
1. Chainring bolts (tighten cross-pattern) — most common, free.
2. Pedal bearings (swap pedals, ride, listen).
3. BB (torque check cups; creak under seated load only).
4. Seatpost/frame interface (remove, grease/carbon paste, torque).
5. Headset (creak on bars-only motion, index notch).
6. DER hanger loose, water bottle bolts, rack bolts — yes, really.

### Disc brake rub
1. Center caliper: loosen 2 bolts, squeeze lever HARD, retighten bolts with lever held.
2. Rotor true: truing tool / adjustable wrench careful bend; >0.3 mm runout = fix.
3. Piston stickiness: push pistons back (plastic lever), pump; uneven pad wear reveals the stuck one.
4. Bent rotor (transport): often replace — truing thin rotors fatigues them.

### Spongy lever
Bleed first (olive/barb or funnel per brand); if fluid was dark, plan yearly bleeds; check pad glaze (light sand on flat surface, clean with isopropyl — never oils near rotors).

## 5. Torque reference (typical — stamp/check your parts)

| Fastener | Nm |
|---|---|
| Stem steerer bolts (alu) | 5 (carbon 5 + paste, face carefully) |
| Stem handlebar bolts | 5–6, cross-pattern ¼ turns |
| Seatpost clamp (alu post) | 5; carbon post 5 + carbon paste, NEVER grease |
| Saddle rails | 6 |
| Brake lever clamp | 4–5 |
| Cassette lockring | 40 |
| BB cups (threaded, external) | 35–50 (huge variance — check) |
| Through-axles | 10–12 (hand tight + lever) |
| Crank bolts | 35–45 (or 12-14 self-extracting per spec) |

Rule: no torque wrench → "snug + ¼ turn" for 5 Nm class bolts; NEVER force small bolts. Carbon + slipping = paste, not more torque.

## 6. Tools honest ladder

**Level 1 ($30):** chain gauge, tire levers, patch kit, pump, hex set (2–8), T25 torx (discs), rag/degreaser/lube.
**Level 2 (+$50):** cable cutters (good ones — the one tool not to cheap out), master-link pliers, cassette lockring tool + chain whip, bottom bracket tool for YOUR standard, torque wrench 2–25 Nm ¼ drive.
**Level 3 (+$100):** derailleur hanger alignment gauge, wheel truing stand (or zip-tie-on-frame trick), bleed kit for your brake brand, cone wrenches.

## 7. Revival checklist (bike asleep 2+ years)

1. Tires: replace if cracked at sidewall flex points; tubes likely porous.
2. Cables/housing: replace (corroded inside where invisible).
3. Chain: measure — often past 1% from storage grime; cassette inspection.
4. Brake fluid/pads: bleed hydraulics; rim brake pads harden with age.
5. Bearings: headset + hubs + BB play check BEFORE first ride.
6. Bolt torque pass, bar tape grip check, quick-release practice.
7. First ride: parking-lot speeds, brakes tested dry-run before traffic.

## 8. Pre-big-ride week-before audit

Tires (pressure + cut scan) · chain measure · pads ≥1 mm · cables indexed under load · wheels true · torque pass · spare tube + levers + CO2/pump + multi-tool + chain link · check you can actually remove your wheels (thru-axle practice, taped race hanger).
