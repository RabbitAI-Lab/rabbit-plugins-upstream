# Negotiation Playbook — Getting an Honest OTD by Email

The dealer's core advantage is the *worksheet fog*: mixed numbers, fatigue, and monthly-payment
tunnel vision. The counter is to move the fight to email, where you can compare bids line-by-line.

## The 4-email sequence

### Email 1 — The OTD request (to 5+ dealers' internet departments)

> Subject: OTD quote request — [Year Make Model Trim]
>
> I'm ready to buy this month. Please reply with a single **out-the-door price** including all
> fees, taxes, and any installed accessories. I am [not] trading a vehicle. I will finance through
> my [bank/credit union] unless you can beat [X.X% APR].
>
> The first dealer to give me a straight OTD number gets my business. No phone calls required.

Rules: never state a target first; never discuss payments; one thread per dealer.

### Email 2 — The cross-shop (after quotes arrive)

> Dealer B offered $[OTD]. If you can beat it by $[200–500], I'll schedule delivery for [day].
> Same spec, same terms — out-the-door number only.

### Email 3 — The add-on refuse (when the winner shows padding)

> Thank you for the worksheet. Please remove [items] — I did not order them. If they cannot be
> removed, deduct their full amount from the selling price. My offer: $[corrected OTD], out the
> door, first visit, no back-and-forth.

### Email 4 — The close

> Confirmed: $[OTD] out the door, itemized as [price / tax @ x% / title-reg / doc fee @ cap].
> Please have the paperwork ready. I'll be there [day/time] and will review every line before
> signing. If the number changes at signing, the deal is off.

## F&I office defense (the last 45 minutes)

The finance office is where deals get un-won. Expect:

1. **Rate bump reveal:** "Your credit came back at 9.9%" when your bank pre-approval says 5.5%.
   Response: show the pre-approval; give them one shot to beat it; otherwise sign with your bank.
2. **Payment re-frame:** "What monthly were you looking for?" — never answer; you negotiate ONE
   number, the OTD. Term extension at the same payment = $2,000–$6,000 extra interest.
3. **Product blitz:** gap, VSC, tire-wheel, key plan — all marked up 3–40×. Only gap has a cheap
   external equivalent (your auto insurer, ~$20–$30/yr). For everything else: "Not today."
4. **The four-square worksheet** (price / payment / down / trade boxes) is designed to confuse.
   Your defense: force everything into ONE line — the OTD — and make them re-draw it if they split
   the discount across boxes.

## Lease-specific counters

- "What's the money factor?" is the magic question. Legit answers include a number like 0.00125.
  Dodges ("it's a factor the bank sets") = walk-away signal. Multiply by 2400 for APR.
- Ask for the **buy rate** MF. Manufacturer subvented deals publish MFs (check the OEM's current
  lease program before visiting). Any markup over the subvented MF is negotiable profit.
- Keep drive-off = 1st payment + DMV + acquisition fee. Cap reduction cash is uninsured — totaled
  car on day 2 = that cash is gone (gap covers the loan, not your down).
- 36-month/10k-yr leases usually beat 48-month on residual math; per-mile overage $0.15–$0.35/mi —
  if you drive 15k/yr, price the extra 5k miles UP FRONT (usually $100–$180 per 5k block prepaid
  vs $750+ post-hoc).

## Walking rules

- Any "market adjustment" on a non-limited car: counter MSRP-minus-rebates or walk.
- Any refusal to itemize the OTD in writing: walk. Supply is normal for 95% of vehicles.
- Any change of numbers at signing vs the email: stand up. The sunk-cost reflex is the dealer's
  best friend; yours is the exit.

## Timing leverage

- Last 2 days of the month / quarter-end: quotas matter.
- Model-year changeover (late summer): outgoing-year discounts stack with rebates.
- Slow weekday mornings: fewer managers competing for the desk.
