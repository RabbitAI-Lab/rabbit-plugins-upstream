# TikTok copyright reference

Official policy: tiktok.com/legal/page/global/copyright-policy/en
(accessed 2026-08-26; last updated: not stated by source)
Counter Notification Form: tiktok.com/legal/report/counternotification
(accessed 2026-08-26; last updated: not stated by source; the page
itself states the form is only accessible via the TikTok app)

## What a creator experiences (classify first, as always)

- **Muted audio**: automated music matching silenced the sound;
  video remains up.
- **Removed video**: a copyright infringement report was actioned;
  the creator receives an in-app notification.
- **Blocked upload**: matching stopped the post before publication.
- **Report against the creator's own work**: someone filed a
  copyright infringement report on content the creator owns.

## The removal-and-appeal flow (verified against the official policy)

1. **The in-app notification is the front door.** Removal arrives as
   an in-app notification, and the appeal is submitted within the
   app, accessed from that notification. The Counter Notification
   Form is likewise app-only. Practical consequence for artists and
   teams: do not dismiss or lose the notification; it is the path.
2. **A silent deletion clock runs.** TikTok states removed content is
   deleted after a period of time per applicable law and its data
   retention policy, and after deletion the content can no longer be
   reinstated. TikTok does not publish the length of this period, so
   per the clock rules this is presented honestly: a real clock, no
   published number, which is itself the reason to act promptly
   rather than a reason to panic.
3. **Two paths run in parallel**: request a retraction from the
   copyright owner directly, or appeal in-app with all required
   information and supporting evidence.
4. **The appeal travels to the reporter.** TikTok states that, where
   appropriate and authorized by law, the entire appeal, including
   the contact information provided, is forwarded to the original
   reporter, who may use it to file a lawsuit, for example a court
   action to keep the content down under the DMCA or similar laws.
   This makes the TikTok in-app appeal carry counter-notification
   weight: Tier A applies at this step, and the contact-forwarding
   fact is delivered as its own plain Heads up before the user
   files, never discovered afterward.
5. **Reinstatement logic is DMCA-shaped**: content may be reinstated
   if it does not infringe or if no notice arrives that the claimant
   filed a court action to keep it down.

## Repeat violations

TikTok operates a repeat infringer approach: accounts that repeatedly
infringe can be removed. TikTok does not publish exact strike counts
on the policy page verified here, so no counts are stated (third-party
sources circulate numbers; per S-06 they are not used). The honest
framing for a creator: repeated actioned reports endanger the whole
account, which is exactly why the dispute-care principle (file what
your paperwork backs) applies here too.

## TikTok Shop is a separate track

Sellers and Shop creators fall under a distinct TikTok Shop
Intellectual Property Policy with its own appeal and
counter-notification channel through Seller Center tickets. If the
user's issue involves a listing, product content, or Shop video,
classify it onto this track and route to Seller Center; the
consumer-app flow above does not apply.
