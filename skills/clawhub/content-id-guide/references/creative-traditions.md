# When creative traditions meet the matching system

Sampling, remixing, covering, interpolating, and reuse are creative
traditions with deep histories, in many communities far older than
the systems that now flag them. This file exists so a creator working
in those traditions gets specific, expert navigation instead of
generic claim handling, and gets it without a flagged upload ever
being treated as a moral failing. A claim on a sample is a
rights-navigation question. That's all it is here.

The structural fact under everything (verified, in the glossary and
platform references): fingerprint matching fires on the recording
regardless of how transformed the use is, and one song carries two
separate copyrights (the composition and the sound recording). Most
confusion in this territory is one of those two facts wearing a
costume.

## The practices, and what specifically catches each

- **Sampling (using part of an existing recording).** Fires the
  sound-recording layer, and often the composition layer too, which
  is why one sample can draw claims from two different parties. "But
  I chopped it beyond recognition" fails as a mental model because
  matching is designed to survive transformation; the navigation
  questions are whether the sample was cleared on both layers and
  what the license's territory clause covers.
- **Interpolation (re-performing a melody or lyric without the
  original recording).** No recording used, so the sound-recording
  layer usually stays quiet; the composition layer is the one in
  play. The paperwork that answers a sampling claim and the
  paperwork that answers an interpolation claim are different
  documents.
- **Covers (new performance of an existing song).** Composition-layer
  territory, with a verified platform path: on YouTube, eligible
  cover videos can share revenue once the publisher claims them,
  shown by the "Sharing ad earnings" notice; whether a given song
  participates is the publisher's call (see
  references/platform-youtube.md, verified). Using any of the
  original recording moves a "cover" back into sampling territory in
  the system's eyes.
- **DJ mixes and megamixes.** Many recordings, therefore many
  potential claimants on one upload; expect multiple simultaneous
  claims and use the multi-claim triage rather than treating each
  as a separate crisis. Territory and per-claimant policies can
  differ across one mix.
- **Remix culture and edits (flips, sped-up and slowed versions,
  mashups).** The transformations that define the tradition are the
  transformations matching is built to survive, so flags are the
  expected system behavior, not a judgment of the work. Navigation
  runs through the same two-layer question, plus the platform's own
  sanctioned lanes where they exist (the Shorts audio library's
  built-in licensing, Creator Music's revenue-sharing tracks, both
  verified) which are the no-clearance-needed paths the platforms
  themselves provide.
- **Vernacular and community reuse (dance sounds, meme audio,
  chants, traditional material).** Often the hardest fit, because
  authorship in community traditions doesn't map cleanly onto
  registered-owner databases, and a registered claimant may hold
  rights in a fixed recording of material a community regards as
  shared. The skill navigates the claim in front of the user
  factually, names this mismatch honestly when the user raises it
  (terrain honesty, S-03), and routes ownership questions to
  counsel, because they are genuinely hard even for experts.

## How the skill behaves in this territory

- Classify the layer first (recording, composition, or both), then
  the practice, then run the normal ladder; the layer question
  resolves most of the confusion before any decision is needed.
- Never a lecture in either direction: no moralizing at the creator
  about clearance, and no editorializing at them about the system,
  unless they open that door, in which case terrain honesty governs
  and the help stays practical.
- The organizer built here is the creator's own record, first and
  always: it serves their dispute if they choose one, and it remains
  theirs for anything else they do with their situation (counsel, a
  publisher conversation, their own records). The dispute machine
  gets a copy; the creator keeps the file.
- S-02 boundary unchanged: explaining why a transformation still
  matched is diagnosis and fully in scope; tuning a transformation
  to defeat matching is not, and the line between those is the
  question being asked, not the words used to ask it.
