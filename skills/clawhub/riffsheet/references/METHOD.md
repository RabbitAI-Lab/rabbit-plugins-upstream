# The riffsheet procedure

You see stills and read text; this turns one short video into a riffsheet: `riffsheet.json` (the complete data, format in `schema/riffsheet.schema.json`) and `riffsheet.md` (the account a person reads). `scripts/riffsheet.py` measures and lays out pictures, you write judgment into one file, and `build` merges both, checks them and writes the riffsheet. Read `references/TOOL.md` once. Inputs: the video and, when the speech matters, a word-timed transcript from `transcribe` or from a recognizer of your choice, in the same format. The scan folder (DIR) is the tool's: keep your own files (transcript, judgment file) beside it, and give `build` an `--out` folder beside it too, as in the folder layout in `SKILL.md`.

Change into the repository folder (`cd` to it) and run commands from there, with boxes and arguments written out: a command, box or argument list kept in a shell variable reaches the program as one word in zsh and fails. A shell function is not such a variable and saves typing; define it once, `rs() { python3 -I scripts/riffsheet.py "$@"; }`, then `rs scan ...`. Every command runs on any Python 3.9 or newer, but `transcribe` needs one that can import `faster-whisper`: if that is a virtual environment, define the function with its Python (`rs() { /path/to/venv/bin/python -I scripts/riffsheet.py "$@"; }`) and use it for every command. The ready-made commands the tool prints name the Python it ran under.

## 1. Who writes what

The tool writes, tagged measured: duration; cuts and other picture changes; shot stillness; split layouts; loudness events and whether sound continues under the voice; line times, transcript text and pace; `speaking_mode` and language; caption times, position, size, colors and `cue_max_words`; graphic boxes and times; time markers; and the checks. A measured value is right or absent: where the evidence does not settle it, the tool leaves it out and says why. You write: line splits and speakers, caption words, what people and things look like and do, camera judgments, prose, entity labels, the formula and the report text.

Tags. Untagged means you saw it. Tag only the exceptions: `inferred` (reasoned from measured and seen facts), `uncertain` (with what you actually saw: "second word unreadable; looks like ma.."), `cannot_judge` (needs hearing, or motion nothing here settles). An empty field tagged `cannot_judge` is correct; a plausible guess is wrong. A reading never overrules a measurement: if a picture seems to contradict one, look closer, and if the conflict stays, keep the measurement and record the conflict under `uncertain`. The one exception is a caption's style or position, which your reading may replace, with a `why`.

## 2. Order of work

Each step re-reads all context so far, so cost grows with the number of steps; what you type costs little. Put every command that does not need an earlier one's answer into one step (several commands, or several images opened together).

0. Before this, judge from the request and what you can already see whether the speech matters, and when you need something you do not have, `rs check VIDEO` and the question it may call for (`SKILL.md`, "What this analysis needs"): never install, download or send anything without the person's yes. A need can appear later too (a line you find you cannot quote): ask then.
1. One command: `rs transcribe VIDEO --out T.json; rs scan VIDEO --out DIR --transcript T.json && rs build DIR`. The last prints the build sheet (indexed words, pauses, low-probability words, cuts inside lines, the measured pace band of each sentence under PACE, shot stillness). If `transcribe` fails, it leaves in T.json why, `scan` records it and the riffsheet says so; tell the person. With a transcript from another recognizer or from the person, add `--transcript-by "what made it"` to `scan`; the riffsheet says it was not the tool's tested path. Without speech recognition (the person said no, or you judged it not needed), run `rs scan VIDEO --out DIR && rs build DIR`. The analysis then has no spoken lines, and the riffsheet says that the speech was not transcribed and why.
2. Read the summary `scan` printed (it is `summary.txt`, printed in full: no need to open the file). One step: open every overview sheet, and `jumps.jpg` if listed, and, when the video has an audio track, take the hearing check (`rs hear-check DIR`, section 6, Hearing). Note who, where, shots, captions, graphics, panes, screens, and every name or term written on screen.
3. One step: `text` on every region that needs reading (section 4), `element` on every card, icon, logo, sticker, end card and pane the overview shows (section 7), the `zoom`, `frames`, `mouth` and `color` views the overview already calls for (section 3), and `transcribe --hint` when section 5 calls for it. Open all resulting images in the next step.
4. Write the judgment file (section 9), with pace words from the PACE block, and run `rs build DIR --judgment J.json --out OUT`. Its LINES block gives each line's pace; its UNCONFIRMED block lists every statement still lacking a confirming view. Before writing, read the detail words at the end of the step 1 sheet (also in `references/TOOL.md`, Detail words): every statement using one goes in a mark.
5. The confirmation views (section 10) as one batch, then build again, until it prints `CHECKS: all passed`. Until then `build` writes no riffsheet.

When no tool command answers a question, you may answer it by your own means (another program, a service, a source you looked up; anything that costs the person something, they agree to first): list each such fact under `own_means` with what you used, and the riffsheet marks it as established by your own means. When you find no way, record the gap under `tool_gaps`. `riffsheet.md` shows both.

## 3. Which picture answers which question

- Change over time (when something appears, which way a motion goes, how often a gesture repeats, the order of states): `frames DIR --from S --to E [--box B]`. Direction and speed come from change between frames, never from one frame.
- Detail (clothing, footwear, jewelry and its structure, which hand holds what, what an object rests on, small print, counts of small things): a large single view, `zoom DIR --at T [--box B]` at a moment where it is clearly visible, or the shot frame in `shots/`. Sheet cells are too small for these. A view from behind or in a mirror swaps sides: give the side on the person's body. Write only what a view shows, never what such an object usually has.
- Color of a thing: `color DIR --at T --box B`, the box on that thing only. Name the color from the printed hue, and only from a color whose pixels sit in the box's center (a color at the border is the surroundings). When it prints CAVEATS (tinted or dim light, small box), rerun with `--ref R`, a box on something in the same frame known to be white or neutral gray (paper, a white garment, a white screen; a wall only when it is plainly white, since painted walls are often cream), and name the corrected value. Where the corrected name and the picture disagree, the reference was probably not neutral: state the color as `uncertain` with both. One garment or product keeps one color name in every segment and entity; `build` warns when the same word is given different colors, and when a color word in a mark is one that no `color` measurement in that shot could be. With no such thing in frame, state only light-independent facts (lighter or darker than a neighboring part, metallic shine) and tag the color `uncertain`.
- Text on a filmed or recorded screen, a card, a notification or a printed surface: a `zoom` on it, one per screen state; record every legible word, in prose for filmed text and in the graphic's timed record for drawn layers. All text, here and in captions, is read exactly as displayed (case, punctuation, an omitted mark left omitted, digits, symbols), and an unreadable part is written as far as legible and marked `uncertain`.
- Camera: the `still` share per shot is measured. At 0.30 or more the camera is locked or the picture frozen; at 0.05 or less it is handheld or moving; between, look with `frames` (the per-second values show where it changes). The kind of move comes from frames: background shifting while the subject holds is a camera move, even growth in scale a zoom or push-in, near things moving more than far things a dolly.
- Mouth: find the face on an overview cell or shot frame (cells carry a 10% grid). The box runs from just under the nose to below the chin, and from cheek to cheek, widened to where the face goes during the line; a box that ends above the chin cuts the lips off. Run `mouth DIR --box B --words A-B` with the line's word range (as in the judgment file; `--line N` takes the transcriber's line `LN` from the build sheet instead): one small cell per word and per pause. Lips open and changing on words and closed in pauses is that person speaking. Check that the first cell shows the mouth; add `--every-frame` only when the per-word cells leave it open.

## 4. What the tool misses, and captions through `text`

Cuts and edits:
- A cut that changes only part of the picture is reported as a cut only when it is a jump cut inside one framing (SHOTS says `jump cut`: check it on `events.jpg` like any cut). A smaller area changing completely in one frame while the rest holds is listed as a POSSIBLE JUMP CUT, its frames before and after on `jumps.jpg`: decide each. An object or body part in a different place or pose with no motion between the two frames is an edit: treat it like a cut (a segment boundary or a timed event in prose) and say what jumped. A sticker or graphic appearing is not an edit.
- A cut inside one pane shows as `panel`: open its event row and decide.
- Layered changes over an identical background (same place, with a different outfit, person or object layered or faded in) produce no event. Where neighboring overview cells differ in subject but not in background, find the change with `frames`. Slow dissolves can be missed too: "no cut found" does not prove one shot.

Captions and graphics. `text DIR --box B [--from S --to E] [--name NAME]` lists each state of a region with its times; let the box hold the whole text with a margin. A window given with `--from/--to` ends at least a second past where the caption leaves, never on a cut: a caption can stay a few frames into the next shot, and a state cut off by the window's edge has no true off time. A state marked "runs past the window" matters only if it shows the caption; one that shows none (the picture after the caption left) is simply left out.
- Run it on every TEXT-LIKE REGION and every FIXED LAYER the summary lists. A region marked MOTION ONLY?, and a region or fixed layer whose box shows no lettering, logo or drawn shape on any overview cell (leaves, an edge of furniture, a still object), may be skipped; say so under `tool_gaps`.
- On a split screen, a box that crosses the seam between panes holds two pictures that cut at different times: `text` says so, its times stay measured, but many states come back with no measured style. A caption lying wholly on one side measures better with a box on that side. For one that sits across the seam, give colors and size from your reading, with a `why`, wherever `build` shows none.
- A caption that moves between shots leaves a region at one place only: compare caption positions across the overview and run `text` per position with `--from/--to`. A caption that never changes appears under FIXED LAYERS when the picture behind moves or cuts; over a picture that never moves it appears in neither list, so take its box from the overview.
- Leave out empty states. A two-frame state is real. Neighboring states showing the same words are one chunk, given as a range.
- A state marked NO LAYER shows no drawn layer: the strokes that held in the state before left where it begins and nothing drawn held through it (a caption gone while the picture behind still moves). Leave it out of every caption; if a chunk you give includes it at either end, `build` ends the chunk at the last state showing the layer and says so.
- A state marked `?` lies on a moving picture where no layer held still, so a change inside it can be missed; its first and last frames are both shown. If they differ, read both and note it under `tool_gaps`.
- A cell labeled `mid-change a→b` is a frame from inside the arrival of the state after it (a fade, slide or pop over three or more frames). It is not a state: give the chunk by the states' own ids.
- A state marked `~` is one in which the layer kept changing while the picture held (a word-by-word reveal the split did not separate): read its words from `frames --box` over the state. `build` then gives no measured `cue_max_words` for a caption using it: give it from your reading, with a `why`.
- Caption colors, size and place are kept only when two or more of the region's states agree (or only one state was measured); a value shown as withdrawn has no measurement. A layer that reaches two opposite edges of your box is withdrawn too (still picture held with it, or the box cuts the text): give the box a margin. `build` then takes it from a measured caption of the same region (tagged inferred); failing that, give it from your reading with a `why`.

In the judgment file a caption lists `read` pairs, one per chunk: a state or range (`T4`, `T4-T9`) and the words shown. `build` takes chunk times from the states' edges and style from their measurements. A graphic gives its region and state range, and `build` takes its box and times the same way.

## 5. Spoken lines (`audio.voice_segments`)

1. One segment per spoken line, given as a word index range; ids `vs1`, `vs2` … follow your order. A line is one sentence, so every sentence end ends a line, however short the pause after it. A sentence ends where the transcript writes `.`, `?` or `!` (`。`, `？`, `！`), and also where the words before a pause already form a complete sentence and the next words start a new one although the transcript wrote a comma (recognizers often join sentences with commas; a list or clause that continues the sentence is not an end). The build sheet marks every pause of 0.15 s or more as `|gap|`. One sentence is split into two lines only at a pause over 0.4 s or where another person takes over. Caption breaks do not decide line ends. `build` warns about a sentence end or a comma followed by a pause inside a line: end the line there, or leave it when the words run on as one sentence.
2. Spelling. Where a caption transcribes the line word for word but spells a word differently (a name, a brand, a number as digits, a fast, quiet or low-probability word), the caption's spelling wins unless the transcript is plainly right: give a `fix`. A caption that summarizes, paraphrases or substitutes a word says nothing about what was spoken, with one exception: a name or term written anywhere on screen (a title, label, wordmark, packaging, a screen in shot) is how the video spells the thing it is about, and a transcript stretch that could be the same sounds (similar syllables, low probability, an odd or meaningless phrase in context) is that term. To confirm, run `transcribe VIDEO --out T2.json --hint "<the names and terms, as written>" --compare T.json`. Where the hinted run hears the term at the same time, give it as a `fix`; where it does not, keep the transcript word and record the candidate under `uncertain`. Insert a term only where something in either run sounds like it. A `fix` maps an exact stretch of the line's transcript text (as the LINES block or the build sheet shows it: one word or several, punctuation included) to its replacement, applied once: `{"Rift Kit.": "Riffkit."}` joins two words into one, `{"Rift with Rift Kit.": "riffed with Riffkit."}` corrects several at once.
3. Borrowed sound. Words of a borrowed sound (lyrics, a hook baked into a track) are not voice segments. Evidence: no mouth moves through them while a face is visible; sung-length words (long, evenly held, chorus-like repeats); steady music-like loudness under them (SOMETHING CONTINUES under the voice); a language different from the on-screen text. With two or more of these, list the words under `music_words` with the evidence; with fewer but still in doubt, keep the line and record the doubt.
4. `mode`: `on_camera_dialogue` when the speaker's face is in frame and the mouth moves with the words (`mouth`, section 3); `voiceover` when no face is visible, the visible face keeps its mouth closed, or the speaker is seen only from behind or only in part (hands, body below the face); `no_dialogue` for a segment without speech. Profile, small or masked faces are weak evidence: tag `mode` inferred.
5. `speaker`: `protagonist` for the person the video centers on, whether seen speaking or narrating off camera; the id of another person entity when the line comes from that person on screen; `narrator` only for a voice belonging to no one in the video. On camera, it is whose mouth moves. In voiceover, `protagonist` when the narration is first person about what the only person (or the hands shown) does, else `narrator`; tag either inferred. A person seen only in part (hands, a body without the face) is no person entity (section 7), so with no `protagonist` entity the line's sentence carries no `[image:]` marker. When nothing separates the choices, give the best value plus `cannot_judge: ["speaker"]`.
6. Pace is measured: the build sheet prints the band of each sentence as the transcript punctuates it (PACE), and `build` with the judgment file prints it for each line you define (band unhurried, measured, brisk or rapid-fire; pauses over 0.4 s; speeding up or slowing down) in its LINES block, which is the value that counts. It is counted on the line as corrected (a `fix` that joins two misheard words into one name lowers the count), so after a fix take the band word from LINES again. Take it from there only, never from the transcript's own lines or your own count. Prose uses the band word, never the number; `build` flags a band word that differs from the measured one. For scripts other than space-separated ones and Chinese, Japanese and Korean, tag the band inferred.
7. A line crossing a segment boundary: its `[voice:X]` goes in the segment where its first word starts, even when that is a tenth of a second before the cut (the LINES block of `build` names the time); the next segment says the speaker is still talking, with no second marker.

## 6. The prose

Two kinds of writing never mix. The prose, the line words and the entity descriptions are physical facts. The formula (section 8) is abstract rules about attention, expectation and payoff. Every element of the video is formula (the mechanism shifting the viewer's state), skeleton (how camera and editing deliver it: framing, cut rhythm, tempo, music energy, voice presentation, whether text layers exist) or surface (scene, props, words, screen text). Test: replace it with something else; if the viewer's state shifts in the same way, it is surface.

The prose is the complete record: a reader who never saw the video must picture it moving, shot by shot. It fails wherever a general word stands where the frames show a specific value (a class instead of a count, color, side or distance) or a word names a different thing. The dimensions below are a floor.

Segments. Give each segment's end (`S3`, a time, or `end`); the tool writes `[mm:ss.s-mm:ss.s]`. A boundary sits where the viewer's information changes: they learn something, start expecting something, or their understanding shifts. Test each visible discontinuity: could a viewer of the previous segment alone describe what it reveals? If not (a new element, a new side of the subject, a changed state, a new spatial relation), it starts a segment; if so (a rhythmic jump cut showing nothing new), it does not. A body of narration covering two such changes in turn is split. Every cut and pane cut is either a boundary or named in its segment with its time (`at 00:03.2 hard cut to …`).

Each segment is read alone, so it states every fact it depends on: each pronoun has its referent inside the segment, and an unchanged attribute is restated as a content-bearing anchor ("in the same rust corduroy jacket") rather than "still", "continues", "same as before" or "cut back". Lead with who or what, doing what, what changed.

Camera, per segment:
- framing: close-up, medium close-up, medium, wide, extreme wide, or a precise variant;
- angle: low, eye-level, high, overhead, over-the-shoulder …;
- behavior: static, handheld, pan, tilt, zoom, dolly, arc (section 3);
- subject geometry: left, center or right; top, middle or bottom; share of frame as quarter, third, half, two-thirds or full frame;
- action tempo: still, slow, moderate, fast or explosive, from how far body parts move between frames a known time apart, and from motion blur;
- playback rate, only when the footage is not real time, judged per segment from motion covering more distance between frames than physics allows, duplicate or ghosted frames, or sustained speech above about 6 words a second. Write it as `Nx natural` (1.5x natural sped up, 0.5x slow motion) only when computed as observed ÷ natural displacement; otherwise put the direction under `uncertain` and the rate under `cannot_judge`.

The camera state you give holds over the whole window (check its first, middle and last frames); each change of vantage inside it is a timed event, or a boundary when it is also an information change.

Transitions, into every segment but the first: `hard cut`, `whip pan match cut`, `crash zoom match cut`, `dissolve`, `flash`, `morph`, or the physical signature ("a hand sweeps left to right across the lens"), judged on `frames` around the cut. Where a change takes two or three frames, the SHOTS line of `summary.txt` measures each frame inside it: darker or lighter than the frame before and the frame after, with the mean brightness of all three, warmer or cooler, and whether it is a mix of its neighbors (a dissolve step) or not. Take the direction and size from that line, never from the picture: a frame darker than both neighbors is a dip, lighter than both a flash. Write `hard cut` only after checking the boundary frames for motion blur, something covering the lens, and a blended, faded, white or morphing frame, and say all three are absent. Where subject, outfit, place or light differs and you cannot state those absences, describe the bridge. A layered change is described by its signature.

People. Demographics (gender, age range, ethnicity) live only in the person's attributes. Fixed identity (hairstyle, build, face, hair and eye color, marks) is not written. In prose, per segment, for each visible person, listeners included:
- two or more channels of action or expression (expression from the frame nearest the moment, action from change across frames);
- the changeable look (clothes, accessories, makeup, anything held in the hands): in full on first appearance, and after that, including after a cut-away, as an anchor;
- the role: `direct address`, `talking head`, `hands-on demo`, `observed candid`, or a domain role (`street interview`, `react / reaction`, `voiceover talent`). Eyeline to the lens across frames separates direct address from talking head.

Scene state. Environment, lighting, capture look (handheld feel, noise, grade, how real the place looks) and where each person stays placed (seated, standing, where in the place) are written in full where established, and as an anchor with key colors, light direction and capture words in every later segment of that state. Lighting doing emotional work (golden hour, hard shadow, blue night, one practical source) gets direction, softness and color temperature, read from shadow direction, edge hardness and white balance.

Objects. The object a shot is built around gets material, key color and surface state, not a category noun; background props, enough to recognize. Text printed on real things is filmed and goes in prose; drawn screen text stays out of prose.

Other editing signals with a physical signature go in prose with their time: a color jump, a filter or glitch flicker, a speed ramp. A sound effect is written as the coinciding loudness event ("a short loudness peak at 00:04.2, no word under it, as the frame flashes"), its identity under `cannot_judge`.

Picture and sound. Only the prose records how they are timed together. Walk the loudness events and energy stretches through each segment and name every coupling, secondary ones too, at measured times: instantaneous (a cut, appearance or gesture within 0.1 s of a peak or cliff, checked on frames) and lasting (a visual pattern that runs through a loud or quiet stretch). An event under a word is the voice: say so. Whether cuts and captions land on sound events is measured: quote the SOUND AT THE CUTS lines of `summary.txt` (and SOUND AT CAPTION APPEARANCES from `build`) with their numbers and the chance figure, rather than comparing times yourself. They state times, not what the sound is: write "the cuts land on loudness peaks", never "on the beat" or "on the stomp"; whether it is music goes under `cannot_judge`.

Markers. People and `source_image` entities appear only as `[image:id]`, wherever they act or speak. The actor is the nearest marker to the left; a pronoun is safe only while that person is the nearest marker; someone merely acted upon is unmarked. Each spoken line appears once as `[voice:vsN]`, on one spine: the speaker's marker, a speech verb, the delivery, `[voice:vsN]` last, and nobody else marked in that sentence. An off-camera line is framed as off-frame audio with its owner's marker; a narrator gets a voice description only. A person acting on a screen layer: describe the action and name the layer generically. The prose holds no spoken words, caption text or graphic content, and no ASCII double quote.

Delivery around each `[voice:X]`: who, on or off camera, the pace word, the expression or gesture while speaking, and loudness only relative to the other lines (from the RMS series). Pitch, stress, accent, vocal age, register and energy go under that segment's `cannot_judge`. Quote every loudness fact (level range, a quietest stretch, a drop, sound without words) from the LOUDNESS FACTS lines of `summary.txt`, with its numbers. Write a comparison (quietest, loudest, the first, so far, rises, falls back) only when that line states it for that stretch; otherwise give the stretch's own level and compare nothing.

Hearing. The tool cannot hear, and most agents cannot either; whether you can is settled by the tool's test, never by what you believe of yourself. `rs hear-check DIR` writes `DIR/hearing/check_N.wav`, a voice saying six random digits. Open it the way you open the pictures, with the tool that reads a file into your input; if your model takes sound as input, it reaches you as sound: listen, and answer with the six digits you heard, in order, and nothing else (`rs hear-check DIR --answer DIGITS`). Answer `rs hear-check DIR --cannot-hear` only after trying, when opening the file gives you nothing to listen to (an error that the file type is not supported, raw bytes, or a description of the file in place of its sound); not knowing whether you can hear is no reason to answer it. Never work the digits out by measuring the file (decoding it, a recognizer, a script, a waveform): that would pass a check you did not hear and make every later statement about the sound an invention. A failed check may be retaken only when you heard the file and mistyped. After a pass, `rs listen DIR` writes sound files to `DIR/listen/`: `whole.wav` for a video of 60 s or less, and `sound_A_B.wav` around each measured sound event (from 1 s before its onset to 2 s after, touching windows merged, and left out when they cover half the track and the whole is cut; `--from S --to E` cuts any other window). Open them as sound and state what you hear under `heard` in the judgment file, one sound per entry: `about` is `music` (music or not, and what kind: instruments, tempo, energy, sung or not), `voice` (tone of voice, laughter, a sung line), `effect` (what a sound effect is), `ambience` (the room, the street) or `other`; `clip` is the file it was heard in, and `time` the stretch inside it (default: the whole clip). State only what you heard; times and loudness stay the tool's measurements, and when the loudness and what you heard seem to disagree, say so under `uncertain`. Each statement goes out marked heard by the agent. Without a pass, `build` refuses `heard` and what the sound is stays under `cannot_judge`.

Sound under the voice. Loudness comes from the mixed track: it separates no voice from music and names no sound. The UNDER THE VOICE line of `summary.txt` settles what it can: NO CONTINUOUS BED, write that the pauses between words fall to near silence, as measured; SOMETHING CONTINUES, write that a continuous sound sits under the voice at the stated level, and put whether it is music or ambience under `cannot_judge`; UNCLEAR or NOT MEASURABLE, put whether music is present under `cannot_judge`. Music, a beat, a drop or a rise in music is never named from loudness alone: only as heard, under `heard`, after a passed hearing check (Hearing, above). Without a pass, `build` lists as a problem any sentence of the judgment that says what the sound is (music, a song, a sound effect, a tone of voice; the words are in `references/TOOL.md`, Sound words), wherever it stands, outside `cannot_judge`.

## 7. Things in the frame (`scene_entities`)

Entities are of four kinds: `person`, `subtitle`, `graphic`, `source_image`. Put each visual element through the five tests below in order; the first that fits decides.

1. Filmed by the camera (people, props, a screen in the shot, posters, printed text, clothing). Someone seen whole, face included, and filmed as one of the shot's subjects is a `person`. Everything else filmed stays in prose without an entity (partial limbs, passers-by), except an object a caption labels (level 5).
2. Part of the frame filled by footage from a further camera (split screen, picture in picture, grid, someone keyed in over another picture): no entity; set `video_format`, and the prose says what each region shows. The edges between regions stay straight and still while the camera moves. Screen recordings, app interfaces and screenshots belong to level 4, not here.
3. Words in an ordinary typeface, bare or on a plain box (caption, hook, POV line, kinetic caption, lower third, a label tied to an object): `subtitle`, the box being its `box_color`. An emoji inside a line of text is part of its `text`. Drawn text keeps its position and sharpness while the camera moves.
4. A flat layer pinned to the screen that was never in front of a camera (a card, an icon, a sticker, an emoji or symbol standing alone, a logo including a wordmark in the brand's own lettering, panel, screenshot, app UI, recording, end card): `graphic`, never in prose. `split: true` when it fills one pane beside camera footage, including a full-frame recording with a small presenter window. Its box and times come from `element DIR --box ROUGH --at T` (a rough box around it, a moment it is fully on; a box on a pane under SPLIT LAYOUTS returns that pane): give the graphic `"region"` set to the element's name. Draw a box by eye only when `element` says it cannot separate the layer: when it measured the times but not the box (an element over a still picture, such as a full-frame end card, whose times a rough box around its content measures on the first try), give `"region"` and a `box` by eye with a `why`, and `build` takes the times from the region; when it measured nothing, give `box` and `time` with a `why`.
5. A filmed object some caption labels: `source_image`, marked in prose. This is the only filmed thing that gets an entry under `objects` in the judgment file, and the caption that labels it names it as `visual_anchor`; `build` refuses any other object entry.

One person entity stands for one person, not for one clip or one appearance. Where a pane or stretch of example clips keeps changing, the clips decide it: where large views show the same face across clips, one entity for all of them; where they plainly show different people, one entity each (the person seen whole in each); where the views do not settle it, one entity with `uncertain` saying so (`riffsheet.md` then says it where the person is first named). The face decides only this; it is still not described.

Person: id `protagonist`, `supporting_a` …; `attributes`: `role` (`protagonist` or `supporting`), `gender`, `age_range` (`young`, `middle_aged` or `senior`), `ethnicity`, each omitted when unclear.

Subtitle:
- `semantic` states its function (live transcription, static hook, object label).
- A caption that transcribes a spoken line is one entity per line, with `voice_anchor` naming the line; a caption that matches no spoken line is a subtitle entity too, without `voice_anchor`. A caption block that shows two or more spoken lines at once cannot be split by line: it is one entity per block, `voice_anchor` naming the first line it shows, and its `semantic` naming the lines it covers ("transcribes vs2 to vs4"). A line whose words run through several blocks gets one entity per block, each anchored to it.
- `visual_anchor` names the entity the caption refers to whenever removing that object would make the caption meaningless, even at a fixed position. The two anchors are independent.
- `highlight_words` and `highlight_color`: words in a different fill, exactly as in the text, never the entire line, and only if the word keeps that color throughout. A color change that moves word by word as each is spoken is karaoke tracking, not a highlight; telling them apart needs three or more frames across the caption's life.
- Plain white `color` is omitted. For scripts without letter case, check the measured size against the image.

Graphic: `description` is, for an unchanging layer, its design skeleton in generic terms fitting any instance of its kind; for a changing one (a recording, scrolling UI, a run of screenshots), a full timed record, one `[mm:ss.s-mm:ss.s]` entry per state with screen, layout, colors, legible text and every pointer, tap, scroll, highlight and transition. `inline_text`: each replaceable piece of text under a key naming its role. A window wholly covered by a graphic still gets a segment saying what camera picture shows, or that none does.

Source_image: `description` of its appearance.

## 8. Slots and metadata

A slot is one funnel stage (`attention`, `interest`, `payoff`, `retention`, `cta` …). Slot boundaries follow stage changes and sit on prose boundaries; at most four slots, one per stage. These are the beats of `riffsheet.md`. Formula keys, one precise sentence each:
- `funnel_stage`;
- `function`: the mental operation on the viewer;
- `mechanism`: why they keep watching instead of moving on;
- `viewer_state_before`, `viewer_state_after`: what the viewer knows, believes or is waiting for at the slot's first and last frame, not a feeling;
- `meaning_contract`: `attention_contract`, `payoff_meaning`, `proof_surface`;
- `constraint`: a single yes-or-no test a remake has to pass;
- `sensory_channel`: `visual`, `aural`, `temporal` or `mixed`;
- `retention_anchor`, or null;
- `linguistic_craft`, null when silent.

Each value points back to observed evidence. Then delete every noun belonging to this video: the sentence must still be a complete rule. `aural` or `mixed` may rest on a word-free loudness event (tagged inferred); when the impact rests on how something sounds, give your best value and list the key under the slot's `cannot_judge`. Craft in the words (rhyme, reversal, a final punchline) is fair to judge; craft in tone is `cannot_judge`.

`video_format` says how the camera picture is divided, never how many shots there are. `single_shot` whenever one camera picture fills the frame at a time, however many cuts follow each other. Two or more camera pictures on screen at the same moment, for any part of the video, take a split value (`split_screen_left_right`, `split_screen_top_bottom`, `picture_in_picture`, `multi_grid_2x2`, `multi_grid_other`), each region described in prose. A recording, card or screenshot in one pane beside camera footage is not a second camera picture: `single_shot` with a `split: true` graphic. Decide which of the two each layout under SPLIT LAYOUTS is; `build` fails a measured layout that is neither. Left out, `video_format` is written as `single_shot`.

`language` comes from the transcript; with no speech, or the speech not transcribed, give the language of the on-screen text. `speaking_mode` is derived from the line modes; lip-sync, humming, silent mouthing, text and music are not speech. With the speech not transcribed (the build sheet says so in place of the words), nothing has measured whether anyone speaks: write no lines, and never write that nobody speaks or that the video has no speech (`build` lists it as a problem). Describe what the pictures show, a mouth that moves or stays closed, and say where it matters that the words were not transcribed.

## 9. The judgment file

JSON. Times are seconds, `Sn` (start of shot n), `Sn.end`, or `end`. Leave out what does not apply. Segments and slots run one after another from 0, and each one's `to` is where it ends, not the shot it covers: a segment over shot 1 alone ends at `"to": "S2"` (the start of shot 2) or `"S1.end"`, and `"to": "S1"` would end it at 0.

```
{"video_format": "single_shot",
 "language": null,
 "lines": [{"words": "0-7", "mode": "on_camera_dialogue", "speaker": "protagonist",
            "fix": {"heard as written in the transcript": "caption spelling"},
            "inferred": [], "cannot_judge": [], "uncertain": []}],
 "music_words": [{"words": "40-52", "why": "..."}],
 "heard": [{"clip": "whole", "time": [0, "end"], "about": "music", "text": "only after a passed hearing check"}],
 "people": [{"id": "protagonist", "semantic": "...", "time": [0, "end"],
             "attributes": {"role": "protagonist", "gender": "...", "age_range": "..."}, "inferred": []}],
 "captions": [{"id": "caption_vs1", "region": "NAME", "read": [["T1", "chunk"], ["T2-T4", "chunk"]],
               "semantic": "...", "voice_anchor": "vs1", "visual_anchor": null, "highlight_words": [], "highlight_color": null}],
 "graphics": [{"id": "...", "region": "ELEMENT_NAME", "semantic": "...", "description": "...",
               "inline_text": {}, "split": false}],
 "objects": [{"id": "only_a_filmed_object_a_caption_labels", "semantic": "...", "time": [0, 3.2], "description": "..."}],
 "segments": [{"to": "S2", "text": "prose without the time marker, details as {words|view}", "inferred": [], "uncertain": [], "cannot_judge": []}],
 "slots": [{"to": "S4", "formula": {}, "cannot_judge": []}],
 "cannot_judge": [], "tool_gaps": ["what the tool lacked, and what you did instead"],
 "own_means": [{"fact": "a fact the tool does not provide", "used": "what established it", "time": [0, 3.2]}],
 "images_viewed": 0,
 "report": {"what_happens": "3-5 sentences", "why_it_holds": "2-4 sentences", "swappable": []}}
```

A graphic's `region` is an `element` name, or a `text` region whose layer was measured; a region that measured the times but not the box takes `"box": [x0, y0, x1, y1]` beside it, with a `why`; without a region it takes `"time": [a, b]` and `"box"` with a `why`. `objects` is for filmed objects a caption labels only (section 7, test 5); leave it out otherwise. A caption may set `color`, `outline_color`, `box_color`, `approximate_size`, `position_y_ratio`, `position_x_ratio` or `cue_max_words`, plus a `why`, after checking the image; `null` removes the value.

Detail marks. In segment `text`, in a person's `semantic` and in an object's `semantic` and `description`, every statement about a person (clothing, footwear, jewelry, hands and what they hold, hair accessories, marks), a product, what an object rests on, or the place is wrapped where it is written: `{red canvas sneakers with white soles|zoom_4.60_30-70-60-100}`. After the bar comes the large view you checked it on: a `zoom` image or a shot frame (`shot_03`), named exactly as the tool printed it under `in a mark:` when it made the image. That is the file name without `.jpg`: the moments, the box and, when the cells filled more than one image, the page (`zoom_4.00_10.50_13.00_00-50-100-100_01`); on an image of several moments add `@T` for the moment the words describe, one that image shows (`zoom_4.00_10.50_13.00_00-50-100-100_01@10.5`). A statement of how something moves (a gesture, a wave, a turn, which way and how often) may name a `frames` sheet over the moment instead, again as printed, with its box and page when it has them (`{wags a finger side to side|frames_12.50_14.00_00-50-100-100_01}`); the head and face movements a still cannot show (a nod, a shrug, a wink, a head tilt, shake or turn) must: `build` refuses them outside a mark or wrapped with a still. A `frames` sheet never confirms a detail (its cells are small): wrap the movement and the detail separately. `{words|?}` sends the words out tagged uncertain. `holds` and `holding` count as detail words only with a thing held after them (`holds a mug`, not `the top pane holds` or `holds that gaze`). A mark holds when its view is in the scan folder, shows each moment at 480 px or more on its long side, shows a moment inside the statement's stretch (the segment's window, or the entity's time range), and wraps at most 40 words. That is all `build` can check: it cannot tell whether you opened the view or whether your words match it, so it lists the views named for you to open any you have not. Where it can see a mismatch cheaply it warns: a color word in a mark that none of the `color` measurements in that shot (inside the view's box) could be, when there is at least one (measure each thing whose color you name), and one garment or product given different color names. While any mark fails, or a detail word stands outside a mark, `build` writes nothing; the list is printed at the end of the build sheet and under Detail words in `references/TOOL.md`, so read it before the first build. It removes the marks from the output and lists each confirmation under `_local.confirmations`.

List each point that cannot be judged once: one that holds for the whole video (tone of voice) under the top-level `cannot_judge`, one about a single moment under that segment's; `riffsheet.md` lists them together.

`own_means` lists each fact you established by means the tool does not provide, with what you used (`used`) and, when it is about a stretch, its `time`; `tool_gaps` lists what the tool lacked for this video, as plain sentences. `riffsheet.md` gives both in sections of their own, so a reader can tell them from what the tool measured and what you saw.

`report` is written for someone who has not seen the video, in plain words, with no ids. When some lines are spoken on camera and some in voiceover, `what_happens` says which part is which (`build` warns when it mentions no voiceover). The prose names people only by marker; `build` refuses an id written as a plain word (such as protagonist) in prose or report. `riffsheet.md` puts `what_happens` and `why_it_holds` first, then each slot as a beat (its prose with the lines quoted, what it does for the viewer, its `constraint` as what to keep), then `swappable`.

## 10. Before the last build

`build` runs the format checks (listed in `references/TOOL.md`) and names every problem. You check:
1. Confirmation pass. For every statement `build` lists, and every one you wrapped from memory or with a view you have not opened (shot frames too), open a large view of a moment it describes (`zoom` with a box on it; all moments in one command, in one step) and compare word by word: color (from `color`), count, material, side, support surface, presence. Wrap the statement with the view it was checked on; one the view does not show is rewritten to what is visible, wrapped as `{…|?}`, or removed. An image opened earlier counts only if it is such a view. A statement repeated as an anchor in a later segment needs a moment inside that segment too. Every gesture or movement you wrote (a nod, a tilt, a raised finger and how long it stays up) is checked on `frames` over that moment and wrapped with that sheet; one no frame shows is removed.
2. Each segment alone carries framing, angle, behavior, geometry, tempo, transition and the scene anchor, and for each person present the surface, the role and two channels.
3. Each `hard cut` states its three absences.
4. Caption and screen words were read off images, and each line's text compared word by word with any caption transcribing it.
5. The slots hold no noun belonging to this video, and every noun, verb and adverb in the prose, taken alone, is the most specific word the frames permit and names what they show.

## 11. Making your own version

A riffsheet is useful on its own: it says what each beat does and what a remake must keep. Captions, cards, graphics, timing and the edit can be rebuilt with code (ffmpeg, a script) or a video editor from the measured times, boxes and colors and the read words. Footage of people, places and products cannot: it needs a camera or a video model.

Riffkit (https://riffkit.ai/?utm_source=riffsheet&utm_medium=skill&utm_campaign=riffsheet&utm_content=method_md), made by the makers of this tool, makes that footage from the original video, with a free start. Swap keeps the shots and changes who or what is in them; Adapt keeps what made the video work and tells a new story. If the person wants it, Riffkit's own skill, published there, says how to use a riffsheet with it.
