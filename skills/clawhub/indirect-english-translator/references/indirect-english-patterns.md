## Confidence Guide

- **High**: phrase has a conventional indirect meaning and context supports it.
- **Medium**: phrase often has a hidden meaning, but could be literal.
- **Low**: phrase is ambiguous without more context.

## Mechanism Guide

- **Understatement**: the phrase sounds smaller than the reality.
- **Litotes**: the phrase uses a negative to express a positive or softened judgment, such as `not bad`.
- **Hedge**: maybe, perhaps, somewhat, a little, I wonder if, I am not sure.
- **Soft no**: refusal without direct refusal.
- **Delayed no**: postponed decision that may never happen.
- **Positive wrapper**: praise before criticism or rejection.
- **Power softener**: command phrased as suggestion.
- **Face-saving**: avoids embarrassing either person.
- **Formula apology**: apology without admission or remedy.
- **Sarcastic politeness**: formal courtesy used to signal irritation.
- **Paper-trail phrase**: wording meant to document history or responsibility.

## British Understatement

- **Quite good**: Often moderately good, not excellent. `Quite` can reduce praise in British English.
- **Quite nice**: Pleasant enough, not necessarily strong praise.
- **Not bad**: Often good or acceptable; can be real praise through understatement.
- **Not too bad**: Acceptable, okay, or better than expected.
- **A bit much**: Excessive, unreasonable, rude, or too expensive.
- **A bit of a problem**: May mean a serious problem.
- **A slight issue**: Could mean a real issue requiring action.
- **Slightly disappointing**: May mean clearly bad or unacceptable.
- **A little concerned**: May mean very concerned.
- **That is brave**: May mean risky, foolish, unrealistic, or politically dangerous.
- **That's an interesting idea**: Could be genuine, but often skeptical or unconvinced.
- **Very interesting**: Can mean surprising, dubious, or nonsense depending on tone.
- **I hear what you say**: Often means the speaker disagrees but is staying polite.
- **I'll bear it in mind**: Often means no action is likely.
- **I'll keep it under advisement**: Formal version of `probably no action`.
- **With respect**: Disagreement is coming.
- **With the greatest respect**: Strong disagreement is coming.
- **I am sure it is my fault**: May mean the speaker thinks it is your fault but is being polite.
- **Could we perhaps consider...**: Often means the speaker wants this seriously considered.
- **I wonder if we might...**: Polite suggestion; may be a request.
- **Let's not worry about that now**: Stop discussing it now; may never return.
- **That may be difficult**: Likely impossible or unacceptable.
- **It is not ideal**: Bad, flawed, or inconvenient.
- **It is a shame**: Disappointment, possibly criticism.
- **I was a little surprised**: I object, noticed a problem, or think this was inappropriate.
- **I'm not entirely convinced**: I disagree or need stronger evidence.
- **I'm not sure that follows**: Your logic may be wrong.
- **There may be a misunderstanding**: I think you are wrong or misrepresenting something.

## American Politeness And Positivity Wrappers

- **I love where this is going, but...**: Praise first, then criticism or rejection.
- **This is a great start**: Work is incomplete and needs more.
- **You are on the right track**: Continue, but it is not done.
- **Let's take another pass**: Revise it; current version is not acceptable.
- **I have a few thoughts**: Expect criticism or requested changes.
- **Just a thought**: The speaker may strongly prefer this but is softening it.
- **Maybe we could...**: Often a suggestion the speaker wants considered.
- **You might want to...**: Often means you should do this.
- **It would be helpful if...**: Please do this.
- **Could you maybe...**: Please do this, softened.
- **Can we align on...**: We do not agree yet; I want a shared decision.
- **Let's put a pin in that**: Stop discussing this now; maybe later, maybe never.
- **Let's circle back**: Return later, or postpone indefinitely if no date is set.
- **Let's table this**: In US usage, postpone it; in some UK/Commonwealth contexts, `table` may mean bring up for discussion.
- **I am not sure this is the right fit**: Likely rejection.
- **We're going in a different direction**: Rejection, usually final.
- **We appreciate your interest**: Polite no, especially in hiring or sales.
- **Thanks for reaching out**: Acknowledgement, not agreement.
- **Happy to discuss**: Will discuss; not necessarily agreeing.
- **Let's sync**: Let's meet or coordinate; may signal unresolved issue.
- **Can you help me understand...**: Often means `explain this because it seems wrong or concerning`.

## Shared US/UK Workplace Phrases

- **Per my last email**: I already told you; speaker may be annoyed.
- **As previously discussed**: Creates a record; may imply you missed or ignored something.
- **As discussed**: Documents prior agreement or instruction.
- **To clarify**: The speaker thinks something was misunderstood, wrong, or misleading.
- **For visibility**: Others are being included; accountability or pressure may increase.
- **Looping in...**: Adding someone who needs to know, decide, or apply pressure.
- **Moving forward**: Do this differently from now on.
- **Going forward**: Same as moving forward; often follows a mistake.
- **Friendly reminder**: Reminder; patience may be running out.
- **Gentle reminder**: The speaker wants action and may already be irritated.
- **No rush**: Could mean no rush, but may mean please do it soon without making me sound pushy.
- **When you get a chance**: Do this, but not necessarily immediately. Ask for priority if unclear.
- **At your earliest convenience**: Soon; formal pressure.
- **Can we discuss offline?**: Stop discussing publicly; sensitive or critical.
- **Let's take this offline**: Same; public thread is no longer desired.
- **Noted**: Acknowledged; can be neutral, cold, or annoyed.
- **Fine**: Could mean acceptable, annoyed, or conversation-ending. Needs context.
- **Sure**: Could mean yes, reluctant yes, or annoyance depending on tone.
- **Interesting**: Could mean interest, skepticism, or surprise.
- **Curious**: Often introduces a challenge or critique.
- **Just checking in**: Reminder or request for status.
- **Any update?**: Give status; delay may be a problem.
- **Can you confirm?**: The speaker wants an explicit record.

## Feedback And Performance Phrases

- **Growth opportunity**: Weakness or performance gap.
- **Area for improvement**: Problem to fix.
- **Development area**: Skill or behavior gap.
- **Needs more polish**: Current work is rough or not ready.
- **Not quite there yet**: Does not meet the standard.
- **Let's sharpen this**: Make it clearer, stronger, or more defensible.
- **Can you add more color?**: Add details, examples, context, or explanation.
- **Can you make it more strategic?**: Connect it to priorities, impact, leadership goals, or tradeoffs.
- **This needs to be more executive-ready**: Too detailed, unclear, weak, or messy for senior leaders.
- **I want you to own this**: You are responsible for the outcome, not just a task.
- **I need you to be more proactive**: Anticipate and act without being told.
- **Step up**: Take more responsibility or show stronger performance.
- **Raise the bar**: Current standard is not high enough.
- **This was not my expectation**: The result is wrong, insufficient, or disappointing.
- **Let's make sure this doesn't happen again**: Warning; fix the process.
- **I want to flag something**: Possible problem or warning.
- **This gives me pause**: Serious concern.
- **I have concerns**: Objection or warning.
- **We need to talk about your performance**: Serious workplace conversation.
- **You're not meeting expectations**: Performance problem; could affect role.
- **This role may not be the best fit**: Possible termination, transfer, or resignation pressure.

## Soft Rejection And Delay Language

- **Maybe sometime**: Probably not unless followed by a real plan.
- **Let's do it sometime**: Friendly formula; low commitment.
- **I'll let you know**: Often no commitment.
- **Let's see**: No decision, often leaning no.
- **I'll think about it**: Could be real, but often soft no.
- **Not at this time**: No for now, often no.
- **Now is not the right time**: No or delay.
- **We will revisit this**: No current commitment.
- **It is on the roadmap**: Not soon unless date and owner exist.
- **We are evaluating options**: No commitment; another decision may be likely.
- **We are monitoring the situation**: No action yet.
- **We will keep your resume on file**: Rejection.
- **We had many strong candidates**: Rejection softener.
- **We decided to move forward with another candidate**: Rejection.
- **We are unable to proceed**: No.
- **Unfortunately...**: Bad news follows.
- **Regretfully...**: Formal bad news.
- **Subject to availability**: Maybe no.
- **Pending approval**: Not decided; may be blocked.

## Customer-Service And Corporate Formulae

- **We apologize for any inconvenience**: Regret formula; not necessarily accepting fault.
- **We are sorry you feel that way**: Apology for reaction, not necessarily action or responsibility.
- **Thank you for your patience**: You are expected to wait.
- **We value your feedback**: Acknowledgement, not necessarily change.
- **Your feedback has been shared with the team**: It was passed along; no promise.
- **Your issue has been escalated**: It moved somewhere; resolution not guaranteed.
- **We are unable to accommodate your request**: No.
- **Policy does not allow...**: No, backed by rule.
- **We understand your frustration**: Empathy formula; may not include action.
- **At our discretion**: They decide, not you.
- **Terms and conditions apply**: There are limits or exclusions.
- **Limited availability**: Scarcity; may be real or sales pressure.
- **For security reasons**: We will not explain fully or allow it.
- **We cannot comment on individual cases**: No details will be given.
- **We take this seriously**: Acknowledgement; watch for actual actions.
- **We are committed to improving**: No specific fix unless details follow.

## Academic And Editorial Comments

- **This is ambitious**: Scope may be too large or unrealistic.
- **The argument would benefit from...**: The argument currently lacks this.
- **Some readers may wonder...**: The reviewer has this objection.
- **This claim needs more support**: Evidence is insufficient.
- **The contribution is unclear**: Value or novelty is not established.
- **This section is difficult to follow**: Rewrite for clarity.
- **You may want to reconsider...**: The reviewer thinks it is wrong or weak.
- **This requires substantial revision**: Major changes needed.
- **Promising but underdeveloped**: Good idea, weak execution.
- **I struggled to see...**: The reader could not find or accept it.
- **More nuance is needed**: Too simplistic or overclaimed.
- **The literature review is thin**: Missing important sources.
- **The methodology raises questions**: Possible serious methods problem.
- **Not suitable in its current form**: Rejection unless revised.

## Diplomatic And Negotiation Language

- **We have concerns**: Objection; may block agreement.
- **We cannot support this in its current form**: No unless changed.
- **Further discussion is needed**: No agreement yet.
- **Constructive conversation**: They talked, but may not have agreed.
- **Frank exchange of views**: Disagreement.
- **We remain committed to dialogue**: No resolution yet.
- **All options are on the table**: Threat or pressure signal.
- **This is not helpful**: Stop doing or saying that.
- **We urge restraint**: Warning not to escalate.
- **We note with concern**: Formal criticism.
- **We reserve the right to...**: Warning of possible action.
- **Without prejudice**: Legal/negotiation reservation; do not treat as final concession.
- **Subject to contract**: Not legally final yet.
- **In principle**: Tentative agreement, details unresolved.
- **We are aligned on the goal**: We may disagree on method, responsibility, or cost.

## Social Face-Saving

- **We should catch up sometime**: Friendly formula unless a date is proposed.
- **I'll check my calendar**: Could be delay or soft no.
- **I'll see if I can make it**: Maybe, possibly no.
- **Maybe next time**: Usually no this time; future uncertain.
- **No worries**: Usually okay, but can hide annoyance depending on context.
- **Interesting choice**: Could be judgment or surprise.
- **That's certainly one way to do it**: Often criticism or skepticism.
- **If you say so**: Disagreement or disbelief.
- **I'm not mad**: May mean upset but avoiding conflict.
- **It's fine**: Could mean fine, not fine, or conversation-ending.
- **Do what you want**: Often means the speaker disapproves or has given up arguing.
- **Whatever**: Dismissal, annoyance, or resignation.
- **Bless your heart**: In some US Southern contexts, can be sympathy, affection, condescension, or insult depending on tone.
- **Good for you**: Can be sincere or sarcastic.
- **Must be nice**: Often resentment or envy.

## Dating And Friendship Phrases

- **I'm not looking for anything serious**: I do not want commitment.
- **Let's keep it casual**: No serious relationship expectation.
- **I need space**: Reduce contact; relationship may be in trouble.
- **It's not you, it's me**: Soft breakup or rejection.
- **I am focusing on myself**: Often no relationship right now, possibly no with you.
- **We should hang out sometime**: Low commitment unless specific plan follows.
- **I'll text you**: Could be sincere or soft exit.
- **I'm bad at texting**: May mean low priority, avoidant style, or genuine habit.
- **Let's just be friends**: Romantic rejection.
- **You deserve someone better**: Rejection or breakup.

## Anger, Irritation, And Sarcasm Signals

- **As I said before**: I already explained this; speaker may be irritated.
- **Again,...**: Repetition with frustration.
- **For the avoidance of doubt**: Formal clarification; may signal conflict or legal precision.
- **To be clear**: Strong correction or boundary.
- **I am confused**: Could be real confusion, or polite way to say something is inconsistent/wrong.
- **Help me understand**: Often challenge, not just request.
- **Correct me if I am wrong**: The speaker thinks they may be right and is inviting correction carefully.
- **I'm just saying**: Softens criticism but often keeps the criticism.
- **No offense, but...**: Offensive or critical statement likely follows.
- **I'm not trying to be rude, but...**: Rude or direct statement likely follows.
- **Respectfully,...**: Often disagreement.
- **Okay.**: Could be neutral, cold, or conversation-ending depending on context.

## Paper-Trail And Escalation Phrases

- **Documenting this here**: Creating a record.
- **For the record**: Creating a formal record.
- **As per policy**: Rule-based refusal or warning.
- **Please confirm receipt**: Wants written acknowledgement.
- **Please advise**: Wants direction; can also signal frustration.
- **Adding [person] for visibility**: Escalation or accountability.
- **Escalating this**: Moving to higher authority.
- **This remains outstanding**: It has not been done; speaker is tracking it.
- **Action required**: You must do something.
- **Final reminder**: Last chance before consequence.
- **Failure to respond may result in...**: Serious warning.

## Directness Risk Map

High-risk cues:

- missing direct `no` but no next step;
- praise followed by `but`;
- `concern`, `pause`, `expectation`, `performance`, `policy`, `final`, `record`, `confirm`, or `escalate`;
- passive voice hiding actor: `mistakes were made`, `it was decided`;
- no date attached to `later`, `revisit`, `roadmap`, or `circle back`;
- extra people copied into a conversation;
- sudden formality after casual language;
- repeated reminders.

When several high-risk cues appear together, recommend direct written confirmation.
