# Reply And Rewrite Playbook

Use this reference when drafting responses or rewriting indirect English into simple direct English.

## General Reply Formula

Use this structure:

1. Acknowledge politely.
2. State your current understanding in direct words.
3. Ask a specific clarifying question.
4. Request deadline, owner, standard, or next step when relevant.
5. Keep the tone neutral.

Template:

`Thanks. Just to make sure I understand: [direct meaning]. Is that right? If so, what should I do next / by when / to what standard?`

## Safe Clarifying Replies

### Possible No

- `Should I treat this as a no for now, or is there a specific next step?`
- `Is this something you want to revisit on a specific date, or should I close it?`
- `What would need to change for this to become a yes?`

### Vague Feedback

- `What are the top two changes that would make this meet the standard?`
- `Can you give me one example of what good would look like here?`
- `Which part is the biggest concern: accuracy, tone, structure, timing, or level of detail?`

### Hidden Urgency

- `Is this urgent, and when do you need it?`
- `Should I prioritize this above my current work?`
- `What happens if this is not done today?`

### Polite Disagreement

- `It sounds like you may see this differently. What is your main concern?`
- `Which assumption do you disagree with?`
- `Would you prefer a different approach?`

### Postponed Decision

- `When should we revisit this, and who owns the next decision?`
- `Should I put a follow-up on the calendar?`
- `Is there anything I should prepare before we revisit it?`

### Workplace Warning

- `Can you clarify what outcome you need and what happens if it is not fixed?`
- `What specific behavior or result should change?`
- `How will we measure whether this is back on track?`

### Customer-Service Refusal

- `Can you confirm whether this is a final decision or whether there is an appeal/escalation path?`
- `Which policy prevents this, and where can I read it?`
- `Is there an alternative you can offer?`

## Rewrite Rules

When rewriting indirect English into simple direct English:

- Keep the meaning, not the politeness fog.
- Use active voice.
- Name the actor when known.
- Convert hedges into clear uncertainty or clear decisions.
- Replace `later` with a date or say no date exists.
- Replace vague praise with concrete praise.
- Replace vague criticism with specific requested change.
- Preserve kindness without hiding the point.

## Direct Rewrite Examples

### Workplace Feedback

Original: `This is a great start, but it could use a bit more polish.`
Direct: `This is not ready yet. Please revise it for clarity, formatting, and completeness.`

Original: `You might want to tighten the intro.`
Direct: `Please make the intro shorter and clearer.`

Original: `I have some concerns about the timeline.`
Direct: `I think the timeline may fail unless we change scope, add resources, or move the deadline.`

### Soft Rejection

Original: `We appreciate your interest, but we are moving forward with other candidates.`
Direct: `You were not selected for this role.`

Original: `Let's circle back later.`
Direct: `We are not deciding now. No next step exists unless we set one.`

### Customer Service

Original: `We apologize for any inconvenience, but policy does not allow us to process a refund.`
Direct: `We will not refund you because our policy says no.`

### Social

Original: `We should catch up sometime.`
Direct: `This is a friendly expression. It is not a plan unless we choose a date.`

Original: `I'll check my calendar.`
Direct: `Maybe. I am not committing yet.`

## Tone Levels

### Gentle

Use when the user wants to preserve warmth:

`Thanks, I understand. Just to avoid misunderstanding, should I treat this as [direct meaning]?`

### Neutral Professional

Use for workplace, customer-service, and academic contexts:

`Thanks. To confirm, my understanding is [direct meaning]. Please let me know if that is incorrect. What is the next step?`

### Firm

Use when the user needs boundaries:

`I need a clear answer so I can act correctly. Is this approved, rejected, or waiting on a specific condition?`

### Blunt

Use only when requested:

`This sounds like a polite no. There is no concrete next step, owner, or date.`

## When To Recommend Written Confirmation

Recommend written confirmation when:

- the message affects job status, money, contract terms, legal rights, safety, immigration, health, or housing;
- the other person gives a vague verbal answer but the outcome matters;
- the phrase includes `policy`, `performance`, `final`, `record`, `confirm`, `escalate`, `failure to`, or `reserve the right`;
- multiple people are copied or senior people are added;
- the user is being asked to accept blame, waive rights, or commit to a deadline.

## Non-Native Speaker Notes

Explain that indirect English is often used to protect face, avoid conflict, maintain hierarchy, or sound professional. It is not always dishonest. However, in workplaces it can hide important decisions, rejections, criticism, and deadlines. Encourage users to ask precise follow-up questions instead of guessing.
