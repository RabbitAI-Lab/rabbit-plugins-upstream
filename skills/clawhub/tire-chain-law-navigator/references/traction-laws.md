# Winter Traction Law — Reference

## United States

### California (Caltrans) — R1/R2/R3
Posted on changeable message signs; enforced CHP.
- **R1**: chains required *unless* snow tires (M+S, 6/32"+ tread) or cables.
  AWD with snow tires fine.
- **R2**: chains unless 4WD/AWD with snow-rated tires (M+S/3PMSF). 2WD:
  chains on drive axle.
- **R3**: chains on ALL vehicles, no exemptions. Rare; usually road closes
  soon after.
- Fines: ~$100-ish base for non-compliance, but **$1,000+ if you're
  involved in a collision/blocking** (misdemeanor obstruction added).
- Info: Caltrans QuickMap / 1-800-427-7623 (ROAD).

### Colorado — Traction Law & Chain Law (separate!)
- **Traction Law (Code 15)**: snow on roadway → snow tires (3/16" tread),
  chains, or AWD/4WD. Fine ~$130; ~$650+ if you spin out and block.
- **Passenger Vehicle Chain Law (Code 16)**: chains/AutoSock on ALL passenger
  vehicles — no AWD exemption. Rare, severe.
- **Commercial**: separate chain law Sept 1–May 31 on I-70 (mountain
  corridor), fines up to $1,000+.
- The I-70 mountain corridor (Dotsero–Morrison) triggers the Traction Law
  proactively on forecast snow — it can be ACTIVE BEFORE IT SNOWS.

### Washington / Oregon
- WA: chains required when signed (WSP enforces); studded tires legal
  Nov 1–Mar 31. Snoqualmie/Stayman passes close to unchained traffic when
  WSDOT posts "chains required on all vehicles except AWD..."
- OR: carry chains in snow zone Nov–Mar when signed; conditional closures
  where AWD+snows may pass without chains.

### BC (Canada)
- Most highways: commercial vehicles carry Nov 1–Mar 31; passenger vehicles
  when signed. Fines $157–$595. Coquihalla/weigh-scale enforcement common.
- "Winter tires or chains" signs = M+S or 3PMSF required Oct 1–Apr 30 on
  many mountain routes.

## Europe (Alps)

- Sign **267** (chain mandatory on snow-covered road) / **268** (chains
  fitted, snow on road) — Vienna Convention, binding in FR/IT/AT/DE...
- France: chains on drive axle when 267 posted; fine ~135 €; many passes
  RECOMMEND carrying Nov–Mar. AutoSock generally accepted as chaînes à
  neige textiles in FR.
- Italy: catene o gomme invernali law A122: chains OR winter tires Nov 15–Apr 15 on signed
  roads (valle d'Aosta etc.); ~80–318 € fine.
- Switzerland: chains when signed; fines modest; some passes closed all
  winter.
- Austria: winter tire obligation Nov 1–Apr 15 (7mm tread), chains when
  signed; Mountain parking areas often require them.
- Germany: situational "situationale Winterreifenpflicht" — fines + blame
  shift in accidents.

## Physics aside: why R3 exists

Chains double the friction coefficient on packed snow/ice vs the best
winter tire. When the road is ice-glazed, even new 3PMSF tires (~µ 0.25)
can't hold grades; chains (~µ 0.5+) can. That's why "no exemptions" exists
at the top of every escalation ladder — it's not bureaucracy, it's µ.

## Chain-up practice

- Locate signed chain-up zones BEFORE the checkpoint (look for "Chain
  Up Ahead" / "Zone de montage").
- Install on drive axle (2WD), all four best (AWD: front AND rear often
  recommended — follow manufacturer; some AWD must NOT use chains at all
  due to clearance: check manual first!).
- 25–30 mph with chains. Re-tighten after first half mile.
- Practice once in a dry garage BEFORE your trip: at -10°C in a slushy
  shoulder at night is the wrong time to learn.
