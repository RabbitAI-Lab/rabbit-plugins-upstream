# Insurance Claims Guide

> What to document **before** you need to file a claim.

## Why Document Now?

After a fire, flood, or burglary, you're stressed, grieving, and under pressure from your insurer. Trying to reconstruct what you owned from memory — with receipts, serial numbers, and values — is nearly impossible. A home inventory built **before** a loss is the single most powerful tool for a smooth, fair claim settlement.

## What to Document for Each Item

| Field            | Why it matters                                             |
|------------------|------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Photo**        | Visual proof the item existed and its condition            |
| **Serial number**| Proves ownership; insurers and police use it to match recovered stolen goods |
| **Brand/model**  | Needed for replacement cost calculation (like-for-like)    |
| **Purchase date**| Establishes age and depreciation                           |
| **Receipt**      | Gold-standard proof of value; store digitally               |
| **Estimated value** | What it would cost to replace today (not what you paid) |

## The 4 Evidence Levels (Strongest → Weakest)

1. **Receipt + photo + serial** — bulletproof
2. **Photo + serial** — very strong
3. **Photo + brand/model** — good
4. **Written description only** — better than nothing, but expect pushback

## Photo Best Practices

- Take photos in good lighting, items in their normal location
- Capture serial number plates up close (legible)
- Show condition — open doors, drawers, turn on electronics
- For sets (dining room, tools), photograph the whole set together
- Store photos **off-site** — cloud, external drive at a relative's house, safety deposit box

## Coverage Types — Know the Difference

| Coverage              | What it pays                                      |
|-----------------------|---------------------------------------------------|
| **Actual Cash Value (ACV)** | Replacement cost **minus depreciation**     |
| **Replacement Cost (RC)**   | What it costs to buy a new equivalent item  |

Always check your policy. RC coverage is worth the extra premium for most households.

## Special Categories Need Extra Attention

### Jewelry & Watches
- Almost always require a separate **rider or scheduled personal property endorsement**
- Get professional appraisals every 3–5 years
- Photos must show the piece clearly (on a neutral background)

### Art & Collectibles
- Professional appraisal required for items above your policy's single-article limit
- Keep provenance documents (certificates of authenticity, purchase records)

### Electronics
- Serial numbers are critical (police check these against stolen-goods databases)
- Note warranty/AppleCare+ status — may cover replacement separately

### Firearms
- Require separate scheduling in most policies
- Record serial numbers, make, model, and caliber

## High-Value Items (>$1,000)

These items are the most disputed during claims. For each:

1. Keep the original receipt (scan it)
2. Take multiple photos
3. Record the serial number
4. If above your policy's per-article limit, **schedule it separately**

## How Often to Update

| Event                          | Action                          |
|--------------------------------|---------------------------------|
| Major purchase (> $500)        | Add within 1 week               |
| Annual review                  | Walk through every room with the inventory |
| Before a move                  | Photo everything, update box assignments |
| Renovation / remodel           | Re-photograph affected rooms    |
| Receipts found                 | Scan and link to item record    |

## After a Loss — Step by Step

1. **Do not throw anything away** — insurers want to see damaged goods
2. **Photograph everything** before cleanup
3. **File a police report** (for theft/vandalism) — get the report number
4. **Contact your insurer immediately** — note the claim number and adjuster's name
5. **Use your inventory** — print the insurance report and mark each item as lost/damaged
6. **Provide serial numbers** for stolen/destroyed high-value items
7. **Keep all receipts** for temporary lodging, meals, and replacement purchases
8. **Document every conversation** with the adjuster (date, time, what was said)
9. **Don't accept the first offer blindly** — compare to your inventory's replacement values
10. **Get independent estimates** if the insurer's offer seems low

## Red Flags During a Claim

- Adjuster pressures you to settle quickly
- Offer based on ACV when your policy specifies RC
- Items "not covered" without specific policy language citation
- Request to sign a release before all items are accounted for

If any of these happen, request the specific policy language in writing and consider consulting a public adjuster.

- **This inventory script** — run `insurance-report` and hand the output to your adjuster
- **Cloud photo backup** — Google Photos, iCloud, or Dropbox
- **QR labels** — label high-value items with QR codes linking to your item records
- **HomeZada / Sortly / Encircle** — commercial inventory apps (if you want mobile)
