# Quitting an Unwanted Habit

Avoid-habits invert almost every mechanic in this skill: there is no completion event, willpower is the wrong lever, and the design work is a trigger inventory rather than a cue.

**Before planning a quit**, read `## Habits` in `~/Clawic/data/habits/memory.md` for previous attempts at the same target, `## What Works` for what this person has responded to, and `artifacts/quit-<thing>.md` if `## Boxes` lists one — a second attempt that ignores the first attempt's trigger list is a wasted attempt.

## Red Flags First

Check the Red Flags table in SKILL.md before designing anything. Two cases stop the conversation:

- **Physical dependence on alcohol or benzodiazepines.** Abrupt cessation can cause seizures and delirium. Do not produce a cold-turkey plan, a taper schedule, or a quit date — route to a clinician, and say why in one sentence.
- **Restriction-shaped food targets.** "Quit sugar", "no carbs after 6", rules that keep tightening — stop coaching and route (SKILL.md Red Flags).

Nicotine, caffeine and opioid cessation have real withdrawal profiles that are unpleasant rather than dangerous, and are helped by pharmacological support the user should discuss with a clinician or pharmacist. Say that once; do not repeat it.

## Why Do-Habit Machinery Fails

| Do-habit tool | What it does to an avoid-habit |
|---|---|
| A cue | There is already a cue — the trigger — and it belongs to the behavior you are removing |
| A floor | There is no smallest version of not doing something |
| A daily completion | Nothing happens, so nothing is logged; the log fills with the absence of evidence |
| A streak | Counts clean days, which is useful, but a single lapse resets a number that took months — the highest-stakes counter in the skill |
| "Try harder tomorrow" | The behavior is usually automatic and cued; effort is applied after the decision has already been made |

Correct framing: an avoid-habit is a **replacement project**. The trigger will fire regardless; the work is deciding what happens next and making the old response expensive.

## The Trigger Inventory

The first deliverable, before any quit date. Two or three days of observation beat an hour of introspection — most users can name two triggers and actually have six.

Log every instance for 2-3 days with four fields: **time**, **place**, **preceding state** (bored, stressed, after a meal, on a call), **who was present**. Then group.

| Trigger class | Typical examples | Strongest lever |
|---|---|---|
| Temporal | First coffee, after lunch, 22:00 on the sofa | Substitution at that exact slot |
| Spatial | The balcony, the car, the desk, the fridge at night | Friction — change or leave the place |
| Emotional | Stress, boredom, loneliness, celebration | Substitution plus an explicit alternative for that state |
| Social | Specific people, specific settings, drinking rounds | A prepared line, and a decision made before arriving |
| Sequential | Follows another behavior (meal → cigarette, laptop shut → phone) | Break the chain by inserting a step |
| Availability | It is simply within reach | Friction — this is the cheapest one to fix and is usually skipped |

Rank the triggers by frequency in the observation window. Address the top two only. A plan that addresses all six is a plan that is followed for four days.

## Substitution

Every trigger gets a named replacement behavior that occupies the same slot and is available in the same place. Unspecified substitution ("do something else") does not survive contact with a real urge.

Selection criteria, in order:
1. **Available at the trigger** without preparation — if it needs fetching, it loses.
2. **Comparable duration** to the behavior being replaced. A 5-minute cigarette is not replaced by a 30-second glass of water.
3. **Serves the same function.** Nicotine after lunch is often the break, not the nicotine; the replacement has to be a break. A stress trigger needs a downregulating action, not a distracting one.
4. **Not the thing being quit in another form.** Vaping to quit smoking is a substitution for the delivery method, not the dependence — legitimate as a taper step, dishonest as a finish line. Say which one it is.

Substitution is what gets logged on an avoid-habit: the presence of the replacement, not the absence of the target.

## Friction

The most reliable and least discussed lever, because it works without the user's participation at the moment of the urge. Target: make the unwanted behavior cost at least one deliberate step more than the substitution.

| Friction move | Applies to |
|---|---|
| Remove it from the house | Anything storable — the decision is made once at the shop, not twenty times at home |
| Move it out of the default room | Phone charging outside the bedroom, snacks off the counter |
| Delete the account, log out, remove the app | Apps, delivery services, shopping — re-login is the friction |
| Change the payment path | Cash-only for the target, card removed from the delivery app |
| Break the sequence | An inserted step between the chained behaviors: shoes off, dishes done, one page read |
| Add a witness | Do it only in a place where someone can see (`accountability.md`) |
| Timer or blocker on the device | Screen-time targets; effective while it is not trivially bypassable |

Rule: **friction beats resolve at the moment of the urge, and resolve is only needed where friction was not applied.** Every remaining reliance on willpower is a friction opportunity that was skipped.

## Abstinence vs Moderation

| Choose abstinence when | Choose moderation when |
|---|---|
| Physical dependence, or a substance where one instance reliably escalates | The target is a normal part of life the user wants less of, not none of |
| Moderation has already been tried and failed twice | No history of escalation from a single instance |
| The daily negotiation itself is the exhausting part | The user would abandon an abstinence attempt outright |
| The trigger set is broad and social | The triggers are few and identifiable |

Moderation is a design, not a hope. It requires: a hard numeric ceiling with a unit (2 drinks, twice a week; 30 min of the app per day), a fixed measurement, and a pre-declared escalation trigger — "if I exceed the ceiling twice in one month, I switch to abstinence for 30 days". Without the escalation trigger, moderation is unmanaged consumption with a number attached.

State the choice explicitly and record it in the roster row. Switching mid-attempt without saying so is how a quit becomes a drift.

## The Quit Date

- **Within 7-14 days of the decision.** Sooner leaves the trigger inventory undone; later dilutes the decision and adds anticipatory consumption.
- **Not on a high-trigger day.** Look at the inventory: avoid the day of the week with the heaviest social or stress load.
- **Preparation completed before the date**, not on it: supply removed, substitutions chosen for the top two triggers, friction applied, the first 48 hours' plan written, support told (`accountability.md`).
- **A landmark helps if it is close.** Month start, birthday, a trip — the fresh-start effect is worth using when it does not cost a two-week delay (`starting.md`).
- **The first 72 hours are a separate plan** for anything with withdrawal: know what the symptoms are, how long they last, and what the substitution is at each of the top triggers.

## Urges

An urge is a wave, not a state: it rises, peaks and subsides, typically within minutes rather than hours. The plan needs one technique the user has actually practiced, not five they have read about.

- **Delay.** Commit to 10 minutes before deciding. Most urges are past their peak by then, and the decision is made by a different person than the one who wanted it.
- **Surf it.** Observe the sensation, name where it is felt, do not argue with it. Suppression and argument both extend it.
- **Leave the trigger.** Physically change room or context — the fastest intervention and the most reliable.
- **Execute the substitution without deliberating.** Deliberation at urge time is a negotiation the urge usually wins.

What does not work: reciting reasons (the urge is not a reasoning failure), promising a reward for resisting (creates a scoring loop around the target), or "just one to take the edge off".

## Tracking an Avoid-Habit

- `Type: avoid` in the roster row, with `Clean since <date>` in place of a floor.
- The daily cell records the **substitution**, not the abstention: `y` = the trigger fired and the replacement happened, or the trigger did not fire; `n` = a lapse.
- The note column carries the trigger on any `n` — this is the data that builds the inventory for the next attempt.
- Report clean days and the lapse count in the last 28 days together. Clean days alone hide a plan that is degrading.
- Freezes do not apply (`tracking.md`).
- Money saved is a strong motivator for paid habits: compute it as `daily_spend × clean_days` with the currency, and keep the running figure in the roster note rather than recomputing it every time. If a subscription was cancelled as part of the quit, remove its row from `~/Clawic/data/finances/subscriptions.md` (`memory-template.md`).

## Per-Target Notes

| Target | The thing that actually decides it |
|---|---|
| Smoking / vaping | Sequential and social triggers dominate; the post-meal and the with-coffee slots need substitutions before the quit date. Pharmacological support exists — point at the clinician or pharmacist once |
| Alcohol | Screen for dependence first (Red Flags). Social triggers dominate; the prepared line for "why aren't you drinking" is a real deliverable, not a detail |
| Doomscrolling / social apps | Availability trigger — friction does nearly all the work. Log out, remove from the home screen, charge the phone in another room. Time limits inside the app itself are bypassed within a week |
| Sugar / snacking | Availability plus emotional. Do not frame as restriction (Red Flags); frame as changing what is within reach, and route if the language becomes rule-tightening |
| Nail biting, skin picking, hair pulling | Body-focused repetitive behaviors: substitution with a competing physical response is the standard approach, and awareness training precedes it. Persistent and distressing cases are clinical, not habit design |
| Caffeine | Withdrawal headache peaks in the first days; a taper of ~25% per week avoids it and is more likely to hold than cessation |
| Procrastination on a specific task | Not an avoid-habit at all — there is no behavior to remove. Convert it to a do-habit with a two-minute floor (`design.md`, `productivity`) |
| Anything else | Build the trigger inventory first; the target's identity matters less than the shape of its triggers |

## The Quit Plan Artifact

Write it as its own file the day the plan is agreed: `~/Clawic/data/habits/artifacts/quit-<thing>.md`, with its `## Boxes` line and the read condition "read before any conversation about `<thing>`, and immediately after a lapse".

Contents: the target and the chosen mode (abstinence or moderation with its ceiling), the quit date, the ranked trigger inventory, the substitution for each of the top two, the friction applied, the first-72-hours plan, the escalation trigger, and who knows about it. Update the trigger inventory after each lapse rather than starting a new file.

**Whenever a quit is planned, a lapse happens, or the mode changes**, write in the same turn: the roster row with `Type: avoid` and `Clean since`; the day's cell and its trigger note in `logs/<year>-<month>.md`; the plan or its updated trigger inventory in `artifacts/quit-<thing>.md` with its `## Boxes` line; and any accountability person as a row in the shared `~/Clawic/data/contacts/contacts.md`, referenced here by name only (`memory-template.md`).
