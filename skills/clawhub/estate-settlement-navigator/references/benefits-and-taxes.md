# Survivor Benefits, Pensions, and the Final Tax Returns

## Social Security survivor screen (US)

| Claimant | Gate | Amount logic |
|----------|------|--------------|
| Widow(er) | age 60+ (55+ if disabled is NOT an SSA rule — 50+ if disabled) | 71.5%–99% of deceased's PIA depending on how early before FRA |
| Widow(er), any age | caring for deceased's child under 16 (or disabled) | 75% of PIA (ends when child does) |
| Disabled widow(er) | age 50–59, disability started within 7 years of death | 71.5% of PIA |
| Child | under 18 (19 if in high school) or disabled before 22 | 75% of PIA |
| Dependent parent | 62+, supported by deceased | 82.5% (one) / 75% each (two) |
| Lump-sum death payment | surviving spouse (or child if none), lived with deceased | $255 one-time, claim within 2 years |

**Family maximum:** survivor benefits on one record cap around
150–180% of the deceased's PIA — a spouse-with-child claim can hit it.

**The switch strategy (real money):** a widow(er) entitled to two
records can take the SMALLER one first (reduced, early) and switch to
the LARGER at full retirement age or 70. Example: own retirement at 70
grows 8%/yr of delayed credits; survivor benefits don't. Take survivor
at 60, switch to own at 70 — or the reverse if the survivor record is
bigger. Model both.

**Medicare:** widow(er) qualifies on the deceased's record at 65 (or
with disability). Check Part B enrollment windows to avoid penalties.

## Pensions and employer plans

- **Defined-benefit pension:** was a survivor annuity elected at
  retirement (joint & survivor)? If yes, payments continue automatically
  — verify the percentage (50/75/100%). If the worker died before
  retirement, a pre-retirement survivor benefit usually exists — claim it.
- **401(k)/IRA:** goes to the NAMED beneficiary regardless of the will.
  Spouse can roll into own IRA (best flexibility). Non-spouse
  beneficiaries: inherited IRA, typically empty within 10 years
  (SECURE Act); do NOT roll into your own account.
- **Group term life:** claim through HR promptly; some plans have
  conversion/portability windows measured in 31 days.
- **Union/veteran benefits:** VA pays a burial allowance and possibly a
  survivor pension (income-tested); union locals often have death
  benefits nobody claims.

## Finding unknown policies

Search: bank autopay drafts to insurers, old checks, HR portals of every
employer, the MIB policy locator service, and the **state unclaimed
property office** (missing benefits eventually land there).

## Taxes — the three returns that may be owed

1. **Final Form 1040** — for the year of death, filed by Apr 15 (or
   extension) of the following year, covering Jan 1 → date of death.
   Marked "deceased". A surviving spouse filing jointly that year can
   still use MFJ (qualifying widow(er) for two more years if a child).
2. **Form 1041 (estate income)** — if estate assets earn over $600 in a
   year (interest, dividends, capital gains during administration).
3. **Form 706 (estate tax)** — only above the federal basic exclusion
   (≈ $14M, indexed — touches <0.2% of estates), 9 months from death.
   States may have their own estate/inheritance taxes far lower — check
   the state.

Income in respect of a decedent (IRD): some inherited amounts (final
paycheck, unpaid IRA distributions, savings-bond interest) are taxable
to the recipient — offset by an estate's 691(c) deduction if the estate
paid tax on them. Hand the 1040 to a CPA the first year.

## UK quick map

- Bereavement Support Payment: £3,500 + 18 monthly £350 (higher rate,
  with children) or 12 × £100 — claim within 3 months for full backpay.
- Widow(er)'s State Pension: class A/B rules by dates — check gov.uk.
- Child Benefit and Guardian's Allowance continue to the caring parent.
- ISA allowances die with the holder (spouse gets an extra permitted
  subscription equal to the value — tell the ISA provider).
- Personal allowance runs to end of tax year of death; executor files
  SA900 / R27.
