# International Procurement Case Studies (De-identified)

> These cases illustrate common patterns in World Bank, WTO GPA, and FIDIC-governed tenders.
> All data is de-identified — entity names, project locations, and bid amounts have been altered
> while preserving the procurement structure and lessons learned.

## Case 1: World Bank — South Asian Water Supply & Sanitation Project (QCBS)

| Dimension | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Project Type** | Urban water supply network rehabilitation and expansion |
| **Procurement Method** | International Competitive Bidding (ICB) under World Bank Procurement Framework (2023) |
| **Estimated Budget** | $120M (World Bank IDA credit + Government co-financing) |
| **Evaluation Method** | QCBS — Technical 80% + Cost 20% |
| **Contract Form** | FIDIC Red Book (Employer-designed works) |
| **Key Qualification Requirements** | Experience in 3+ similar water supply projects (>$30M each); ISO 9001; Environmental & Social Management Plan (ESMP) |
| **Mandatory Criteria** | Local content ≥30%; Gender-based violence prevention plan; SEA risk assessment |
| **Standstill Period** | 10 calendar days between contract award notice and contract signing |

### Bidder Profiles (De-identified)

| Bidder | Technical Score | Bid Price ($M) | Composite Score | Outcome |
|--------|:---:|------:|:---:|--------|
| **Bidder A** (European-Asian JV) | 92.5 | 108.0 | 94.0 | **Won** |
| Bidder B (Middle Eastern) | 88.0 | 105.0 | 90.4 | Second |
| Bidder C (Local consortium) | 76.0 | 98.0 | 80.8 | Third |
| Bidder D (European) | 90.0 | 125.0 | Disqualified (exceeded budget ceiling) | — |

### Key Lessons

1. **Technical quality dominated**: At 80% quality weight, the technical score differential (+4.5 points over the second bidder) more than compensated for a 3% higher price.
2. **Local content compliance was decisive**: Bidder A's detailed local subcontracting plan and technology transfer program scored full marks; Bidder B's vague commitment cost them 3 points.
3. **ESMP rigor**: Bidder A's environmental mitigation plan included specific biodiversity offsets and a grievance redress mechanism — both scored separately.
4. **Budget ceiling is absolute**: Bidder D had the second-highest technical score but was disqualified for a price 4.2% over the published ceiling, with no opportunity for correction.

## Case 2: WTO GPA — Pan-Asian IT Infrastructure Framework Agreement

| Dimension | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Project Type** | Multi-country cloud infrastructure and cybersecurity framework agreement |
| **Procurement Method** | Restricted procedure under GPA rules (pre-qualification + invitation to tender) |
| **Framework Value** | SDR 45M (approx. $60M) over 4 years |
| **Procuring Entities** | 3 GPA member governments (joint procurement agreement) |
| **Evaluation Method** | MEAT (Most Economically Advantageous Tender) — Technical 55% + Price 30% + Sustainability 15% |
| **Key Requirements** | ISO 27001, SOC 2 Type II, data residency in each member jurisdiction; local SME participation ≥20% |
| **Standstill Period** | 10 calendar days (GPA Art. XVIII) |

### Evaluation Breakdown

| Criterion | Max Score | Bidder X (Won) | Bidder Y | Bidder Z |
|-----------|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **Technical Solution** | 35 | 32.0 | 30.5 | 28.0 |
| **Cybersecurity Maturity** | 20 | 18.5 | 16.0 | 17.0 |
| **Price (life-cycle, 4 years)** | 30 | 27.0 | 28.5 | 24.0 |
| **Sustainability & SME participation** | 15 | 13.5 | 9.0 | 10.0 |
| **Total (weighted)** | 100 | **91.0** | 84.0 | 79.0 |

### Key Lessons

1. **Sustainability was the differentiator**: Bidder X's 13.5/15 in the sustainability category created a 4.5-point gap over Bidder Y — larger than the price advantage Bidder Y held. Their carbon-neutral data center certification and concrete SME subcontracting commitments were decisive.
2. **Life-cycle costing vs. headline price**: Bidder Z submitted the lowest headline price but scored lower on price overall because maintenance, upgrade, and exit costs were higher over the 4-year evaluation window.
3. **GPA joint procurement complexity**: The three procuring entities had slightly different technical requirements; Bidder X's modular architecture accommodated all variants without cost penalty, while others required change orders.
4. **Remedy risk**: Bidder Y filed a GPA challenge alleging the sustainability criteria were disproportionate; the independent review body dismissed it because the criteria had been published in the contract notice and all bidders had equal opportunity to respond.

## Cross-Cutting Observations

| Pattern | Implication for Bidders |
|---------|------------------------|
| **Technical quality trumps price in QCBS/MEAT** | Invest in technical proposal depth; a 5% technical score margin can offset a 10–15% price disadvantage |
| **Local content and sustainability are scoring differentiators, not just compliance items** | Treat them as competitive scoring opportunities, not box-ticking exercises |
| **Framework agreements require modular, future-proof design** | Demonstrate flexibility across multiple procurers' requirements without cost escalation |
| **Standstill periods are longer internationally (10 days vs. 3 days PRC)** | Build this into project timelines; a challenged award can delay contract signing by weeks |
| **De-identified case data is essential** | When using past performance evidence, ensure compliance with confidentiality agreements and data protection laws |
