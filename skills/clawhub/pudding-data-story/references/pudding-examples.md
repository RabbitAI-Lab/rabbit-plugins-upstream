# Pudding story pattern library

These are references for editorial and interaction logic. Do not imitate their typography, illustration style, logos, or exact layouts.

## Bears Will Be Boys

[Bears Will Be Boys](https://pudding.cool/2025/07/kids-books/)

Useful patterns:

- a playful, concrete object opens a quantitative argument;
- custom illustration and statistical marks share one visual language;
- an experiment involving more than 1,300 participants is explained with methodology and limitations;
- the story progressively reveals the distribution instead of presenting a finished chart;
- examples stay embedded in the narrative so the data never becomes anonymous.

Use this model when a story needs to move from perception or culture to measured evidence.

## A Map of Places in the US with the Same Name

[A Map of Places in the US with the Same Name](https://pudding.cool/2023/03/same-name/)

Useful patterns:

- the map is an exploratory object, not a decorative backdrop;
- search matches the reader's natural curiosity;
- curated examples provide a meaningful starting state;
- caveats sit close to the data;
- exploration leads into an authored explanation rather than replacing the story.

Use this model for place/name lookup, geospatial curiosity, and reader-led entry points.

## The United States of Abortion Mazes

[The United States of Abortion Mazes](https://pudding.cool/2024/10/abortion-mazes/)

Useful patterns:

- a playable maze embodies administrative and legal friction;
- small multiples make all states comparable;
- sorting, progress, and jump controls support orientation;
- personal accounts add human stakes;
- the interaction is the argument, not a reward layered onto it.

Use this model when a system's constraints can be represented as rules. Treat sensitive subjects with care and provide non-playable access.

## 30 minutes with a stranger

[30 minutes with a stranger](https://pudding.cool/2025/06/hello-stranger/)

Useful patterns:

- a concrete pair introduces a corpus of roughly 1,700 conversations;
- conversation time and visual chronology stay synchronized;
- readers can select people and explore without losing the authored path;
- pseudonyms and contextual notes keep privacy visible;
- the single example remains legible after the wider dataset appears.

Use this model for interviews, transcripts, event sequences, and concrete-to-corpus stories.

## The Flipbook Experiment

[The Flipbook Experiment](https://pudding.cool/projects/flipbook/)

Useful patterns:

- reader participation is both interaction and data collection;
- more than 22,000 drawings become an emergent visual object;
- video, frame scrubbing, and starting-point selection support different ways to explore;
- warnings and accessibility notes are part of the experience;
- participation is optional; observation still tells a story.

Use this model only when contributed input is essential to the question and can be collected responsibly.

## Process reference: Can an AI make a data-driven, visual story?

[Can an AI make a data-driven, visual story?](https://pudding.cool/2024/07/ai/)

Useful lessons:

- generic prompts produce safe, generic story ideas and conventional charts;
- prototyping is easier than editorial iteration;
- a specific example can open into the full dataset;
- idea, data, storyboard/prototype, and development/writing are distinct stages;
- interaction can expose process when process is the actual subject.
