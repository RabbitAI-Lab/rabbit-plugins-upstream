# Personal and Consumer Matters

Individual matters follow different economics from commercial ones: the amounts are small enough that legal fees dominate, the counterparty is usually an organisation with a process, and the cheap routes — the complaint channel, the regulator, the small-claims track — resolve most of it. The right advice is usually the cheapest path that works, not the strongest legal position.

**Before answering**, read `## Matters` and `## Due` in `~/Clawic/data/lawyer/memory.md` for anything already open, and `## Legal Context` for the user's jurisdiction. Family, immigration, criminal, personal injury and anything with a filed case are Red Flags rows: specialist counsel, not self-help.

## Pick The Cheapest Path That Works

In order. Each step costs more than the last, and most matters end at step 2 or 3.

| Step | Cost | Works when |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The organisation's own complaint process | Free, days | Almost always tried first; also usually a precondition for step 3 |
| 2. Written formal complaint with a deadline and a stated next step | Free, 1-2 weeks | The first line said no; escalation reaches someone with authority |
| 3. Ombudsman, regulator or ADR scheme | Free to the consumer in most sectors, weeks to months | Financial services, energy, telecoms, housing and travel have binding or persuasive schemes |
| 4. Chargeback or statutory payment protection | Free, weeks | Paid by card; UK credit-card purchases between £100 and £30,000 carry joint liability under section 75 of the Consumer Credit Act |
| 5. Small claims / low-value track | Modest filing fee, months | The amount is within the limit and the other side ignores everything else |
| 6. Full litigation with representation | Disproportionate below five figures | Rarely correct for a personal matter |

The single highest-return action across all of these is putting it in writing with a specific request and a deadline. Organisations route written complaints with deadlines to a different queue than phone calls.

## The Complaint That Works

Six elements, one page: who you are and the account/reference number · what happened, dated · what was promised or what the law requires · what you want, specifically (a refund of £X, a repair by a date, a cancellation confirmed) · the deadline (14 days is standard) · what you will do next if it is not met (name the ombudsman or the court, and mean it).

Keep it factual and unemotional, send it by a method that proves delivery, and keep the thread. Never accept a resolution offered verbally without written confirmation.

## Consumer Rights

Statutory rights sit on top of the contract and cannot be signed away in most systems.

- **Goods** must be as described, of satisfactory quality and fit for purpose. Remedies are typically a short-term right to reject, then repair or replacement, then a price reduction or final rejection. The UK Consumer Rights Act 2015 gives a 30-day short-term right to reject; the EU regime gives a two-year conformity guarantee with a reversed burden of proof for the first year (extended to two years in several member states).
- **Digital content and services** carry the same conformity concept in the EU and UK, including updates for the expected period.
- **Distance and online purchases** carry a cooling-off right — 14 days in the EU and UK, from delivery for goods — with exceptions for personalised goods, sealed items opened, and digital content where the consumer expressly consented to immediate performance and acknowledged the loss of the right.
- The US has no equivalent federal cooling-off right for most online purchases; the FTC's cooling-off rule covers door-to-door and similar sales, and returns are otherwise a matter of the seller's policy plus state law and warranty rules (Magnuson-Moss).
- Warranty versus statutory right: a manufacturer's warranty is extra, never a replacement. "Out of warranty" does not end a statutory conformity claim inside its own period.
- Faulty goods claims go to the **seller**, not the manufacturer, in the UK and EU. Retailers routinely misdirect consumers to the manufacturer; the seller is the correct target.

## Subscriptions And Cancellation

- Find the cancellation clause and the notice period before cancelling. Cancel in writing through a route that produces proof, even where a button exists.
- Auto-renewal rules increasingly require an easy cancellation path, renewal reminders and clear pre-purchase disclosure; California's Automatic Renewal Law is the strictest US baseline, and the EU and UK approach the same conduct through unfair-terms and consumer-contract rules. If cancelling is materially harder than subscribing, that is itself a likely breach worth citing in the complaint.
- Continuing payment authority on a card can be cancelled with the bank as well as the merchant, and the bank must stop it on instruction in the UK and EU — but the underlying contractual obligation survives, so cancel both.
- Free trials that convert: the disclosure and consent at signup determine whether the charge is challengeable. Screenshot the signup flow when disputing one.

## Renting

- **Deposits**: many jurisdictions require the deposit to be held in a protection scheme within a short window, with penalties for failure and, in some, a bar on eviction until it is remedied. Check the scheme registration first — it is often the strongest point in a deposit dispute.
- **Inventory and condition**: a dated, photographed condition report at move-in and move-out decides almost every deposit dispute. Without it, the argument is one word against another.
- **Repairs**: the landlord's repairing obligations are largely statutory and cannot be contracted out of in most residential systems. Report in writing, keep the record, allow reasonable access, and never stop paying rent as leverage — rent withholding is a breach in most systems even when the landlord is also in breach, and there is usually a specific statutory procedure to use instead.
- **Eviction and notice**: strictly procedural. Notice periods, prescribed forms and preconditions (deposit protection, safety certificates, licensing) all have to be right, and a defect in the process invalidates it. Any eviction notice is a deadline that needs immediate specialist advice — housing is one of the few areas where free specialist advice services are widely available.
- **Rent increases** follow the contract or the statutory mechanism, whichever governs, and usually require notice in a prescribed form.
- Commercial leases work on entirely different principles (`agreements.md`).

## Employee Side

The mirror of `employment.md`, read from the worker's position.

- **Deadlines are short.** UK employment tribunal claims are generally 3 months less one day from the act complained of, with mandatory ACAS early conciliation first, which pauses the clock. US EEOC charges are 180 or 300 days depending on the state. These are among the shortest limitation periods in law and are missed constantly.
- Get the contract, the handbook and the pay records early — there is usually a statutory right to a copy, and a data subject access request produces far more (`privacy.md`).
- Raise concerns in writing through the grievance process before resigning. Constructive-dismissal claims turn on whether the employer was given a chance to fix the breach, and on not delaying, which can be treated as affirming the contract.
- A settlement or severance offer is negotiable, and the first offer is rarely the last. Check what is being waived, whether the statutory formalities were met (US ADEA/OWBPA 21/7 timing; UK independent advice requirement), and whether anything unwaivable is being purported to be waived (`employment.md`).
- Non-competes: check enforceability in the jurisdiction before assuming the clause binds. Many are void or narrowed, and some require payment during the restricted period.
- Unpaid wages have their own fast routes in most systems, separate from and quicker than general claims.

## Small Claims

Designed for people without lawyers, and effective for straightforward money claims.

- Limits vary: US state small-claims ceilings run roughly $2,500-$25,000 (California allows $12,500 for individuals and $6,250 for entities); England and Wales uses a small-claims track with restricted cost recovery. Verify the current limit before filing.
- Costs are generally not recoverable from the loser on this track, which is the point: it caps downside, and it also means winning does not pay for a lawyer.
- What wins: documents, dates and arithmetic. A one-page chronology, the contract, the correspondence showing the demand, and a clear calculation of the amount. Emotional narrative does not help.
- Sue the correct legal entity, with its registered name and address, or the judgment is unenforceable.
- Send the demand letter first (`disputes.md`); many jurisdictions expect pre-action contact and many defendants pay on receipt of a filing that looks competent.
- Winning is not collecting. Check that the defendant has assets before spending the filing fee (`disputes.md`).

## Debt Collectors

- Ask for validation of the debt in writing before paying or acknowledging anything. In the US, the FDCPA gives the right to request verification, restricts contact times and methods, prohibits harassment and false statements, and requires the collector to stop contact on written request.
- **Acknowledging a debt in writing or making a payment can restart the limitation period** in many systems, reviving a time-barred debt. Never pay or acknowledge before checking the age of the debt.
- Disputed debts should be disputed in writing, with the dispute recorded with any credit reference agency involved.
- Collectors buying debt often hold incomplete documentation; requiring the underlying agreement and the assignment chain resolves a meaningful share of claims.

## Neighbours, Deposits And Small Disputes

Boundaries, noise, trees, shared drives and parking. Legal escalation is almost always uneconomic and permanently damaging to something the user cannot move away from. Sequence: talk, then write, then mediation (community mediation services are free or cheap in many places), then the relevant council or authority for statutory nuisance, and only then legal action. Note that boundary disputes are a category with a reputation for costing multiples of the land's value; that reputation is earned.

## Traffic And Fines

Check whether the notice is a statutory penalty (issued by an authority, with a formal appeal route and deadlines) or a private parking charge (a contractual claim by a company, with a different and often weaker basis). The appeal route and the deadline are printed on the notice and are usually short; early payment discounts typically expire in 14-21 days, so the decision to challenge has to be made immediately. Points, licence consequences and insurance disclosure obligations can matter more than the fine.

## Signing Things As An Individual

Personal guarantees, joint loans, guarantor agreements on a family member's tenancy, and joint accounts all convert someone else's obligation into the user's. Rules of thumb: never guarantee an amount that could not be paid from savings; require a cap and an end date; ask what happens if the primary obligor defaults or disappears; and understand that joint and several liability means the creditor can pursue whichever party is easiest, for the whole amount, not a share.

## Documents Worth Having

Not estate planning (`estate-planning`), but the practical set: a signed tenancy or purchase file with the condition report, employment contract and payslips, insurance policies with their limits, warranties and receipts for anything valuable, and a list of accounts and where they are. Where a document only exists in an app or an email, export it — most disputes start when access to an account is lost.
