# Audience Retention and Serialization

Use this reference when the goal is not just a coherent episode, but a series that holds attention, earns comments, and creates demand for the next episode.

## 1. Audience desire engine

Every episode should activate at least three:

- **curiosity:** an unanswered question or incomplete pattern;
- **stakes:** something important can be gained or lost;
- **novelty:** a new rule, image, reversal, or social situation;
- **emotional contrast:** comedy followed by tenderness, safety followed by threat;
- **identity:** the audience recognizes a self, a family member, or a social role;
- **social currency:** a line, image, or dilemma worth screenshotting or arguing about;
- **justice/reversal:** an unfair situation with a visible chance of payback.

A pretty shot without one of these is not a retention event.

## 2. Opening hook ladder

- 0–1.5 seconds: visual contradiction, danger, humiliation, impossible image, or decisive action;
- 1.5–3 seconds: establish who wants what and why it matters;
- 3–10 seconds: concrete obstacle and a promise of escalation;
- 10–20 seconds: first small payoff or reversal.

Do not spend the first three seconds on weather, logos, empty scenery, or background exposition unless the image itself is the hook.

## 3. Episode retention ladder

Each 90–120 second episode should contain:

1. a fast resolution or consequence from the prior cliffhanger;
2. a new question;
3. a small payoff every 20–30 seconds;
4. one midpoint turn;
5. one major cliffhanger or irreversible consequence;
6. one next-episode tease.

The audience should never finish an episode with the same information they had at the start.

## 4. Serialization engines

Maintain at least three simultaneous engines:

- **A plot:** the concrete external goal;
- **B relationship:** trust, rivalry, family, romance, or alliance;
- **C mystery/secret:** a hidden identity, event, power, debt, or past.

Each episode should advance at least two engines. A season finale should close one engine and open the next.

## 5. Cliffhanger taxonomy

Rotate among:

- decision;
- discovery;
- danger;
- reversal;
- deadline;
- emotional rupture;
- moral compromise;
- identity reveal;
- public exposure;
- loss of control.

Rules:

- one major cliffhanger per episode;
- no more than 2–3 major open loops at once;
- pay off one meaningful loop every 1–3 episodes;
- do not end every episode with the same device;
- do not use a cliffhanger that has no planned payoff.

## 6. Comment engineering

A comment prompt should not be “你怎么看”. Use one of:

- identity choice: “如果是你，会救孩子还是保证据？”
- moral dilemma: “老人该不该原谅她？”
- prediction: “你觉得红券背后的人是谁？”
- camp split: “你站火猴还是站村民？”
- hidden clue: “门锁上的编号，有人注意到了吗？”
- character defense: “如果你是火花猴，会解释还是直接走？”

Pin one comment question per episode. Reply with an in-character line when possible. Seed 2–3 clue comments rather than fake arguments.

## 7. Shareable moment

Each episode needs one of:

- a screenshot line;
- a visual before/after;
- a costume or power reveal;
- a moral dilemma;
- a comic humiliation;
- a protective gesture;
- a reversible assumption;
- a memorable sound or gesture.

The shareable moment should be understandable without watching the full episode.

## 8. Next-episode tease

End with one clear reason to return:

- a new question;
- a revealed threat;
- a relationship rupture;
- a deadline;
- a character entering the frame;
- a sentence that changes the meaning of the previous scene.

Do not spoil the answer in the title or thumbnail.

## 9. Release cadence

A practical launch cadence:

- D-7: 6s mystery hook;
- D-5: character poster and one-line premise;
- D-3: 15s conflict cut;
- D-1: 30s main trailer;
- D0: first 3 episodes, 30s trailer, pinned comment;
- D1–D7: high-comment clips, creator reactions, clue posts, paid boost.

For serialized release, keep a consistent time slot and preserve one unanswered question across each release window.

## 10. Metrics and diagnosis

Track:

- 1.5s/3s hook retention;
- 10s retention;
- completion rate;
- follow conversion;
- comment rate;
- share rate;
- replay rate;
- next-episode click;
- paid unlock conversion where applicable.

Diagnosis:

- high 3s, low 10s: promise unclear or setup too slow;
- high completion, low follow: character not sticky enough;
- high comments, low completion: wrong audience or outrage bait;
- high completion, low comments: no camp, dilemma, or clue;
- high replay, low next click: cliffhanger weak or payoff too complete;
- high first-episode completion, low episode-2 retention: episode 1 closed too much or episode 2 did not inherit pressure.

## 11. Retention QC

Before release:

- first 1.5 seconds contain a visual or emotional event;
- the opening question is explicit by 10 seconds;
- every episode has a midpoint turn;
- every episode has a comment prompt;
- every episode has one shareable moment;
- every cliffhanger has a planned payoff;
- no more than three major open loops;
- the next-episode tease is clear;
- the release cadence and pinned comment are scheduled.

Do not promise virality. Optimize for clarity, curiosity, emotional contrast, conversation, and return behavior.
