## Description:

Use when sharpening kitchen, pocket, or workshop knives (whetstone, guided rod, or pull-through) — matches the correct edge angle to knife class (Japanese 12-15° vs German 20° vs cleaver 25°+), builds the grit-progression ladder from chip repair to polish, computes the physical sharpening-pass height to hold an angle by hand, and explains burr formation/checking so the edge actually gets sharp instead of just shiny.

This skill is ready for commercial/non-commercial use.

## Use Case:

Home cooks, knife owners, and workshop users use the guide to select a suitable edge angle and sharpening-stone progression for a knife's class and condition. It also explains hand-angle height, burr checks, and steel-specific sharpening expectations.

Global

## Known Risks and Mitigations:

Risk: General angle recommendations may not match a knife's factory bevel, especially for asymmetric or single-bevel blades.

Mitigation: Check the knife manufacturer's guidance and preserve the blade's intended bevel geometry.

Risk: Testing a sharpened edge can cause injury.

Mitigation: Do not draw a thumb across the edge; use the paper-slice or tomato-skin checks described in the safety guidance.
