# Smoke & CO Alarms — Full Reference

## 1. Why alarms "randomly" chirp — the actual mechanisms

- **Low battery**: voltage sags below threshold; ~1 chirp per 30–60 s.
  Cold nights dip voltage further, which is why chirps "start at 3AM."
- **End of life (EOL)**: the sensor or CO cell degrades. Most brands
  signal a distinct pattern (Kidde: 5 chirps/min; First Alert often
  3 chirps or a voice). A new battery does NOT fix EOL.
- **Nuisance alarms**: cooking smoke, steam, dust in the chamber, spiders
  (really), temperature swings at a marginal battery.

The back label is the ground truth: it carries the manufacture date and
the brand's exact chirp table. Photograph it during any audit.

## 2. Lifespans (the part everyone forgets)

| Unit | Life | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Smoke (ionization or photoelectric) | 10 y from **manufacture** date | sensing chamber drifts |
| CO alarm | 7–10 y, label governs | electrochemical cell consumed |
| Combo smoke+CO | the CO cell usually dies first (~7 y) | replace whole unit |
| Sealed 10-y lithium | battery engineered to outlast the sensor | chirp = replace unit |
| Replaceable batteries | 9V/AA: yearly swap or at first chirp | lithium AA resists cold sag |

Roughly two-thirds of US homes have at least one alarm past its 10-year
date — the most common home-safety gap after missing units entirely.

## 3. Coverage requirements (NFPA 72 consumer pattern, most US codes)

- Smoke alarm **in every bedroom**
- **Outside each separate sleeping area** (hallway serving bedrooms)
- **On every floor**, including the basement; stairwell tops are prime
  spots (smoke rises through stairwells like a chimney)
- **Interconnected** (hardwired or wireless-radio) so any trigger sounds
  everywhere — required in new construction in most jurisdictions
- CO: outside each sleeping area + near fuel-burning appliances + one per
  floor with bedrooms in many states; RVs/boats have their own rules
- Hearing-impaired household: strobe (177 candela typical for bedrooms)
  or bed shaker, interconnected

## 4. Placement anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Why it fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Inside the kitchen / <10 ft of cooking | repeated false alarms → disabled unit | hall outside kitchen, photoelectric |
| Bathroom | steam triggers sensors | hall outside bathroom |
| Garage (smoke alarm) | exhaust + dust false-trigger | heat detector in garage; smoke/CO just inside the house door |
| Within 4 in of a wall/ceiling corner | "dead air" — circulation skips the pocket | center of ceiling, or wall mount 4–12 in below ceiling |
| Next to supply diffusers / ceiling fans | conditioned air washes smoke away from the sensor | ≥ 3 ft from any diffuser/fan |
| Peak of a cathedral ceiling | hot air stratification keeps smoke below | wall mount near the peak on the stair side |
| CO "low because CO is heavier" myth | CO (28 g/mol) ≈ air (29) — mixes nearly evenly | place for *audibility while asleep* and near sources |

## 5. The beep decoder, precisely

| Sound | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 chirp / 30–60 s | low battery | swap battery; if it persists → EOL |
| 3–4 fast beeps, pause, repeating | SMOKE | evacuate; verify from outside |
| 4 fast beeps + voice "Warning: carbon monoxide" (First Alert) / 4 beeps repeating (Kidde) | CO | full CO script (below) |
| 5 chirps/min (Kidde) / 3 chirps/min (several First Alert models) | fault/EOL | check date; replace unit |
| same-time-nightly chirp | cold-sag battery | replace battery; consider lithium AA |

Any CO alarm pattern + symptoms (headache, nausea, confusion, dizziness)
= medical emergency. CO binds hemoglobin ~200× tighter than oxygen;
symptoms that return indoors mean the source still runs.

## 6. CO event script (condensed)

1. Everyone out to fresh air; count heads.
2. Symptomatic person → emergency care.
3. Ventilate en route only if it costs seconds.
4. Fire dept / gas utility finds the source — do not "check and reset."
5. Appliance inspected before next run.
6. Alarm that reached full CO threshold has consumed cell headroom —
   replace it after the event even if it "seems fine."

## 7. Maintenance calendar

- Monthly: test button (everyone in earshot).
- Yearly (time-change ritual): replace 9V/AA batteries (unless sealed),
   vacuum the vents with a soft brush — dust is the false-alarm fuel.
- 10-year (or label): replace the unit regardless of health.
- Any renovation/paint: bag the unit (dust/paint kills chambers), un-bag
  after.

## 8. Shopping guidance

- Sealed 10-year combo units end the battery-chirp era; slightly more,
  zero maintenance.
- Photoelectric-only or dual-sensor: photoelectric nuisance-alarm less
  near kitchens and is faster for smoldering fires; ionization faster
  for flaming. Dual covers both.
- Interconnected: wireless-radio models retrofit without wiring.
- Heat detectors: garage, attic, kitchen (as supplement) — they react to
  temperature, not particles.
- Smart alarms (app alerts, self-testing) solve the "second home /
  rental" monitoring gap.

## 9. Renters

Landlord duties vary; the physics doesn't. On move-in: test every unit,
photograph back labels, request replacements in writing (creates the
paper trail). Battery removal by a prior tenant is common — check every
unit has its battery AND its date.
