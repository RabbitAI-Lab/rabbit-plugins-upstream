# Indian Meal Moth — Biology and Elimination Reference

## 1. The enemy

*Plodia interpunctella* — the Indian meal moth (IMM). The adult: ~10 mm at
rest, wings two-toned (basal third cream, rest coppery-bronze). The larvae:
dirty-white to pinkish, ~12 mm, with a dark head capsule — this is what you
find webbing in the flour and crawling up walls.

Not to be confused with:
- **Clothes moths** (*Tineola*): uniform golden, no copper, hide from light,
  larvae eat wool — different protocol entirely.
- **Angoumois grain moths**: attack grain in the field/storage, rare in homes.

## 2. Life cycle (why 4–8 weeks is the real clock)

| Stage | Duration @ 25–30°C | Location | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Egg | 2–14 days | on or near food | ~60–400 per female, often on package seams |
| Larva | 13–288 days (2–6 wk typical) | inside food, later wandering | the webbing/clumping stage; 5–7 instars |
| Pupa | 1–2 weeks (up to 5) | AWAY from food: cracks, corners | diapause possible — can overwinter in a wall |
| Adult | 1–2 weeks | flying at dusk | ♂ follows ♀ pheromone plumes; adults don't eat |

Implications:
- A warm kitchen completes a generation in ~4 weeks; the visible-moth
  population you see is 2–3 generations deep.
- **Diapause**: late-season larvae can pause development and sit in a crack
  for months — one more reason "it came back" stories are really "it never
  left" stories.
- Adults without food (mated females) still lay — on the NEXT suitable
  substrate they can find. That's why the pull must be total.

## 3. Import vectors (ranked)

1. **Birdseed and dry pet food** — the champions; often infested at the store.
2. **Bulk bins** (grains, nuts, dried fruit) — open scoops, high turnover.
3. **Dried fruit, chocolate, nuts** in retail packaging.
4. **Spices** (red pepper, paprika, dried chili) and herbal tea.
5. Decorative dried flowers/corn, old rodent bait, under-appliance spills.

## 4. Kill methods — validated numbers

| Method | Spec | Kills | Caveats |
|---|---|---|---|
| Freezing | **−18°C/0°F, ≥ 4 days** | all stages | home freezer cycles defrost — a full week is safer for thick/thaw-resistant packages; smallest chest freezers run coldest |
| Heating | **60°C/140°F, 30 min** internal | all stages | oven-only; watch for scorching; not for sealed bags (burst) |
| Disposal | — | those items | must leave the house same-day, sealed |
| Vacuum | — | larvae/pupae in cracks | empty OUTSIDE; the bag is now a moth farm |
| Soapy water / vinegar wipe | — | eggs on surfaces | let dry; no residue needed |

Not effective: bay leaves, cedar, ultrasonic repellers, essential-oil
diffusers. Aerosol insecticides in food cabinets: unnecessary contamination,
no effect on larvae inside packages.

## 5. Pheromone traps — the honest manual

- They lure **males only**, with synthetic female sex pheromone.
- Use: confirming identity of the moth, dating the collapse, catching
  stragglers. **Monitoring, not control** — even 100% male catch leaves
  mated females laying.
- Placement: 1 per zone, eye level or above, ≥3 ft apart, away from
  windows/drafts. Date each one. Replace every 4–6 weeks.
- Reading: rising counts after day 14 = missed source; steady zero for 3
  weeks = clean.

## 6. Storage hardening (post-infestation defense)

- Transfer all keepers to **glass or metal with gasket lids**. Moths chew
  plastic film and paper, and enter fold seams; they cannot chew glass.
- Freeze-on-arrival quarantine for bulk goods, pet food, birdseed: 4+ days
  at −18°C BEFORE shelving.
- Clean under/behind appliances and the pantry once a season; IMM loves
  the forgotten spill.
- Keep susceptible goods in the fridge or freezer long-term in warm
  climates.
- First-in-first-out rotation: old bags at the back are nurseries.

## 7. Failure modes (autopsies of "it came back")

1. Only visibly-infested items discarded → surviving larvae in other
   packages re-seed. Fix: total pull.
2. Cabinets cleaned but not the ceiling corners/hinges/under-lip seams →
   pupae emerge over weeks. Fix: vacuum every seam.
3. Pet food bag "looked fine" in another room → ongoing source. Fix: pet
   food goes through the same protocol.
4. Traps used as the only action → eggs/larvae untouched. Fix: food
   removal + crack cleaning is the control; traps are the scoreboard.
5. Declared clean at day 10 → stragglers + diapause. Fix: 3 zero-catch
   weeks minimum, and re-check at week 8.

## 8. When to escalate

Licensed pest operator (IPM-focused) if: catch >5/week at day 30, larvae
found in multiple rooms, or the household includes stored-goods businesses.
For commercial kitchens, IMM is a regulatory matter — professionals only.
