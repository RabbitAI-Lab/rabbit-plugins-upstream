# Used Bike Buying — Fit-First Checklist

A used bike is the best value in cycling IF the frame fits you. Everything
else is replaceable; the frame isn't adjustable.

## Step 1 — Screen the listing on geometry, not adjectives

Ask the seller (or read the geo chart) for:

1. Frame size (seat tube c-t, or S/M/L + actual number)
2. **Stack and reach** (published for any modern frame)
3. Head tube length
4. Standover height if it's a step-over frame

Run them through `size` / `standover` in this skill. If the frame is more
than ~2 cm outside your band, walk away no matter the price — a wrong-size
frame never becomes right.

## Step 2 — Decode condition by component class

| Component | Wear tell | Replace cost |
|---|---|---|
| Chain | 0.75% stretch gauge or 12" ruler (12 1/16" = worn) | $20–60 |
| Cassette | shark-fin teeth, skips under load | $40–150 |
| Tires/crack sidewalls | cracks, squaring | $40–100/pr |
| Cables/housing | fraying, sluggish shifting | $30–60 |
| Brake pads | < 1.5 mm (rim: visible lines gone) | $20–50 |
| Bar tape/grips | age, tears | $15–40 |
| Bearings (hub/BB/head) | grind/play — spin + rock tests | $15–80 |

Budget rule: add up wear items; if they exceed 30–40% of the used price,
negotiate or pass. "New tires!" in an ad is worth $0 if they're cheap ones.

## Step 3 — Contact points are personal (budget to replace)

Saddles, bars, and tape are fit items the previous owner chose for THEIR
body. Expect to replace: saddle ($50–150), possibly stem ($30–80), tape.
A used bike that needs $150 of "your" contact points can still beat new —
just price it in.

## Step 4 — 15-minute inspection protocol

1. Frame: cracks at welds/lugs, especially around the BB, head tube, and
   seat cluster. Paint bubbles on steel = rust. Tap along tubes listening
   for dull spots (carbon damage).
2. Align check: stand behind, sight the wheels — should be in one plane.
3. Headset: front brake on, rock bike forward/back — no knock.
4. BB: crank arms at 3/9, rock sideways — no play.
5. Hubs: lift each end, spin — smooth, no side-play.
6. Wheels: true check between brake pads/stays; spoke tension by feel.
7. Shifting through all gears under load (ride it), brakes full-power stop.
8. Serial number legible + seller matches the name on record (theft screen).

## Step 5 — After purchase: reset the fit

Never ride the previous owner's position:

1. Wipe down, new tape, check bolts with torque (especially stem/steerer).
2. Set YOUR saddle height (inseam × 0.885), setback, and level.
3. Set bars level with saddle as a start; adjust drop after 2–3 rides.
4. Tires to rated pressure, chain lube.
5. Log the starting numbers — every future change is relative to these.

## Red flags — walk away

- Cracks or repairs at joints (a repainted "fix" is worse)
- Mismatched serial or seller can't state provenance
- Carbon with deep scratches/dull tap sounds
- "Size 56" refusal to give actual geometry + your band says 52
- Headset/BB grind on a "fully serviced" bike (services weren't done)
