# Bike Fit Science — Reference

## The measurement chain

Everything derives from two numbers:

- **Inseam** (book method): barefoot, feet ~10 cm apart, pull a hardback
  book firmly into the crotch, measure floor → book top. ±5 mm here moves
  your saddle 4 mm; measure twice.
- **Height** without shoes.

## Saddle height

Two independent methods; use both:

1. **LeMond / 0.885:** BB-axis → saddle top along the seat tube =
   inseam × 0.885. The classic. Works because pedal+shoe stack roughly
   cancels across riders.
2. **Holmes knee angle:** with the crank at bottom dead center (6 o'clock),
  pedal horizontal, heel dropped — the knee should bend **25–35°** (measured
  behind: thigh line vs shank line). < 25° = too high, > 35° = too low.
   More precise; needs a friend or video.

Heel-pedal check (no goniometer): pedal with HEELS on the pedals; if hips
rock → too high; if heels lose contact at bottom → too high. Set saddle so
heels just reach at BDC, then ride on the balls of your feet.

**Only one reference is stable:** BB → saddle top. Floor measurements change
with tire pressure; "seatpost showing X" changes with frames.

## Setback (fore-aft)

- **KOPS (knee-over-pedal-spindle)** is a STARTING point, not gospel: crank
  at 3 o'clock, front of kneecap vertically over the pedal axle.
- The real goal is hip position relative to the BB — which determines
  glute/hamstring recruitment and how the knee tracks.
- More setback (saddle back): opens the hip, favors power, loads hamstrings;
  too much → lower back rounds, reach effectively lengthens.
- Less setback (forward): spins easier, closes hip; too far forward → front
  knee pain and hands unload.
- Seatpost types: zero-offset vs 15–20 mm layback. Change post class before
  running the saddle at rail extremes.

## Crank length

- Rule of thumb: inseam(cm) × 10 × 0.20 → mm, snapped to available sizes
  (165/167.5/170/172.5/175).
- Why it matters: longer cranks close the hip more at the top (aero/fit
  limits), demand more knee flexion; shorter cranks (165–167.5) help tight
  hips, smaller riders, and high-cadence styles.
- Upshot of the modern re-evaluation: most people are on cranks 2.5–5 mm
  too long for comfort at the top of the stroke.

## Stack and reach — the real frame numbers

- **Stack:** vertical BB → top of head tube. **Reach:** horizontal
  BB → top of head tube. These survive geometry fashion; "56 cm" doesn't.
- Compare ANY two frames (road/gravel) by stack/reach ratio
  (fit Index ≈ stack ÷ reach: ~1.45 endurance, ~1.40 race).
- Fit latitude: stem ±20 mm effective (flip + spacers), seatpost offset,
  saddle rails ±15 mm. Beyond that you're fighting the frame — buy the
  right size instead.

## Standover

Minimum clearance over the top tube (straddling, feet flat):
road ≥ 2 cm, commuter 3, gravel 3–5, MTB 5+.

## Flexibility gates

- Toe-touch past knees without rounding the back = high flexibility.
- Fingertips mid-shin = medium. Knees or worse = low.
- Low flexibility → bar drop is EARNED over months; start bars level with
  the saddle. Fighting drop with a tight posterior chain = neck, traps,
  lower back, perineum pain.

## Cleats

- **Fore-aft:** ball of the foot over the pedal axle as a start; moving the
  cleat BACK 3–5 mm reduces metatarsal pressure ("hot foot") and calf load —
  the most under-used fix in cycling.
- **Rotation:** sit on a table edge, let feet hang relaxed — that natural
  foot angle is what cleat rotation should reproduce. Forced angles →
  lateral/outside knee pain (IT band).
- **Float:** fixed cleats are for dialed fits only; 4–6° of float is the
  safe default.

## The pain table — reasoning

| Symptom | Mechanism | First fix |
|---|---|---|
| Front knee | Knee closes too much (saddle low/forward) → quads overwork | +3 mm height |
| Back knee | Overextension (saddle high/back) loads posterior chain | −3 mm height |
| Outside knee | Foot angle forced by cleat → IT band friction | Reset cleat rotation |
| Numb hands | Weight too far forward (drop/reach), or saddle nose-down | Raise bars / level saddle |
| Saddle numbness | Perineum pressure: drop, tilt, or wrong saddle width | Less drop, level, width by sit bones +2–3 cm |
| Hot foot | Metatarsal pressure: cleat forward, tight shoes | Cleat back 3–5 mm |
| Neck/traps | Cervical extension from deep drop/long reach | Raise bars, shorter stem |
| Lower back | Posterior chain limits → pelvis rocks | Shorten reach, raise bars |
| Hip rocking | Saddle too high → compensation | Lower 2–3 mm |

Sit-bone width measurement: corrugated cardboard or memory foam, sit,
measure the center-to-center impressions, add 20–30 mm → saddle width.

## Adjustment protocol

1. ONE variable per change.
2. 2–3 rides (≥ 50 km total) before judging.
3. Increments: saddle height 2–3 mm, setback 5 mm, reach 10 mm, cleats 2 mm.
4. Log: date, what changed, what hurt, at what km.
5. New shoes/pedals/saddle = recheck saddle height (stack changes).
6. 3–4 failed iterations → professional fit. Any symptom that persists
   off the bike, worsens at night, or includes numbness/weakness → medical.

## Tools worth having

Tape measure, spirit level (or phone app), 4/5 mm hex, marker toscribe
seatpost height, cardboard for sit bones. A $20 goniometer replaces the
knee-angle guesswork.
