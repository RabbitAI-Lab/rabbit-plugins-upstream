# Chapter 1: Foreword — Crypto's Purgatory

## Core Idea
The journal frames crypto culture as Dante's Purgatory: the soul of Crypto won't reach Paradise soon, but unlike Hell, Purgatory offers redemption through work, reflection, and time — and the common thread to redemption is *recognition of the sin*.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Maximum Extractable Memento Mori**: the volume sandwiches crypto's redemption arc between two meditations on crypto's relationship with death (Wilson's death-performance opening, LunarDAO's squad-wealth closing).
- **Purgatorial redemption arc**: "Can we make it to level 2?" — Dante's seven P's (peccatum/sin) marked on the forehead, removed one by one through praxis. Applied to crypto: sins must be *recognized and worked off*, not denied.

## Key Concepts
- **Purgatory vs Hell**: Hell is terminal; Purgatory still has an exit to Heaven. Choice of frame matters for a movement's self-understanding.
- **Seven deadly sins applied to Crypto**: pride, envy, wrath, sloth, lust, greed, gluttony — the Foreword doesn't enumerate which maps to which crypto failure; it asserts the soul carries them.
- **Dante's first level of Heaven is the Moon**: associated with inconstancy — those who abandoned their vows, full of good intent but "falling at the last hurdle." The Foreword locates crypto *in the mix*: still able to decide well.

## Mental Models
- Think of crypto culture as a soul mid-journey, not damned: the question is not "is it evil" but "which sin is being refused recognition."
- Use the sandwich structure (death–redemption–death) as a reading frame for the whole volume: praxis in the middle, mortality on both ends.

## Anti-patterns
- **Reading crypto as Hell (terminal)**: forecloses praxis. The Purgatory frame keeps the redemptive work live.
- **Assuming intent suffices**: the Moon level holds those with "most good intent" who still fell — intent without fortitude lands you in inconstancy.

## Worked Example
The Foreword's own gesture: it quotes Dante's P-marking, then immediately names the redemption path as "acceleration and escalation of counter-economic practice in all its forms — cypherpunk, solarpunk, lunarpunk. Free will, it's our choice. Contribute to the corruption or transcend it." The framework applied to itself: the journal IS counter-economic praxis (a philosophical journal as agorist act).

## Key Takeaways
1. Redemption requires recognition of the sin — a movement that can't name its failures can't shed them.
2. Purgatory (exit exists) beats Hell (terminal) as a frame for fallen systems.
3. Counter-economics is presented as the *work* of Purgatory: cypherpunk/solarpunk/lunarpunk praxis as sin-removal.
4. "We are in the mix" — the Foreword's stance is that the outcome is still decidable, which is the agorist wager in miniature.
