# Chapter 9: The Crisis of Homogenous Authoritative Systems and the Future of Modern Tribes — Pavol Lupták

## Core Idea
"Space Age technology under Bronze Age rulership" (Rosenberg): heterogeneous internet society can no longer be governed by homogeneous political systems — the solution is radical decentralization into *modern tribes*, physical and virtual, the most natural human grouping.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Information monopoly collapse**: state education and media oligopoly lost the "truth" monopoly — "anyone can become an information provider." Result: highly individualistic information society, opinion heterogeneity at billions-scale (and "an extremely large number of conspiracies" as ambient cost).
- **Homogeneity crisis**: parliaments, direct democracy, monarchies, dictatorships — all *homogeneous* systems enforced on millions with nothing in common but "a language and passport or national ID." "Despite their physical proximity, we live in completely different worlds" — shared interests now live thousands of miles away. Virtual communities are replacing traditional ones for the first time in history, yet remain politically ignored.
- **The violence-expense spiral**: "homogenous systems are too fragile to survive without violence. The enforcement of violence within a well-informed society is extremely expensive." Well-informed peer-to-peer society renders large homogeneous systems inefficient and obsolete → high conflict.
- **Modern tribes (the solution)**: decentralized society composed of physical dwellings (private cities, natural communities) and virtual tribes with autonomous zones, legal systems, rule-sets. Claimed benefits: better cooperation, maximum loyalty, improved in-tribe conflict resolution, decreased transaction costs. "The most natural human grouping" — the extension of individuality, not its suppression.

## Key Concepts
- **National tags**: the passport/ID basis of political community — arbitrary vs. value/interest-based association.
- **Free-minded Internet people**: the generation that knows (from the internet's own existence) that decentralized voluntary systems are possible — women, LGBT, discriminated races, weed smokers, and netizens as co-victims of enforced homogeneity.
- **Autonomous zones + rule-sets**: tribes as jurisdictional technology, not just social clubs.

## Mental Models
- Use *"Bronze Age rulership"* audit: for any institution, ask whether its governance form matches its society's information topology — mismatch = crisis.
- Use *tribe-optimal scale*: loyalty, conflict resolution, transaction costs all improve when the unit matches actual affinity, not geography.
- Use *virtual-first politics*: political representation should follow community formation — and communities have already gone virtual.

## Anti-patterns
- **Enforced homogeneity**: prescribing one system for heterogeneous millions — always adverse impact on whoever doesn't fit.
- **Geographic political gravity**: assuming governance must follow physical proximity when lived community doesn't.
- **Ignoring virtual communities politically**: representation lag creates the legitimacy vacuum the crisis feeds on.

## Worked Example
The essay's own diagnostic loop: state education "among the most boring" information channels → information chaos from hundreds of thousands of outlets → billions of heterogeneous opinions → yet a parliament of a few hundred decides for all of them → enforcement cost explodes → conflict. Each step follows from the previous; the exit is at the community-formation step — let the already-formed virtual tribes (open-source projects, crypto communities, gaming guilds, privacy circles) acquire the trappings of governance: zones, law, rules. LunarDAO is precisely this move executed.

## Key Takeaways
1. Information abundance dissolved the monopoly preconditions of homogeneous governance.
2. Homogeneous systems now survive only through expensive violence — they are obsolete, not merely imperfect.
3. Political community should track affinity, not geography.
4. Modern tribes (physical + virtual) are the natural unit: loyalty, resolution, and cost all improve.
5. The free-minded Internet generation already knows the alternative exists — legitimacy has already migrated.
