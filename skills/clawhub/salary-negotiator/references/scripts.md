# Negotiation Script Library

Fill-in-the-blank scripts for every common scenario. Numbers in [brackets]
come from `salary_negotiator.py offer/raise` output. Rehearse OUT LOUD —
a script you've only read is a script you won't deliver.

## Universal Rules

1. **One number.** Never a range ("150–170" → they hear 150).
2. **Say the number, then stop talking.** Silence is a tool, not rudeness.
   The next person to speak loses money — make sure it's them.
3. **Enthusiasm first, number second.** They must believe you want the job;
   otherwise the counter reads as ambivalence.
4. **Never negotiate against yourself.** If they're silent, hold. Repeating
   or lowering your number unprompted is the #1 self-inflicted wound.
5. **Everything in writing.** Verbal "yes we can do 160" is worth nothing
   until it's in the offer letter.
6. **Buy time.** "I'd like 48 hours to review the full package" is always
   available, always professional, and costs nothing.

## Scenario 1: Countering a New Offer (Round 1)

**Setup: run `offer` first. Have target, floor, and one rationale ready.**

> "Thank you — I'm genuinely excited about this role and the team. I want to
> say that up front.
>
> [next call/email] I've done my research on this scope for [location/level]:
> market medians run around [market median], and given [specific
> qualification — a metric, a scale, or a scarce skill], I was targeting
> [target]. Is there flexibility to get there?"

**If "that's above our band":**
> "I understand bands are real. If base is capped, I'm open to solving it
> another way — a sign-on, a larger initial equity grant, or a six-month
> review with agreed criteria. What's workable on your side?"

**If "let me talk to the team":**
> "Of course — I know you have to run it up. When should I expect to hear
> back?" (Lock a date. Follow up on that date.)

## Scenario 2: Round 2 — They Moved, But Not Enough

> "I appreciate you moving — that tells me we can get this done. If you can
> get to [midpoint between their new number and your target], I'm ready to
> sign this week."

Naming a signing condition ("I'm ready to sign") converts your ask from
haggling into a trade they want to make.

**If they move less than halfway:**
> "We're closer. Here's my constraint: [your floor rationale — another
> process, comp data, a written commitment you have]. If base truly can't
> move, can we close the gap with [sign-on / equity / review-in-6-months]?"

## Scenario 3: The Raise Conversation

**Setup: run `raise`. Bring 2–3 impact bullets with numbers, your market
delta, and one ask.**

> "Thanks for making time. I wanted to talk about my compensation.
>
> Over the [period]: [impact bullet 1 with a number]. [Impact bullet 2 with a
> number]. [Impact bullet 3].
>
> The market for this scope has also moved — for [role/level] in
> [location], medians now run around [market median], and I'm at [current].
>
> I'd like to get to [ask]. What would it take to make that happen this
> cycle?"

**If "budget is already locked for this year":**
> "I understand the cycle. Two options: can we agree in writing on [ask]
> effective next cycle, with the criteria spelled out? Or if there's any
> mid-cycle flexibility — a one-time adjustment, a spot bonus for [specific
> project] — I'd rather solve part of it now than wait a year."

**If "you need to take on more scope first":**
> "Happy to. Can we define exactly what 'more scope' means — which
> responsibilities, what results — and put the comp adjustment in writing as
> of the date I deliver them? I want us both held to the same definition."

## Scenario 4: Competing Offers (Use Carefully)

Only name a real competing offer. Fabricated leverage collapses on contact
with reality.

> "I want to be transparent: I have another offer at [number]. It's not my
> first choice — I'd rather be here because [genuine reason]. If you can
> match [number], or come close and beat them on [equity/PTO/remote], I'll
> sign with you and withdraw my other process today."

---

## Scenario 5: Rescinding Risk / Exploding Offer

> "I'm very interested. To give you a real answer I need until [date] —
> I have [a competing process / a family decision] to close out. Can you
> hold the offer until then?"

If they refuse any extension on a reasonable timeline, that's data about
how they'll treat deadlines and pressure once you work there.

---

## Scenario 6: The Silence Technique

After they state their number or a small bump:

> [Say nothing. Count 5 seconds in your head. Nod slowly.]

Most people fill silence by negotiating against themselves ("...but I'm
flexible on that..."). Do not be most people. If the silence stretches:

> "I hear you. What flexibility exists on your side?"

You've made them name the next number without showing your hand.

## Pushback Q&A Quick Sheet

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "This is our standard band." | "Bands have exceptions — what would justify the top of the band here?" |
| "Budget is fixed this year." | "Then let's solve it with equity/sign-on now and base next cycle — in writing." |
| "Everyone starts at this level." | "Given [qualification], could I start at [level+1]? Or get [level] pay at the top of band?" |
| "We can't do that number." | "Understood. What's the closest you can get, and what else can move — sign-on, equity, PTO?" |
| "Take it or leave it." | "I need 48 hours." (Then decide against your floor. Never decide on the call.) |
| "New hires can't negotiate." | "I hear that a lot — and yet comp is a range, not a point. Who owns the final number?" |
| "You're already paid well." | "Compared to market median for this scope, I'm [X%] under — and here's the impact delta." |

---

## After Agreement

1. Get the final package in writing — every component, including any
   verbal promises about refresh, review dates, or remote arrangements.
2. Confirm start date and any contingencies.
3. Thank everyone. Negotiation ends at the relationship, not the number.
4. Keep the market data you gathered — it seeds next cycle's `raise` run.
