# Never-fabricate red lines

The single most valuable behavior of this skill: **it refuses to invent facts a
manufacturer cannot back up.** Inventing a spec, price, or certification in a B2B
reply creates commercial and legal risk and destroys buyer trust on first contact.

## Never state as fact (unless the user supplied a verified source)

- ❌ Prices, unit costs, or discounts
- ❌ MOQ, sample lead time, production lead time
- ❌ Certifications, safety standards, compliance marks
- ❌ Test results (load, vibration, water resistance, pressure, temperature…)
- ❌ Compatibility guarantees ("fits model X")
- ❌ Stock levels, capacity, "already in mass production"
- ❌ Dimensions, materials, tolerances, weights the buyer didn't give and that
  aren't in a confirmed SKU / drawing / spec sheet
- ❌ Company history, founding date, factory size, or client logos that aren't
  verified for the exact legal entity sending the reply

## Verified source = one of

- A value the **buyer** stated in their inquiry.
- A field in an **approved SKU sheet, drawing, or sample** the user explicitly
  provided in this session.
- A fact from the user's **confirmed company profile** they told you to use.

If none apply → it's an open question, not a statement.

## Safe transformations

| Tempting to write | Write instead |
|---|---|
| "MOQ is 500 pcs" | "We'll confirm MOQ once the spec is locked." |
| "Lead time 30 days" | "We'll advise sample/production lead time after the interface is agreed." |
| "CE/EN certified" | "We'll share the exact test evidence we can document for this product." |
| "Fits all scooters" | "Please share the target model/mounting so we can confirm fit." |
| "Price is $8/pc" | "Pricing depends on final material, quantity and packaging — we'll quote once confirmed." |

## Self-check before sending

1. Every number in the draft — did the buyer or an approved source provide it?
2. Every "we are / we have / we can" — is it process capability (safe) or a
   specific unverified claim (unsafe)?
3. Is anything unconfirmed phrased as a promise instead of a next step?

If any check fails, move the item into the clarification checklist.
