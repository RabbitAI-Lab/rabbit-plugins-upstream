# English reply templates

Pick by **project stage**. Fill `{{...}}` placeholders. Keep the tone confident
but honest — confirm *process* capability, never invent *specifics*. Delete any
line you cannot back up.

---

## 1. Supplier sourcing / first contact

> Subject: Re: {{PRODUCT_TYPE}} OEM/ODM inquiry — {{COMPANY_ABBR}}
>
> Hi {{CONTACT_NAME}},
>
> Thank you for reaching out about {{PRODUCT_TYPE}} for {{TARGET_MARKET}}. We are
> an OEM/ODM manufacturer of {{PRODUCT_LINES}} and can support your project from
> sampling through tooling, testing, mass production and private-label packaging.
>
> To prepare an accurate proposal, could you confirm a few details?
>
> {{CLARIFICATION_QUESTIONS}}
>
> Once we have these, we'll send a spec confirmation sheet and the next-step plan
> (sample and tooling scope). If you have a drawing, reference sample or target
> model, sharing it will speed things up significantly.
>
> Best regards,
> {{SALES_NAME}} — {{COMPANY}}
> {{CONTACT_EMAIL}}

---

## 2. Concept / new model

Same opener, then emphasize co-development: "We can help translate your concept
into a manufacturable design. After reviewing the interface and target comfort/
performance, we'll propose a sampling plan." Ask for use case, target vehicle,
comfort/performance goal, volume, and any industrial-design references.

---

## 3. Prototype / sample request

> Thanks {{CONTACT_NAME}} — to prepare a sample we need to lock the interface and
> key spec first:
>
> {{CLARIFICATION_QUESTIONS}}
>
> After we confirm the drawing/interface, we'll advise the sampling scope and
> what the sample validates. We'll confirm sample lead time and cost once the
> spec is locked — we don't quote those before the interface is agreed, to avoid
> giving you a number that changes later.

---

## 4. Replacement supplier

Acknowledge they have an existing part. Ask for the current sample / drawing /
supplier spec, failure or improvement reason, annual quantity, and target price
band. Position on matching fit + improving the weak point. Do **not** claim you
can beat a competitor's price or quality before you've seen the part.

---

## 5. Mass production / ready to order

Confirm you understand they're ready to scale. Ask for the final approved spec /
sample, annual + per-order quantity, delivery schedule, packaging/branding, and
destination. Then: "We'll issue a spec confirmation sheet and a formal quotation
once the final sample/spec is approved."

---

## Reusable closing lines (safe phrasings)

- "We'll confirm MOQ, sample and production lead time after the spec is locked."
- "Certifications and test evidence: we'll share exactly what we can document for
  this product — we don't list standards we can't back with real reports."
- "Pricing depends on final material, quantity and packaging; we'll quote once
  those are confirmed."
- "If helpful, we can start with a sample to validate fit before committing to
  tooling."
