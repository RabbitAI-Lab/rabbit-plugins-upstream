# Platform playbook

Per-platform format, length, cadence and hook conventions. Use this when
drafting in Stage 1. No account or credentials are needed for anything here.

## X

Hard limit 280 characters on the standard tier, and X counts every link as 23
characters and emoji and most non Latin characters as 2. Threads work when each
post stands alone; a thread that only makes sense read end to end loses people
at post two. Links carry no ranking penalty, but a bare link with no context
converts badly, so give the reason to click above it.

Long-form Articles are a separate surface with a far higher ceiling. If the user
has X Premium and the content is over roughly 1,000 words, write it as an
Article (`create_x_article_draft`) and post the link on its own.

Hashtags: zero or one. More reads as spam.
Cadence: one to three a day is sustainable. Reply volume matters more than post
volume for reach. Publishing to X needs a paid Socialync plan.

## LinkedIn

3,000 characters. Only about the first 210 show before the "more" link, so the
first two lines are the entire hook; treat them as a headline. Line breaks after
almost every sentence; dense paragraphs get skipped in feed.

Documents and carousels reliably outperform plain text. Outbound links in the
post body suppress reach; put the link in the first comment instead.

Hashtags: three to five, specific rather than broad.
Cadence: three to five a week. Daily posting on LinkedIn fatigues an audience
faster than on any other platform.

## Instagram

Captions to 2,200 characters, but the first 125 show before truncation. The
first line does the work. Every post needs an image or a video; a video
publishes as a Reel.

Feed posts, Reels and Stories are different products. Reels reach non-followers;
feed posts serve existing ones. Match the format to whether the goal is reach or
depth.

Hashtags: three to five in the caption. Blocks of thirty are a 2020 tactic and
now read as low quality.
Cadence: three to five a week plus Stories daily if the user has the material.

## TikTok

Captions to 2,200 characters. The caption is secondary; the first two seconds of
video decide everything. When drafting, write the on-screen hook text as well as
the caption. Scheduled publishing only, one video or a photo post of images, and
the user picks who can view each post.

Hashtags: three to five, mixing one broad discovery tag with specific ones.
Cadence: daily is normal here. Under three a week and the account stalls.

## Threads

500 characters and at most 5 links. Conversational register; a post that reads
like a press release dies. Replies drive reach more than the root post does.

Hashtags: one, maximum.
Cadence: one to three a day.

## YouTube

Titles to 100 characters, though under 60 avoids truncation in most surfaces.
Descriptions to 5,000, with the first 157 characters serving as the search
snippet, so write those as a standalone summary containing the primary keyword.
Scheduled publishing only, video only, `title` required.

Shorts and long-form are separate strategies. A Short is a hook plus a payoff in
under 60 seconds; long-form needs a stated promise in the first 30.

Cadence: whatever the user can sustain. Irregular uploads hurt less than
abandoned schedules.

## Bluesky

300 characters, at most 4 images. The audience skews technical and reacts badly
to marketing register. Plain, direct and specific works; hype does not.

Hashtags: optional and lightly used.
Cadence: one to three a day.

## Facebook

8,000 characters, but engagement falls off sharply past roughly 80 characters
in feed. Pages reach far less organically than the other platforms here, so set
expectations accordingly and treat it as a distribution floor rather than a
growth channel.

## Cross-posting rules

Rewrite for each platform instead of truncating. A LinkedIn post cut to 280
characters reads as broken on X. The idea travels and each platform gets its own wording.

Stagger timing across platforms rather than firing everything at once. Users who
follow the same brand in two places notice simultaneous identical posts.

Keep a single source of truth for the claim and let each platform's version make
that claim in its own register. One publish with several platforms still counts
as one post against the monthly allowance.
