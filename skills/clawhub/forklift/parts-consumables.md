# Forklift Parts, Wear Items and Consumables

Use the serial-specific parts catalogue and approved service manual. No current prices are stored here; obtain dated local service/dealer quotations. Generic examples do not establish interchangeability.

## 1. Categories

| Category | Examples | Planning basis |
|---|---|---|
| Consumables | Oils, coolant, brake fluid, grease, filters, plugs | Manual intervals and condition |
| Wear items | Forks, chains, rollers, tyres, seals, DC brushes | Specified wear/damage limits |
| Critical assemblies | Pumps, motors, controllers, steering, brake cylinders, BMS/OBC/DC-DC | Approved diagnosis and serial-specific compatibility |
| Major assemblies | Engine, transmission, axle, battery, mast, frame | Condition, repair approval and economic assessment |

## 2. Hydraulics

### Fluids

Common references include L-HM 46 antiwear oil, L-HV 32 for suitable low-temperature service, synthetic options and NSF H1-registered fluids for assessed food applications. These are not interchangeable merely because viscosity looks similar. Check actual temperature range, seals, additives and OEM approval. Mobil, Shell, Kluber and Great Wall are supplier examples, not blanket product recommendations.

### Filters

Specify part number, media, filtration rating/beta ratio, flow, pressure, bypass setting, seals and port dimensions. Suction, return and pressure filters serve different functions. HYDAC, Pall, MANN and approved regional alternatives require exact matching. Change at the manual interval or restriction criterion.

### Pumps and valves

Gear, piston and vane pumps differ in displacement, pressure, speed, rotation, mounting and ports. Match all relevant parameters. For directional valves, verify circuit centre condition, pressure, flow, sections and controls. Husco and OEM/regional products are not interchangeable by brand alone. Internal leakage, sticking or abnormal noise needs qualified diagnosis.

### Cylinders

Identify lift versus tilt duty, bore, stroke, mounting, operating pressure, rod and seals. Lift cylinders can be single-acting or other designs; do not assume every cylinder is double-acting. Use approved seals and repair procedures. Release pressure and support raised components before work.

## 3. Electrical Parts

| Part | Compatibility information |
|---|---|
| DC / induction / permanent-magnet motor | Power, voltage, speed, feedback, mounting, cooling and duty |
| Curtis / ZAPI / Sevcon / regional controller | Voltage/current, motor type, firmware, CAN, safety configuration |
| Contactor / relay | DC rating, current, coil voltage, suppression, contact arrangement |
| Fuse / breaker | DC voltage and interrupting rating, time-current curve and mounting |
| Sensor | Supply, output, connector, calibration, environment and software |

Do not substitute an AC fuse for a DC circuit without verified rating. Never bypass a fuse or interlock. Controller replacement may require approved configuration and commissioning.

## 4. Batteries and Chargers

Lead-acid suppliers include EnerSys/Hawker, Trojan and regional manufacturers. Lithium packs require exact chemistry, voltage, capacity, mechanical restraint, BMS, connector and communication compatibility. Supplier names alone do not prove compatibility.

Battery mass can be part of the truck's stability design. A lighter lithium conversion requires manufacturer approval and appropriate ballast/capacity documentation. Do not select a replacement by Ah alone. Nominal kWh = nominal V × Ah / 1000; usable energy differs.

Charger matching covers chemistry, profile, voltage/current, BMS protocol, connectors, electrical supply and installation. An automotive charging standard or IP67 claim cannot be assumed for an industrial pack. Never open a traction battery or replace cells as an operator task. Capacity/cycle life depend on duty and warranty terms.

## 5. Forks and Attachments

Specify rated load at load centre, length, section, hook/shaft mounting, ISO 2328 mounting class where applicable and compatibility with carriage. Standard, long, wide and stainless options need engineering approval; length alone is not enough.

| Attachment | Application | Checks |
|---|---|---|
| Sideshift | Pallet alignment | Offset, hydraulic supply, revised capacity |
| Fork positioner | Variable loads | Fork capacity, opening and controls |
| Paper-roll / drum clamp | Specific cargo | Grip force, load security and contact surfaces |
| Push-pull | Slip-sheet handling | Load compatibility and capacity |
| Rotator | Controlled rotation | Torque, retention and load chart |
| Tipping / bale clamp | Assessed specialist duty | Locking and load retention |
| Lifting jib / hook | Approved suspended loads only | OEM approval, lifting plan and applicable rules |

Never treat ordinary forks as a crane or personnel platform. Attachment mass and offset require a configuration-specific capacity plate.

## 6. Mast, Chains and Rollers

Mast stages, free lift and cylinder arrangement are model-specific. Match chain series, pitch, plate arrangement, strength, anchors and length using the catalogue; do not confuse leaf chains with roller chains. Inspect elongation, corrosion, cracks and anchors against approved limits. Use suitable chain lubricant.

Rollers/bearings require exact dimensions, load rating, mounting, clearance and materials. Supplier examples include IWIS, Sedis, Donghua, SKF, NSK and FAG; verify actual approved parts. Do not promise service life by generic hours.

## 7. Tyres

Pneumatic, resilient solid, press-on and polyurethane tyres differ in rim compatibility, load, speed, heat, cushioning, floor suitability and stability. Typical size strings such as 6.50-10 or 28x9-15 are not sufficient to select pressure or replacement construction. Use truck/tyre specifications.

Non-marking tyres are not automatically electrically conductive or explosion-protected. Tyre conversion/rotation requires approval. Supplier examples include Camso, Michelin, Bridgestone, Goodyear and regional makers; match actual requirements.

## 8. Brakes

Match friction material, dimensions, hydraulic cylinders, seals, cables and actuators to the truck. Dry, wet-disc and other systems use different fluids and service procedures. Do not assume DOT 3/4 is correct for every brake circuit. A generic minimum friction thickness or cable life is not an OEM limit. Verify braking after repair through a competent technician.

## 9. Stock Planning

| Fleet size | Candidate stock, subject to commonality and lead time |
|---|---|
| 1-5 | Approved service kits and frequently used consumables |
| 5-20 | Add common contactors/relays/sensors and wear items |
| 20-50 | Consider critical controller, motor, pump and chain assemblies |
| 50+ | Modular replacement strategy and annual supply agreement |

Annual quantities must follow operating hours, fleet models, reservoir capacities and change intervals. Do not assume every 2-3 t truck consumes one or two 200 L drums of hydraulic oil annually.

## 10. Purchasing Checklist

Record truck serial, OEM part number and supersession, compatibility evidence, supplier authorization, batch/traceability, warranty, lead time and return terms. Prefer approved parts for safety-critical assemblies. Quality aftermarket consumables may be suitable when specifications are verified. Unusually low price is a prompt to investigate, not proof of counterfeiting. Photograph packaging and mismatch evidence. Return rights vary by jurisdiction and B2B contract; do not promise universal 7/15-day rights.
