# Forklift Safety, Operator Qualification, Insurance, and Operating Procedures

Use this guide with the manufacturer's manual, site risk assessment, current local law and a competent supervisor. Requirements differ by truck, jurisdiction and application. See `international-markets.md` for official UK, EU, US and other regional sources.

## 1. China: Special-Equipment Framework

Check the Special Equipment Safety Law, the Regulations on Safety Supervision of Special Equipment, the applicable TSG 11 edition and amendments, operator qualification rules, and incident-reporting requirements through SAMR. Confirm whether the truck falls within the special-equipment catalogue.

Before operation, verify applicable registration, inspection, rental responsibilities, ownership records and modification approval. Establish inspection dates from current official requirements and the actual certificate. Do not confuse an operator certificate's renewal period with a truck inspection interval. After major repair or modification, determine whether additional inspection and approval are required.

## 2. Operator Qualification and Training

Check the current local qualification category and issuing authority; do not rely on obsolete N1/N2 mappings. Confirm age, medical fitness, education, training, examination and renewal requirements with the authority rather than using generic training-hour figures.

Training should cover legal duties, truck controls, capacity plate, stability, pedestrians, visibility, gradients, loading, attachments, charging/refuelling, daily checks and emergency response. Provide truck-specific familiarisation and site authorization. A certificate is not proof of competence for every truck or attachment.

## 3. Insurance

| Cover | Questions to resolve with the insurer |
|---|---|
| Equipment / third-party liability | Mandatory local requirements, injury and property limits, deductibles |
| Employer liability / worker protection | Operator and employee cover under local law |
| Property / equipment damage | Truck, attachment, battery and charger valuation |
| Lithium-battery endorsement | Fire, thermal events, charging area and storage exclusions |
| Product liability | Manufacturer, importer and distributor responsibilities |
| Business interruption | Covered downtime and qualifying events |

Obtain written confirmation of lithium-battery, rental, used-equipment, modification and cross-border coverage. Ownership transfer may require notification, endorsement or a new policy; do not assume insurance transfers automatically or universally cannot transfer. Avoid universal premium or coverage recommendations without an actual risk assessment.

## 4. Pre-Shift Checklist

- [ ] Tyres, wheel fasteners, forks, carriage, mast, chains and overhead guard show no unsafe damage.
- [ ] Fluid levels and battery condition meet the manual; no leaks are visible.
- [ ] Service and parking brakes, steering and controls operate correctly.
- [ ] Horn, warning devices, lights and required interlocks work.
- [ ] Seat, restraint and access steps are sound.
- [ ] Attachment is secure and the capacity plate covers the configuration.
- [ ] Display self-test shows no unresolved warning.
- [ ] Route, loading dock, floor, pedestrian separation and emergency access are safe.

Remove unsafe equipment from service and report the defect. Never bypass an interlock to complete a shift.

## 5. Travel and Load Handling

Follow the site's assessed speed limits; fixed example speeds are not universal legal limits. Reduce speed for turns, pedestrians, wet floors, doorways, blind corners and slopes. Carry loads low, maintain a stable load, and use the restraint provided. Do not carry passengers except in an approved designated position.

Maintain visibility in the direction of travel. Use a planned safe route, approved visibility aids or a trained spotter where needed; stop if safe travel cannot be assured. Never leave a moving truck to check a blind area.

Use the manufacturer's gradient limits and load orientation instructions. For conventional loaded counterbalance trucks, keep the load upgrade as instructed; travel straight on slopes and avoid turning or parking there. Empty-truck rules and other truck designs can differ. Do not use one generic slope angle for all trucks.

Read the residual-capacity plate for the actual lift height, load centre and attachment. Fully support the load using the appropriate fork insertion and spacing. Never exceed rated capacity, use one fork for an unapproved lift, push with the mast, or use ordinary forks as a work platform. Approach racks squarely and use smooth controls. Do not tilt an elevated load contrary to the manual.

## 6. Parking

Park in the designated safe area, lower forks, neutralize controls, apply the parking brake and isolate the truck according to the manual. Remove the key or secure access. Keep fire exits, stairs, chargers and emergency equipment clear. Do not prescribe one parking spacing for every site.

## 7. Emergency Response

- Loss of control: follow the truck-specific emergency procedure, warn others and stop as safely as possible. Do not abruptly apply a parking brake at speed unless the manual specifies it.
- Fire or suspected battery thermal runaway: stop if safe, evacuate, isolate the area and call emergency services. Do not handle a smoking battery or improvise extinguishing methods. Fire-service advice and battery chemistry determine response; lithium-ion batteries must not be confused with lithium-metal fires.
- Tip-over: follow training for the truck type. For a seated counterbalance truck, remain restrained, brace and lean away from impact as instructed; do not jump. Stand-up truck escape instructions can differ.
- Electric shock: isolate the supply only if safe; do not touch an energized casualty. Call emergency services and provide trained first aid once the scene is safe.
- Falling or unstable forks: keep everyone clear. Only qualified personnel may install approved supports; never enter beneath an unsupported mast or load.

## 8. Special Environments

| Environment | Required assessment |
|---|---|
| Cold store | Manufacturer-rated truck, battery heating and charging limits, condensation protection, suitable fluids, operator exposure |
| Potentially explosive atmosphere | Area classification and a complete approved truck configuration; no unapproved electrical additions |
| High racking | Actual residual capacity, rack condition, floor tolerance, visibility aids and operator competence |
| Containers and docks | Trailer restraint, dock capacity, floor strength, overhead clearance, lighting and ventilation |
| Container yards | Purpose-built handler/attachment, wind limits and lifting plan from the manufacturer |
| Food / pharmaceutical | Hygiene, compatible fluids, cleaning and contamination controls |

LFP chemistry, an earthing chain, or a group-standard reference alone does not establish explosion protection.

## 9. Safety Devices

Assess overhead guard, load backrest, operator restraint, brakes, horn, warning lights, descent-control protection, emergency isolation and electrical protections against the applicable standard and manual. Device requirements vary by type and configuration. Cameras and radar supplement route planning and pedestrian separation; they do not replace them. Never claim every listed device is mandatory on every industrial truck.

## 10. Overseas Compliance

- UK: separate GB and NI market-access rules. Check HSE guidance on lift trucks, PUWER and LOLER, plus current government machinery marking guidance. Thorough Examination does not replace maintenance.
- EU: check Machinery Directive 2006/42/EC transition to Regulation (EU) 2023/1230, generally applicable from 20 January 2027. Verify applicable legislation, declaration, technical file, instructions and markings; standards such as EN ISO 3691, EN 1175 and EN 12895 require scope and edition checks.
- US: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.178 governs relevant general-industry operation. Employer training, evaluation and certification apply; performance evaluation is at least every three years, with refresher training for specified triggers. Inspect before service and at least daily, and after each shift for continuous use. Check state-plan and industry-specific requirements.
- Canada: provincial/territorial OHS rules and applicable CSA B335 provisions.
- Australia: state/territory WHS authority, high-risk work licence class and AS 2359.
- Japan: occupational safety law, operator qualifications and applicable JIS.
- Battery export: check UN 38.3 evidence and mode-specific dangerous-goods requirements separately from product certification.

Official websites, regional manufacturers and procurement portals are listed in `international-markets.md`.

## 11. Safety Culture and Incident Learning

Use daily briefings, scheduled refresher training, emergency exercises and practical competence assessments at intervals justified by risk. Record near misses and correct unsafe conditions promptly. Stop and retrain after unsafe operation; do not substitute a generic three-strike rule for immediate risk control.

| Incident type | Typical contributors | Prevention focus |
|---|---|---|
| Tip-over | Excess speed, overload, gradients, unstable loads | Capacity plate, restraint, route and speed controls |
| Pedestrian collision | Blind areas, reversing, mixed routes | Segregation, visibility, controlled crossings |
| Falling load | Poor support, damaged pallets, unsuitable attachment | Load assessment and attachment selection |
| Fire | Fuel leaks, electrical faults, battery damage | Inspections, compatible charging, emergency planning |
| Electric shock | Damaged wiring, unsafe charging or repair | Isolation and qualified electrical work |

Do not present unsourced accident percentages as measured statistics.
