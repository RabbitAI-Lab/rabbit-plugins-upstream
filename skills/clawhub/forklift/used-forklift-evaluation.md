# Used Forklift Evaluation and Purchasing

Assess operating history and critical systems, not paint alone. Actual transaction value depends on brand, age, hours, condition, battery, region, support and demand. No current transaction price is stored here.

## 1. When Used Equipment Can Fit

Temporary projects, seasonal peaks, backup duty and training can justify used equipment. Compare purchase plus refurbishment and downtime against rental and new equipment. High utilization, specialist hazardous/cold/food environments, unknown history and unavailable service require stronger scrutiny. Used equipment is not universally prohibited from export; establish actual destination conformity and documentation requirements.

## 2. Valuation Framework

```text
Indicative value = comparable local transaction value
                   adjusted for verified hours, condition, battery, history,
                   configuration, warranty, compliance and refurbishment cost
```

A linear remaining-life formula is only a rough screening tool and may be misleading. Do not apply generic residual percentages as an appraisal. Obtain comparable dated quotations for the exact market, currency and tax basis. Verify hour-meter evidence against service records and component condition.

## 3. Documents and Identity

- [ ] Manufacturer plate, exact model, serial, manufacture date and capacity plate.
- [ ] Original invoice/contract and clear ownership; check liens where relevant.
- [ ] Applicable registration, inspection/examination and transfer documents.
- [ ] Operating manual, service and overhaul records.
- [ ] Battery/charger identifiers, age, tests and replacement history.
- [ ] Insurance/incident history where lawfully available.
- [ ] Asset number and serials consistent; no unexplained re-stamping.

## 4. Physical Inspection

| System | Inspect / verify |
|---|---|
| Forks / carriage | Wear, cracks, deformation, hooks and locking devices; competent measurement |
| Mast / chains | Straightness, welds, anchors, elongation, rollers and approved lubrication |
| Hydraulics | Leaks, oil condition, smooth movement, noise and specified drift/pressure tests |
| Electric drive | Codes, contactors, motor noise/temperature, controls, approved feedback checks |
| Battery / charger | SOH evidence, cell consistency, damage, compatibility and charging history |
| IC engine | Cold start, idle, smoke, leaks, coolant, codes, emissions system |
| Transmission / axle | Engagement, noise, leaks and approved functional tests |
| Brakes / steering | Safe controlled performance to specification |
| Safety / electrics | Restraint, guard, warnings, lights, interlocks, wiring |
| Tyres / wheels | Correct construction, wear marks, pressure where applicable, damage and fasteners |

Do not substitute a universal 3 m stopping distance, 30% parking slope, 16-18 MPa relief pressure, 0.05 V cell difference or 50 mm drift limit for the actual manual. Fork wear and chain elongation require the applicable standard/manufacturer criteria and competent inspection. Never inspect under an unsupported load.

## 5. Test Drive

A competent operator should perform a planned cold-start check, warm operation and representative duty cycle in a controlled area. Begin unloaded, then use an approved load within the actual capacity chart. Record smoothness, noise, response, temperatures, energy/fuel use, alarms and leaks. Do not impose the same 1.5 t load, lift height, emergency stop or slope test on every truck. Stop immediately for unsafe symptoms.

Where practical, simulate the intended duty long enough to expose thermal or runtime issues. Battery capacity testing requires an approved specialist procedure, not merely watching SOC for an hour.

## 6. Seller Due Diligence

Verify seller identity/business, ownership, operating history, written disclosures, repair responsibility and return/warranty terms. Use an independent competent inspection when needed. Manufacturer-certified used equipment can improve traceability, but certification scope, actual warranty and premium require written confirmation. Do not assume every brand offers the same programme.

## 7. Transfer

Confirm destination registration/examination obligations, valid report dates, insurance arrangement, asset records and battery warranty transfer. Cross-region or cross-border transactions can require new documentation. Do not assume every transfer universally requires or avoids a new inspection/policy.

## 8. Risks

Watch for altered hours, structural accident repairs, overstated battery SOH, counterfeit components, expired examinations, unavailable insurance, discontinued parts and unsupported software. Preserve evidence and verify plate/invoice/service records. Do not buy a truck with unexplained identity or disabled safety devices merely because it is cheap.

## 9. Decision Matrix

| Duty | Compare |
|---|---|
| Temporary project | Short rental versus used purchase plus resale |
| Backup / training | Inspected used truck with suitable support |
| Long-term multiple shifts | New or supported refurbished equipment with verified capacity |
| Heavy / hostile duty | Appropriate specification and robust service agreement |
| Export resale | Actual destination compliance, age and documentation requirements |

## 10. Refurbishment Budget

Price fluids/filters, battery testing or replacement, brakes, tyres, chains/rollers, repairs, documentation, transport and examinations separately. Obtain local written quotes. Battery cell work belongs with qualified specialists. Compare the full ready-to-work cost and risk against a new equivalent; a fixed 70% rule is only an optional screening assumption, not a valuation standard.

## 11. Example Scenarios

Backup warehouse: prioritize safe battery/controller condition and quick availability. Six-month relocation: compare rental including maintenance against acquisition/resale risk. Training fleet: prioritize intact safeguards, known origin and suitability for training tasks. Do not invent budgets without current local evidence.
