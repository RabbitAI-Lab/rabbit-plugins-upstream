# Mandarin ↔ English Translation Notes

## Register and Honorifics

- 你 (nǐ) → informal "you" | 您 (nín) → formal "you" (business, respectful)
- Default to 您 for business and legal contexts unless relationship is established informal
- Titles: 先生 (Mr.), 女士 (Ms./Mrs.), 博士 (Dr.) — place before surname
- Business emails: use 全名 + 职位 (full name + title) in formal openings

## Idioms and Chengyu (四字成语)

- Never translate chengyu literally word-by-word
- Provide functional equivalent + cultural note
- Example: 画蛇添足 (draw legs on a snake) → "gilding the lily" / "unnecessary addition"
- Example: 对牛弹琴 (play lute to a cow) → "casting pearls before swine"

## Legal Conventions

- Chinese legal documents use formal written style (书面语)
- Doublet expressions common in English law ("indemnify and hold harmless") → single concept in Chinese
- "Force majeure" → 不可抗力 (direct equivalent, well-established)
- Chinese contracts often state purpose clause first (目的条款) before operative clauses
- Number formatting: 万 (10,000) and 亿 (100,000,000) — convert carefully

## Formatting

- Chinese text does not use spaces between words
- Punctuation: 。instead of .，instead of ，？instead of ? ！instead of !
- Quotation marks: 「」 or "" both acceptable; prefer "" for international business context
- Dates: Chinese uses YYYY年MM月DD日 — convert to target locale format

## Common Pitfalls

- 是 (shì) is not always "is" — it links nouns, not adjectives
- 了 (le) indicates completion or change of state — not always past tense
- 的 (de) possessive particle — used differently from English possessives
- Measure words (量词) have no English equivalent — drop in translation unless counting
