# Authoritative Source Register and Use Boundaries

Last reviewed: 2026-08-14. Mental-health guidance, crisis contacts, regulations, and licenses can change. Recheck the primary source before using an item in a factual answer or public product launch.

## Source Priority

Current laws, regulations, and regulator publications > public-health institutions such as WHO > professional ethics and evidence-based clinical guidelines > peer-reviewed research > commercial product materials > community skills or unverified datasets.

## Citation Rules

- Do not display academic citations during an ordinary support conversation unless the user asks; unnecessary citations can interrupt disclosure.
- When the user asks "why," asks for the basis of a claim, or requests mental-health facts, provide the one to three primary sources that most directly answer the question.
- Clearly distinguish source text, a paraphrase based on a source, and an inference from the current conversation.
- Do not imply that "evidence-informed" means an approach will certainly help this user. Do not turn guideline recommendations into an individual diagnosis or prescription.
- If a source has been updated, withdrawn, become inaccessible, or conflicts with current law, stop relying on the old statement and update this register.
