# Radon Reference — Numbers Behind the Verdicts

## 1. Units and action levels
- 1 pCi/L = 37 Bq/m³ (exact). WHO recommends action below 100 Bq/m³ where feasible ≈ **2.7 pCi/L**.
- EPA: mitigate at **≥ 4.0 pCi/L**; 2.0–3.9 "consider reducing" — the 4.0 line dates to 1986 technology-cost compromise, not a health threshold.
- Indoor average (US): ~1.3 pCi/L. Outdoor: ~0.4.

## 2. EPA lifetime lung-cancer risk table (deaths per 1,000)
| pCi/L | Ever-smoker | Never-smoker |
|---|---|---|
| 20 | 260 | 36 |
| 10 | 150 | 18 |
| 8 | 120 | 15 |
| 4 | 62 | 7 |
| 2 | 32 | 4 |
| 1.3 | 20 | 2 |
| 0.4 | 3 | <1 |

(EPA "A Citizen's Guide to Radon" Exhibit — linear interpolation between rows is the script's method; risk is approximately proportional to cumulative exposure.) Compare for scale: 4 pCi/L ≈ the lung dose of ~200 chest X-rays/yr or ~8.5 mSv/yr — one of the largest natural radiation doses a person can easily control.

## 3. Why smoking multiplies the risk
Radon daughters and tobacco smoke act synergistically — the smoker table isn't "smoking risk + radon risk," it's several times the sum at mid-levels. An ex-smoker's risk sits between the columns, closer to smoker early after quitting, drifting toward never-smoker over 15+ years.

## 4. Test device classes
| Device | Class | Valid window |
|---|---|---|
| Charcoal canister / liquid scintillation | short-term passive | 2–7 days |
| Continuous radon monitor (CRM) | short-term active | ≥ 48 h |
| Electret ion chamber (E-Perm) | short OR long-term | 3–7 d / 91+ d variants |
| Alpha-track (ATD) | long-term | 91–365 days |

Protocol: closed-house conditions 12 h prior + throughout for ALL short-term tests. Long-term tests need no closure protocol — that's their advantage.

## 5. Season & building physics
Stack effect: heated winter air rises, drawing soil gas up — winter short-term readings run 1–2 pCi/L above the annual average; summer the reverse. Heavy rain and high wind spike readings; a house open all weekend zeroes them. This is why a winter ≥ 4.0 deserves action while a summer 2.9 deserves a long-term confirmation.

## 6. Mitigation reality
- **Active soil depressurization (ASD)**: a fan pulls soil gas from under the slab/membrane and vents above the roofline. $800–$1,500 (slab) to $1,500–$2,500 (crawlspace/multiple suction points) in most US markets.
- Operating cost: $65–$150/yr fan electricity + fan replacement ~$150–$300 every 5–10 yr.
- Expect post-mitigation levels of 0.2–1.5 pCi/L (typically 50–99% reduction); verify with a test 10 days–2 yr after install, then retest every 2 yr and after any renovation, HVAC change, or addition.
- Mitigator credentialing: many states license radon contractors (e.g., NEHA/NRPP or AARST-NRPP certified); ask for the credential and a written post-install guarantee of < 4.0 (good ones guarantee < 2.0).

## 7. Real-estate playbook
1. Radon testing is a standard inspection-contingency option (US). The 48-h CRM during the inspection window is normal and valid if closed-house rules are honored by the occupant — sellers sabotage tests by airing the house (a tamper flag: readings that collapse overnight or implausibly low results with windows observed open).
2. Result ≥ 4.0 → request: mitigation by a licensed contractor BEFORE closing (seller pays, buyer picks contractor), OR a credit equal to two high bids plus post-mitigation verification test. The letter template the script prints itemizes this.
3. "Unfinished basement, nobody's down there" is not a defense: future finishing, and stack effect moving soil gas upstairs — upstairs readings are typically 30–50% of the level below.
4. New-construction: ask for RRNC (Radon-Resistant New Construction) rough-in verification — a passive stack already installed; adding a fan later is cheap if the pipe exists.
