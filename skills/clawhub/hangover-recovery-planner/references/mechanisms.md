# Hangover Mechanisms and Evidence Grades

## The six stacked mechanisms

### 1. Diuresis / dehydration
Ethanol suppresses vasopressin (ADH) → urine output rises. Net loss
≈10 mL per gram ethanol beyond normal. Symptoms: thirst, dry mouth,
dizziness on standing, headache (mild — the headache is mostly NOT
dehydration, see 6). Fix: 1.5× deficit over 4–6 h with sodium
(0.7 g/L; 1.0 g/L if sweating). Evidence for rehydration: STRONG for
symptom relief, though trials show only partial improvement — because
dehydration is only one stack member.

### 2. Acetaldehyde load
Ethanol → acetaldehyde (ADH) → acetate (ALDH2). Acetaldehyde is
toxic, flushing, nauseating. Most people clear it fast; ALDH2
deficiency variants (≈30–45% of East Asians) accumulate it — flushing,
tachycardia, markedly worse hangovers, established esophageal-cancer
risk association. Fix: nothing acute; cysteine-rich food (eggs)
supplies substrate for glutathione conjugation — evidence WEAK but
harmless. The real fix is dose.

### 3. Methanol metabolism
Beverage alcohol contains trace methanol. ADH prefers ethanol, so
methanol waits — and is metabolized AFTER ethanol clears, into
formaldehyde → formic acid. This delayed tail explains why hangovers
worsen for hours after BAC hits zero, and why hair-of-the-dog
"works": re-dosing ethanol re-pauses methanol metabolism. Fix: time.
Nothing accelerates it safely at home. Evidence: MECHANISTIC
(strong chemistry, limited RCTs).

### 4. Gastritis
Ethanol irritates gastric mucosa directly; dark drinks and
carbonation (champagne) worsen it. Nausea, stomach pain. Fix: small
sips, dry carbs, antacid if acid-burn dominant, ginger (evidence
MODERATE for nausea generally). Avoid NSAIDs on this mucosa.

### 5. Sleep disruption
Ethanol sedates then fragments sleep and suppresses REM in the first
half of the night; REM rebounds in the second half. "Slept 8 hours,
feel wrecked" = architecture damage, not duration. Fix: none acute;
protect the NEXT night; cap morning naps at 20 min before 15:00 to
preserve it. Evidence: STRONG.

### 6. Inflammatory/immune response
Hangover severity correlates with elevated inflammatory cytokines
(IL-6, IL-10, TNF-α, CRP). This — not "toxins" — explains the
flu-like malaise, and why the headache responds only partly to
fluids. Fix: time; light activity may help clearance modestly.
Anti-inflammatory drugs help the headache but irritate mechanism 4.
Evidence: STRONG correlation, moderate causal understanding.

## Countermeasure evidence table

| Remedy | Verdict | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Water + sodium, spread out | WORKS (partial) | fixes mechanism 1 only |
| Sleep (next night) | WORKS | fixes 5 |
| Food (carbs first, then eggs/banana/broth) | HELPS | B vitamins, potassium, cysteine, sodium |
| Light walk, daylight | HELPS (mild) | mood, GI motility; intense exercise backfires |
| Ibuprofen with food, after fluids | HELPS headache | stomach risk if gastritis active |
| Coffee after rehydration | OK | not before; not instead of water |
| More alcohol | BACKFIRES | re-pauses methanol clearance; dependence risk |
| Acetaminophen | AVOID | CYP2E1 competition → NAPQI, hepatotoxic load |
| "Sweating it out" | BACKFIRES | adds loss to a deficit |
| IV fluids | Only if can't keep fluids down (ER) | not a shortcut for able drinkers |
| Hangover pills/supplements | INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE | no RCT support worth the price |

## Congener table (per typical serving)

| Drink | Congeners (0–5) | Ethanol g |
|---|---|---|
| Vodka, seltzer, light beer | 0 | 14/14/11 |
| Gin, champagne | 1 | 14/12 |
| Beer, shot (mixed) | 1–2 | 14 |
| White wine | 2 | 14 |
| Whiskey, rum | 3 | 14 |
| Brandy, fortified wine | 4 | 14/20 |
| Craft beer (high-esters) | 2 | 20 |

Dark-spirit hangovers are worse per gram of ethanol — controlled
trials (most cited: Rohsenow et al., bourbon vs vodka) show more
intensity with high-congener drinks at equal BAC.

## Units used by the script

- US standard drink = 14 g ethanol (a 355 mL 5% beer, 150 mL 12%
  wine, 44 mL 40% spirit)
- Widmark r: 0.68 male / 0.55 female (body water fraction)
- Elimination: 0.015 %BAC/h mean (0.010–0.020 range; zero-order
  kinetics — same rate regardless of concentration)

## When this tool is used weekly

One screening question worth asking aloud (AUDIT-C's cousin):
"How many times in the past year did drinking keep you from
obligations?" If the honest answer is ≥1, the recovery math is
treating symptoms of something the math can't fix — a doctor
conversation has far better tooling. Harm reduction includes
harm-pattern reduction.

## Red flags repeated (the ER list)

- Vomiting >6 h, or no fluids kept down >12 h
- Confusion, slow/odd responses, seizure
- Cold/clammy skin with fast heartbeat that doesn't settle
- Blood or coffee-grounds vomit
- Crushing chest/abdominal pain
- Witnessed breathing pauses → recovery position, emergency call
