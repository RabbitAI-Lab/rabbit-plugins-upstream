# Interpreting Home BP: Thresholds, Patterns, Prognosis

## Why Home Thresholds Differ from Office Thresholds

Home readings average 3-5 mmHg lower systolic than office readings (no alerting reaction,
familiar environment, repeated measurement). Guidelines therefore use setting-specific triggers:

| Category | Office | Home (self) | Daytime ambulatory |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | <120/80 | <120/80 (AHA) / <130/80 (ESH) | <130/80 |
| Elevated / high-normal | 120-139 / 80-89 | 120-129 / <80 (AHA) | 130-139/80-89 |
| Hypertension | ≥140/90 | ≥135/85 | ≥135/85 |
| Stage 2 / severe | ≥160/100 | ≥150/95 | ≥160/100 |
| Crisis (with symptoms) | ≥180/120 | ≥180/120 | ≥180/120 |

ACC/AHA 2017 lowered the office definition of hypertension to ≥130/80 (Stage 1) while Europe
(ESC/ESH 2023) retains ≥140/90. The tool prints the AHA category and the 135/85 home trigger —
the operational overlap between the systems.

## The Four Patterns (office × home)

| Office | Home | Pattern | Prevalence / risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| High | High | Sustained hypertension | the classic; full risk |
| High | Normal | White-coat hypertension | ~15-30% of "hypertensives"; intermediate risk — needs monitoring, not dismissal |
| Normal | High | Masked hypertension | ~10-15% of "normotensives"; risk approaches sustained hypertension |
| Normal | Normal | True normotension | reference |

Why masking happens: young stressed professionals (daytime work strain), smokers, and those on
"white-coat-free but job-high" patterns read normal in the calm office. The only detector is a
home log — which is exactly what this skill formalizes.

## Prognosis Framing (for user education)

- Each 20/10 mmHg increase in usual BP roughly doubles death from heart disease, stroke, and
  other vascular causes (Lancet prognosis studies framing).
- Home BP predicts outcomes at least as well as office BP; some analyses better (more readings,
  no alerting noise).
- Masked hypertension: target-organ damage rates similar to sustained hypertension.
- White-coat: lower risk than sustained, higher than true normotension — follow-up monitoring
  advised rather than reassurance alone.

## How Physicians Use a Home Log

Expect a clinician to want: ≥12 readings over ≥6-7 days (day 1 excluded), correct-technique
attestation, device validation status, and the morning/evening split. This tool's report is
formatted to hand over: the averaging note, the average ± SD, the pattern vs the clinic value,
and crisis-range callouts. Bring the monitor to the appointment for a side-by-side check.

## When Readings Warrent Same-Day Action

- ≥180/120 WITH symptoms (chest pain, dyspnea, neuro deficit, vision change, severe headache):
  emergency services — hypertensive emergency until proven otherwise.
- ≥180/120 without symptoms, twice, 15 min apart, after quiet rest: urgent same-day contact
  (hypertensive urgency territory) — do not self-titrate medication.
- Sudden drop with dizziness/ fainting: also same-day contact (over-treatment or other cause).

## Special Populations, Briefly

- **Diabetes/CKD**: treatment targets individualized; home logs drive decisions even harder.
- **Pregnancy**: thresholds and escalation differ (≥140/90 home = call the OB team same day);
  the tool's generic output should not be used in pregnancy without clinical framing.
- **Elderly with stiff vessels**: oscillometric cuffs may overread vs intra-arterial; if
  numbers disagree with symptoms, get auscultation.
- **Children**: separate percentile-based norms — this tool is adults-only.
