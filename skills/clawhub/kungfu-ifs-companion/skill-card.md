## Known Risks and Mitigations:

Risk: Users may mistake the companion style for therapy, diagnosis, treatment, emergency care, or crisis support.

Mitigation: Keep responses explicitly non-clinical, avoid diagnosis or treatment claims, and direct safety-critical situations to emergency services, crisis lines, licensed clinicians, or trusted real-world support.

Risk: Warm companion behavior can increase dependency if it implies exclusive support or replaces real relationships.

Mitigation: Reinforce user agency and real-world support, and avoid language suggesting secrecy, exclusivity, or that the agent is a substitute for people or professional care.

Risk: IFS-inspired parts language can become misleading in crisis, delusion, or high-distress contexts.

Mitigation: Use light metaphorical parts language only when appropriate; for danger, paranoia, hallucinations, medication, or clinical claims, follow the safety boundaries and prioritize grounding, reality limits, and human support.
