# Desk Ergonomics Guide

## Workstation Setup Checklist

### Chair
- [ ] Height: Feet flat on floor, knees at ~90°
- [ ] Seat depth: 2-4 fingers between seat edge and back of knee
- [ ] Backrest: Supports natural lumbar curve
- [ ] Armrests: Elbows at 90°, shoulders relaxed
- [ ] Tilt: Slight backward lean (100-110°) for reading; upright for typing

### Desk
- [ ] Height: Forearms parallel to floor when typing
- [ ] Clearance: Knees and thighs fit comfortably underneath
- [ ] Surface: Non-reflective to reduce glare
- [ ] Depth: Monitor at arm's length (20-26 inches from eyes)

### Monitor
- [ ] Height: Top of screen at or slightly below eye level
- [ ] Distance: Arm's length (20-26 inches)
- [ ] Angle: Tilted slightly back (10-20°)
- [ ] Position: Directly in front, no neck twisting
- [ ] Dual monitors: Primary screen directly ahead, secondary at angle

### Keyboard & Mouse
- [ ] Keyboard: At elbow height, wrists neutral
- [ ] Mouse: Next to keyboard, same height
- [ ] Wrists: Straight, not bent up/down/sideways
- [ ] Hands: Float over keyboard, don't rest wrists while typing

### Lighting
- [ ] Natural light from the side (not directly behind or in front)
- [ ] Task lighting for document reading
- [ ] Monitor brightness: Similar to room brightness
- [ ] Reduce glare: Anti-glare screen, window shades

### Phone
- [ ] Headset or speakerphone for long calls
- [ ] Don't cradle phone between ear and shoulder
- [ ] Screen at face height to avoid "text neck"

## Laptop-Specific Adjustments

Laptops are ergonomically terrible by design (screen and keyboard are connected).

### Quick Fix
- Use an external keyboard and mouse
- Put laptop on a riser/stand so screen is at eye level

### Standing Desk Conversion
- Use a laptop stand on a standing-height surface
- External keyboard/mouse on lower surface
- Alternate sitting and standing every 30-60 minutes

## Ergonomic Equipment Recommendations

### Essential
1. **Adjustable chair** with lumbar support (Herman Miller, Steelcase, IKEA Markus)
2. **Monitor stand or arm** to achieve proper height
3. **External keyboard and mouse** (especially for laptop users)

### Recommended
4. **Standing desk converter** (Varidesk, Fully Jarvis)
5. **Footrest** if feet don't reach floor
6. **Wrist rest** for keyboard/mouse (use between typing, not during)

### Optional
7. **Ergonomic keyboard** (split: Kinesis, Microsoft Sculpt)
8. **Vertical mouse** (Logitech MX Vertical) for wrist comfort
9. **Monitor light bar** (BenQ ScreenBar) for task lighting
10. **Anti-fatigue mat** for standing desk use

## Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem: Neck pain
**Likely cause**: Monitor too low, looking down
**Fix**: Raise monitor to eye level. If laptop, use stand + external keyboard.

### Problem: Lower back pain
**Likely cause**: Poor lumbar support, sitting too long
**Fix**: Lumbar cushion or ergonomic chair. Stand every 30 min.

### Problem: Wrist/hand pain
**Likely cause**: Wrists bent during typing/mousing
**Fix**: Lower keyboard, use wrist rest between (not during) typing. Try split keyboard.

### Problem: Shoulder tension
**Likely cause**: Mouse too far away, reaching
**Fix**: Move mouse closer. Check armrest height.

### Problem: Eye strain
**Likely cause**: Monitor too bright, glare, too close
**Fix**: 20-20-20 rule (every 20 min, look 20 ft away for 20 sec). Adjust brightness.

### Problem: Hip/leg numbness
**Likely cause**: Chair pressing on thighs, sitting too long
**Fix**: Adjust chair height/depth. Stand and walk regularly.

## Standing Desk Guidelines

### Optimal Standing Setup
- Desk at elbow height (forearms parallel to floor)
- Weight evenly distributed on both feet
- Knees slightly bent (not locked)
- Monitor at eye level (same as sitting)
- Anti-fatigue mat

### Sit-Stand Ratio
- **Beginner**: 1:1 (sit 30, stand 30)
- **Experienced**: 1:2 (sit 20, stand 40)
- **Maximum**: Don't stand more than 4 hours total per day
- **Key**: Transition frequently — the best posture is the NEXT posture

### Standing Desk Pitfalls
- Standing all day is as bad as sitting all day
- Don't wear high heels or hard-soled shoes
- Don't lock your knees
- Don't lean on the desk (shifts weight forward)

## Quick Daily Routine

### Morning Setup (2 minutes)
1. Adjust chair height
2. Position monitor
3. Organize desk surface
4. Do 5 chin tucks and 5 shoulder rolls

### Every 30 Minutes
1. Check posture (ears over shoulders?)
2. Do 3 shoulder blade squeezes
3. Look 20 feet away for 20 seconds
4. Stand if you've been sitting

### Every 2 Hours
1. Full standing break (2 min walk)
2. Doorway chest stretch
3. Hip flexor stretch
4. Drink water

### End of Day
1. Log your posture score
2. Do a full-body stretch (5 min)
3. Review your daily pattern
