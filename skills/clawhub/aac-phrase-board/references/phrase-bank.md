# Phrase bank

Phrases that survive across situations, plus what has already been built.

## Core phrases

Put these in almost every board, in this order. They carry the parts of a
conversation that are not about the errand.

| Phrase | Why it is core |
|---|---|
| Hello. I can't speak. I use this phone. | Sets the mode before anything else. Without it the other person talks over the phone. |
| Please give me a moment to type. | Buys the ten seconds the board cannot. |
| Say that again, please. | The single most used line in practice. |
| Slower, please. | Different from the above — the words were heard, the speed was not. |
| Please write it down. | Works when speech is fast, accented, or in a noisy room. |
| I understand. | Lets the other person stop repeating and move on. |
| Thank you. That's all. | Ends the exchange cleanly. |

## Situations already built

Keep this list short. Add a row only after a board has been used.

| Situation | Notes worth reusing |
|---|---|
| Pharmacy counter | Ask for the medicine by name before describing symptoms; the name is faster than the description. |
| Clinic reception | Bring the appointment time and the doctor's name as their own phrases — reception asks for both immediately. |
| Cafe | Size and milk come before the drink name in some languages; order the phrases the way the cashier asks. |

## Words that break speech synthesis

Avoid these in a phrase that will be spoken aloud:

- Abbreviations — `Dr`, `appt`, `min`, `approx`. Spell them out.
- Slashes and ampersands — `and/or`, `R&D`. Split into two phrases.
- Digits under ten — write `two`, not `2`. Some voices read `2` as `to`.
- Brackets and notes — a synthesiser reads them out or swallows the sentence.
- All caps for emphasis — many voices spell out short all-caps words.
