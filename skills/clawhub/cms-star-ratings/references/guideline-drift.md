# Reviewing Apparent Differences Between Clinical Guidelines and Quality Measures

Clinical guidelines and quality measures have different purposes and update on different schedules. An apparent mismatch requires **source verification** before it is described as a confirmed conflict.

## Review questions
1. Which clinical guideline is being referenced, and what is its publication date/version?
2. Which CMS or MIPS measure is being compared, and what is the exact specification year/version?
3. Are the populations, indications, interventions, and outcomes truly comparable?
4. Is the difference clinical, methodological, or just a reporting-definition difference?
5. Does patient-specific clinical judgment change the interpretation?

## How to report an apparent difference
Summarize the two sources in plain language, include publication or specification dates, state whether the comparison is confirmed or preliminary, and identify who should review it. Keep clinical appropriateness separate from measure performance.

## Sources to consult
- Current CMS Star Ratings Technical Notes and measure documents.
- Applicable MIPS quality measure specifications: https://qpp.cms.gov/
- Original professional-society clinical guidelines (such as ACC/AHA, ADA, KDIGO, AGS, or USPSTF guidance, depending on the topic).
- FDA labeling and safety communications when relevant.

This public reference does not supply proprietary detection rules, scoring schemes, a clinical conflict registry, or automated treatment recommendations.
