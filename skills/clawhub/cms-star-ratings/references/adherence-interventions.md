# Medication Adherence Review: Clinical Context and Barriers

Pharmacy claims may suggest medication access or supply gaps, but they cannot establish whether the prescription is still appropriate, taken as directed, or intentionally discontinued.

## Before discussing adherence
- Verify the applicable measure specification, eligibility, and pharmacy claims data.
- Reconcile the active medication list against the patient's report and the prescriber's plan.
- Ask whether therapy was intentionally stopped, changed, held, or replaced.
- Review possible adverse effects, affordability, access, complexity, health literacy, and patient preferences.
- Consider clinical context such as recent hospitalization, renal function, hypoglycemia risk, and competing treatment goals when relevant.

## Patient-centered questions
- "What gets in the way of taking or obtaining this medication?"
- "Has anyone asked you to hold, stop, or change it?"
- "Have you noticed side effects or unexpected costs?"
- "Would refill synchronization, delivery, or a simpler schedule help?"

## Possible supports for clinician/team discussion
Affordability assistance, refill coordination, adherence aids, patient education, pharmacist consultation, prescriber follow-up, or a comprehensive medication review may be appropriate **after** confirming the clinical situation.

## Safety rules
- Do not push adherence to a medication that was appropriately discontinued or is creating harm.
- Do not assume that filling a prescription confirms ingestion or effective therapy.
- Do not claim that a single fill automatically satisfies a separate adherence measure's denominator requirements.
- Do not use a fixed numerical ranking from this file to automate patient-level clinical prioritization.
- Document uncertainty and refer medication changes to an authorized prescriber.

Use the current CMS/PQA measure specifications for all formal PDC calculations and reporting decisions.
