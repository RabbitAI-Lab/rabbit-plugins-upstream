# Anti-Patterns And Quality Bar

Use this before finalizing or reviewing a presentation.

## Core Anti-Patterns

Do not produce:

- A deck with animated slide transitions.
- Title plus bullet walls.
- Repeated fades as the main motion language.
- Card grids posing as scenes.
- Dense SaaS dashboard layouts unless operational monitoring is the subject.
- Gratuitous glassmorphism.
- Purple-blue gradient panels as a default visual identity.
- Random particles, blobs, parallax, or spinning 3D objects without explanatory purpose.
- A static report converted into screens.
- Decorative scroll effects that do not advance the argument.
- Tiny unreadable technical diagrams.
- Text that explains what the visual should have demonstrated.

## Quality Bar

A successful immersive presentation:

- Has a clear central tension.
- Opens with a visual hook.
- Uses scenes with narrative purpose.
- Makes at least one concept visible through transformation.
- Keeps key objects persistent across scenes.
- Uses camera movement intentionally.
- Gives the presenter control.
- Supports keyboard and reduced-motion access.
- Works on realistic desktop and mobile sizes.
- Has a final memory image or decision framework.

## Design Review Questions

Ask:

- Could this be replaced by a normal slide without losing anything? If yes, redesign the scene.
- Does the motion teach anything?
- What does the audience know after this scene that they did not know before?
- Which object carries continuity?
- Where is the audience's eye supposed to go first?
- Can the presenter recover if they jump backward?
- Does reduced motion preserve the idea?
- Are the labels readable from the back of a room?

## Text Rules

Use text for:

- Short claims.
- Labels.
- Captions.
- Speaker prompts.
- Data annotations.
- Decision criteria.

Avoid:

- Paragraphs as the primary scene.
- More than 3-5 visible bullet-like lines in a scene.
- Repeating the speaker notes on screen.

## Final Acceptance

Before considering the work done, the output must answer:

```text
What is the story?
What are the scenes?
What persists?
What transforms?
What can the audience interact with?
How does the presenter control it?
How does it remain accessible and responsive?
Where should gsap-motion take over?
```
