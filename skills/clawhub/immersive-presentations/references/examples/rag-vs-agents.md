# Example: RAG vs Agents

## Brief

Audience: product directors and technical leaders.

Goal: decide when to use RAG, agents, or a hybrid architecture.

Tone: cinematic, practical, skeptical of hype.

## Narrative Promise

By the end, leaders will know how to choose between retrieval, agents, and hybrids because the presentation makes information access, planning, action, risk, and control visible.

## Central Tension

Search can find knowledge, but business outcomes often require action. Autonomy can act, but action increases risk.

## Visual System

The world is a command center. Documents, tools, people, and tasks are persistent objects. The camera moves from messy information space to retrieval beam to agent workspace to governance control tower.

## Acts And Scenes

### Act 1: The Information Flood

S01 - Document Storm

Documents appear until the room becomes unusable. A question enters and fails to find a path.

S02 - Retrieval Beam

The query becomes a beam that embeds, searches, ranks, and pulls relevant evidence into a context tray.

### Act 2: From Answer To Action

S03 - RAG Works When The Job Is Evidence

The retrieved evidence assembles into a cited answer. The camera highlights grounding and traceability.

S04 - The Moment RAG Stops

The answer reaches a locked door labeled "action." The system can advise but cannot complete the workflow.

S05 - Agent Workspace

Planner, tools, memory, evaluator, and human checkpoint appear around the same question. The object persists, but its role changes from search request to task goal.

S06 - Failure Mode

The agent loops, calls the wrong tool, or acts without enough evidence. Guardrails and human approval become visible as brakes and checkpoints.

### Act 3: Decision Framework

S07 - Architecture Map

Use an interactive map with axes: evidence need, action complexity, risk, reversibility, and human oversight.

S08 - Hybrid Pattern

RAG becomes the evidence layer inside an agentic workflow. Persistent documents feed a planner and evaluator.

S09 - Memory Image

The final command center shows three labeled zones: find, decide, act. The takeaway: use RAG for grounded knowledge, agents for controlled action, hybrids for governed workflows.

## Interaction

- Toggle scenario cards into the architecture map.
- Scrub a request through RAG and agent paths.
- Adjust risk and autonomy sliders to see recommended control patterns.
