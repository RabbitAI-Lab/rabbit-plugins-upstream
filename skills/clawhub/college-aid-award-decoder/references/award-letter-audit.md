# Award Letter Audit Reference

## Why award letters mislead
A financial aid award letter is a **marketing document written by the school that benefits from you believing it**. Since 2024-25, federal law requires FAFSA-offering schools to provide a standardized Aid Summary (COA, grant vs loan breakdown, renewal conditions) — but compliance is uneven, the template has weak enforcement, and schools using only the CSS Profile or institutional aid have more latitude. Treat every letter as unaudited.

## The four money classes (the only mental model that matters)

| Class | What it is | Examples | Reality check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gift aid | Free money | Pell Grant, SEOG, state grants, merit/tuition scholarships | Renewable? GPA floor? % receiving it after year 1? |
| Work-study | A job, capped | Federal work-study, campus employment | ~$1,800-2,500/yr typical; taxed; NOT applied to the bill automatically |
| Federal student loans | Student repays | Direct Sub (in-school interest paid by ED), Direct Unsub (accrues now) | Annual caps: yr1 $5,500, yr2 $6,500, yr3+ $7,500 (combined sub+unsub) |
| Parent/private loans | Parents/credit repay | Parent PLUS (~9%, 4.2% origination), private, institutional | Excluded from honest comparisons entirely |

## Net Price — the only cross-school number
```
Net Price = Cost of Attendance − Gift Aid
Gap        = Net Price − (family contribution from SAI + work + student loans you accept)
```
COA must include: tuition + mandatory fees + housing + meals + books/supplies + transport + personal. If the letter shows only "direct costs" (billed by school), the comparison is rigged against commuter-heavy schools; rebuild COA consistently for all schools.

## Front-loaded grants — the sophomore cliff
Common patterns to verify in writing BEFORE depositing:
- Merit scholarship "renewable up to 8 semesters, minimum 3.0 GPA" — what % keep it? (Ask the admissions office directly; many schools know and must tell you.)
- One-time grants ($3,000 "first-year grant") explicitly stated in the name or buried in terms.
- Tuition-flat guarantees vs fee escalation: fees routinely rise 3-6%/yr even at tuition-freeze schools.

## 4-year projection rules (conservative defaults)
- Tuition/COA inflation: 3%/yr (5% at high-tuition privates).
- Grant decay: assume 90% of year-1 grant aid in years 2-4 unless renewal is contractual.
- Unsubsidized interest: accrues from disbursement; at $2,000/yr over 4 years that's ~$700-900 capitalized at graduation (the script models cumulative-balance accrual).
- Standard repayment: 10 years, fixed. Income-driven plans lower the payment but raise total interest — student-loan-strategist handles post-graduation strategy.

## The appeal (Professional Judgment / Special Circumstances)
Financial aid administrators have statutory authority (HEA Sec. 428A; 34 CFR 673.4) to adjust data elements when documented circumstances changed: job loss/income reduction, divorce/separation, medical/dental not covered by insurance, K-12 private tuition supporting family, death of parent, natural disaster. Process:
1. Call the aid office; ask for their "special circumstances / PJ review form" (names vary).
2. Submit letter + third-party documentation (termination letter, medical bills, divorce decree).
3. Ask explicitly: "recalculate SAI and reconsider GRANT aid, not just additional loans."
4. Timing: appeal AFTER receiving the initial award; repeat per aid year if circumstances persist.
Results: thousands of dollars in added Pell/state/institutional grants are common for genuine income drops — aid offices expect these requests, and most families never make them.

## Verification questions to ask each school (verbatim)
1. "What percentage of students retaining the [X] scholarship after freshman year?"
2. "Is this award renewable for all four years, and at what GPA?"
3. "What was your average COA increase the last three years?"
4. "If I win an outside scholarship, does it reduce my grant aid?" (scholarship displacement — some schools claw back grants dollar-for-dollar)
5. "What is the 4-year graduation rate?" (a 5th year is often a $28k-60k decision by itself)
