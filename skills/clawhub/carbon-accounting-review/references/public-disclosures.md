# Public-disclosure review

1. Record company/report selection, publication vintage and the years in its tables. Use official report sources chosen or authorized by the user. A single report containing historical years is not independent evidence from several annual publications. Done: source URL or supplied file, edition, page/table locators and retrieval date are attributable.
2. Transcribe only the necessary facts. Check visually ambiguous tables/footnotes when supported; otherwise flag extraction uncertainty. Preserve group/parent boundaries, units, periods and Scope 2 alternatives. Do not treat unspecified rounding, restatements or methodology as known. Done: values and unknowns have traceable locators.
3. Mark aggregates as reported assertions. Do not invent activity records, factors, calculation lineage or review sign-off. A company's assurance statement is an attributed source claim, not the reviewing agent's opinion. Done: independently checked facts and unavailable upstream evidence are separate.
4. Compare periods only on a disclosed basis. A reduction in reported emissions does not prove operational abatement; acquisitions, boundary changes, factors and restatements may matter. Do not rank companies without defensible comparable methods. Done: comparisons state scope and unknown causal attribution.
5. Deliver only the requested report/table. If assessing an existing software pipeline, inspect only available authorized stages; do not build or install a graph, parser or application implicitly. Test extraction/reproduction with independent expected facts where available; do not claim generic PDF coverage from one example. Done: actual checked outputs and limitations are listed together.
