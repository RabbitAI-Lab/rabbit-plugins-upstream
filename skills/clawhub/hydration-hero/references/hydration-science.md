# Hydration Science

## Why Water Matters

The human body is approximately 60% water. Every system depends on it:

| Function | Role of Water |
|----------|--------------|
| Brain | Cerebrospinal fluid, neurotransmitter transport, temperature regulation |
| Blood | Plasma is 90% water; carries oxygen, nutrients, hormones |
| Digestion | Saliva, gastric juices, nutrient absorption, waste elimination |
| Joints | Synovial fluid lubrication |
| Skin | Elasticity, temperature regulation through sweating |
| Kidneys | Filtration of toxins, waste removal |
| Metabolism | Chemical reactions, energy production |

## Dehydration Effects by Percentage Body Weight Loss

| % Body Weight Lost | Effects |
|--------------------|---------|
| 0-1% | Normal — no symptoms |
| 1-2% | Thirst onset, mild cognitive impairment, reduced mood |
| 2-3% | Headache, fatigue, decreased alertness, dry mouth |
| 3-4% | Reduced physical performance (10%+), increased heart rate |
| 4-5% | Difficulty concentrating, irritability, muscle cramps |
| 5-6% | Heat exhaustion risk, sleepiness, tingling |
| 6-8% | Dizziness, weakness, confusion |
| 8-10%| Heat stroke risk, organ stress |
| 10%+ | Medical emergency — unconsciousness, organ failure |

## Daily Water Requirement Calculation

### Formula (used by this tool)
```
Base = Body weight (kg) × 35 ml/day
```

This is derived from:
- European Food Safety Authority (EFSA): 2.5L/day for men, 2.0L/day for women
- U.S. National Academies: 3.7L/day for men, 2.7L/day for women (from all sources)
- Institute of Medicine: 1 ml water per 1 calorie of energy expenditure
  (average adult ~2000-2500 kcal → 2.0-2.5L from beverages)

### Factors That Increase Requirements

#### Exercise
| Intensity | Duration | Additional Water |
|-----------|----------|-----------------|
| Light (walking) | 30 min | +350 ml |
| Moderate (jogging, cycling) | 30 min | +500 ml |
| Intense (running, HIIT) | 30 min | +700 ml |
| Endurance (marathon) | 1+ hour | +800-1000 ml/hr |

#### Weather
| Condition | Additional Water |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Hot (>25°C) | +500 ml per 5°C above 20°C |
| Very hot (>35°C) | +1000 ml minimum |
| High humidity (>70%) | +300 ml (reduced sweating efficiency) |
| Dry climate (<30% humidity) | +200 ml (increased insensible loss) |
| Cold (<5°C) | +200 ml (respiratory loss in dry cold air) |
| Altitude (>2500m) | +500 ml (dry air, increased respiration) |

#### Caffeine
- **Mild diuretic effect**: increases urine output by 50-100ml per cup
- **Compensatory water**: +150ml per cup of coffee/strong tea
- **Tolerance**: Regular caffeine consumers experience reduced diuretic effect
- **Note**: Energy drinks with high caffeine need even more compensation

#### Alcohol
- **Strong diuretic**: suppresses vasopressin (ADH), causing excessive urination
- **Compensatory water**: +400ml per standard drink
  - 1 standard drink = 12oz beer / 5oz wine / 1.5oz spirits
- **Post-drinking**: Drink 500ml water before bed after any alcohol

#### Health Conditions
| Condition | Additional Water |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Fever | +200ml per 1°C above normal |
| Diarrhea/vomiting | +500-1000ml per episode |
| Pregnancy | +300ml/day |
| Nursing (breastfeeding) | +700ml/day |
| Kidney stones | +500ml above normal target |
| Urinary tract infection | +500ml |

## Signs of Adequate Hydration

1. **Urine color**: pale yellow (like lemonade), not dark yellow or clear
2. **Frequency**: urinating every 2-4 hours during waking time
3. **Thirst**: rarely experiencing strong thirst
4. **Energy**: no mid-afternoon fatigue crashes
5. **Skin**: elastic, not dry or tight
6. **Headache-free**: no dehydration headaches

## Urine Color Chart

| Color | Level | Status | Action |
|-------|-------|--------|--------|
| Very pale / nearly clear | 1 | Overhydrated | Reduce intake slightly |
| Pale yellow | 2 | Well hydrated | ✅ Maintain current intake |
| Light yellow | 3 | Well hydrated | ✅ Good |
| Yellow | 4 | Adequately hydrated | 🟡 Drink a glass |
| Dark yellow | 5 | Mildly dehydrated | 🟡 Drink 2 glasses |
| Amber/honey | 6 | Dehydrated | 🔴 Drink immediately |
| Brown/cola | 7 | Severely dehydrated | ⚠️ Medical attention |
| Pink/red (not food-related) | — | Possible blood | ⚠️ See a doctor |

## Hydration Myths

### Myth 1: "8 glasses a day"
**Reality**: There's no scientific basis for exactly 8 glasses. Your needs
depend on body size, activity, climate, and individual factors.

### Myth 2: "Coffee dehydrates you"
**Reality**: Caffeine has a mild diuretic effect in non-habitual consumers,
but regular coffee drinkers build tolerance. The water in coffee still counts
toward hydration.

### Myth 3: "You need sports drinks"
**Reality**: Only needed for exercise lasting >1 hour, especially in heat.
Water is sufficient for daily hydration and most exercise.

### Myth 4: "If you're thirsty, you're already dehydrated"
**Reality**: Thirst is an early warning system that activates at ~1-2%
dehydration. Responding to thirst is adequate for most people. However,
proactively drinking is better for optimal performance.

### Myth 5: "Clear urine is the goal"
**Reality**: Completely clear urine may mean overhydration, which can lead
to hyponatremia (dangerously low sodium). Pale yellow is the target.
