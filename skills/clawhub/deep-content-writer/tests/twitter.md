The Curse of Knowledge is the single biggest reason you're bad at explaining things.

And you don't even know you have it.

Here's how to break free 👇

2/10
What is the Curse of Knowledge?

Once you know something, you can't imagine what it's like NOT to know it.

Your brain has been "opened" — and you've forgotten what "closed" feels like.

This destroys communication.

3/10
The classic experiment:

In 1990, Elizabeth Newton asked people to tap a song's rhythm on a table.

"Tappers" predicted listeners would guess correctly ~50% of the time.

The actual result: 2.5%.

Why? Tappers heard the full song in their head. Listeners heard "tap tap tap."

You're the tapper. Every. Single. Day.

4/10
Where it shows up at work:

Engineer: "We need to rearchitect the message queue to handle backpressure."

Product manager: "So... two days?"

The engineer sees the whole system. The PM sees a button.

Neither is wrong. Both are frustrated.

5/10
Three signs you're under the Curse:

• People's eyes glaze over when you talk
• You get asked "what does that mean?" constantly
• You find yourself saying "it's obvious" or "basically"

All three mean: you're not explaining — you're performing.

6/10
How to break it (4 techniques that actually work):

① Use analogies, not definitions

❌ "An API defines interfaces for component communication"
✅ "An API is a restaurant waiter — you order, they fetch, they serve"

② Start with WHY, not WHAT

People need to know WHY it matters before they can process WHAT it is.

7/10
③ Lead with a story, not an abstraction

❌ "Microservices are a distributed architecture pattern..."
✅ "Imagine an e-commerce site. Search is one team. Checkout is another. Payment is a third. Each does one thing well."

④ Make them repeat it back

"Can you explain that in your own words?"
They can't? You didn't explain well enough.

8/10
The best communicators aren't the smartest people in the room.

They're the ones who can cross the bridge back to "not knowing."

Einstein (allegedly) said it best:
"If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it well enough."

9/10
A quick checklist before your next explanation:

□ What does my audience already know?
□ What's the ONE thing they care about?
□ Can I tell this as a story?
□ Did I start with "why"?
□ Can they repeat it back?

If any answer is no — rewrite.

10/10
The Curse of Knowledge isn't a sign of ignorance.

It's a side effect of expertise.

The goal isn't to know less. It's to be able to step back into the shoes of someone who doesn't know.

That skill? That's real mastery.
