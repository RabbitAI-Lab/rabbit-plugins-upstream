# Resume Interview Prompts

## Headline

Interview for One Job, Then Draft a Resume From Confirmed Numbers Only, in Plain Words

## Hook

Give your AI agent one job description. Answer one question at a time. Get a one page resume draft, made from your real answers only, ready to review.

## Pain, in plain words

Most resumes start with a blank page. People copy old lines, guess at numbers, or use fancy words that do not sound like them. A recruiter reads it and cannot tell what the person really did. The fix is not a longer resume. The fix is a short interview first, one question at a time, then a draft that uses only the answers the person confirmed.

## What you get

An interview for one job, then a resume draft, made for that job only. The interview asks about skills, strengths, and experience for one job description. The draft uses confirmed numbers only. It gives you a draft resume to review, never a sent resume by itself.

## Who this is for

People applying for a job, who want a clear resume in plain words, for any job. People changing jobs, who need to show what they really did, with real numbers. People who want help to write, but want every word checked before it is used.

## Why this saves money

A clear resume saves time for the person who applies, and for the recruiter who reads it. A short interview stops the writer from guessing, adding a number that is not real, or using words that do not match the job.

## Technical, for coders

Structured interview and draft resume for one job description: interview questions, a numbers confirmation check, a job match check, a humanize pass, and a one page resume draft format, ready to brief any AI agent, or a human writer. No guarantee of an interview, a job, or a reply, and no claim that a draft alone gets a job. The draft is for owner review, not a sent application.

## Purpose

Interview the user for one job, then draft a resume from confirmed answers only. The skill asks first, then drafts. It does not send a resume, apply for a job, contact a recruiter, or publish anything by itself.

## How to use it

Fast path: If the user already gives the job description, an old resume, and numbers in the first message, do NOT force all 12 questions one at a time. Ask only the missing answers, in one short list, then in the SAME reply do the numbers check and the full resume draft. If the user answers one question at a time, after the last answer, always finish with the numbers check and the full resume draft in that same reply, never stop at the questions alone.

Step 1: ask the user these questions, one at a time, in plain words.
Step 2: run the numbers check, and list what is confirmed, missing, or not confirmed.
Step 3: match the answers to the job description, and list what fits and what is missing.
Step 4: do the humanize pass, in plain words.
Step 5: write the resume draft, labelled DRAFT, NOT SENT, with owner approval needed.
Step 6: run the missing checks at the end, and list what is complete, missing, or not confirmed.

Ask these questions first, one at a time:
1. What job are you applying for, and paste the job description, in plain words?
2. What is your name, and how should a recruiter contact you, by phone or email?
3. What is your current or last job title, and where did you work?
4. What did you do in that job, day to day, in plain words?
5. What are your top 3 skills for this job, with one real example for each skill?
6. What are your strengths, in plain words, with one real example?
7. What is one result you are proud of, with a number, and how was that number measured?
8. What is a second result, with a number, and how was that number measured?
9. What is a third result, with a number, or say none?
10. What education, course, or certificate do you have that fits this job?
11. What tools, languages, or systems do you use for this work?
12. What is missing for this job, and is that Not confirmed?

If the user does not know an answer, mark it Not confirmed, and use the most common answer for that job type, and say so, marked Suggested, not confirmed. Do not invent an answer. If the user says use the old resume for a question, use only what is in that old resume, and say so.

## The 3 steps, in plain words

### Step 1: Interview
Ask the 12 questions above, one at a time, for one job description only. Listen for skills, strengths, and experience that match that job. Write down each answer in the user's own words, where you can. Do not add a skill the user did not say. Do not add a job the user did not say.

### Step 2: Quantify, honestly
For every result, ask: what is the number, how was it measured, and who can confirm it? A number is real only if the user confirms it in the interview. A number with no source is Not confirmed, and must not go in the resume. A guess is not a number. An estimate must be marked Estimate, not confirmed, and the user must approve it before it is used.

### Step 3: Humanize
Rewrite the draft in plain words, in the user's own voice. Use short sentences. Use simple words. Avoid fancy words. Avoid repeating the job description word for word. Avoid claiming what the user did not do. Read each line out loud in your head. If a line sounds like a robot wrote it, rewrite it in the user's own words. If a line is not clear to a non technical reader, rewrite it.

## Numbers check

After the interview, run this check, and show it to the user before the resume draft:
- Number: [the number, in plain words]
- Where it came from: [the job, the work, or the user answer]
- Confirmed: [Yes, confirmed by the user, or No, Not confirmed]
- Use in resume: [Yes, only if confirmed, or No, remove it, or mark it Not confirmed]

Use a number in the resume only if the user confirmed it. If a number is Not confirmed, remove it from the draft, or leave it out and list it under Missing numbers. Never guess a number to fill a gap.

## Job match check

After the interview, match the answers to the job description, in plain words:
- Skill the job asks for: [skill, in plain words]
- Your evidence: [example from the interview, or Not confirmed]
- Match: [Yes, with evidence, or No, missing]
List what fits, and list what is missing. Do not claim a skill with no evidence. If a skill is missing, say so, and do not hide it.

## Extra checks

These are not part of the resume draft above, but report them separately as Notes. Do not mix them into the draft score.
- Dates: list the start and end date for each job, or Not confirmed, in plain words.
- Contact: say if the phone and email are confirmed, or Not confirmed.
- Spelling: say if names, job titles, and company names are spelled as the user confirmed them.
- Gaps: say if there is a gap in work, and if the user wants to explain it, or Not confirmed.
- Length: say if the draft fits on one page, or if it is longer, and what to cut.

## Hard rules

- Never mark Pass without evidence.
- Never invent a number, a job, a date, an employer, a skill, a result, a certificate, or a testimonial.
- Never claim a result unless the user confirmed the number, and how it was measured.
- Never send a resume, apply for a job, contact a recruiter, or publish anything, without a separate approval from the owner.
- Never use a real person as an example. Examples in this skill are fictional, labelled Example only.
- Suggested items must be marked Suggested, not confirmed.
- A resume draft is not a promise of an interview, a job, or a reply.
- Keep the draft in plain, short sentences. The owner may not be a technical reader.

## Resume draft format

Always finish with this format, in this order, labelled DRAFT, NOT SENT, Owner approval needed:

### Resume draft: [name, or Example Candidate in a fictional example]
### Job: [job title from Question 1] | Contact: [phone or email, or Not confirmed]

**Verdict: Ready to review / Needs answers first / Not ready to review**

Use Ready to review only if the job description, the top skills with evidence, and every number in the draft are all confirmed. Otherwise use Needs answers first, or Not ready to review if a blocker was found.

**Summary, 2 lines only**

- [One line, in plain words, what the person does, with evidence]
- [One line, in plain words, what the person wants in this job]

**Top skills for this job**

| # | Skill the job asks for | Your evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [skill, in plain words] | [example from the interview, or Not confirmed] |
| 2 | [skill, in plain words] | [example from the interview, or Not confirmed] |
| 3 | [skill, in plain words] | [example from the interview, or Not confirmed] |

**Experience**

- [Job title, company, dates, or Not confirmed]
- [What you did, day to day, in plain words]
- [Result, with a confirmed number only, and how it was measured, or No number confirmed]

**Education and certificates**

- [Course, certificate, or education, or Not confirmed]

**Tools and languages**

- [Tools, languages, or systems, in plain words, or Not confirmed]

**Numbers confirmed**

- [Number, where it came from, confirmed Yes, or Not confirmed]

**What is missing**

- [For example: second result not confirmed, or a skill with no evidence]

**Owner steps, needs the client**

- [For example: confirm every number in the draft]
- [For example: approve the summary before the resume is sent]
- [For example: approve the final resume, and where to send it]

**Notes**

- [Any note from the Extra checks section, or None.]

**What was not sent, and why**

- [For example: no resume was sent, owner approval is needed.]

## Missing checks

After the draft, run these 8 checks, and list the result for each one:
1. Job description given, for one job?
2. Interview answers complete, all 12 questions, or marked Not confirmed?
3. Every number in the draft confirmed by the user, or removed?
4. Top skills matched to the job, with evidence?
5. Resume draft made, one page aim, labelled DRAFT, NOT SENT?
6. Humanize pass done, in plain words, in the user's own voice?
7. No invented number, job, date, employer, skill, result, or testimonial?
8. Owner reviewed the draft, or approval still needed?

List the results as Complete, Missing, or Not confirmed. Do not add a missing item without owner approval.
