# Toilet Triage Ranger — Reference

## Scale of the problem
Toilets are the #1 indoor water use in most homes (~25-30% of indoor
consumption). Leaking toilets are the #1 source of silent water waste:
US EPA WaterSense estimates household leaks waste ~3,700 L/month per
household on average, with running toilets the largest single
contributor; a badly stuck flapper can pass 9,000+ L/day alone. Water
utilities' "high bill" hotlines report running toilets as the most
common cause of bill shock — commonly 2-5× normal bills.

## How a toilet actually works (30-second mental model)
Two boxes, one passage:
- **Tank** (clean water storage above): flush = flapper drops open,
  6-13 L fall into the bowl through the rim jets and direct jet.
- **Bowl + trap**: the sudden influx fills the trap's siphon leg and
  STARTS A SIPHON — that's what pulls waste down. The siphon breaks
  with a gurgle when air enters; then the fill valve refills tank AND
  bowl (bowl via the refill tube into the overflow).

Everything that "runs" is one of three failures:
1. Flapper passes water → tank level drops → fill valve refills
   (phantom cycles) or never catches up (constant run).
2. Fill valve doesn't stop → water rises to overflow tube → trickles
   to bowl continuously.
3. Refill tube mispositioned → siphon loop through the overflow.

Weak flush = weak siphon START: insufficient drop speed (level low,
jets clogged, flapper not lifting fully) or siphon break early
(venting).

## Diagnosis tests in detail
- **Dye test** (flapper): 5-10 drops food coloring (or a dye tablet)
  into the TANK. No flushing, 15-20 min. Color in the BOWL = tank
  water passing to bowl = flapper (or a cracked overflow tube, rarer).
- **Float-lift test** (fill valve): lift the float arm by hand to the
  full-up position. Water must stop within ~2 seconds. Continues =
  valve diaphragm worn → replace valve (adjusting won't help).
- **Mark-and-wait** (level drop): pencil mark at waterline, 30 min no
  flush. Dropping level with valve silent = tank losing water past
  the flapper.
- **Meter isolation** (whole-system): all fixtures off, read meter,
  wait 30-60 min, re-read. Zero movement + still-running toilet =
  impossible; one of them is wrong. Movement with everything "off"
  + toilet silent → look elsewhere (irrigation, softener, slab leak
  — the last is plumber+insurance territory).

## Parts and buying notes
- **Flappers**: universal ones fit ~90%; match the general shape
  (standard 2", 3" flush valve for newer 1.6 L toilets, adjustable
  for dual-flush). Bring the old one or a phone photo to the store.
  $5-12. Buy 2 — they're consumable (5-10 yr life; chlorinated water
  halves it).
- **Fill valves**: height-adjustable (Fluidmaster 400A-style) covers
  most tanks; check for 3/8" vs 1/2" supply connection. $10-18.
- **Wax rings**: standard + extra-thick (for flange below floor);
  waxless (rubber gasket) are reusable and cleaner. Bolt sets cheap —
  always replace on any reset.
- **Tank-to-bowl sponge gasket**: match by tank brand if possible.

## Water-waste tables (worked)
At $4/kl:
- Phantom refill every 10 min, ~3 L/event: 432 L/day, ~$52/month.
- Constant quiet run 1.5 L/min: 2,160 L/day ≈ 65 kl/month ≈ $260/mo —
  4-5× a household's TOTAL normal use.
- Loud run 4 L/min: 5,760 L/day ≈ 173 kl/mo — bill territory people
  post screenshots of.
Always meter-verify before confronting anyone (landlord, utility
dispute): the meter is the fact; the table is the estimate.

## Rental playbooks
- Document first: photos of the running tank, meter readings with
  timestamps, dye-test video. Written notice (email/text, dated)
  citing the waste numbers and asking for repair within a reasonable
  window.
- Running toilets are treated as habitability/maintenance items in
  most jurisdictions; the written record is what moves slow
  landlords.
- DIY in a rental: flapper replacement is low-risk and usually fine,
  but check the lease/notify anyway — you want credit, not blame.

## When to stop and call a plumber
- Cracked porcelain anywhere (tank, bowl) — replacement, not repair.
- Broken/corroded flange, recurring base leaks after one reset.
- Venting symptoms (gurgling sinks when toilet flushes).
- Sewer smell after any base work — the ring didn't seal; reseat.
- Supply valve snapped or won't stop water with the main off upstream.

## Maintenance cadence that prevents the 2 AM surprise
- Annual: dye test both toilets (2 min each), check waterlines are
  1-2 cm below overflow tops.
- Every 5-8 years: preemptive flapper swap ($8) in hard-water homes
  (scale kills them); check fill valve shut-off crispness.
- Never: in-tank bleach tablets; they eat rubber faster than hard
  water does.
