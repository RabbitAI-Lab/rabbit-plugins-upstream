# Style Routes

## Ancestral sparkling

Use when the commission values immediacy, a single fermentation, vivid fruit and small-lot identity.

Material requirements: moderate potential alcohol, retained acidity, clean fruit, viable fermentation and low phenolic pressure unless a darker style is intended.

Critical decisions: pressing fraction, native or selected route, YAN, bottling density, pressure calculation, bottle rating, lees duration and disgorgement/cloudy release.

## Traditional-method sparkling

Use for formal events, banks, anniversaries, long-horizon editions and gastronomic service.

Material requirements: stable high-acid base, moderate alcohol, complementary texture/aroma roles and capacity for secondary fermentation and lees maturation.

Critical decisions: component vinification, assemblage trials, base-wine stability, tirage dose and yeast, pressure-rated packaging, lees time, disgorgement and dosage.

## Still red

Use for dinners, culinary partnerships, clubs and product launches where food and material depth matter.

Material requirements: phenolic maturity, freshness, stable VA, controlled extraction and suitable maturation vessel.

Critical decisions: extraction, blend by measured chemistry, microbial route, malolactic plan, oxygen/headspace, protection, barrel/lees time and bottling stability.

## Still white or pale rose

Use for receptions, design events, seafood, hospitality and earlier release schedules.

Material requirements: clean fractions, acidity, aromatic definition and temperature capacity.

Critical decisions: direct press or fraction split, turbidity, oxygen protection, fermentation temperature, lees contact, malolactic choice and stabilization.

## Skin-contact (orange) route

A maceration route on a white base, not a separate colour: the constructor takes `white` and the skin contact is a G5 route decision. Use only when the brand and audience benefit from visible texture, tannin and process character.

Material requirements: healthy skins, phenolic maturity and controlled extraction.

Critical decisions: maceration duration, cap management, oxygen, microbial state, pressing point and tannin balance. Do not choose it merely because it sounds unconventional.

## Route selection by schedule

- Short horizon: still white/pale rose or carefully scoped ancestral route.
- Medium horizon: still red with defined maturation or disgorged ancestral.
- Long horizon: traditional-method sparkling and annual vintage programmes.

The actual schedule begins only after harvest timing, fruit availability, fermentation and legal review are known.
