# Brand and Label Construction

## Translate the brand into wine

Do not begin with a logo. Extract:

- material language: mineral, textile, metal, wood, glass, leather or paper;
- colour and contrast;
- pace: immediate, energetic, contemplative or long-aged;
- audience relationship: gift, membership, ceremony or hospitality;
- geographic or cultural associations;
- desired permanence: one event or annual series.

Map these to wine functions. Examples:

- precision -> clean fraction, retained acidity, narrow release tolerances;
- depth -> structured component and longer maturation;
- lightness -> direct press, pale colour and moderate alcohol;
- transformation -> ancestral or secondary-fermentation route;
- heritage -> vintage series and long lees or vessel ageing.

## Label system

### Front

- commissioned wine name;
- commissioning organization or event where legally permitted;
- vintage and wine category;
- restrained visual device derived from the route or origin;
- individual bottle or allocation number when required.

### Back

- vineyard and region;
- principal varieties or components;
- concise construction route;
- verified farming/certification language;
- bottling, allergen and legal particulars;
- QR link to the evidence dossier when approved.

### Personalized surfaces

- recipient name or client tier;
- event/date;
- shared serial number with a luxury product;
- gift message on secondary packaging rather than regulated label space;
- NFC/QR access to vineyard, measurements and release record.

## Campaign architecture

Create a sequence:

1. Commission announcement.
2. Vineyard or vintage decision.
3. Label reveal.
4. Bottling or maturation checkpoint.
5. Event service or recipient delivery.
6. Evidence-page follow-up.
7. Next vintage or allocation invitation.

The wine should become a recurring brand asset when the organization has an annual programme.
