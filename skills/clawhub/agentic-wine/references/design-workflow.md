# Vin-Q Constructor Workflow

## 1. Define the target

Record wine type, colour, dryness, aroma, freshness, texture, structure, carbonation, maturation horizon, food use, release date and certification target.

Decision: the target is coherent enough to search for fruit, or it must be revised.

## 2. Admit vineyard and vintage

Use only co-creation lots. Assess parcel identity, farming record, harvest state, disease pressure, weather, yield, maturity and certification eligibility.

Required vintage measurements:

- Brix or potential alcohol;
- pH and titratable acidity;
- YAN;
- malic acid when freshness or malolactic route matters;
- sanitary state;
- phenolic maturity for red, rose and orange routes;
- temperature and harvest timing.

Decision: admit the lot, change the style, add a complementary lot or hold.

## 3. Reception and extraction gate

Measure lot identity, temperature, Brix, pH, TA, YAN, sanitary state and intended fraction.

Choose among direct press, split fractions, skin contact, whole-berry/intracellular route or exclusion. Define oxygen protection and temperature immediately.

## 4. Microbial succession gate

Choose native succession, selected culture or sequential culture. Record viable population where available, starting density, temperature, YAN and oxygen strategy.

Hold when the microbial route cannot plausibly complete the target wine. Redirect through nutrition, temperature, inoculation or a simpler style.

## 5. Active fermentation gate

Track density/Brix, temperature, sensory state, volatile acidity, H2S/reduction, oxygen/ORP where useful, cap condition for skin fermentations and microbial analysis when trajectory departs from plan.

Actions include continue, cool, controlled aeration, feed, change extraction, press, isolate or redirect.

## 6. Product branch

### Still

Confirm dryness or intended residual sugar, pH, TA, VA, malolactic state, protection, vessel, headspace, lees and maturation plan.

### Ancestral sparkling

Define bottling density/residual sugar, target pressure, bottle rating, closure, active yeast state and temperature. Unresolved pressure blocks bottling.

### Traditional-method sparkling

Finish a stable base, confirm alcohol, pH, TA, VA, malolactic status, protein/tartrate stability, tirage sugar, yeast, bottle rating and maturation time.

## 7. Maturation gate

Track vessel, temperature, headspace, free/total SO2 where used, VA trend, lees condition, sensory evolution and sparkling pressure/bottle condition.

Decision: continue, rack, top, adjust protection, shorten, disgorge or redirect.

## 8. Bottling and release gate

Confirm final alcohol, pH, TA, VA, residual sugar, free/total SO2, CO2 pressure where applicable, microbial stability, sensory approval, label legality, traceability and certification dossier.

Release only when every critical value has an evidence status: measured, calculated, target, estimated or missing. Missing critical safety or legal evidence blocks release.

## Four-gate graph

The operational path can be displayed as four connected gate families:

1. Reception chooses admissible material and extraction.
2. Microbial succession selects the biological route.
3. Fermentation follows and corrects the trajectory.
4. Bottling/release commits the wine to still, ancestral or tirage-based realization.

Alternative branches remain visible because a measurement can force redirection.
