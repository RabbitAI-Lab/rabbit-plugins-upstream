# 片段类型：词汇

## 原文片段

Our operationalization of joint physical attention was inspired by coding schemes developed for quantifying joint physical attention during naturalistic interactions. The primary objective was to develop a nonverbal scenario that would enable preverbal infants to infer third-party joint attention without eye contact, using physical cues to convey interpersonal sharedness and to signal the referent of mutual attention. Drawing upon Little et al.'s (2016) definition of "physical triadic engagement," we established that one of the two actors touched their partner in conjunction with the object, creating the person–object link physically. Moreover, both actors were required to gaze at the object held by the object-handling actor, aligning with research suggesting that mutual looking at one's own or a partner's hands on objects can establish joint attention without eye contact in naturalistic interactions (Yu & Smith, 2013, 2016).
