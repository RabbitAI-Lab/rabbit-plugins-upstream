# Capsule Wardrobe — Rules & Doctrine (reference)

## 1. Why wardrobes fail: structure, not taste
- Average adult: 100+ garments, ~20% worn regularly. The failure mode is **orphan items** (pair with nothing) crowding out the interchangeable core.
- A capsule works by **combinatorics**: 8 tops × 5 bottoms at 80% compatibility = 32 base outfits. 8 tops × 5 bottoms at 15% compatibility = 6. Item count identical; wardrobe opposite.
- The three numbers that matter: **distinct outfits** (≥20 for daily rotation), **laundry depth per role** (wears + 1 + buffer), **cost-per-wear** (≤$3 for frequent categories).

## 2. Cost-per-wear doctrine
| CPW band | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ≤ $1/wear | Workhorse. Buy multiples on sight. |
| $1–3/wear | Fair for daily categories. |
| $3–8/wear | Marginal — only for loved, long-hold items. |
| > $8/wear | Rental/borrow territory. The $25 top worn twice costs $12.50/wear — 38× a $250 coat at $0.33/wear. |

**Realized vs projected CPW**: post-mortem your last 5 purchases with `price ÷ wears-so-far`. If realized runs >3× projected, your lifetime estimates are fiction — discount future "investment piece" arguments accordingly.

**Break-even math**: `wears needed = price ÷ target-CPW`. A $180 blazer at $2/wear needs 90 wears ≈ 45 work weeks if worn 2×/week — one full year of faithful use. If that won't happen, it's a $180 costume.

## 3. Compatibility matrix method
1. List tops (rows) × bottoms (columns). Mark each pair ✗ / ✓ / ✓✓ (great).
2. Compute `compatible fraction = ✓-pairs ÷ total pairs`.
3. Row/column totals reveal **orphan items** (nearly empty rows/columns) — replace first, they're dead weight.
4. Upgrade path: replace the 3 lowest-compatibility items with neutrals pairing ≥70% of the opposite category. Typical result: outfits nearly double while item count falls.
5. Keep ~20% statement/contrast pieces that work with ≥70% of the base — a 100% neutral capsule has many technically-distinct, visually-identical outfits.

## 4. Laundry-cycle queueing
- `items needed = wears-per-cycle ÷ wears-per-item + 1 (in the wash) + 1 (spill buffer)`.
- Weekly wash, shirts worn 1× → 9 shirts. Jeans worn 3× → 3 pairs for a 7-day cycle.
- "Always behind on laundry" for one role = that role is under-depthed. Buying depth is the fix; stylists cannot fix queueing.
- Travel application: trip_days × wears/day ÷ (trip_days ÷ laundry_access_interval) sizes the packing cube.

## 5. Lifestyle allocation audit
1. Take an honest week: hours by outfit-role (work / casual / active / dress-up / home).
2. Sort daily-rotation items by role. Compare each role's item share vs life share.
3. Deficit >3 points → buy (max 3 items per audit, neutral basics with pairing leverage). Surplus >3 points → donate candidates (past CPW threshold: anything realized >$5/wear after 6 months).
4. Re-audit quarterly and after life changes (job, climate, weight, baby).

## 6. Uniform doctrine (the endgame)
High-performers often converge on a **personal uniform**: 2–5 identical/near-identical daily outfits (jobs: Bartali, Zuckerberg, Obama's suits; countless engineers). Benefits: zero decision fatigue, CPW → floor, packing trivial. Capsule doctrine is the general case; uniform doctrine is the limit. Suggest when the user reports decision fatigue or repeated frustration, not as virtue.

## 7. Shopping trip protocol
1. Run `gaps` before leaving; carry the ≤3-item list.
2. Any candidate must beat the top of the buy-list on **outfit leverage** (new pairs created).
3. Try-on test: name 3 existing items it pairs with. Can't name 3 → orphan risk, walk away.
4. Post-purchase: log price + date; review realized CPW at 6 months.

## 8. Declutter protocol (CPW-based)
1. Everything unworn 12 months → candidate pile.
2. Keep: high-CPW-core basics (plain tees, dark denim, leathers) regardless of joy — they're infrastructure.
3. Donate: orphans (compatibility ≤1 pair), duplicates past depth+1, realized CPW > $5/wear trend items.
4. Never purge the interchangeable base during a joy-pass — that's how capsules collapse into single-outfit wardrobes.
