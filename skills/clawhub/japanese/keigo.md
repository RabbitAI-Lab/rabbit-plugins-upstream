## The Five Categories

文化審議会's 敬語の指針 (2007) replaced the traditional three-way split with five. Use the five: they are what makes 申す vs 申し上げる and 参る vs 伺う explainable.

| Category | Raises / lowers | Marker | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 尊敬語 | Raises **the other party's** action or thing | おっしゃる, お〜になる, れる/られる, お名前 | 部長がおっしゃいました |
| 謙譲語Ⅰ | Lowers **your** action *toward a specific person* | 伺う, 申し上げる, お〜する, ご〜する | 部長に申し上げました |
| 謙譲語Ⅱ (丁重語) | Lowers **your** action toward the *listener*, no target needed | 参る, 申す, おる, いたす, 存じる | 明日、大阪へ参ります |
| 丁寧語 | Politeness toward the listener, nothing else | です, ます, でございます | 雨が降っています |
| 美化語 | Beautifies a noun with no target at all | お茶, お食事, ご飯 | お茶をどうぞ |

The distinction that matters daily: **謙譲語Ⅰ needs someone to be lowered toward**. 先生のところに伺います works because 先生 is the target. 明日は家に伺います (your own house) does not. With no target, use 謙譲語Ⅱ: 明日は家に参ります.

## The Suppletive Verbs

These do not follow any frame; they are memorised or they are wrong. The 謙譲 column is Ⅰ unless marked Ⅱ.

| Plain | 尊敬語 | 謙譲語 |
|---|---|---|
| する | なさる | いたす (Ⅱ) |
| 言う | おっしゃる | 申し上げる / 申す (Ⅱ) |
| 見る | ご覧になる | 拝見する |
| 聞く | お聞きになる | 伺う / 拝聴する |
| 行く / 来る | いらっしゃる / おいでになる / お越しになる | 伺う / 参る (Ⅱ) |
| いる | いらっしゃる | おる (Ⅱ) |
| 食べる / 飲む | 召し上がる | いただく |
| 知る | ご存じ (だ) | 存じる (things) / 存じ上げる (people) |
| 思う | お思いになる | 存じる (Ⅱ) |
| 会う | お会いになる | お目にかかる |
| 与える | くださる | さしあげる |
| もらう | — | いただく / 頂戴する |
| 借りる | — | 拝借する |
| 読む | お読みになる | 拝読する |
| 着る | お召しになる | — |
| 死ぬ | お亡くなりになる | — |

Traps inside the table: **ご存じ is 尊敬語** (ご存じですか = do you know), while 存じる is 謙譲語 (存じております = I know). They look like a pair and point in opposite directions. **いただく vs くださる**: 見ていただく makes the user the subject (I receive the favour), 見てくださる makes them the subject (they grant it) — both polite, and mixing the subject mid-sentence is a common break.

## The Regular Frames

For verbs with no suppletive form:

| Frame | Direction | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| お〜になる | 尊敬 | お + 連用形 + になる | お待ちになる, お帰りになる |
| ご〜になる | 尊敬 | ご + 漢語 + になる | ご利用になる, ご出席になる |
| れる / られる | 尊敬 (light) | passive form used as honorific | 待たれる, 出席される |
| お〜ください | 尊敬 imperative | お + 連用形 + ください | お待ちください, ご確認ください |
| お〜する / お〜いたす | 謙譲Ⅰ | お + 連用形 + する | お待ちします, お送りいたします |
| ご〜する / ご〜いたす | 謙譲Ⅰ | ご + 漢語 + する | ご説明します, ご案内いたします |
| 〜ております | 丁重 | 〜ている の 謙譲Ⅱ | 承知しております, 拝見しております |

**お is for 和語, ご is for 漢語** — お待ち, お知らせ, お call vs ご確認, ご連絡, ご説明. The exceptions are memorised and few: お電話, お食事, お返事 (ご返事 also exists), お礼, お約束, お時間.

**れる/られる is the weakest 尊敬語**, which makes it useful: it is the lowest-friction way to be respectful in speech and the only frame that never becomes 二重敬語 on its own. It is also ambiguous with the passive and the potential (社長が話された = the president spoke / was spoken about / could speak), so it is avoided where the sentence could be read two ways.

## 二重敬語

One verb, one honorific marker. Pick ONE of {suppletive verb, お〜になる frame, れる/られる}.

| Wrong | Why | Right |
|---|---|---|
| お読みになられる | お〜になる + られる | お読みになる / 読まれる |
| ご覧になられる | 尊敬 verb + られる | ご覧になる |
| おっしゃられる | 尊敬 verb + られる | おっしゃる |
| お伺いになる | 謙譲 verb + 尊敬 frame — direction reversed too | いらっしゃる / お越しになる |
| 拝見させていただく | 謙譲 verb + 謙譲 frame | 拝見します |
| ご確認させていただきます | 謙譲 frame + させていただく | ご確認いたします |
| お召し上がりになられる | three markers | 召し上がる |
| いただかせていただく | 謙譲 verb + 謙譲 frame | いただきます |

**The naturalised exceptions are a closed list**, accepted by 敬語の指針 as 慣用: お伺いする, お伺いいたします, お伺いします, お召し上がりください, お見えになる. They are technically double and nobody objects. Anything outside the list reads as trying too hard, which in Japanese is its own kind of rude — 慇懃無礼.

**敬語連結 is not 二重敬語.** Two verbs each carrying their own honorific, joined by て, is correct: お読みになっていらっしゃる (reading + being), お書きになってくださる (writing + granting). The test is whether the markers sit on one verb or on two.

## させていただく

Two conditions, both required (敬語の指針, 2007): the other party's **permission** is genuinely involved, and the action **benefits** the speaker.

| Sentence | Permission? | Benefit? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 本日は休業させていただきます | yes (customers tolerate it) | yes | Correct |
| 会議に参加させていただきます | yes, if they invited you | yes | Correct when invited; 参加します when you simply signed up |
| ご説明させていただきます | no — nobody granted it | yes | Inflated → ご説明します |
| 資料を送らせていただきます | no | marginal | Inflated → お送りします |
| 拝見させていただきます | no, and 拝見 is already 謙譲 | — | 二重 → 拝見します |
| 担当させていただいております | yes (assigned) | yes | Correct, and standard in 社外 self-introduction |
| させていただきたく存じます | — | — | Correct grammar, maximum inflation; use only in 最敬語 written requests |

The reason to hold the line: the inflated form is now audible as evasive. Three of them in one email reads as someone avoiding responsibility for their own actions, which is the opposite of the intended effect. When a sentence fails the test, the fix is always the plain 謙譲語Ⅰ form.

Related inflation, same family: **さ入れ言葉** — 読まさせていただく, 帰らさせていただく. Godan verbs take せる, not させる: 読ませて, 帰らせて. This one is not contested; it is simply wrong.

## お and ご on Nouns

Three different jobs wearing the same prefix:

- **尊敬**: the noun belongs to the other party — お名前, ご住所, ご意見, お荷物.
- **謙譲**: the noun is yours but directed at them — お手紙 (that I send you), ご連絡 (that I make to you). This is why ご連絡いたします is correct and not self-elevating.
- **美化**: neither — お茶, お金, ご飯, お天気. Register decoration only.

Where it breaks: **お on foreign loanwords** (おビール, おトイレ) is either service-industry register or a joke; **お on your own attributes** (お名前を申し上げます about yourself) is self-elevation; **ご on a 和語** (ご返事 is an accepted exception, ご待ち is not). When in doubt on a 漢語 noun, ご; on a 和語, お; on a katakana word, neither.

## 謙譲語Ⅰ vs 謙譲語Ⅱ

The pair that separates a competent speaker from a fluent one.

| Situation | Ⅰ (has a target) | Ⅱ (no target, politeness to the listener) |
|---|---|---|
| Going to the client's office | 御社に伺います | — |
| Going to Osaka on holiday | — | 大阪へ参ります |
| Telling the client something | 申し上げます | — |
| Saying your own name | — | 田中と申します |
| Being somewhere | — | 会議室におります |
| Doing something | ご案内いたします (to them) | 対応いたします (in general) |

Test: is there a specific person the action reaches or affects? Yes → Ⅰ. No → Ⅱ. This is why 明日は雨が降ってまいります works (丁重語 applied to rain, which respects nobody) and why 弊社に伺います does not (you cannot be humble toward your own office).

## でございます and Its Limits

でございます is 丁寧語, one notch above です — a copula, not a universal politeness upgrade.

- Correct: 田中でございます · こちらが資料でございます · 〜がございます (the polite あります).
- **Not a verb ending.** 行くでございます is not Japanese. Verbs take ます, nouns and na-adjectives take でございます.
- **Not for the listener's attributes.** お客様でございますか is service-register and contested; お客様でいらっしゃいますか is the 尊敬語 form and is correct, because the listener's identity is theirs, not yours.
- Density matters: three でございます in four sentences reads as a department store announcement. One in the 名乗り and です for the rest is the normal shape of a 敬語 email.

## Keigo Chains

Long polite sentences fail at the joints. Two rules keep them intact:

1. **Keigo goes on the final verb of the clause, not on every verb in it.** ご確認いただき、ご返信いただけますと幸いです — the middle verb is plain 連用形, the last one carries the ending. Marking every verb produces the 二重 feel even when each mark is individually legal.
2. **The subject must not change silently.** ご確認いただき、こちらから送付いたします switches from their action to yours mid-sentence with no marker; the reader has to reconstruct who does what. Split it, or make the switch explicit with こちらで / そちらで.

The two most common polite request frames, in increasing weight: 〜ていただけますか (can you) · 〜ていただけますでしょうか (could you) · 〜ていただけますと幸いです (I would be grateful) · 〜くださいますようお願い申し上げます (formal written). Above that is 最敬語 territory and belongs in `documents.md`.

## The Denial Diagnostic

When a keigo sentence feels wrong but parses, run these in order:

1. **Whose action is it?** Their action wants 尊敬語; yours wants 謙譲語. Half of all errors die here.
2. **Am I うち or そと to this listener?** Talking about your own 部長 to a customer strips his title and his 尊敬語 (`register.md`).
3. **How many markers on this one verb?** More than one → 二重敬語.
4. **Does させていただく pass both conditions?** No → plain 謙譲語Ⅰ.
5. **Is 謙譲語Ⅰ pointed at somebody?** No target → 謙譲語Ⅱ.
6. **Does お/ご match the word's origin?** 和語 takes お, 漢語 takes ご, katakana takes neither.
7. **Would the sentence survive without the honorific?** If the plain sentence is unclear or wrong, the keigo is decorating a broken sentence — fix the sentence first.

## What Gets Written Down

Destinations, all in `memory-template.md`:

- **A native correction to a keigo form** — the one from the actual reader, not a rule from a book — → `### Corrections` with who said so, in the same turn. These outrank every table on this page.
- **A keigo cheat-sheet derived for one role** (the twelve forms a 営業 in this company actually uses, with the house's preferences already applied) → `artifacts/keigo-<role>.md` with its `## Boxes` line. Deriving one costs a real conversation with a native colleague; nobody should pay it twice.
- **A per-person exception** ("小林様 dislikes させていただく, he said so") → the person's `## Recipients` row, in the Notes column.
