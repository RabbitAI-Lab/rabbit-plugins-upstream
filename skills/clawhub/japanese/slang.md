# Slang — Shelf Life and Reader Fit

Slang is the fastest-decaying content in this skill and the only kind where being current is part of being correct. Governed by `slang_appetite`. **Before using any term from this file**, read `### Retired Slang` and `## Profile` in `~/Clawic/data/japanese/memory.md`: a term already retired for this reader is retired, and the reader's age band decides more than any table here.

## The Two Questions

Before any slang term goes into output, both have to pass:

1. **Can you name roughly when it peaked?** If not, do not use it. A term with no date attached is a term you cannot judge, and the failure mode is silent: it reads fine to the writer and dates the writer to the reader.
2. **Is the reader inside the band that still uses it?** A term two to three years past its peak reads as trying; five years past reads as a 死語 and is heard as a joke whether or not one was intended.

Two rules on top:

- **One term, not three.** A single current word inside otherwise ordinary Japanese reads as fluency. Three in a paragraph reads as an impersonation, and that is exactly how a non-native's slang lands.
- **Prefer the terms that crossed over.** A word that has entered dictionaries and ordinary adult speech (ヤバい, エモい, ググる, ドタキャン) has no expiry risk left. The tail is where the cost is.

## Decay By Class

Slang does not age at one rate. The class predicts the shelf life better than the term does.

| Class | Example | Typical shelf life | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Semantic shift of an ordinary word | ヤバい, 微妙, 神, エグい | Decades — these become the language | Safe |
| Derivational morphology | 〜み, 〜すぎ, 〜活, 〜ハラ, 〜ガチャ | The pattern outlives every instance | Safe as a pattern, date the instance |
| Verbing a noun | ググる, バズる, ディスる, タクる | Long, if the underlying thing lasts | Safe |
| Net-culture vocabulary | 草, 炎上, 垢, 界隈, 語彙力 | 5-15 years, platform-bound | Check the platform is still live |
| Media and award coinages | 忖度, 神ってる, インスタ映え | 2-5 years as slang, then archive | Dates the text hard |
| Youth abbreviations | りょ, り, あざす, とりま | 2-4 years | Highest risk |
| Reaction words | ぴえん, 〜しか勝たん, 卍 | 2-3 years | Highest risk, and the ones models reach for |
| Business jargon | リスケ, なるはや, エビデンス | Very long; changes by industry, not by year | Safe inside its industry |

## Dated Terms and Their Peak

Anchored to the years the terms were nationally visible — the ユーキャン新語・流行語大賞 (annual since 1984), 三省堂's 辞書を編む人が選ぶ「今年の新語」 (since 2015) and the JC・JK流行語大賞 are the published records, and they are what makes a peak year checkable rather than a feeling.

| Term | Peaked around | Now |
|---|---|---|
| ナウい, チョベリバ, マブい | 1980s-90s | 死語; usable only as a deliberate joke |
| KY (空気読めない) | 2007 (流行語大賞 top ten) | Dated; the concept survives, the abbreviation does not |
| じぇじぇじぇ, 倍返し | 2013 (年間大賞) | Dead, and tied to the drama that produced them |
| ワンチャン | ~2013-14 | Crossed over into ordinary casual speech |
| それな, あーね | ~2014 | Still current, now unmarked |
| 神ってる | 2016 (年間大賞) | Dead; 神 as a prefix survives |
| エモい | 2016 (三省堂 今年の新語 大賞) | Crossed over — ordinary vocabulary, safe |
| インスタ映え, 忖度 | 2017 (年間大賞) | 映える survives as a verb; 忖度 survives as a political term |
| 卍 | ~2017 (JC・JK) | 死語 |
| 3密 | 2020 (年間大賞) | Frozen to its year; using it now dates the text to 2020 |
| ぴえん | 2020 | Retired for most readers; still alive as a self-aware joke |
| 〜しか勝たん | ~2020-21 | Fading; safe only with under-25 readers |
| 親ガチャ | 2021 | The 〜ガチャ pattern outlived it |
| 推し, 推し活 | ~2021 onward | Fully mainstream, including in marketing |
| タイパ | 2022 (三省堂 今年の新語 大賞) | Current in business and media writing |
| 蛙化現象 | 2023 (流行語大賞 top ten) | Fading, and it was always a media word more than a spoken one |
| マルハラ | 2024 (media coverage) | Current; the phenomenon is described in `chat.md` |

The list is a snapshot and ages by construction. Its value is the shape it teaches: **the award-winners are the fastest to die**, because national visibility is what exhausts a term.

## The Productive Patterns

The patterns outlive their instances, which makes them safer than any word and worth knowing as generators.

- **Four-mora abbreviation.** Japanese compresses compounds to four morae: スマホ, パソコン, コスパ, タイパ, リモワ, パワハラ, ドタキャン, ファミレス. A coinage that lands on four morae survives; one that does not, does not.
- **〜ハラ** attaches to any pressure someone wants named: パワハラ, セクハラ, モラハラ, マタハラ, カスハラ, マルハラ. Reads as slightly journalistic, understood by everyone.
- **〜活** turns an activity into a project: 就活, 婚活, 終活, 妊活, 朝活, 推し活. Fully mainstream, including on 履歴書-adjacent text.
- **〜ガチャ** frames something as unchosen and random: 親ガチャ, 上司ガチャ, 配属ガチャ. Casual, slightly bitter, under-40.
- **〜み** nominalises an adjective past where the grammar allows: わかりみ, つらみ, しんどみ, 尊み. Under-30 register, and one instance is the maximum in adult writing.
- **〜すぎ / 〜すぎる** as a standalone reaction: 早すぎ, 良すぎ. Neutral and ageless.
- **Verbing with 〜る**: ググる, バズる, ディスる, タピる. The pattern is permanent; the noun attached is what dates.
- **Truncation ladder**: 了解 → 了解です → りょ → り. Each rung is younger and shorter, and the last rung has the shortest life.
- **〜ニキ / 〜ネキ, 〜民, 〜勢, 〜界隈** name a group from anywhere: 筋トレ民, 早起き勢. Net-native, safe among under-35 online.

## Reader Bands

Age matters more than any other variable, and in Japanese the bands are visible in the writing itself.

| Band | Laughter | Emoji | Slang tolerance | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Under ~25 | 草, 笑笑, w | Sparse; スタンプ instead | High, and current only | Anything past peak reads as parental |
| ~25-35 | w, （笑）, 😂 | Moderate | Medium; crossed-over terms only | The 〜み and 卍 line falls here |
| ~35-50 | （笑）, ｗｗｗ full-width | 絵文字 freely | Low; business slang instead | 顔文字 reads as their native register |
| Over ~50 | （笑） | Decorative, heavy | None outside 死語 they grew up with | Slang from any later decade reads as a stranger's language |

If the band is unknown, `slang_appetite: light` is the default and it means: crossed-over vocabulary only, zero award-winners, zero abbreviations. When the reader's band is discovered, it is a `## Profile` line, not a guess to repeat.

## おじさん構文

A named and widely mocked register in Japan: the way middle-aged men are perceived to write on LINE. Naming it matters because a model reproduces it by accident whenever it tries to be warm.

Its markers: 絵文字 after almost every clause · 中黒 or 三点リーダー used as spacing (「今日は・・・お疲れ様～😊」) · long vowels written with 〜 · カタカナ for ordinary words (ボク, ゴハン) · the reader's given name repeated inside the message · a question about their private life attached to a work message · 顔文字 mixed with 絵文字 · 〜だネ, 〜かナ.

The fix is not coldness. It is: one emoji or none, no name repetition mid-message, one topic per message, and contractions instead of decoration (`chat.md`).

## Business Slang

Not youth slang, no age signal, and a different failure mode — it is opaque to outsiders rather than dated.

| Term | Means | Register |
|---|---|---|
| なるはや | As soon as possible, with no deadline | Convert it to a date, always (`etiquette.md`) |
| リスケ | Reschedule | Universal |
| アサイン / ジョイン | Assign / join | 外資 and IT; reads as jargon elsewhere |
| エビデンス | Evidence, a record that proves it happened | Universal in IT and consulting |
| 一旦 / とりま | Provisionally | 一旦 is the business form of とりま |
| 握る | Get an agreement, informally, before the formal step | Very common, invisible to non-natives |
| 巻き取る | Take over someone else's task | Common |
| ペンディング / ペンディる | On hold | |
| ざっくり | Roughly, at low resolution | Neutral and useful |
| 全体感 / 肌感 | The overall shape / the felt sense | Consulting register |
| ドタキャン | Cancel at the last minute | Universal, including personal |
| テレコ | Swapped, transposed | Older, still current in 製造 and 広告 |
| ケツ | The final deadline | Crude-adjacent; fine internally, never 社外 |
| 一丁目一番地 | The top priority | Political and corporate |
| 温度感 | How strongly someone feels about it | Very common in email |

**Industry decides these, not age.** A term standard in 外資IT (アサイン, コミット) reads as posturing in a 製造業 office, and the reverse is also true. When the user's industry vocabulary shows up in their own text, it is a `## Profile` line.

## Where Slang Is a Liability

Zero slang, no judgement call needed: 社外 email · 契約書 and any legal text · 履歴書 and 職務経歴書 · 公式アカウント announcements · 議事録 · anything to a reader over ~50 · anything a stranger will read years later · anything translated onward.

Two further cases that catch people out:

- **Archived text ages with its slang.** A note article or a product page is read for years; a term at peak today dates the page in three years while the rest of it stays valid. In long-lived text, the slang is the part that expires.
- **A non-native's slang carries extra risk.** The reader has no baseline for the writer's register, so an off-peak term reads as a mistake rather than as a joke. When in doubt, the ordinary word costs nothing.

## Ironic Revival

Dead slang comes back as a deliberate joke — 死語 used *because* it is dead. ナウい in a message from someone under 30 is a bit, not an error, and 一周回って (it has come full circle) is the phrase Japanese uses for exactly this.

Two conditions before using a term this way: the reader must be able to tell it is intentional (the surrounding register has to be current), and the relationship must carry a joke. Both missing, it reads as a writer who never noticed the term died. **This is why `### Retired Slang` keeps the peak year and not just the verdict**: "retired" with no date cannot be re-evaluated when the term returns as a joke.

## What Gets Written Down

Destinations, all in `memory-template.md`:

- **A term judged stale, or adopted** → a row in `### Retired Slang` with the year it peaked and the verdict, in the same turn the judgement is made. Without the year the row is unreviewable.
- **The reader's age band, region and industry vocabulary** once they are known → `## Profile`. This single line changes more output than the whole table above.
- **A term the user's own reader used first** → `### Terms`, marked as reader-sourced. A term the reader used is licensed; one from a list is a bet.
- **A slang calibration pass** — a snapshot of what is current for this reader, taken deliberately → `artifacts/slang-calibration-<date>.md`, plus its `## Boxes` line, and a row in `## Due` at a six-month cadence. This is the one section of this skill that goes out of date on a clock.
