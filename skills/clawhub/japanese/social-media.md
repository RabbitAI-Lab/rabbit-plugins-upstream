# Social Media — Platform Voices

**Before writing for a platform**, read the channel's row in `## Channels` and the `styles/<channel>.md` file its `## Boxes` line points to in `~/Clawic/data/japanese/memory.md`. Each platform is a different register with a different length mechanic, and the user's account has a voice that predates this session.

## X (Twitter)

Japan is one of X's largest markets and the platform's Japanese conventions diverge sharply from its English ones.

- **The character budget is 140 for Japanese.** X counts most CJK characters as weight 2 against a 280-unit limit, so Japanese posts get 140 characters where English gets 280 — and those 140 characters carry roughly the information of 250-300 English ones. A Japanese post is not a shortened English post; it is a denser one.
- **Register is 常体 or light 丁寧**, rarely 敬語. 敬語 on a personal account reads as a corporate account or as sarcasm.
- **体言止め is the native rhythm** here more than anywhere else: ending a line on a noun compresses and sounds like a headline rather than a sentence.
- **Line breaks are the formatting.** No bold, no headings; a three-line post with two breaks reads far better than one block.
- **Anonymity is the norm**, not the exception. Real-name accounts are the minority and are read as professional or promotional (below).
- **リプ, 引用, RT (リポスト), FF外から失礼します** — the last one opens a reply to a stranger and is still current politeness on the platform.
- **ぶら下げ (threading)** is how anything above 140 characters is published; the first post must stand alone, because it is the only one most readers see.
- **エゴサ, バズる, 伸びる, 拡散希望** are the platform's own vocabulary and are safe; 拡散希望 attached to commercial content reads as begging.

## note

The default long-form platform for Japanese practitioners, essayists and founders. Closer to a personal magazine than a blog.

- **Register is 丁寧 throughout**, 一人称 私 or 僕 depending on the writer's public voice, and it never drops to 常体 mid-article.
- **Titles carry the whole click.** Roughly 30 characters, with the concrete outcome in the first 15 — feeds truncate. A title with a number and a specific noun outperforms a clever one.
- **見出し every ~400 字**, 2-4 sentences per paragraph, blank line between paragraphs rather than 全角一字下げ.
- **The opener is a scene or a number, never a definition.** 「◯◯とは、〜のことです」 as a first line is the most reliable way to lose a Japanese reader in this format.
- **Do not close with いかがでしたか, まとめ as a heading, or 最後までお読みいただきありがとうございました** — all three are SEO-blog residue and mark the article as content-farm output (`ai-tells.md`).
- **有料note** changes the shape: the free portion must end on an unresolved question, and the paywall is placed after the reader has already received something usable.
- 見出し画像 is expected; a note article without one reads as unfinished.

## Instagram

- **Caption length is bimodal**: either one line plus tags, or a 500-1,500 character essay that begins with a hook and a 続きを読む break. Nothing in between performs.
- **The first two lines are the whole caption** — the rest is behind 続きを読む.
- **Register 日常**, heavy 絵文字, line breaks between every sentence, and often a decorative separator line (⁡ or ・・・) to force spacing.
- **ハッシュタグ in Japanese and English both**, 10-20 of them, placed after the caption behind blank lines or in the first comment.
- **ストーリーズ is a different register again**: fragments, スタンプ, questions, and text that assumes the reader watched yesterday's.
- **保存 (saves) is the metric Japanese accounts optimise for**, which is why 保存版, まとめ and チェックリスト formats dominate.

## TikTok and Shorts

- **Text on screen is the copy.** The caption is secondary; the first frame's text is what stops the scroll, and it is 10-15 characters maximum.
- **Register is 親しい**, contracted, with 語尾 doing the work: 〜すぎ, 〜しか勝たん (dated, check `slang.md`), 〜てみた, 〜のコツ.
- **Subtitles are near-universal** in Japanese short video, burned in, and their line breaks matter: break on a 文節 boundary, never mid-word.
- **〜してみた / 〜やってみた** is the native format label and signals the video's genre in three characters.

## YouTube

- **Titles run long by Japanese standards** — 40-60 characters is normal — and lead with the concrete result plus a bracketed tag: 【検証】【解説】【初心者向け】.
- **概要欄** opens with two or three lines of summary, then chapters with timestamps, then links, then the fixed boilerplate. Chapters are expected on anything over ~8 minutes.
- **Register in the video is 丁寧** for explainer content, 親しい for entertainment; the 概要欄 matches the video.
- **コメント欄 replies from the creator are 丁寧 regardless** of the video's register.

## はてなブログ and 技術ブログ

- **Register is 敬体 or である, chosen once.** Japanese technical writing accepts である far more readily than any other online format, and a 技術記事 in である reads as authoritative rather than stiff.
- **Code and prose spacing**: a half-width space between Japanese and Latin identifiers is near-universal here even where the user's house style says otherwise (`punctuation.md`).
- **English technical terms stay in English or katakana**, not translated — デプロイ, ビルド, リポジトリ. Translating them is what marks a machine-translated technical post.
- **The structure is 課題 → 試したこと → 結果 → 学び**, and the 試したこと section is what the audience reads. A post that only presents the working answer gets read as an advertisement.
- **参考リンク at the bottom** is expected and its absence reads as either arrogance or plagiarism.

## LinkedIn and Wantedly

- LinkedIn has a smaller but real Japanese presence, and Japanese posts there are **丁寧, first-person, and much less self-promotional** than their English equivalents. English LinkedIn's achievement-forward voice reads as boastful in Japanese and depresses engagement.
- **Wantedly is the domestic equivalent** for hiring and its register is deliberately warm and casual — 常体 or light 丁寧, 私たち as a company voice, and stories rather than requirements.
- 職務経歴 in a profile follows the 職務経歴書 conventions (`documents.md`), not the platform's English template.

## Company Accounts

The 中の人 (the person behind the account) is a recognised role in Japan and a genuine strategic choice:

| Voice | Register | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| 公式 announcement voice | 敬語, no 絵文字, 平素より | Zero engagement, zero risk |
| 中の人 personality voice | 丁寧 or 日常, 絵文字, jokes | High reach, and one bad post is a 炎上 |
| Product voice (first person as the product) | 日常, no company self-reference | Distinctive, hard to sustain |

Rules for the 中の人 voice: never comment on politics, disasters, or a competitor's failure; suspend all scheduled posts when a disaster occurs (an automated cheerful post during an earthquake is the canonical 炎上 pattern); and never argue with a reply. The apology shape when it goes wrong is in `etiquette.md` — first-person, specific, no 〜させていただく, and posted from the same account.

## Hashtags

- **Japanese hashtags do not use spaces** and often run long: #おうち時間 #買ってよかったもの #新社会人と繋がりたい.
- **〜と繋がりたい** is a live convention for finding a community (#エンジニアと繋がりたい).
- **Volume by platform**: X takes 0-2 and more reads as spam; Instagram takes 10-20; note takes 3-5; TikTok takes 3-5 broad ones.
- **Mixed-script tags work** (#Web制作) but a tag mixing full-width and half-width characters splits the tag's feed in two — pick one form and keep it.

## Anonymity and Handles

Japanese social media is more anonymous than the English-language equivalent, and the norm shapes the writing:

- **本名 accounts are read as professional or commercial.** A personal account under a real name invites a formality the writer may not want.
- **Handles carry register.** A handle in katakana reads differently from one in Latin letters; @ mentions of a person use their handle, not their real name, even when the real name is known.
- **中傷 and 特定** are live risks: posting a photo with a station name, a school badge or a name tag visible is the standard 特定 vector, and Japanese users routinely blur them. Flag it when drafting a post that describes a location and a routine together.
- **リアタイ / 裏垢 / 鍵垢** — the vocabulary of a culture where one person runs several accounts with different registers. When the user mentions a second account, that is a second `## Channels` row, not a variation of the first.

## What Gets Written Down

Destinations, all in `memory-template.md`:

- **A platform's settled voice** — title length, register, emoji density, opener and closer, tags, what never to write → `styles/<channel>.md`, read whole before the next post, with its `## Boxes` line in the same turn.
- **A post that was published** → a row in `pieces/<year>.md` with its channel, its length in 字, its register, and how it landed. The last column is the one that makes the file worth keeping.
- **A platform fact that cost effort** — a character limit that counts differently than expected, an editor that strips blank lines, a tag that split in two, an audience older than assumed → `## Environment`.
- **A headline or title bank that worked** → `artifacts/headline-bank-<channel>.md`; titles are re-derived far more often than articles are.
