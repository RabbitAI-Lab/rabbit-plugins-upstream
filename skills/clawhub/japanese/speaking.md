# Speaking — Calls, Presentations, and Ceremonial Speech

Spoken Japanese is not written Japanese read aloud: it is shorter, more particled, repeats its topic, and carries keigo that written text can compress away. **Before writing anything to be spoken to a named audience**, read `## Recipients` and `## Profile` in `~/Clawic/data/japanese/memory.md` — a script is delivered at one politeness level and cannot be adjusted mid-delivery.

## Written vs Spoken

| Written | Spoken |
|---|---|
| 〜ですので | 〜なので |
| 〜ですが | 〜ですけど / 〜ですけれども |
| 〜という | 〜っていう |
| 〜ではない | 〜じゃない |
| 〜しなければならない | 〜しないといけない / 〜しなきゃ |
| 現在 | 今 |
| 約30% | 3割くらい |
| 及び / 並びに | と / や |
| 従いまして | なので / ですから |
| Long pre-nominal modifiers | Broken into two sentences with a topic repeated |

Three properties of spoken Japanese a written script has to be rewritten to acquire:

1. **The topic is repeated, not carried.** In writing, a topic set with は governs three sentences. Out loud, listeners lose it: 今回の施策ですが、この施策は… is redundant on the page and necessary in the room.
2. **Numbers are spoken in their spoken form.** 1,440,000円 is 百四十四万円 out loud; a script that leaves digits produces a stumble at exactly the moment the number matters.
3. **Fillers are structure, not noise.** えーと buys thinking time, あのー signals a topic shift, まあ downgrades what follows, なんか hedges, で resumes. A script with zero fillers delivered verbatim sounds like a recording; the fix is not to write them in but to leave the sentences short enough that the speaker inserts their own.

## 相槌

Japanese listeners backchannel far more frequently than English ones — every few seconds, and its absence is read as inattention or disagreement, not as polite listening. On a phone call, where the speaker cannot see a nod, it is the only signal that the line is alive.

| Form | Register | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| はい / ええ | 丁寧, 敬語 | I am listening (**not** "I agree") |
| はいはい | Casual only | Impatient upward; normal between friends |
| そうですね | All | Agreement, or thinking time |
| なるほど | Peer and downward | Understanding — avoid upward (`business.md`) |
| おっしゃる通りです | 敬語 | The upward form of なるほど |
| そうなんですね | 丁寧 | New information received |
| へえ / ほんと？ | 日常 | Surprise |
| うんうん | 親しい | Casual continuous listening |
| かしこまりました / 承知しました | 敬語 | Received and will act |

**はい does not mean yes.** It means "I am receiving this". A Japanese speaker saying はい throughout a proposal has not agreed to it, and treating those はい as consent is one of the most expensive misreadings in cross-language business.

## Telephone

- **Answering as a company**: お電話ありがとうございます、株式会社◯◯の田中でございます. **もしもし is not used** when answering a business call — it is casual, and its business use is limited to checking a bad line (もしもし、お電話遠いようですが).
- **Calling out**: いつもお世話になっております、株式会社◯◯の田中と申します。◯◯部の小林様はいらっしゃいますでしょうか.
- **Putting someone on hold**: 少々お待ちいただけますでしょうか, and 30 seconds is the working ceiling before coming back to say it will be longer.
- **The person is out**: あいにく田中は席を外しております — own colleague, no 敬称, 謙譲語 (`register.md`). 戻り次第、こちらからご連絡差し上げましょうか offers the callback rather than making them call again.
- **Taking a message**: repeat back the 会社名, the name, the number and the ask, in that order, then 私、田中が承りました — naming yourself is what makes the message traceable.
- **Ending**: 失礼いたします, and the caller hangs up first. Between a company and a customer, the customer hangs up first regardless of who called.
- **Bad news is delivered on a call, not in writing** (`etiquette.md`), then confirmed in an email that references the call.

## Presentations

- **Structure**: 挨拶 → 自己紹介 (one sentence) → 本日お話しすること (three points) → the three points → まとめ → ご清聴ありがとうございました. The three-point announcement is not optional filler; a Japanese audience uses it to allocate attention.
- **Level is 丁寧 with 敬語 on the audience's actions**: ご覧ください, お手元の資料, ご質問がございましたら.
- **ご清聴ありがとうございました** closes it — 清聴 (attentive listening), not 静聴 (silent listening); the second is a common 変換ミス projected onto a slide.
- **Questions**: ご質問はございますか, then repeat each question before answering it — half the room did not hear it.
- **Slide Japanese is 体言止め**: 売上の推移, not 売上が推移しています. A slide in full sentences competes with the speaker.
- **Numbers on slides in 算用数字**, spoken in 漢数字 (above).
- **Silence after a question is normal** and Japanese audiences take longer to volunteer. Wait through it, or name a specific person if the room's norms allow it.

## 自己紹介

Three lengths, and picking the wrong one is the mistake:

| Context | Length | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Business first meeting | 15-20 seconds | 会社名 → 部署 → 名前 → 担当 → よろしくお願いいたします |
| New team / 入社挨拶 | 60-90 seconds | 名前 → 経歴一行 → 担当 → 一言 (a concrete detail, not a hobby list) → 抱負 → よろしくお願いいたします |
| Casual / event | 30 seconds | 名前 → what they do → why they are here → one hook |

- **The name is said slowly and, if it is unusual, spelled out**: 田中太郎と申します。「田んぼの田」に「中」で田中です. Doing this for a foreign name is expected, not apologetic.
- **精一杯頑張ります** is the standard 抱負 closer and reads as sincere despite being formulaic; an original one is not an improvement.
- **A hobby list is not a hook.** One specific fact that gives someone a reason to speak to you afterwards is.

## 乾杯 and Party Speech

- **Length: 1-3 minutes for a 挨拶, under 30 seconds for the 乾杯 itself.** The most common failure is a 乾杯 speech that keeps a room holding full glasses; the second most common is a 挨拶 that runs past three minutes.
- **The 乾杯 shape**: one sentence of thanks → one sentence about the occasion → それでは、皆様ご唱和ください。乾杯！ and the glass goes up on 乾杯.
- **Who gives which**: the most senior person present gives the 開会の挨拶, a slightly less senior one gives the 乾杯 — being asked for the 乾杯 is a compliment and a signal about rank.
- **締めの挨拶** closes the party, often with 一本締め (one clap after よぉー) or 三本締め; 手締め is announced (お手を拝借) so nobody is caught out.
- **Do not speak while people are holding raised glasses.** Everything before 乾杯 happens with glasses on the table.

## Wedding and Funeral Speech

The 忌み言葉 list in `documents.md` applies to spoken text with more force, because a spoken slip cannot be edited.

- **Wedding speech (3-5 minutes)**: congratulate the couple → address the families → one specific story about the person you know → a wish → congratulate again. Remove 切れる, 別れる, 終わる, 帰る, 重ね重ね, 再び, 最後 — including in idioms, where they hide (最後になりましたが is the single most common slip and becomes 結びに).
- **Never mention** a previous relationship, a job change, an illness, or money.
- **弔辞 (funeral address)**: read from paper, slowly, in a flat register. ご冥福をお祈りいたします is Buddhist and is wrong at a Christian or a Shinto service (安らかなお眠りをお祈りいたします / 御霊のご平安をお祈りいたします). 度々, 重ね重ね, and 追って are removed for the same repetition reason.
- **お悔やみの言葉** in person is short and unfinished on purpose: この度は誠にご愁傷様でございます — trailing off is correct, completing the sentence is not.

## Pitch Accent Traps

標準語 distinguishes words by pitch, and a written script cannot show it. The pairs that matter in a business or a social context:

| Word | Two readings |
|---|---|
| 箸 / 橋 / 端 | chopsticks / bridge / edge |
| 雨 / 飴 | rain / candy |
| 神 / 紙 / 髪 | god / paper / hair |
| 今 / 居間 | now / living room |
| 生 / 名 (in compounds) | — |
| 二階 / 二回 | second floor / twice |
| 市立 / 私立 | municipal / private — spoken as いちりつ and わたくしりつ precisely to disambiguate |
| 化学 / 科学 | chemistry / science — spoken as ばけがく to disambiguate |

The last two are the useful pattern: where pitch alone cannot carry the distinction and the stakes are real, Japanese speakers **substitute a different reading**. A script that will be read aloud should carry those substitutions, and any rare name should carry its ルビ (`furigana`).

## Reading Aloud

For a script that someone else will deliver:

- **ルビ on every name, every 難読 kanji, and every number that is spoken differently than it is written.**
- **Pause marks**: a blank line for a full stop, / for a breath. Japanese speakers pause at 文節 boundaries; a mark mid-modifier produces a stumble.
- **One sentence per line**, under 60 characters, because a line that wraps loses the speaker's place.
- **Mark the 相槌 gaps** in a dialogue script — the places where the other party is expected to say はい — or the delivery runs over them.
- **Read the whole thing aloud once before delivering it.** The sentence a native would not say out loud is the one to rewrite, and in spoken text it is usually the one with two modifiers before the noun.

## What Gets Written Down

Destinations, all in `memory-template.md`:

- **Any script that will be delivered or reused** — a 自己紹介, a 乾杯の挨拶, a presentation script, a 弔辞 — → `artifacts/script-<what>.md` with its ルビ, its pause marks and the date it was delivered. Add its `## Boxes` line in the same turn. A speech is re-derived from scratch every time it is not saved.
- **A delivered speech** → a row in `pieces/<year>.md` with its length in minutes and how it landed.
- **A name's reading confirmed out loud** (「東海林です、しょうじと読みます」) → `### Name Readings` with the source, in the same turn. A reading heard from the person is the strongest source there is.
- **A room's conventions** — this client expects the 乾杯 from the second-most-senior person, this company closes with 三本締め, this team never asks questions until asked by name → `## Environment`.
