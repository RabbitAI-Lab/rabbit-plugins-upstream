# Regions — Dialects and the Standard Language

Dialect in Japanese writing is almost always a deliberate act: dialogue, regional marketing, personal chat, or a character. Everything else is 共通語. Governed by `dialect`. **Before writing regional forms for a named reader**, check `## Profile` and their `## Recipients` row in `~/Clawic/data/japanese/memory.md` — using a reader's own dialect back at them is warmth if it is theirs and mockery if it is not.

## 標準語 vs 共通語

Two different things, and Japanese keeps them apart:

- **標準語** is the codified norm built in the Meiji period on educated Tokyo speech — prescriptive, and the word carries a faint judgement that other varieties are deficient.
- **共通語** is the variety people actually use to communicate across regions: standard grammar and vocabulary, tolerant of a regional accent. Broadcasters, textbooks and business writing use this term.

**Write 共通語 and say 共通語.** A Kansai speaker writing a business email writes 共通語 with a Kansai pitch accent when reading it aloud, and nobody calls that a dialect. The default for every channel in this skill is 共通語 unless `dialect` says otherwise.

## The Map

| Group | Covers | The markers you notice first |
|---|---|---|
| 東日本方言 | 関東, 東北, 北海道, 中部 (east) | 〜だ copula, 〜べ / 〜だべ, 東京式 accent |
| 西日本方言 | 近畿, 中国, 四国, 中部 (west) | 〜や copula, 〜へん negative, 〜とる / 〜よる aspect |
| 九州方言 | Kyushu | 〜と？ questions, 〜ばい, 〜たい, 〜けん |
| 琉球語群 | Okinawa, Amami | Not dialects of Japanese — related languages; UNESCO's 2009 atlas lists several as endangered |

The east/west copula line (だ vs や) runs roughly through 中部 and is the single most reliable regional tell in writing, because the copula appears in almost every sentence.

**ウチナーヤマトグチ** — Okinawan-accented Japanese with Okinawan vocabulary — is what most Okinawans under 60 actually speak, and it is distinct from ウチナーグチ, the heritage language. Writing 「めんそーれ」 into a character's mouth is closer to costume than to speech.

## Accent

Pitch is not written, so it never appears in text — and it decides whether a spoken script works.

| System | Where | Property |
|---|---|---|
| 東京式 | Kanto, Hokkaido, most of Tohoku, Kyushu (much of it) | The system 共通語 and broadcasting use |
| 京阪式 | Kinki, Shikoku | Distinguishes more patterns; **箸 and 橋 are pitched the reverse of Tokyo** |
| 無アクセント | Parts of Kyushu and southern Tohoku | No lexical pitch contrast at all |

Consequence for anything read aloud: a script written in 共通語 and delivered by a Kansai speaker is still Kansai-accented, and that is normal on radio and in business. It only matters when a pitch minimal pair carries the meaning, in which case the substitution trick applies (`speaking.md`).

## 関西弁: the Systematic Part

Kansai is the dialect most likely to be written, and the one non-natives most reliably write wrong. The grammar, not the vocabulary, is what makes it real.

| 共通語 | 関西 | Note |
|---|---|---|
| だ / です | や / やねん / どす (Kyoto, dated) | The copula is the whole signature |
| 〜ない | 〜へん / 〜ひん / 〜ん | 行かへん, 食べへん, できひん |
| 〜ている | 〜てる / 〜とる | とる is blunter |
| Ongoing vs completed | 〜よる (in progress) / 〜とる (resultant) | Sharpest in 中国・四国・九州; Osaka mostly uses とる |
| だめ | あかん | From 埒が明かん |
| 違う | ちゃう | ちゃうちゃう as a full denial |
| 本当 | ほんま | |
| とても | めっちゃ / ごっつ / むっちゃ | めっちゃ has crossed nationwide (SKILL.md Word Choice) |
| いい | ええ | ええで, ええやん |
| ありがとう | おおきに | Now mostly shopkeepers and set phrases |
| そうだから | せやから / ほんで | |
| いくら | なんぼ | Also "however much": なんぼ言うても |
| 捨てる | ほかす | |
| 疲れた | しんどい | Kansai origin, now nationwide and unmarked |
| 〜だよね | 〜やんな / 〜やろ | |
| Imperative, soft | 〜し / 〜な | はよ行き, 見てみ |
| Honorific, light | 〜はる | 先生来はった — see below |

**〜はる is the piece outsiders miss.** It is a light honorific used far more casually than 共通語 尊敬語 — in Kyoto for neighbours, family members, even animals; in Osaka more narrowly, for people outside the immediate circle. It lets a Kansai speaker be polite without going up a whole rung, and nothing in 共通語 does that job.

**わ is gender-neutral in Kansai.** 知らんわ from a man is ordinary; the same particle in 共通語 reads as feminine or as fiction (`register.md`).

## Kyoto, Osaka, Kobe

Lumping them together is the amateur move; locals hear the difference immediately.

| | 京都 | 大阪 | 神戸 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Register | Indirect, softened, ornate | Direct, fast, comic | Between the two |
| Progressive | 〜てはる, 〜たはる | 〜とる / 〜てる | 〜とう / 〜よう |
| Signature | 〜どす (archaic, 花街), 〜え | 〜ねん, 〜やん | 〜とう, 〜やんか |
| Famous trait | Meaning delivered obliquely — ぶぶ漬けでもどうどす as a request to leave is the folk example | 商売 register, ボケ/ツッコミ rhythm as ordinary conversation | Softer than Osaka, no Kyoto indirection |

| Region | Markers |
|---|---|

| 熊本・鹿児島 | 〜ばい/〜たい shared; 鹿児島 is among the least mutually intelligible varieties |
| 広島・岡山 | 〜じゃけん, 〜とる, わし |

## Words That Are Not Standard

The dangerous class: ordinary-looking words whose meaning changes by region. These cause real misunderstandings in business, not just flavour.

| Word | 共通語 | Regional |
|---|---|---|
| なおす | Repair | Put away (西日本) — 資料をなおしといて means file it, not fix it |
| 投げる | Throw | Throw away (北海道・東北) |
| えらい | Admirable, important | Tired, or "very" (中部・西日本) |
| かたす | — | Tidy up (関東) |
| ほかす | — | Throw away (関西) |
| しあさって | Three days from now | Order swaps with やのあさって by region — confirm with a calendar date |
| ばんそうこう | Adhesive plaster | カットバン (九州), サビオ (北海道), リバテープ (熊本) |
| 肉まん | Steamed pork bun | 豚まん (関西) |
| 今川焼き | — | 大判焼き, 回転焼き, 御座候 depending on the region |
| マクドナルド | — | マック (関東) / マクド (関西) |

**Any date, instruction or quantity phrased in a regional word gets restated in a neutral one.** なおす in a work instruction and しあさって in a deadline are the two that produce a missed hand-off.

## Half-Dialect Is Worse Than None

The characteristic non-native failure: standard grammar with dialect vocabulary sprinkled on top — 「そうですね、めっちゃ大変やと思います」 mixing 敬体, a Kansai copula and a Kansai intensifier in one sentence. A native reads it as parody, and in fiction it makes a character unplaceable.

Writing a dialect convincingly means committing to its **grammar**: the copula, the negative, the aspect, the question particle, the sentence-final particles. Vocabulary alone is costume.

Three practical positions, in order of safety:

1. **共通語 throughout.** Never wrong, and the default.
2. **共通語 with one or two crossed-over regional words** (めっちゃ, しんどい, ほんま in casual chat). Low risk, reads as natural.
3. **Full dialect**, only when the user or a native speaker of that variety can check it, or when it is being reproduced from something they wrote.

## When Dialect Is a Liability

- **社外 writing, documents, contracts, 履歴書**: zero dialect, without exception.
- **A national audience**: regional forms narrow the reader; a note article in 関西弁 is a stylistic commitment, not a neutral choice.
- **Fiction with a region the writer does not know**: readers from that region are the ones who notice, and they are the ones who comment.
- **Translation into "a dialect" to render a source's regional speech**: mapping a Southern US accent onto 東北弁 imports a class stereotype that was not in the source. Flag the decision to the client rather than making it silently (`fiction.md`).
- **Comedy by dialect** is the oldest device in the book and reads as dated at best.

The inverse is real too: **regional marketing copy in 共通語 underperforms** in the local market, and municipal and tourism copy uses local forms deliberately.

## Regional Business Differences

Not dialect, but they travel with it and cost the same when missed:

- **お中元 timing differs**: roughly July in Kanto, August in Kansai (`documents.md`).
- **香典 and 祝儀 customs vary by region and sect** — the envelope, the ink, the amount conventions.
- **Kansai business does not write 関西弁.** Email is 共通語 敬語; the dialect lives in the meeting and the phone call, and 〜はる may appear in internal chat.
- **Company self-reference** and honorific habits differ by industry more than by region (`business.md`).

## What Gets Written Down

Destinations, all in `memory-template.md`:

- **The reader's or the user's region** → `## Profile`, in one line, because it changes vocabulary defaults everywhere and is guessed wrong otherwise.
- **A regional preference the user states** ("we write to Kansai customers in 共通語, always") → `dialect` in `config.yaml`; it is a declaration, applied from then on.
- **A regional term settled for a project** — the local word chosen for a campaign, a character's variety → `### Terms`, or the character's `characters/<name>.md` sheet.
- **A native check of dialect text** — who verified it and which variety they speak → `### Corrections`, with the reviewer named. A dialect verified by a speaker of the wrong region is not verified.
