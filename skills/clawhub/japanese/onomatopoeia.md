# 擬音語, 擬態語, 擬情語

The vocabulary English has no equivalent for, and therefore the one a translated or model-written text is missing entirely. Japanese uses these words where English uses adverbs, adjectives and verbs of manner — so a text with none of them is not neutral, it is flat. **On a fiction, game or manga project**, read the project's bank in `## Boxes` and the character's sheet in `~/Clawic/data/japanese/characters/<name>.md` before writing: a coined sound has to stay identical across chapters.

## The Five Classes

| Class | Covers | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 擬声語 | Voices, human and animal | わんわん, にゃあ, げらげら, ぺらぺら |
| 擬音語 | Sounds made by things | ガタガタ, ざあざあ, カチッ, ドン |
| 擬態語 | States and appearances, silent | きらきら, つるつる, ぐちゃぐちゃ |
| 擬容語 | Manner of movement or behaviour | うろうろ, のろのろ, てくてく |
| 擬情語 | Internal feeling | いらいら, どきどき, もやもや |

The last three carry no sound at all, which is what makes them invisible to a translator working from English: there is nothing to imitate. **擬態語 and 擬情語 are where the density belongs** in ordinary prose; 擬音語 belongs in fiction, manga and children's writing.

## Sound Symbolism

The system is productive, which means the nuance of a word you have not met is derivable. This is the highest-value section in the file: it turns a vocabulary list into a generator.

| Feature | Adds | Pair |
|---|---|---|
| 清音 (k, s, t, h) | Light, small, clean, quick | さらさら (silky) |
| 濁音 (g, z, d, b) | Heavy, large, coarse, unpleasant | ざらざら (rough, gritty) |
| 半濁音 (p) | Light, bursting, small, cute, childish | ぱらぱら (scattered lightly) |
| っ (促音) | Sudden onset and abrupt stop | ぱっと (in an instant) |
| ん (撥音) | Resonance, elasticity, lingering | ぼんやり (blurred, drifting) |
| ー (長音) | Prolongation, a state held | すーっと (gliding, sustained) |
| り ending | The action completes and settles | ゆっくり (unhurried, arriving) |
| Reduplication (ABAB) | Repetition or continuation | きらきら (twinkling on and on) |

The pairs make it concrete: ころころ (something small rolling) / ごろごろ (something heavy rolling, or idling) · きらきら (sparkling) / ぎらぎら (glaring) · とんとん (light tapping) / どんどん (pounding) · さらさら (smooth) / ざらざら (gritty) · ぱらぱら (light scattering) / ばらばら (falling apart). **Voicing one consonant makes the thing bigger, heavier and usually worse.**

## The Shape Templates

| Template | Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ABAB | Continuous or repeated | きらきら, どきどき, ばたばた |
| ABっと | One sudden instance | ぱっと, きゅっと, さっと |
| ABり | One instance, gently completed | きらり, ぽつり, にやり |
| AっBり | An intensified, settled state | ぐっすり, さっぱり, きっちり, ぴったり |
| AんBり | Relaxed, lingering, soft-edged | のんびり, ほんのり, しんみり, ぼんやり |
| ABん | A single impact | どーん, がーん |

The same root moves between templates and the meaning moves with it: きらきら (twinkling continuously) → きらっと (one flash) → きらり (one flash, with a literary tone). Choosing the wrong template produces a word that exists and describes the wrong duration.

## Grammar

Four frames, and most words take only some of them.

| Frame | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 〜と + verb | Manner of the action; と is optional on many 擬態語 | しっかりと確認する · ぱっと消える |
| 〜する | The state is the predicate | どきどきする · いらいらする · ぼーっとする |
| 〜だ / 〜です | The state is the copula complement | 机の上がぐちゃぐちゃです |
| 〜の + noun | Attributive | ふわふわのパン · つるつるの床 |
| 〜になる | Change into the state | ぼろぼろになる · びしょびしょになる |

Words that only take a verb are the trap: **ぐっすり needs 眠る** (ぐっすりする is not Japanese), しっかり prefers a verb, ばたばた takes both する and と+verb. When unsure, use the と+verb frame — it is the one that almost never fails.

## Script Choice

| Script | For | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ひらがな | 擬態語, 擬情語, soft and organic sounds, prose | ふわふわ, もやもや, しとしと |
| カタカナ | Sharp, mechanical, loud, artificial sounds; manga SFX; emphasis | ガタン, カチッ, ピカピカ, バン |
| 漢字 | Almost never; a few lexicalised ones | 悠々, 続々, 各々 |

The alternation is a stylistic tool, not a rule: writing a normally-hiragana word in katakana makes it sharper and slightly detached (キラキラ in advertising, きらきら in a story). Manga SFX are katakana and hand-lettered, and a translated manga that sets them as body text loses the layer (`fiction.md`, `kanji-and-kana.md`).

## Density By Register

| Register | Density | Which classes |
|---|---|---|
| Casual speech and chat | Very high — several per exchange | All five |
| Children's writing, manga, games | Highest | All five, including coined ones |
| Advertising, food, product copy | High | 擬態語, especially 食感 |
| note, blog, social copy | Moderate — one or two per paragraph | 擬態語, 擬情語 |
| Business email | Low — one, or none | Only the business-safe set below |
| 報告書, 議事録, 稟議書 | Near zero; they read as imprecise | Replace with a figure |
| 契約書, 公文書 | Zero | — |

The reason business writing avoids them is not formality but precision: **ざっくり200万** and **だいたい200万** both say the number is soft, and a 稟議書 needs the number hard (`documents.md`).

## The Working Sets

| Domain | Words |
|---|---|
| Emotion | いらいら (irritated) · わくわく (excited) · どきどき (heart racing) · はらはら (anxious for someone) · もやもや (unresolved unease) · そわそわ (restless) · ほっと (relieved) · しょんぼり (dejected) · うきうき (buoyant) · びくびく (fearful) |
| Body state | ぐっすり (sleeping deeply) · うとうと (dozing) · ぼーっと (vacant) · すっきり (cleared, refreshed) · さっぱり (clean, unburdened) · ぐったり (drained) · へとへと (exhausted) · ふらふら (unsteady) |
| Movement | うろうろ (wandering aimlessly) · ぶらぶら (strolling) · てくてく (plodding on foot) · すたすた (walking briskly) · のろのろ (crawling slowly) · ばたばた (rushing about) · ぐるぐる (going round) · さっさと (get on with it) |
| Weather | しとしと (light steady rain) · ざあざあ (pouring) · ぱらぱら (spitting) · じめじめ (humid, unpleasant) · むしむし (muggy) · ぽかぽか (pleasantly warm) · ひんやり (cool) · しんしん (snow falling in silence) · からっと (dry, clear) |
| Surfaces | つるつる (slippery smooth) · ざらざら (rough) · べたべた (sticky) · さらさら (dry and smooth) · ぬるぬる (slimy) · でこぼこ (uneven) · ぼろぼろ (falling apart) |
| Light and sound | きらきら (twinkling) · ぴかぴか (gleaming, new) · ぎらぎら (glaring) · ガタガタ (rattling) · カチッ (a click) · シーン (silence — the sound of no sound, a Japanese invention) |

シーン is worth its own line: **Japanese has an onomatopoeia for silence**, and it is the clearest single proof that these words are not sound effects.

## Business-Safe 擬態語

The handful that appear in 敬体 business writing without lowering the register:

| Word | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| しっかり | Thoroughly, solidly | しっかりと確認いたします |
| きちんと | Properly, to the standard | きちんとご説明いたします |
| はっきり | Clearly, unambiguously | はっきりとした回答をいただけますでしょうか |
| じっくり | Taking the time it needs | じっくり検討いたします |
| ぎりぎり | Right at the limit | 納期ぎりぎりの状況です |
| どんどん / だんだん | Steadily increasing / gradually | だんだん改善しております |
| そろそろ | It is about that time | そろそろご返信をいただければ幸いです |
| ばたばた | Chaotically busy — the standard business excuse | ばたばたしておりまして、ご返信が遅れました |

ちゃんと is the casual twin of きちんと and does not belong in 社外 writing. ざっくり and なあなあ are business slang, not 擬態語 usage (`slang.md`).

## Pain and Symptoms

Japanese medicine runs on onomatopoeia: the question a doctor asks is どんな痛みですか, and the answer is expected to be one of these. A patient who cannot supply one is hard to treat.

| Word | The pain it names |
|---|---|
| ずきずき | Throbbing, in time with the pulse |
| がんがん | Pounding, usually a headache |
| きりきり | Sharp and twisting, usually the stomach |
| ちくちく | Prickling, like needles |
| ひりひり | Burning, stinging on the surface |
| じんじん | Numb tingling |
| しくしく | Dull and persistent |
| ぐらぐら | Unsteady, or a loose tooth |
| むかむか | Nauseous |
| ぜーぜー | Wheezing |

## Food and Texture

食感 vocabulary is most of Japanese food writing, and a food description without it reads as a specification.

もちもち (chewy-elastic) · さくさく (crisp and light) · ふわふわ (fluffy) · とろとろ (melting, thick) · しゃきしゃき (crunchy-fresh, vegetables) · ぱりぱり (crackling thin) · ぷりぷり (springy, prawns) · ほくほく (floury-warm, potatoes) · こってり (rich, heavy) · あっさり (light, clean) · ジューシー (loanword, now standard) · とろける (verb, melts).

こってり and あっさり are the axis on which ramen, curry and broth are described and ordered; they are not decorative.

## What Models Get Wrong

- **Under-production.** There is nothing in the English source to translate, so nothing appears. A Japanese text describing rain, food, fatigue, or a feeling with zero 擬態語 is one of the reliable machine signals (`ai-tells.md`).
- **Over-production in the wrong register.** Three 擬態語 in a 報告書 reads as childish, which is the opposite failure and just as visible.
- **Wrong template for the duration.** きらきら for a single flash, ぱっと for a continuous state.
- **Wrong voicing.** ざらざら where さらさら was meant inverts the sentence's judgement of the thing.
- **Inventing one in prose.** Coining is legitimate in manga, games and children's writing and nowhere else; in an article or an email a coined sound reads as a typo.
- **Treating them as informal by default.** しっかり and きちんと are register-neutral; ぐちゃぐちゃ and べたべた are not. The class is not the register.

## What Gets Written Down

Destinations, all in `memory-template.md`:

- **A coined or project-specific sound** — a manga SFX set, a game's UI sounds, a brand's texture vocabulary → `artifacts/onomatopoeia-bank-<project>.md`, with the script it is set in and where it has already appeared, plus its `## Boxes` line in the same turn. Consistency across chapters is the whole value.
- **A character whose voice is 擬態語-heavy or 擬態語-free** → their `characters/<name>.md` sheet, in the vocabulary line.
- **A rendering settled for a recurring term** — the katakana form of a product sound, a 食感 word the client insists on → `### Terms`.
- **A native correction to a choice** (ずきずき for きりきり, ぎらぎら for きらきら) → `### Corrections` with who said so. These corrections are worth more than most, because the distinctions are invisible to non-natives and obvious to readers.
