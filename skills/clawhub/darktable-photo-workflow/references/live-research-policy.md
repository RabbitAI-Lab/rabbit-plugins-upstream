# Live research policy

## Use the local foundation without browsing when

- the task is a tentative formal comparison to an established, well-documented tradition;
- no exact date, quotation, work title, current exhibition, or disputed historical claim is needed;
- the analogy remains explicitly bounded and the reference cards cover it.

## Browse and verify when

- a photographer, movement, region, or period is absent or thinly covered here;
- the work is contemporary, emerging, local, or likely to have changed context;
- exact series titles, dates, chronology, affiliations, quotations, or biographical claims matter;
- the user asks for sources, reading, or publication-ready text;
- a comparison is ambiguous or could reproduce a familiar but inaccurate art-historical shortcut.

## Source order

Prefer:

1. the artist's or estate's official archive;
2. museum collection and exhibition pages;
3. original books, catalogues, interviews, and scholarly articles;
4. established galleries or editorial publications with named authors;
5. tertiary summaries only for discovery, not as the sole support for a precise claim.

Search the photographer plus a series/work and one institution. Verify the claim on the opened page rather than relying on a search snippet. For contested interpretation, use at least two independent authoritative sources.

## Citation and update discipline

- Link each factual historical claim near the sentence it supports.
- Paraphrase rather than accumulating quotations.
- Keep live findings in the answer unless the user explicitly asks to update the project skill or long-term memory.
- Add a new card only after it recurs in actual analyses or fills a demonstrated geographic, historical, or genre gap.
