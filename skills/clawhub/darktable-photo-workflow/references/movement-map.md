# First-edition movement and practice map

This is a routing map, not a closed canon. Use mechanisms and differences, not labels alone.

## Pictorialism

**Context:** Late nineteenth- and early twentieth-century practitioners argued for photography as expressive art, often using labor-intensive printing and manipulation. The movement included international networks such as the Linked Ring and the Photo-Secession. Sources: [The Met — International Pictorialism](https://www.metmuseum.org/fr/essays/international-pictorialism), [The Met — Pictorialism in America](https://www.metmuseum.org/en/essays/pictorialism-in-america).

**Look for:** controlled softness, atmospheric tonal mass, handworked surface, allegorical or poetic staging, and a print object whose material treatment matters.

**Do not infer from:** shallow depth of field, mist, or warm toning alone.

**Editing implication:** preserve luminous transitions; diffusion or grain is optional and must not erase important structure.

## Straight photography and modernist description

**Context:** Early twentieth-century debates increasingly valued camera-specific clarity and direct description; Stieglitz's circle and Paul Strand are central to the transition. Source: [The Met — Our Future Is In The Air](https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/listings/2010/our-future-is-in-the-air).

**Look for:** precise description, disciplined geometry, tonal clarity, everyday forms treated as sufficient subjects, and minimal evidence of painterly handwork.

**Do not infer from:** sharpness alone.

**Editing implication:** protect edge clarity and tonal separation; avoid decorative effects that obscure structure.

## New Vision

**Context:** In the 1920s and 1930s, portable cameras and experimental modernist practice encouraged unusual viewpoints, cropping, photograms, montage, and new relations between design and photography. Source: [The Met — The New Vision of Photography](https://www.metmuseum.org/ko/essays/the-new-vision-of-photography).

**Look for:** steep angles, radical scale shifts, diagonal organization, abstraction from ordinary objects, fragmentation, or graphic shadow.

**Do not infer from:** any tilted frame or architectural geometry.

**Editing implication:** reinforce directional structure and graphic separation without forcing a generic high-contrast poster look.

## Social, lyrical, and personal documentary

**Context:** Documentary photography ranges from descriptive social evidence to highly personal sequencing. Walker Evans joined plain description to a “lyrical documentary” approach; Robert Frank's *The Americans* used selection and sequence to form a subjective account. Sources: [MoMA — Walker Evans](https://www.moma.org/artists/1777-walker-evans), [National Gallery of Art — Looking In: Robert Frank's The Americans](https://www.nga.gov/exhibitions/looking).

**Look for:** culturally specific detail, social relation, off-center or interrupted moments, ambivalence, and meaning created across a sequence.

**Do not infer from:** black-and-white street subject matter alone.

**Editing implication:** retain contextual details and mixed tonal information; do not polish away tension or ordinary evidence.

## New Documents and subjective street observation

**Context:** MoMA's 1967 *New Documents* presented Diane Arbus, Lee Friedlander, and Garry Winogrand as redirecting documentary photography toward more personal ends. Source: [MoMA — New Documents](https://www.moma.org/calendar/exhibitions/3487).

**Look for:** self-aware framing, social friction, reflections or obstructions, peripheral incidents, awkwardness, and an observer whose position remains visible in the picture's logic.

**Do not infer from:** crooked horizons or casual timing alone.

**Editing implication:** protect edge activity and ambiguity; correct only distractions that are not structurally useful.

## New Color and the everyday visual diary

**Context:** William Eggleston made color and ordinary subject matter central through a “democratic” way of looking. Stephen Shore used changing formats to record vernacular American culture; *American Surfaces* operates as a color visual diary. Sources: [MoMA — William Eggleston](https://www.moma.org/artists/1690-william-eggleston), [MoMA — Stephen Shore](https://www.moma.org/calendar/exhibitions/3769), [MoMA — American Surfaces](https://www.moma.org/calendar/exhibitions/4839).

**Look for:** color relationships carrying structure, ordinary things granted equal attention, vernacular signs, descriptive daylight/flash, and serial accumulation.

**Do not infer from:** saturation or banal subject matter alone.

**Editing implication:** tune relationships between colors before global saturation; retain plausible material color and unforced light.

## New Topographics

**Context:** The 1975 exhibition framed a restrained view of human-altered landscapes and opposed inherited Romantic landscape expectations. Sources: [George Eastman Museum — New Topographics](https://www.eastman.org/new-topographics-tour-organized-george-eastman-house-and-center-creative-photography-begins-june-13), [Getty AAT — New Topographics](https://www.getty.edu/vow/AATFullDisplay?find=&logic=AND&note=earthenware&subjectid=300411288).

**Look for:** subdivisions, industrial margins, parking, utility systems, measured distance, repeated structures, and restrained or clinical description.

**Do not infer from:** empty landscapes or overcast light alone.

**Editing implication:** preserve spatial information, neutral relationships, and modest contrast; avoid romantic skies or heroic color unless the image contradicts the tradition deliberately.

## Typology and Düsseldorf-associated practice

**Context:** Bernd and Hilla Becher photographed industrial structures frontally under consistent conditions and presented them in grids. Later Düsseldorf-associated artists expanded systematic description, monumental scale, and questions about image surface. Sources: [MoMA — Winding Towers](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/136060), [MoMA — Thomas Ruff, Portrait](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/50870), [MoMA — Thomas Struth, Le Lignon](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/49427), [MoMA — Andreas Gursky](https://www.moma.org/calendar/exhibitions/170).

**Look for:** frontal consistency, serial comparison, neutral light, large descriptive fields, repetition, system-scale viewpoint, or deliberate psychological blankness.

**Do not infer from:** symmetry or architecture alone.

**Editing implication:** maintain consistency across the series, precise verticals where intentional, even tone, and legible fine structure.

## Provoke and postwar Japanese experimental photography

**Context:** Founded in 1968, *Provoke* pursued experimental photographic language often summarized as *are-bure-boke*—rough, blurred, out of focus—within a wider response to postwar Japanese media and urban experience. Sources: [MoMA — For the Sake of Thought: Provoke](https://www.moma.org/explore/inside_out/2013/01/25/for-the-sake-of-thought-provoke-1968-1970/), [SFMOMA — Focus on Japanese Photography](https://www.sfmoma.org/publication/focus-japanese-photography/contents/).

**Look for:** unstable perception, fractured urban space, aggressive grain/contrast, movement, cropping, serial rhythm, and tension between record and subjective experience.

**Do not infer from:** blur, grain, or black-and-white alone.

**Editing implication:** preserve purposeful instability; avoid simulated grain or crushed tone when the capture lacks the underlying spatial and temporal logic.

## Intimate diary and chosen community

**Context:** Nan Goldin's *The Ballad of Sexual Dependency* is a long-form slideshow and diary built from intimate access to friends and chosen family. Source: [Whitney Museum — The Ballad of Sexual Dependency](https://whitney.org/collection/works/8274).

**Look for:** reciprocal proximity, flash or available light, private environments, emotional duration, vulnerability, and meaning accumulated across relationships and time.

**Do not infer from:** a candid party snapshot alone.

**Editing implication:** prioritize bodies, skin, ambient color, and relational detail; excessive cleanup can remove lived specificity.

## Conceptual and staged photography

**Context:** Conceptual artists used photography as procedure, evidence, sequence, and distribution rather than only as autonomous print. Cindy Sherman's *Untitled Film Stills* deploy generic cinematic codes and performed roles without illustrating actual films. Sources: [The Met — Conceptual Art and Photography](https://www.metmuseum.org/ko/essays/conceptual-art-and-photography), [MoMA — Cindy Sherman](https://www.moma.org/interactives/exhibitions/1997/sherman/).

**Look for:** rule-based series, captions or instructions, repetition, constructed identity, generic media codes, self-performance, and tension between image and claim.

**Do not infer from:** any posed portrait.

**Editing implication:** preserve the conceptual rule and cross-image consistency; polish only when it serves the constructed code.

## Contemporary urban and everyday lyric practices

**Context:** Asian photographic practices are internally diverse. Fan Ho used Hong Kong streets, layered light, and cinematic construction; Michael Wolf examined high-rise systems and street vernacular; Rinko Kawauchi uses restrained lyrical attention to ordinary life's small events. Sources: [M+ — Fan Ho and the decisive moment](https://www.mplus.org.hk/en/magazine/ho-fan-on-the-decisive-moment/), [M+ — Michael Wolf](https://www.mplus.org.hk/en/magazine/from-high-rises-to-the-street-a-look-back-at-michael-wolfs-photographs/), [SFMOMA — Rinko Kawauchi](https://www.sfmoma.org/artist/%E5%B7%9D%E5%86%85%E5%80%AB%E5%AD%90/).

**Look for:** the specific mechanism—layered theatrical light and human scale; compressed urban repetition and found vernacular; or delicate color/light and ordinary transience.

**Do not infer from:** “Asian mood,” generic minimalism, silhouettes, or high-rise facades. Keep the comparisons specific and avoid regional essentialism.

**Editing implication:** protect the actual mechanism: directed light, repeating system detail, or delicate tonal/color transitions.
