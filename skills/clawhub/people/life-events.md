# Life Events — The Messages That Are Hard To Write

These are the moments where the address book earns everything it cost: knowing that it happened, knowing when to say something, and knowing what not to say. They are also the moments where a generic message is actively worse than silence.

**Read the person's record before drafting anything** — the last log entry, `do not raise`, their channel, and whether an earlier event is still live. **Read `do-not-surface.md`** before any message is proposed.

## Timing Table

| Event | First message | Second message | Register |
|---|---|---|---|
| Death of someone close to them | Within 48 hours | 4-6 weeks later, when everyone else has stopped | Short, no philosophy, no silver lining |
| Their own serious diagnosis | Within days of being told | On their stated terms; ask once how they want contact | Normal, not funereal — most people report the shift in tone as the worst part |
| Job loss or layoff | Within a week | 3-4 weeks, when the initial support fades | Warm, zero advice |
| Business failure | Within a week | 1-2 months | Treat as a loss, not a lesson |
| New job or promotion | Within 2 days | 6-8 weeks: "how is it actually going" | Specific, unfussy |
| Birth or adoption | 2 weeks after, not week one | 3 months, when help has evaporated | Practical over lyrical |
| Wedding or engagement | Within days | — | Whatever fits the relationship |
| Separation or divorce | Only if they told the user directly | Later, and about them, not about it | No side-taking, no opinion on the ex |
| Move to a new place | Day of arrival | 30 days | Curious, low-effort to answer |
| Retirement | Within a week | 2-3 months, which is when it gets strange | Ask about the days, not the plans |
| A public failure or humiliation | Within 48 hours, privately | 2 weeks | Say nothing about the event's merits |
| Anything else that changed their life | Within 48 hours of learning it | When the crowd thins | Specific, short, no ask |

The pattern across every row: **the second message is the one that matters and almost nobody sends it.** Support arrives in a wave and then vanishes, usually right as the person starts actually needing it.

## Bereavement

- **Say their name.** "I'm so sorry about Peter" beats "sorry for your loss" by a distance; the fear of causing pain by naming the dead person is misplaced, and the avoidance is what stings.
- **No reply needed, stated explicitly.** A grieving person is managing an inbox of obligations. "No need to reply" removes the message from that pile.
- **Offer something with a shape, or offer nothing.** "Let me know if you need anything" transfers the work of asking. "I'm bringing food Thursday unless you say otherwise" does not.
- **Nothing about meaning.** Not a better place, not everything happens for a reason, not how strong they are being. If it would appear on a condolence card, it has already been said forty times this week.
- **Mark the follow-up at 4-6 weeks** as an open loop with a date, because the calendar is the only thing that will remember it.
- Store the date only if they mark it; the rules for hard anniversaries are in `dates.md`. The person who died goes on `do-not-surface.md` in the same turn.

## Illness And Diagnosis

- **Ask once how they want to handle it** — updates or distraction, direct questions or none, who else knows — and record the answer as the handling instruction on their record. Asking every time makes them the manager of the user's uncertainty.
- **Do not become an information channel.** Never relay their medical news to a third party, including the parts already public; the person decides who knows.
- **Record the topic, not the content**, at `sensitive_details: minimal` (`privacy.md`).
- **Keep treating them normally.** The most consistently reported grievance is that everyone started speaking carefully. Ordinary conversation is the gift.
- **Practical offers beat emotional ones**: a specific lift, a specific meal, a specific afternoon.
- Never send an article about their condition. Not once.

## Job Loss And Business Failure

- **First message within a week, with no advice in it.** Advice arriving in the first weeks lands as an explanation of the person's failure.
- **Do not ask what happened.** They have told the story too many times. "That's rough — I'm around" is enough.
- **Offer the concrete thing the user can actually do**: an introduction, a reference, a name. One offer, delivered, beats a list of offers implied.
- **The second message at 3-4 weeks** is the useful one: by then the sympathy has stopped and the search is real.
- Update the record: employer field to `between roles, since <date>`, not deleted. Deleting the employer erases the search path to everyone they worked with.
- A business failure is a bereavement in disguise; it is not a case study, and "what will you do differently" is the wrong question for months.

## New Job, Promotion, Launch

- **Within two days, or it is a formality.** The window where congratulations feel like attention is short.
- **Name the specific thing** — what they are leaving as well as what they are joining, or the detail of the launch that shows the user actually looked.
- **The 6-8 week message is the differentiator**: "how is it actually going" arrives exactly when the honeymoon ends and nobody is asking anymore.
- Update role, employer, and start date on the record; the start date becomes the work anniversary (`dates.md`).
- If the move is a downgrade or a sideways step they are unhappy about, congratulate the move and nothing else.

## Birth And Adoption

- **Week one is noise.** The message that arrives at two weeks is read; the one on day one is not.
- **Practical over lyrical.** Food, an errand, an hour of childcare, or simply "no reply needed".
- **Do not ask for photos**, do not comment on the mother's recovery or appearance, and do not ask about the next one.
- Record the child's **name and birth year** on the parent's record. The child is not a contact.
- **Three months** is the follow-up: help has evaporated, leave has ended, and it is the hardest stretch.
- Adoption follows the same timing, without any reference to how the child arrived unless the parents raise it.

## Separation And Divorce

- **Only respond if they told the user directly.** Knowing through the network is not the same as being told, and acting on it exposes the network.
- **No opinion on the ex, ever** — including agreement. Reconciliations happen, and a recorded opinion outlives the split.
- **Do not pick a side of a shared friendship**; maintain both separately and do not carry information between them. If both are in the roster, note the split on both records and note that they must never be briefed about each other.
- Update the partner field on both records the same day. Congratulating someone on an anniversary that ended is one of the few record failures with no recovery.
- The children stay on both records.
- There is no anniversary to mark and nothing to schedule.

## Moves

- **Day of arrival**: a short message, nothing required.
- **Thirty days**: the real one. The novelty has ended, the boxes are unpacked, and no one has called.
- Update the city on the record immediately — it is a search field, and a stale city sends the user to the wrong dinner (`search.md`).
- If they moved to a city where the user knows others, an introduction is the highest-value thing available (`introductions.md`).
- A move for a bad reason (a split, a job loss, a family illness) is that event first and a move second.

## The Universal Rules

1. **Specific beats eloquent.** One concrete detail from the record outperforms any amount of well-written sympathy.
2. **Short.** Every one of these messages is better at three lines than at ten.
3. **No ask, ever, in any of these messages** — not a favor, not a meeting, not a reply.
4. **Never on a schedule and never automated.** A message sent by a system for an event of this weight is a discovered fraud (SKILL.md Traps).
5. **The second message is the differentiator.** Set it as an open loop with a date at the moment the first one is sent, or it will not happen.
6. **The user sends it.** Drafts are produced here; nothing is dispatched.

**Write in the same turn**: the event and its date on the person's record; the changed field (employer, partner, city, child) updated rather than appended; the second-message follow-up into `## Open Loops` in `~/Clawic/data/people/memory.md` with its date; any recurring date into `## Dates`; a death onto `~/Clawic/data/people/do-not-surface.md`. A message that worked and is worth having as a pattern goes to `~/Clawic/data/people/artifacts/<kebab-name>.md` with its `## Boxes` line (`memory-template.md`).
