# Network — Weak Ties, Favors, And Not Being Transactional

The professional half of an address book behaves differently from the personal half: the value sits in the people the user barely knows, the maintenance cost per person has to be near zero, and the whole thing collapses the moment it starts feeling like accounting.

**Read the person's record and `## Open Loops` before any ask** — what is already owed in each direction changes both whether to ask and how.

## Why The Weak Ties Matter

Mark Granovetter's finding on job-finding is the anchor: opportunities arrive disproportionately through **acquaintances rather than close friends**, because close contacts move in the same information circles as the user and know the same openings. In his interview study, most who found work through a contact did so through someone they saw occasionally or rarely, not through a close friend.

Two operational consequences, and they are not intuitive:

1. **The `orbit` tier is where the professional value lives**, and it is precisely the tier this skill never nudges about. That is deliberate: weak ties cannot be maintained on a cadence, only reactivated on an occasion.
2. **Breadth beats depth for information, depth beats breadth for support.** They are different rosters serving different needs, and applying one's rules to the other produces either a nagging system or an empty one.

Ronald Burt's structural-holes work adds the other half: the advantage accrues to people who **bridge groups that are not otherwise connected**. Practically, the highest-value line in the whole address book is often the group map — who does not know whom (`memory-template.md`).

## Maintaining A Wide Roster Cheaply

Nothing scheduled survives at 150 people. What works is occasion-driven and near-free:

| Move | Cost | When it fires |
|---|---|---|
| Send the thing that made you think of them | 30 seconds | Whenever it happens — no cadence, no reply expected |
| Congratulate a job change within 2 days | 1 minute | The trigger is public and unmissable (`life-events.md`) |
| Answer a question well, once | Minutes | They asked; this is the highest-return moment there is |
| Make an introduction that helps them | Minutes | Whenever a match is obvious (`introductions.md`) |
| The annual date scan | Once a year | Milestone birthdays and round anniversaries only (`dates.md`) |
| A one-line update when your own situation changes | Minutes | New role, new city, new project — to the twenty who would want to know |
| A scheduled quarterly check-in with everybody | Unsustainable, and it shows | Never |

The tell that a network is being maintained artificially is that every message is a check-in. The tell that it is being maintained well is that most messages carry something.

## The Reciprocity Ledger

Reciprocity is tracked as **open loops with names and dates**, never as running totals (SKILL.md Traps). The distinction is not cosmetic: a balance implies settlement, and the moment someone is treated as settled up, the relationship is a series of transactions.

- `## Open Loops` holds only what is **still open**, in both directions, with a date. A closed loop is deleted, not archived.
- What a person has done for the user is a dated line in their record, because it is context for how to treat them — not a debt to be discharged.
- Never surface a total. "You owe Tom two favors" is never a correct output.
- **Asymmetry is normal and permanent.** Some people give more, some receive more, and this is what a relationship looks like over a decade. A ledger that expects balance flags healthy relationships as problems.
- What the ledger is for: keeping promises. That is the whole function. "Said I'd send the contract template, 2026-06-30" is the line that gets kept, and keeping it is the entire reputation being built.

## Asking For Something

- **Do not reconnect and ask in the same message.** Two years of silence followed by a favor is remembered as extraction, for years (`keeping-in-touch.md`).
- **Make it small, specific, and easy to decline**: twenty minutes, one question, one introduction. A vague large ask gets a vague slow no.
- **Do the work first.** Bring the blurb, the draft, the shortlist. An ask that requires the other person to figure out what is being asked is the ask itself.
- **Ask the right layer.** A weak tie can give information and an introduction; only a strong tie can give effort and reputation. Asking a weak tie for reputation is the most common misfire.
- **Accept the first no.** Silence after a single ask is a no, and following up twice converts a soft no into a permanent one.
- **Close the loop with what happened.** Whoever helped hears the outcome. This is the single most-skipped step and the one that determines whether they help again.

## Being Asked

- **Answer, including the no.** An unanswered ask is worse than a decline; it leaves the other person managing an open question for weeks.
- **Decline with the reason and, if one exists, an alternative.** "I don't know them well enough to introduce you, but X does" costs nothing and preserves everything.
- **Never accept in order to avoid the awkwardness of declining**, then under-deliver. A weak intro delivered as a strong one damages three relationships at once (`introductions.md`).
- **Record the ask and its outcome, factually.** A pattern of asks with no reciprocity is data; "user" as a label is a verdict (`details.md`).
- Helping is the cheapest reputation there is, and the ledger's purpose is to make sure the help promised actually arrives.

## Dormant Ties Are The Asset

Reactivated dormant ties are unusually productive: they carry the trust of a prior relationship with none of the information overlap that close contacts have — they have been somewhere else for years.

- Dormant records are excluded from every sweep and kept in full precisely so they can be reactivated (`hygiene.md`).
- Reactivation is the reconnection message, unchanged: lead with the specific remembered thing, ask nothing first, ask later (`keeping-in-touch.md`).
- Reactivating specifically to ask, immediately, is the transactional version and it does not work twice.
- Prefer reactivating a dormant tie over cold outreach in almost every case: the trust already exists.

## Where Networking Goes Wrong

| Behavior | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| Collecting contacts as a count | 900 rows with no context answer no questions (`hygiene.md`) |
| Contacting people only when raising, hiring, or job-hunting | The pattern is visible in the timestamps, and people notice by the second cycle |
| "Let's grab coffee" with no agenda | Costs an hour each and produces nothing anybody can act on |
| Treating every relationship as a potential lead | This is `crm`, and mixing it into a personal address book kills the personal half |
| Keeping score | The tracking shows up in the writing before it shows up in the behavior |
| Optimizing who to know | Value shows up from the person nobody would have ranked; the roster is not a portfolio |
| Ignoring the people already known | Reactivating a dormant tie beats a cold introduction nearly every time |

**Write in the same turn**: every promise, favor and ask into `## Open Loops` in `~/Clawic/data/people/memory.md` with a name, a direction and a date; the outcome as a dated line on the person's record; a closed loop deleted, not marked done; group bridges and who-does-not-know-whom into `## Groups` (`memory-template.md`).
