# Customs & Import Quick Guide for Souvenirs

> Not legal advice. Rules change. Always check your destination→home pair on the official customs site before buying food, plants, or animal products. This guide tells you **which questions to ask**.

## Risk Tiers

| Tier | Examples | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|---|
| 🟢 Green | Textiles, ceramics, wood carvings, metalwork, paper | Almost always fine; mind weight/fragility |
| 🟡 Yellow | Dried food, tea, spices, honey, cured meats, cheese | Declared and often OK in checked luggage; quantities capped |
| 🟠 Orange | Fresh produce, seeds, soil-containing items, animal-derived (feathers, shells, wool untreated) | Often prohibited; destination- and pest-status-dependent |
| 🔴 Red | Ivory, tortoiseshell, coral, skins of protected species, certain hardwoods (rosewood/ebony) | CITES — can be illegal to import even in small "souvenir" amounts |

## By Traveler Home Region

### United States (CBP)
- **Food:** meat products (even canned) from most countries prohibited; hard cheese OK if declared; dried spices/tea generally fine. Honey OK (declared).
- **Wood:** must be free of bark/insects; some hardwoods need CITES.
- **Absolutes:** ivory (including "pre-ban" antiques without paperwork), tortoiseshell, most coral, agarwood (oud) products without permits.
-declare everything — undeclared food fines start high even for an apple.

### Australia & New Zealand (biosecurity, the strictest)
- **Everything organic gets inspected.** Wooden items must be declared and may be fumigated.
- **Food:** most meat/dairy/egg prohibited; honey prohibited into NZ and AU requires declaration.
- **Shells/feathers/sand:** sand is confiscatable; some shells (abalone limits) restricted.
- Practical tip: pack organic souvenirs in one accessible bag section for inspection.

### European Union
- **Within EU:** free movement.
- **Into EU:** meat/dairy from outside generally prohibited; cheese from some neighbors OK. CITES applies as everywhere.
- Counterfeit goods ("designer" markets in Italy/Turkey/Thailand) can be seized **and fined at the border**, even for one fake bag for personal use in France/Italy.

### United Kingdom
- Post-Brexit rules mirror EU for food; personal meat/dairy allowances from the EU now limited.
- Ivory Act 2018: deals with ivory items broadly banned, narrow antiques exemption with registration.

### Canada
- Similar to US for food; wood items must be bark-free or declared.

## Common Surprise Confiscations

1. **Unglazed terracotta with soil traces** (flowerpots, some tags) — soil imports are universally bad news.
2. **Coral & shells** — picked up on a beach, still restricted in many home countries (especially into AU/NZ/US for coral).
3. **Oud/agarwood perfume & incense** — CITES Appendix II; needs permit, commonly seized.
4. **Dried sausages from Spain/Italy into US/AU** — classic holiday confiscation.
5. **"Rosewood" chopsticks/boxes from Asia** — Dalbergia species are CITES II; even small items technically need paperwork.
6. **Kava** (Pacific) — restricted into several countries.

## Packing Strategy

- Fragile ceramics: wrap in clothes, carry on if under weight limits.
- Liquids >100ml (olive oil, wine, spirits above duty-free): checked luggage only; mind airline weight.
- Food: original sealed packaging wherever possible; keep receipts for declaration value.
- Documents: keep CITES/origination receipts with the item, not in your wallet.

## Declaring Is Cheap, Seizure Is Not

In nearly every jurisdiction, a declared prohibited item is simply confiscated (or mailed back at your expense). An undeclared one is a fine and a flag on your passport record. When in doubt: declare.
