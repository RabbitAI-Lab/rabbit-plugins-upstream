# Authenticity Tells: Real Handcraft vs Factory "Handmade"

A per-material field guide. Mass producers know travelers want "authentic" — the word on the label means nothing; the object's construction means everything.

## Leather

| Real | Factory fake |
|---|---|
| Pores/fat wrinkles visible grain, slight color variation | Uniform "corrected" grain, plastic sheen |
| Vegetal smell (slightly barnyard) | Chemical smell or no smell |
| Cut edges show fibrous structure | Smooth sealed edges, fabric middle layer |
| Burns to ash without melting | Melts/balls — contains PVC |

Ask: "vegetable-tanned or chrome?" Real artisan workshops in Fez/Tuscany/Florence love this question; resellers deflect.

## Textiles & Weaving

- **Look at the reverse.** Handwoven (Pais/Peruvian/Oaxacan) shows slight irregularities and carried threads; machine weave is perfectly uniform.
- **Selvedge:** hand-loomed fabric has natural selvedge; power-loom imitates it — check for float length consistency.
- **Dye:** natural dyes (cochineal red, indigo) bleed slightly onto a damp white cloth **before washing** and shade varies across the piece. Azo "hand-dyed" is uniform.
- **Kilims/carpets:** count knots per inch and look at the fringe — real hand-knot fringe is the warp threads continuing; sewn-on fringe = machine-made.

## Ceramics

- **Feel the base.** Hand-thrown has a slightly rough foot ring; mold-made is perfectly smooth with mold seams.
- **Glaze edges:** hand-painted decoration shows brush ridges and tiny overruns; transfer-print decals look photographic under a loupe.
- Delft/Blue pottery outside the licensed region: the genuine article carries a maker's mark; "Delftware-style" is the tell word for imitation.
- Tap: high-fired porcelain rings; low-fired earthenware thuds (fine for decorative, bad for food).

## Metalwork (silver, brass, pewter)

- **Silver:** look for 925 hallmarks, but in bazaars test with a magnet (silver non-magnetic — though so are nickel alloys; combined with weight and tarnish it's a decent field check). Real silver tarnishes; "never tarnish" alloys are plated or fake.
- **Damascene/Bidri/engraving:** hand-engraved lines vary in depth; etched/electroplated patterns are uniform.
- Weight: solid brass/bronze is heavy; "brass-look" aluminum is suspiciously light.

## Wood

- **Balsa-light "ebony/rosewood"** is dyed softwood. Real dense hardwoods are heavy for their size.
- Hand-carved faces/figures: asymmetric detail, tool marks under magnification; CNC copies are flawless but lifeless — flawlessness in "carved" work is the fake tell.
- Intarsia/marquetry: gaps filled with sawdust glue = workshop; perfect hairline joins = factory laser.

## Paper & Print (washi, marbled, block prints)

- Deckled (rough) edges and visible fibers/laid lines = handmade paper.
- Block-printed textiles/paper show slight misregistration between colors and pressure variation; screen prints are flat-uniform.

## Food & Consumables

- Single-origin/specialty items: ask for the producer, harvest year (tea, olive oil, saffron), and sealed packaging. Open-air "saffron" sold loose is very often dyed corn silk — real saffron threads are trumpet-shaped, deep red with a lighter tip, and smell honey-hay, not metallic.
- Market stalls selling "local honey" in reused branded jars: verify the beekeeper name; crystallization is normal and a mild positive signal.

## The Three Question Test (works anywhere)

1. **"Who made this?"** Artisan-sourced sellers name a person/workshop; trap sellers name a style.
2. **"Can I watch it being made?"** In real craft districts (Fez tanneries, Murano glass, Chiang Mai umbrellas) the workshop is adjacent and proud.
3. **"What's it made of, exactly?"** Specific material answers beat "genuine quality."

If all three get vague answers, you're buying a memory, not a craft — fine if you pay memory prices, not craft prices.
