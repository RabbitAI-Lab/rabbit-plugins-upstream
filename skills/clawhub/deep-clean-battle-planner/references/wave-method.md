# The Wave Method — Theory of Order in Deep Cleaning

## Why order matters more than effort
Cleaning moves soil from one place to another until it leaves the house.
Every out-of-order step creates rework:
- Dusting after mopping → re-mop.
- Cleaning floors before the shelves above them → dirt rains on the clean.
- Wiping before dwell chemistry works → 3× scrubbing.

The wave method sequences work so soil only moves DOWN and OUT.

## Soil taxonomy
| Soil | Solvent/action | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Loose particulate | vacuum/dry microfiber | dust, crumbs, pet hair |
| Greasy film | alkaline surfactant + dwell | kitchen cabinets, hood |
| Mineral deposit | acid dissolves scale | hard-water spots, faucets |
| Organic stain | enzyme or oxidizer | grass, blood, pet, mold |
| Biofilm | surfactant + dwell + agitation | shower grout, sink drains |

Pick the wrong chemistry and effort multiplies: alkaline on scale does
nothing; acid on grease does nothing. Hence `products` before spraying.

## Wave ordering rules (the sort behind `plan`)
1. **Dust/shedding work first** — fans, blinds, tops of cabinets: these
   emit particulate DURING the session; do them before anything below.
2. **High-traffic wet rooms early** — kitchen and bathrooms demand energy;
   fatigue-rooms (bedroom dusting) come later.
3. **Entry before interior floors** — the entry mat zone feeds dirt inward
  ; reset it first so you're not tracking through cleaned space.
4. **Top-down inside every room** — non-negotiable physics.
5. **Floors dead last** — the final destination of every moved particle.

## Parallelism: your hands are the bottleneck
Machines clean unattended. The plan front-loads machine starts:
- T-0: laundry in, dishwasher running, oven cleaner applied (overnight
  dwell), hood filter soaking in degreaser, tub sprayed for dwell.
- While machines work: do cabinet fronts, dusting, surfaces.
- The 90-minute kitchen assumes the dishwasher and hood soak run
  concurrently — serial execution turns it into 130 minutes.

## Dwell chemistry (the 10-minute multiplier
Cleaners are contact-time dependent:
- Alkaline degreasers: 5-10 min to emulsify grease.
- Bleach gel on mold/grout: 15 min (cling gel keeps contact).
- Limescale removers (acids): 5-15 min until fizzing stops.
- Enzyme cleaners: 10-30 min, must stay damp.
- Oven cleaner (NaOH): hours to overnight, cold oven.

Spray → SET A TIMER → do the adjacent task → return to a 1/3-effort scrub.
The bathroom task list encodes this: spray tub first, toilet+sink during
dwell, tub scrub last.

## Where the minutes come from
Task times are paced for a competent adult with tools staged:
- kitchen 90m: 8 tasks, degreaser dwell overlapped, dishwasher parallel.
- bathroom 60m: dwell-overlap architecture as above.
- livingroom 55m: two-bag declutter discipline limits scope creep.
- bedroom 40m: assumes washer started first (5m), bed strip at T-0.
First deep clean runs 20-30% slower (supply hunt, unfamiliarity); the
schedule's 85% capacity absorbs it.

## The two-bag declutter rule
Every room starts with two bags: KEEP (things that belong elsewhere) and
DONATE (things that belong to no one in the house). No third bag, no
"deal with it later" pile — that pile is how deep cleans die. 10 minutes,
hard cap (see livingroom task 1).

## Upkeep cadences (keeping the win)
The recurrence days per room come from soil re-accumulation rates:
- bathroom 5d: biofilm/grout mold starts winning after a week.
- kitchen 7d: grease film becomes bonded after 2 weeks.
- entry 14d: tracked-in grit re-saturates the mat zone.
- hallway 30d: low-traffic, scuffs and edge dust only.
A 20-minute bathroom pass every 5 days beats a 60-minute rescue every 3
weeks — same monthly minutes, one third the effort, because soil never
bonds.

## Move-out mode (deposit variant)
- Run `plan` with all rooms, hours = honest.
- Add: walls scuff pass everywhere, inside ALL cabinets/appliances,
  windows inside+out, and photograph every room after cleaning (deposit
  disputes are evidence fights).
- Oven and bleach-grout dwell start T-0 (overnight class chemistry).
