# Minimal fallback interview

Run this fallback only when the Coverage receipt shows no useful accessible
history, when the user declines history access but wants to continue, or when
the post-scan confirmation exposes a material unknown that blocks a safe route
decision. Do not use it as the default before the permission request, and do
not turn it into an automatic seven-question sequence.

Ask the single minimum next question. Use the answer with the scanned portfolio
and current conversation, then reassess whether routing is possible. Ask again
only while one material unknown still blocks the Revenue Profile, Run
Authorization, payable test, delivery boundary, or external-action gate. Never
ask a fact already supplied or supported by a source receipt.

## Data minimization before the first question

Before asking for intake details, tell the user not to paste names, direct
contact details, exact addresses, credentials, authentication tokens, payment
card or bank data, confidential client/customer/employee files, private legal
or health records, or other raw personal or regulated data. Use aggregates,
ranges, pseudonyms, and sanitized excerpts by default—for example, an
approximate budget band rather than an account balance, or a buyer role rather
than a person's identity.

If the host's logging, training, retention, or deletion behavior is not known,
assume the conversation may be retained and collect only what is necessary to
route the test. If sensitive material is already present, do not repeat it in
the Revenue Profile; abstract it to the minimum relevant fact and stop if safe
handling requires controls the current host cannot confirm.

## Select the next question

Choose the earliest unresolved item that could change or block the route:

1. **Cash reality** - What has actually paid, been contracted, been booked, or
   produced buyer intent recently, and what evidence label is supportable?
2. **Current buyer and offer** - Which reachable buyer has a current problem,
   and what bounded offer or renewal is closest to a payable event?
3. **Control and timing** - What next action can the user control, and by when
   is payment evidence needed?
4. **Delivery economics** - Can the offer be delivered safely at a positive
   margin within the available time and budget?
5. **Authority and blockers** - What access, consent, qualified review, account
   authority, or external dependency is missing?

Phrase only the chosen item as one short conversational question. For a user
with no accessible history and no current facts, start with cash reality rather
than generic idea generation.

## Question bank, only when selected

### Starting reality

- What are you doing now: starting from zero, operating an offer, or improving
  an existing business?
- What result do you need first, and by when: a paid trial, a deposit, a signed
  agreement, or a settled payment?
- For an experienced operator, ask first: What is the existing offer, its basic
  economics, the bottleneck, and the highest-leverage point you can change?

### Assets and access

- What skills, work samples, tools, customer relationships, local access, or
  distribution do you already control?
- How many hours and how much cash can you risk on this test?
- What can you personally approve, and what requires another person's approval?

### Buyer and proof

- Who has the problem, what evidence says they have it, and how can they be
  reached with permission?
- What offer, price, sales history, outcomes, testimonials, or other proof is
  real today? Label missing proof as unknown, not as a negative or a promise.

### Constraints

- What geography, licensing, employer, contract, privacy, consent, platform,
  or regulatory rules apply?
- What delivery capacity, quality standard, and fulfillment boundary can you
  commit to only after approval?

### Speed and ambition

- What is the fastest acceptable evidence of demand, and what cost ceiling
  would make the experiment not worth continuing?
- Is the goal to repair an existing cash engine, sell a service, grow an owned
  audience, or explore a new capability?

## Skip and stop rules

Skip known stage, assets, time, budget, audience, offer, proof, geography,
constraints, and permissions. Do not re-ask facts in a source-linked portfolio,
the current conversation, or the user's confirmation. Stop questioning as soon
as one route can be chosen honestly. When an existing cash engine is visible,
clarify only the unresolved offer, economics, bottleneck, or leverage point.
When a beginner lacks business vocabulary, use one concrete example; examples
are prompts, not claims about their skills or demand.

## Fallback output

After the minimum facts are available, return to the MoneyPrinter workflow and
produce or update:

1. a Revenue Profile separating known facts, assumptions, and open questions;
2. a Route Decision scoring no more than three choices, with one route and one
   fallback; and
3. an Experiment Brief, one conversational route confirmation, and then a
   confirmed Run Authorization for a bounded payable test.

If a material constraint is unknown, show a safe discovery action and downgrade
the certainty instead of filling the gap with an assumption.
