# Portrait Art Direction

Treat every portrait as a designed character image, not a painted copy of a photograph. The person must remain recognizable, but the face, clothing, crop, and surrounding field must look jointly authored. Make the technique itself carry a visible tension; do not infer a diagnosis, hidden biography, morality, or protected trait.

## Let the user choose treatment, then choose presentation

In the first-turn suitability reply, offer these treatments and state that `conceptual_portrait` is the default when the user confirms without choosing:

- `conceptual_portrait` — aggressive, source-derived conceptual re-authoring closest to the game's authored portrait logic. It may use bounded symbolic pressure, a single psychic or structural intrusion, stronger plane collisions, and a more forceful portrait field while preserving identity topology.
- `restrained_character_portrait` — the earlier, milder character-painting treatment. It retains expressive planes and the portrait field but excludes unrequested psychic anatomy, extra faces, and icon-like symbolic imagery.

Then choose one presentation before writing the prompt:

- `character_portrait` — a vertical head-and-shoulders or bust composition, provided the source permits it. Keep face angle, gaze, decisive hair, accessory, and collar or upper-torso silhouette.
- `environmental_portrait` — retain a single essential place relationship when it explains the pose or story, but keep the person visually primary.

Do not silently crop away a pose, garment, gesture, or environmental fact that carries the image. This baseline is not a general full-body portrait solution: retain the source composition when that is requested or necessary.

## Identity topology, not surface resemblance

Recognition survives through a limited constellation: face axis, landmark spacing, silhouette, gaze, signature asymmetry, hair or accessory pattern, and gesture. Protect four to six of these together, then discard the photograph's solution for everything around them.

Do not preserve realistic pores, smooth skin gradients, lens softness, beauty lighting, specular skin, hair strands, or camera color balance. Replace them with a deliberately constructed system of planes and marks.

## Choose one painting grammar and one technique thesis

Use one primary grammar; add at most one compatible secondary behavior:

- `physical_hard_edge`: angular plane blocks, black resisting edges, compressed verticals, and abrupt value cuts;
- `measured_restraint`: softer veils, quieter transitions, and one exact gaze or mouth anchor;
- `graphic_assertion`: contiguous flat masses, sharp color boundaries, and poster-like silhouette pressure;
- `eroded_instability`: dragged paint, missing cheek or shoulder edges, scraped revisions, and disintegrating contour;
- `spectral_distance`: dark absorption, muted field masses, low-contrast lost edges, and one precise focal zone.

Name a technique thesis as a visible mechanism, such as “warm diagonal pressure interrupts a contained cool face,” not as a personality label. `Cross-grain friction` is a mark action that may support any grammar: strokes may cut across hair, skin, clothing, or anatomy rather than obediently describing them.

## Build the face as a character-card system

1. Reduce the head, hair, neck, and collar into five to eight large value-color planes.
2. Protect four to six identity anchors collectively.
3. Place one graphic light mass and one or two shadow cavities; they may be non-literal but must clarify the facial structure.
4. Alter two non-essential planes through compression, offset, flattening, partial obscuring, merging, or asymmetric enlargement.
5. Put the highest refinement in one small zone, often one eye with an adjacent brow, nose, or mouth plane.
6. Lose or merge at least one other eye, cheek, hair, jaw, neck, or shoulder passage.
7. Treat hair mass, collar, shoulder, and face contour as one silhouette system, not as separate realistic objects.

Do not enlarge both eyes, sharpen every feature, or distort the entire face uniformly. The result should retain a recognizable landmark constellation while showing a chosen hierarchy of attention.

## Use relational color, not cosmetic color

Build a dominant temperature and a counter-temperature. Add one deliberately non-natural color event through the forehead, eye socket, cheek, nose bridge, jaw, or reflected collar edge. Its first job is compositional: it must create a tension or balance between face, clothing, and field.

Use high saturation only at the focal event or one supporting field. Contaminate nearby neutrals so the picture feels painted as one world. Avoid fixed franchise palettes and universal teal-purple-red grading.

## Make every mark matter

Favor decisive block-ins, dry interruptions, scraped passages, short opaque corrections, and visible changes of mind over polished blending. Let selected marks cross the direction of hair, facial volume, or clothing folds.

Keep the decisive likeness anchors readable at normal size, but permit close-up collisions, roughness, and unfinished edges. Use economy: describe only what the portrait needs.

## Re-author the setting as a portrait field

Normally discard the literal photographic backdrop and distill its visible structure into one source-derived field grammar. Read [portrait-background-geometry.md](portrait-background-geometry.md) for the complete construction rules.

The field is a small, irregular painted system that shares edges with the person. It may cut through hair, press toward a collar, shelter the gaze, merge with a shoulder, or continue a facial or clothing angle. It is not a decorative poster placed behind an unchanged figure.

## Reject false success

Reject the result even when likeness is excellent if any of these are true:

- the skin still reads as smoothly modeled photography;
- only the background, clothing, or color grade changed;
- both eyes, nose, lips, and jaw are rendered with equal realistic precision;
- brush texture sits on top of unchanged camera lighting;
- the portrait could be produced by applying the same filter to anyone;
- the field is a blurred photo, generic smoke, random splatter, or unrelated geometry;
- the face, collar, shoulder, and field do not form one composition;
- the selected treatment was ignored, or a restrained portrait gained unrequested psychic anatomy, extra faces, or icon-like imagery;
- the image copies a recognizable game portrait, character, crop, or motif.
