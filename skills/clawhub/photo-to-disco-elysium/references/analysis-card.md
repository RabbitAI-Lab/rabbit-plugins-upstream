Write the psychological proposition as a visible claim, not a diagnosis. Example: “The figure remains outwardly still while dark vertical masses press toward the shoulders.”

Write memory residue as the one collision, silhouette, gaze, gesture, window, road, or pressure pattern that should remain after a glance.

```

Prefer relational anchors over generic labels. “Close-set eyes beneath a high left brow, long straight nose, narrow jaw, black rectangular glasses” is useful; “attractive middle-aged person” is not.

Separate `identity topology anchors` from `photographic properties to discard`. The former preserves recognition. The latter prevents realistic skin, camera lighting, literal complexion, lens softness, and hair-by-hair detail from surviving the transformation.

If the user confirms without selecting a portrait treatment, use `conceptual_portrait`. Use `restrained_character_portrait` only when the user explicitly chooses the milder treatment. Use `character_portrait` when a compact head-and-shoulders or bust crop does not destroy a decisive pose, garment, gesture, or place relationship; otherwise use `environmental_portrait` and preserve the essential composition.

For multiple people, preserve count, relative scale, gaze direction, overlap, and relationship. Assign a compact identity topology to each face. If faces are too small or blurred, state the likeness limit rather than hallucinating precision.

```

Describe only visible human traces—wear, signage, repairs, abandoned objects, traffic, cultivation, or occupation. Do not assert a location's real history from appearance alone.

Write `scene narrative` and `worldbuilding interpretation` as visible concept-art decisions, not unsupported factual history. For example, “a large institutional block isolates a narrow warm entrance” is valid; “this building was used by a repressive government” is not supported by appearance alone.

For `semantic detail allocation`, budget detail by both information density and identity or narrative importance. A large ornate facade may receive selective fine marks while a small generic car is compressed; a small bird on a branch may receive a high detail budget when it is the scene's visual anchor.

For natural landscapes, leave `human traces` empty when none are visible. Do not invent occupation, architecture, roads, or props. Treat terrain, water, atmosphere, vegetation, rock, and animals as the identity system.
