# Visual Language

Use these principles as an art-direction system, not a decorative-effects checklist.

## 1. Preserve truth beneath expressive disorder

Establish identity topology, gesture, perspective, mass, and large-value organization before breaking surfaces apart. Let painterly anarchy act on a stable relational structure.

For portraits, truth means the recognizable constellation of features—not realistic skin, exact camera lighting, or untouched anatomy. For environments, truth means navigable spatial and landmark relationships—not photographic texture.

Aim for relative accuracy: the image reads clearly at normal size and becomes broken, suggestive, and unfinished up close.

## 2. Make subject and world interact

Do not place a painted subject on generic smoke. Give the surrounding field a specific action:

- pressure closes inward;
- atmosphere erodes an edge;
- light shelters a fragile area;
- architecture isolates a figure;
- color radiates from a gaze or gesture;
- weather receives and dissolves structures;
- rigid geometry resists an organic field.

Choose one dominant relationship and support it throughout the image.

For portraits, build that relationship from the uploaded background. Distill walls, windows, openings, objects, shadows, and directional cues into one coherent field grammar: flat counter-blocks, vertical pressure, diagonal pressure, an arc or enclosure, a black erasure field, or organic absorption. Use only one to four irregular painted masses, plus a line rhythm only when it supports the figure. Field meanings must be supported by visible evidence or user narrative; they are not a diagnostic dictionary.

## 3. Use edge hierarchy as structure

Assign edges by meaning:

- Hard edges hold identity relationships, physical resistance, focal gestures, or structural load.
- Soft edges mediate volume, weather, uncertainty, and secondary forms.
- Lost edges merge a form with the world, imply memory, or remove unimportant description.

Do not sharpen or blur everything equally. Concentrate the strongest edge contrast near the visual and psychological center.

### Portrait emphasis

Protect only a small constellation of decisive relationships. Allow expressive cuts, scraping, merging, and lost edges to pass through the facial interior. One eye may be more resolved than the other; a cheek may become a flat color collision; part of the jaw or hair may disappear into the field.

The face must look drawn and painted anew. If realistic facial modeling survives beneath painterly texture, the edge hierarchy has failed.

The portrait background must also look designed anew. Use one source-derived field grammar with one to four irregular masses, plus an optional restrained line rhythm. Make at least two field actions meet the gaze, skull, hair, jaw, collar, or shoulders; avoid a uniformly soft backdrop.

### Environment emphasis

Preserve only the essential place anchors, key route relationships, and decisive silhouettes needed for recognition. Use B2.5 freedom to redesign atmosphere, palette, depth, sky proportion, non-essential scale, and spatial emphasis. The photograph supplies identity evidence; it does not dictate literal weather, color, or depth.

Allocate mark scale by `information density + narrative importance`:

- use broad low-frequency masses for sky, water, fog, snow, roads, ordinary walls, roofs, grass fields, distance, and large shadows;
- use selective finer directional marks for landmark ornament, structural joints, distinctive entrances, window rhythms, signs, railings, lamps, wires, trunks, main branches, foliage clusters, flowers, rock ridges, birds, and animals;
- compress generic repetition without blurring the few details that make the place or natural form recognizable.

Give foreground, middle ground, and background different visual jobs. Let atmosphere and large shadow create lost edges; keep only the most important landmark, path, subject, or animal edges firm.

## 4. Treat color as a relationship

Derive color from the source tension, local light evidence, and psychological proposition. Define:

- a dominant temperature family;
- a counter-temperature that creates tension or relief;
- one limited saturation maximum;
- neutrals contaminated by nearby colors.

For portraits, literal flesh color is optional. Drive a non-natural hue through a facial plane when it balances the composition or intensifies the technique thesis. Avoid a universal teal-purple-red recipe.

## 5. Modulate refinement and generalization

Use detail to direct attention. Refine one small meaningful area and generalize a much larger area. Describe important passages through simplified planes, dragged paint, dry interruption, scumbling, abrupt correction, or partial disappearance.

Do not distribute facial detail evenly. Fully rendered paired eyes, realistic lips, smooth cheeks, and individually described hair usually signal insufficient transformation.

## 6. Give geometry a psychological job

Use shapes in response to the source rather than pasting symbols on top:

- verticals may constrain, dignify, or divide;
- diagonals may destabilize or pull attention;
- enclosing arcs may shelter or trap;
- rectangles may impose institutional weight;
- interrupted rhythms may suggest erosion or friction.

Integrate geometry into facial planes, architecture, shadow, clothing, foliage, or brush direction.

## 7. Paint against the obvious direction

Include at least one mark family that contradicts the depicted surface: cut across falling hair, flatten a rounded cheek, scrape through a clothing fold, or drive a background stroke into the silhouette. Use this counter-direction to declare that the image has been re-authored rather than filtered.

## 8. Allow controlled accidents

Use unexpected color collision, scraped texture, splatter, broken contour, or merged paint when it strengthens the technique thesis. In portraits, accidents may cross the face, but they must not erase the entire protected landmark constellation.

## 9. Keep style subordinate to the person or place

Every source needs a distinct expressive system. Vary palette, mark scale, pressure direction, deformation target, refinement zone, and degree of dissolution. Technique should reveal something visible about this image; the same filter should not fit everyone.

## Common false equivalents

Reject these shortcuts:

- a realistic portrait with an oil-canvas texture;
- an unchanged face against an expressive background;
- uniform impasto or grunge over the whole image;
- neon grading without structural redesign;
- smooth beauty-painting anatomy with a few broken edges;
- random facial distortion that loses identity topology;
- generalized darkness with unreadable values;
- direct copies of game portraits, UI, typography, or character designs.
- a photographic or blurred portrait background left behind the repainted face;
- generic smoke, splashes, circles, or squares that do not derive from the uploaded setting or serve the portrait's tension;
- a natural landscape that reads only as a conventional oil painting after transformation.
