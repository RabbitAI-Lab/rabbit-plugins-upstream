# Benefits Deadlines Deep Dive: COBRA, 401(k) Loan, Equity, Unemployment

## COBRA — the 60-day option nobody prices correctly

Facts (US, employer 20+):

- Election window: **60 days** from loss of coverage OR from the date
  the COBRA notice was sent, whichever is LATER.
- Coverage is **retroactive** to the loss date — you can wait out the
  whole window uninsured, and if disaster struck on day 20, elect on
  day 55 and the day-20 claim is covered (you pay premiums back to the
  loss date).
- Duration: 18 months (29 with SSDI determination on the same event;
  36 for divorce/dependent events).
- Premium = 102% of full plan cost — commonly $500–800/mo individual,
  $1,500–2,000/mo family.

**The optimal play (for most people):**

1. Do NOT elect on day 1. Mark calendar day 45 and day 55.
2. If a major claim occurs inside the window → elect, pay back premiums
   to loss date, claim is covered. You effectively bought insurance
   after the accident, legally.
3. If no claims → let it lapse and buy marketplace coverage (or get
   hired) — with severance-only income, premium tax credits often make
   a Silver plan near-free where COBRA would cost full price.
4. Special-enrollment right: losing employer coverage is a qualifying
   event for the marketplace for **60 days**, too — you can enroll
   late, but marketplace coverage is NOT retroactive (unlike COBRA).
   So the marketplace is the "no disaster happened" lane; COBRA is
   the retroactive safety net.

**45-day rule for the employer's notice** and 14-day rules matter for
penalties on THEM, not you. Your clock is 60 days regardless.

## 401(k) loan at termination — the silent conversion

The sequence people miss:

1. At termination the loan can't be repaid via payroll anymore.
2. The plan document sets a cure period — older plans: repay by the
   tax-return due date (including extensions) of the year the
   separation occurred; many plans: shorter (30–90 days). READ THE
   PLAN DOCUMENT — this is the one date the skill cannot know.
3. If unpaid by then → the outstanding balance is DEEMED DISTRIBUTED:
   1099-R next January, taxed as ordinary income, **+10% early
   distribution penalty if under 59½**.
4. Repayment options: lump-sum from severance; some employers allow a
   direct rollover of the offset amount into an IRA (loan-offset
   rollover — allowed until the extended tax due date); or a new
   employer plan that accepts loan rollovers (rare but exists).

Example: $8,000 loan, 24% bracket, age 47 → deemed distribution costs
$8000 × (0.24 + 0.10) = **$2,720** in avoidable tax+penalty, plus the
lost compounding. A payoff from severance is almost always cheaper.

**Age-55 separator rule:** if you separate at 55+ (50+ for some
public-safety plans) and LEAVE the money in the employer plan, the 10%
penalty on withdrawals disappears. Rolling to an IRA kills this. If
you're 55–59½ and will need money from the 401(k), leave at least the
needed portion in the plan — this is a five-figure decision people
stumble into backwards.

## Equity windows

| Instrument | At termination | Deadline |
|-----------|----------------|----------|
| RSUs (vested) | Usually cashed out / settled near final pay | none — owed to you |
| RSUs (unvested) | Usually forfeited (unless "retirement-eligible" provisions) | — |
| NSOs (vested, in the money) | Must exercise post-termination | commonly **90 days**, check grant agreement |
| ISOs (vested) | Exercise window often 90 days BUT loses ISO status if not exercised while employed rules met → AMT planning | CPA BEFORE exercising |
| ESPP | Purchase period rules vary; shares bought are yours | check offering doc |

Post-termination exercise windows can be 90 days, 3 months, 1 month
("for cause"), or negotiated longer at exec level. The grant agreement
overrides hearsay — pull every agreement from the equity portal TONIGHT.

## Unemployment — timing rules worth money

- File in the **first eligible week**. Most states start benefits the
  Sunday/Saturday of the claim week — waiting "until severance ends"
  usually forfeits weeks (and some states pay through severance anyway).
- Severance effect varies: some states treat lump-sum severance as
  non-wages (no delay), others delay the start. File anyway; the state
  will adjudicate.
- Weekly claims certification is mandatory — miss two certifications
  and the claim closes; reopen costs weeks.
- Job-search requirements: typically 2–5 verifiable activities/week —
  log them from day one (a spreadsheet beats memory in an audit).
- Severance does NOT reduce unemployment in ~half the states; the
  release agreement's non-disparagement doesn't affect it.

## WARN Act (mass layoffs)

Plant closing / mass layoff (50–499 employees at a site, or 500+;
employer 100+ total): **60 calendar days' advance notice** or 60 days'
back pay + benefits. If you got 0 notice and were part of a group,
the severance may be partly OWED — that's leverage in the conversation
`severance` benchmarks. Complaint window: 2 years to file (US DOL or
court). Not applicable to routine single layoffs.

## Severance review pass (the 15-minute inventory)

Before signing anything:

1. Per-week benchmark: 1–2 weeks per year of service is the common
   band (exec higher). Below 1 wk/yr with no WARN pay = negotiate.
2. Items owed regardless of signing: accrued PTO (state law), completed
   bonus/proration per plan terms, vested equity, expense reimbursements.
   These are NOT consideration — they're wages.
3. The release buys: non-disparagement, no-rehire clauses, reference
   terms, outplacement services, extended benefits, exercise-window
   extensions — price every one.
4. 40+: ADEA gives 21 days to consider (45 for group layoffs) + 7-day
   revocation. A deadline shorter than that on an age-discriminating
   release is a lawyer's gift.
5. Non-compete enforceability is state-dependent (banned in CA/OK/ND;
   income-thresholded elsewhere) — a 5-minute read of your state's rule
   changes the signature calculus.
