# Conversion-focused landing pages: promise, comprehension, action

Use when the page is meant to help an arriving visitor make an informed decision or take a defined action. Identify **what conversion means in this project** (trial start, sign-up, purchase, booking, download, application) and whether the author has supplied the linked journey. Never invent a funnel, analytics result, or CTA destination.

## Read as a cold visitor

1. **Arrival context:** ad, email, search result or direct link, if supplied. Does the page fulfill the incoming message?
2. **First viewport:** who is it for, what does it actually do, why care now, what can be clicked next? These are editorial checks, not rules that every page must have four sentences.
3. **Evidence where the claim is made:** testimonials, certifications, numbers, cases and guarantees should be grounded, dated when appropriate, and not misleading.
4. **Objection sequence:** show the necessary information (price, security, effort, compatibility, prerequisites or conditions) before readers must commit. A text-only draft cannot establish visual hierarchy or actual button behavior: mark those unobserved.
5. **CTA semantics:** describe the real next step. “Start free trial” must lead to a genuine free trial, not a sales call or undisclosed billing step.
6. **One dominant action if appropriate:** multiple paths may be justified by different user journeys (e.g. “Read documentation” vs “Try online”). Do not remove a legitimate second route in the name of a universal CRO law.
7. **Claims and consent:** pressure, countdowns, guaranteed results, fake scarcity or hidden conditions require scrutiny, not optimisation.

## Specific edit decisions

**Edit — message mismatch**
- Ad: “Read the quickstart.” Landing hero: “Book a demo to see pricing.”
- If the landing is actually a demo booking, revise the ad/hero copy consistently or flag that the destination needs to change. Do not invent a quickstart.

**Edit — vague CTA**
- Button: “Get started.” Actual next step supplied as a product sandbox with no registration.
- Proposed: “Open the sandbox.” Reader gains a truthful expectation of the action.

**Leave — multiple justified actions**
- A documentation hub offers “Start a tutorial” and “Look up API parameters.” Those reflect two different jobs; do not force them into a single acquisition CTA.

**Leave — sharp brand promise**
- “Read it twice. Send it once.” Don't replace with verbose keyword-stuffed copy unless the author explicitly requires feature-level clarity in that location.

## Evaluation

A proposed text change is **not** a proven conversion improvement. If the author supplies funnel analytics or a controlled experiment, interpret the evidence carefully; otherwise recommend a hypothesis/test without inventing an uplift percentage.
