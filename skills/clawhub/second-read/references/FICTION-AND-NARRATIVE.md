# Fiction, memoir and narrative: preserve effects

Track chronology, narrator knowledge, scene goal, motivations actually established, cause and consequence, pace, revelation timing and repeated images. A narrative does not need citations, a universal thesis, or argument-map exposition. Check the author's desired emotional and thematic effect before smoothing anything.

## Editorial operations with risk

- **Broken chronology:** a character reacts to information the scene has not made available (unless this is deliberate dramatic irony). Offer a moved beat, a corrected tense, or a small cue.
- **Point of view:** a close-limited narrator knows another character's private thought without a shift. Repair only if the narration's contract excludes omniscient knowledge.
- **Motivation and turn:** a consequential choice arrives with no preparation *where* the writer needs readers to believe it; move an already written cue rather than invent a backstory.
- **Pacing:** repeated setup can obscure the actual reversal; cut the repeat and preserve the stronger sentence.
- **Subtext:** a narrator implies fear, anger, affection or uncertainty indirectly; don't explain it unless the intended reader cannot follow the scene at all.
- **Voice:** regional expressions, slightly irregular grammar, specific diction and cadence can characterize a narrator. Do not sanitize them under a global “naturalness” check.

## Paired decisions

**Edit — accidental knowledge jump**
- P1: “Nina had never read the letter.” P2: “Nina quoted the letter's final line to him.” Without an intervening revelation, flag the contradiction and propose a grounded bridge **only if source context permits**, otherwise request author choice.

**Leave — purposeful suspense**
- “She put the key down. Neither of them reached for it.” Do not add “because they were afraid of discussing their divorce” merely to clarify a suggestive scene.

**Edit — unnecessary explanation**
- Original: “She checked the empty chair twice. She was sad and missed him very much.”
- If the second sentence only restates intended subtext, propose deleting it and leaving the concrete action. If the explicit admission is a meaningful character beat, preserve it.

**Leave — striking fragment**
- “Not this time.” A sentence fragment may be the exact narrative rhythm needed; grammar alone does not require revision.

## Sequence question

Immediately after the relevant source scene, optionally ask: “What is a reader entitled to know about this person *now*, before the reveal?” The writer may deliberately choose ambiguity; do not mark that as wrong.
