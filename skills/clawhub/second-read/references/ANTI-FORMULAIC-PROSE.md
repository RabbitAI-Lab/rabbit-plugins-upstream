# Anti-formulaic prose: concrete flaws, not an AI detector

Check a passage when it sounds mechanically templated, generic, inflated or repetitive. **Never infer who authored it or assign an AI-likeness percentage.** A rhetorical form isn't wrong by itself; show how this exact use harms meaning, specificity, rhythm or voice.

| Inspect | Usually edit when | Often leave when |
|---|---|---|
| "In today's fast-paced world", "now more than ever" | Placeholder opening before the real point | Deliberate irony or quoted text |
| "seamless", "game-changing", "innovative", "world-class" | Unsupported benefit substitutes for a concrete supplied fact | Proper product name or supported, bounded description |
| "Experts agree", "studies show" | Unnamed authority conceals lack of evidence | The expert and study are actually attributed nearby |
| Repeated "Furthermore", "Moreover", "In conclusion" | Transitional padding obscures actual causal link | Complex specification where signposts clarify hierarchy |
| "not just X; Y" or rhythmical triads everywhere | Formula replaces distinct explanation | Well-placed contrast or deliberate speech cadence |
| Decorative em dashes, long sentences, passive voice | Reader loses track of agent or logic | Grammatical precision, cadence, important unknown agent |
| Excessive abstraction, noun stacks or over-hedging | Reader cannot imagine or test the claim | Genuine scientific uncertainty or accurate technical usage |
| Mandatory cheerful/personal language | Insincere familiarity or invented experience | Established sender relationship and verified genuine detail |

**Operation:** quote the exact phrase → identify the reader cost → propose the smallest specific replacement **only using facts present in the source/brief** → compare with leaving the original alone. If the proposed fix introduces fictional specifics or blandness, keep the sentence and suppress the finding.

**Edit:** "Our cutting-edge platform unlocks strategic synergies." Approved product feature is a command checker. Suggest "Check whether the setup commands in your docs actually run" only when supported.

**Leave:** "A timetable is a promise." "Read it twice. Send it once." An aphorism or balanced cadence isn't inherently boilerplate.

**Leave:** "The results were inconclusive." Polished grammar, formal register and second-language English are not pathologies.

A Vale lint rule that bans every em dash reflects an **author-specific preference**, never a universal editorial law. Keep original author syntax where effective.
