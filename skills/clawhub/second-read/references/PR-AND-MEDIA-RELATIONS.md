# Public relations and media relations: credibility over conversion

Load for press/media releases, media alerts, journalist pitches, public and crisis statements, stakeholder announcements, spokesperson quotes, press Q&As, executive briefings, and media fact sheets. **PR is not conversion copy or independent journalism.** Its job is to communicate on behalf of a disclosed interest in ways that withstand independent scrutiny, attribution and questioning. Do not apply ad-copy urgency and CTA rules by default.

## Editorial checks by asset

- **Release/announcement:** what happened (and when)? What is actually new, relevant or verifiable? Put the truthful development before generic organizational praise; attribute forecasts and corporate interpretations rather than presenting them as measured outcomes.
- **Pitch to a journalist:** why is this useful to this outlet or beat? Specify a real reporting angle and available sources. Respect editorial independence; sending material doesn't entitle the sender to coverage. Never fake prior relationships, endorsements or exclusives.
- **Executive/spokesperson quote:** an exact quote requires an approved/supplied source. Flag vacuous "we are thrilled" language as optional, but **never fabricate a quotation or attribute invented wording**. Offer suggested language explicitly for approval when asked.
- **Holding/crisis statement:** distinguish known, unknown, under investigation and next promised update. Accuracy, accountability and affected people take priority over persuasion, denial or reputation varnish. Do not invent apologies, root causes, actions or approval.
- **Media Q&A / fact sheet:** anticipate reasonable scrutiny; identify documented evidence, who can substantiate each claim, qualifications, ranges and the latest approved policy.

## Verification and provenance

For each significant factual assertion, preserve time, entity, relevant denominator and source. Company materials verify **what the organization asserts or announces**, not independent correctness. Keep signed-off releases, approved quotes and product/runtime evidence distinct. Treat forward-looking plans as forecasts/intentions, not shipped functionality. Disclose material interests and paid sponsorship when applicable; seek appropriate human/legal approval on regulated or crisis claims.

An adversarial reader test: **Could a reporter quote this out of the release without mistakenly treating a sponsor's allegation, projection or interpretation as an established independent fact?** If not, fix attribution/scope instead of amplifying tone.

## PR measurement is not copywriting performance

AMEC separates communication outputs, audience uptake, outcomes and impact; readership counts or press placements alone do not establish changes in trust or behavior. Never invent earned reach or declare reputational uplift from one revised headline.

## Paired edits

**Edit — sourced prediction masquerading as fact**: "The platform will cut hospital waiting times by 30%." Actual supplied evidence is only a vendor projection. Suggest: "The company projects a 30% reduction in waiting time, subject to its model's assumptions." Include numerical detail only if the source supports it.

**Edit — manufactured independent voice**: "Researchers agree our launch is transformative." No named independent study. Replace with the verified announcement and its attribution; do not manufacture experts.

**Leave — concise accountable holding statement**: "We are investigating the outage and will publish another update at 16:00 UTC." If both investigation and update are confirmed, do not add promotional brand flourishes.

**Ask, not invent**: "CEO Jane Doe said: '...'" with no approved quote. Ask for the exact approved language or mark any draft quotation as a proposed quote awaiting sign-off.
