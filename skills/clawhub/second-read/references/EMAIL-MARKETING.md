# Email pathway: one message in a larger relationship

Load for broadcast marketing, lifecycle, nurture, onboarding, transactional-adjacent announcements, newsletters and personal outreach. Identify *which one* before applying conventions; not every email is promotional.

## Project and sequence checks

- **Who is sending and why now?** Determine project, campaign stage, recipient familiarity, earlier promises, approved offer, and what message follows. Don't infer subscriber preferences or engagement metrics.
- **Subject + preheader + opening:** test the first impression as a unit. The subject should not promise more than the body and linked destination supply. Never write a fabricated deadline or result to increase open rates.
- **One clear primary job:** what should readers know, decide, or do? A newsletter may aim at understanding rather than clicks. Identify the action without insisting on a CTA if none belongs.
- **Order:** reader context → why it matters now → relevant substantiation → action/expectation. Preserve an unusual structure that performs better for this audience according to supplied rules.
- **Sender and destination:** use provided approved names/links; confirm rather than fabricate footer/legal requirements when not visible. When jurisdiction and marketing status make disclosures or unsubscribe obligations applicable, flag missing evidence instead of pronouncing compliance based on partial text.
- **Continuity:** the landing page or product should fulfill the email's specific promise. Look for price, date, feature-status, and audience changes across campaign assets.
- **Metrics:** do not claim an open-rate or click lift without a real experiment. A/B variant is an **optional proposed test**, not a predicted result.

## Limits of numerical heuristics

Treat the author's email-scoring dimensions as **latent review lenses**—attention, clarity, trust, relevance, action friction and authenticity—rather than a required visible 1–5 scorecard. No universal 30–50-character subject rule, grade-level target, spam-trigger-word penalty or projected CTR uplift is reliable without project- and provider-specific evidence. A/B testing validates observed outcomes, not guesses. Sender authentication and unsubscribe requirements are technical/compliance checks conditional on jurisdiction, sending volume and email type: https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126 . Do not claim to have checked them from body text alone.

## User rule pack matters

If the organization has email writing principles, load those first using `AUTHOR-RULESETS.md`: greetings, line length, humour, vocabulary, sender voice, audience segmentation, preheader conventions, footer requirements, frequency or disclosure. Describe where the rule applied, not every rule that passed.

## Paired decisions

**Edit — subject/body mismatch**
- Subject: “Your account is live.” Body: “Join the early-access waitlist.”
- Suggested subject: “Join the early-access waitlist” unless the brief includes a real activation link. The original incorrectly asserts access.

**Leave — a genuine personal note**
- “I read your interview about hospital shifts. Your last answer stayed with me.”
- Do not convert to a mass-marketing template with a generic “Hello valued customer” or a sales CTA.

**Edit — vague ask**
- Original: “Explore our exciting offerings.” Destination supplied as an approved pricing comparison page.
- Suggested: “Compare the plans” or the project's documented wording; do not invent a pricing URL.

## Questions to yourself after reading this once

Place by the relevant original passage only if a proposed edit depends on the answer: “After this subject and opening, does the reader expect immediate access or an invitation to apply?”

## Reference boundary

Email compliance varies by jurisdiction and message type; no universal assertion that every email needs the same footer. Verify requirements against the actual recipient market and available campaign context. For US marketing claims/disclosures, https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing is a starting reference, not a substitute for legal review.
