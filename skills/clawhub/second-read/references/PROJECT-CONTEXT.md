# Wider-project context: retrieve before asking

Load for company, product, publication, political campaign, brand program, announcement series or another ongoing project. A mention of an organization does not establish that the author represents it. Keep the project ledger private, source-grounded and small.

## Authority ladder

1. Read explicit author intention, supplied brief, attachments and known conversation context; these decide **what the author intends**, unless contradicted by verifiable facts.
2. If editing a local or connected document, follow [KNOWLEDGE-BASES](KNOWLEDGE-BASES.md) for project notes, links and targeted search. Inspect which local tools/connectors are truly available; access only relevant resources in the user's authorization scope. Do not infer a connector's existence merely from an example.
3. For a known public organization/product/campaign, inspect its *official site* only when web access is available and materially useful. Start with about/product/press/pricing/brand/campaign pages directly relevant to the draft. Respect robots, rate limits and privacy boundaries; never bypass authentication or scrape private dashboards.
4. Ask for the smallest consequential missing item: intended conclusion, campaign identity, authorized claim, actual CTA destination or latest approval. Do not request a complete project intake unless the task truly cannot proceed otherwise.

## Internal evidence ledger

| Field | Store only what was observed |
|---|---|
| Publisher and relationship | Author/sponsor vs subject mentioned in text |
| Purpose and audience | Stated goal, reader knowledge and decision stage |
| Product/project | Name, version, capabilities and unverified claims |
| Positioning | Intended audience, alternative, differentiator and reason to believe |
| Campaign | Current asset's place in sequence, prior promise, following action |
| Voice/rules | Approved vocabulary and examples, source and revision date |
| Evidence | Claim; exact document/path/URL; status: author-stated, source-corroborated, runtime-observed, contradicted or unknown |

Company sites are authoritative for **company messaging**, not for independent substantiation. A party's website establishes its *stated platform*, not whether its empirical assertions hold. In Notion/Obsidian, an older draft is context, not automatically an approved or current rule. Source conflicts warrant the smallest meaningful caveat or author question.

## Applying project rules

Read [AUTHOR-RULESETS](AUTHOR-RULESETS.md) when provided. Respect user/project voice and campaign briefs within documented scope. For **all documentation**, [DOCUMENTATION](DOCUMENTATION.md) uses Canonical as the only house *writing style*; use the actual product for factual validation. For other content, apply its own supplied house style when authorized.

**Example:** a message offers a six-month free trial, but approved project terms specify six weeks. Suggest the exact factual correction. A hypothetical nurse scenario offered in an opinion column remains a hypothetical, not a claim about real incident frequency.

Only expose project-context findings when they explain a consequential proposal; do not introduce a new UI section or dump the ledger.
