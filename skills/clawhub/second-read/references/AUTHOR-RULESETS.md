# User and organization rule packs

A writer's validated, versioned rules deserve priority over generic editorial conventions **within their proper scope**. Do not require every writer to produce a formal rules file: natural-language examples in the supplied brief count as rules. Never pretend missing company rules were retrieved.

## Read and record each rule

For each rule, capture, if available: `id`, `scope`, `source`, `version_or_date`, `kind` (`must`, `must_not`, `prefer`, `example`, `context`), exact instruction, rationale, exceptions, and **verification method**. User-provided preferred phrases and anti-examples are particularly valuable. An example is *illustrative*, not an automatically universal mandate; establish what general principle it teaches.

Use `assets/project-context.example.json` as one possible structured format. Do not demand JSON from a user who has already explained the rules in ordinary conversation.

## Convert experience into conditional checks, not slogans

- “Our emails never open with corporate politeness.” Check for needless salutations **in email campaigns governed by this rule**; leave one-to-one personal correspondence alone.
- “Avoid vague claims such as ‘transform your workflow’.” Suggest wording grounded in a supplied benefit. Do not invent numbers or outcomes.
- “Our ads place the offer before the product story.” Apply only to ads carrying that campaign offer, not brand storytelling with no transactional objective.
- “Always title-case headings.” Apply this only outside documentation when adopted. For documentation, Canonical's sentence-case guide is the sole house writing style in Second Read; explain the scope only when the conflict changes a real edit.

## If rules conflict

1. Verify that both rules apply to this artifact and publication date.
2. If one is out of scope or superseded, use the relevant rule and mention why only when useful.
3. If both remain active, flag the conflict and ask the author/owner to choose; you may propose alternatives without declaring either accepted.
4. Never fabricate a law, legal requirement or platform policy from an internal preference. Official, jurisdiction-specific obligations need separately checked sources.

## Output constraint

Identify the one or two material rule violations that actually harm this draft. Show: unchanged source quote → specific rule ID / origin → proposed replacement → reader consequence. Mark subjective rules as optional unless the brief makes them mandatory. Avoid a checklist of 30 “passes” the writer never asked to see.

## Leave-alone cases

Do not turn every customer story into the same message template. A campaign series can deliberately change rhythm; a brand may deliberately use an unexpected sentence fragment. If the rule pack explains the reason and the writing achieves it, preserve it.
