# Copy and brand communication: first impression, real promise, distinct voice

This route covers brand positioning, announcements, short posts, emails, advertising and landing-page copy. **Load the narrower channel reference** (`EMAIL-MARKETING.md`, `LANDING-PAGES.md`, `AD-COPY.md`) where appropriate; don't impose all the checks on every two-line post. Load `PROJECT-CONTEXT.md` and `AUTHOR-RULESETS.md` when an organization or campaign is involved.

## Baseline checkable rules

- Facts agree with the brief: names, dates/weekdays, figures, prices, deadlines, product state and CTA destinations. Count rather than guess when limits are known.
- Promise and outcome match: “available now” vs waitlist; “download” vs book a call; “free” vs payment requirement. Never invent an offer or legal disclosure.
- Support material claims with supplied product evidence, not plausible marketing rhetoric. Ask for missing substantiation when important.
- Where an action is part of the content, say what will actually happen. Do not force a sales CTA into community speech, satire or identity expression.
- Protect context: an apparently cryptic two-word reply may depend on the immediately preceding message, and that is an advantage, not an error.
- Protect specificity and memorability. Shortness can produce extraordinary emotional force; don't confuse economy with insufficient information.
- Detect generic AI-looking filler only if it weakens the actual message; use `ANTI-FORMULAIC-PROSE.md` without inferring authorship.

## Paired cases

**Edit — misleading promise**
- Source: “Get instant access” in a campaign whose actual step is “Join the waitlist.”
- Proposed: “Join the waitlist.” Do not invent an instant-access page.

**Leave — memorable parallelism**
- “Read it twice. Send it once.” Concise and brand-specific; do not rewrite as “Optimize your messaging with a comprehensive review workflow.”

**Leave — purposeful context-dependent reply**
- Brand response: “18 million.” Previous customer message asks for a high threshold. Without that conversational context, the reply means little; in context, it works. Don't manufacture a descriptive headline.

**Edit — contradictory microcopy**
- Button: “Start free trial.” The next supplied screen is an immediate paid checkout without a trial. Propose honest button text or flag the destination mismatch; don't assume the checkout can be changed.

## What not to over-enforce

Mandatory CTA, emotional language, urgency, SEO keywords, “benefits not features,” and quantified claims are not universal laws. Follow project/brand-specific rules where supplied and show exact, contextual reasons to edit.
