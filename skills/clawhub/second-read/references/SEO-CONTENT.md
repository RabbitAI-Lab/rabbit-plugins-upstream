# Editorial SEO for articles and blog pieces

Use when the publication's purpose includes discoverability through search. **People-first content is the criterion; keyword padding is not.** Google explicitly warns against writing primarily to manipulate rankings and states there is no preferred article word count. Do not impose a 3–5% keyword density, so-called LSI keywords, or placement of the exact keyword in every heading/alt-text field; write descriptive headings, links and alternatives that serve actual readers. Consult its current official documentation rather than inherited folk rules.

## Decide the actual search job

- Identify target reader question, stage, intent (learn, compare, troubleshoot, navigate), and the project's credible expertise. Do not invent a search volume, ranking or audience query.
- Does the opening answer the promised question soon enough? Are definitions introduced before jargon, with examples that demonstrate the general claim?
- Does the article add original reporting, first-hand experience, a tested procedure, or a useful synthesis rather than rephrase sources?
- Verify title/H1 and introduction agree with the page and do not overpromise. Google may rewrite visible title links/snippets; never guarantee the displayed search result matches a supplied meta description.
- Use informative subheadings and internal links when they truly help readers find the next action. Do not invent website routes.
- Product comparisons and recommended options need sources, dates, limitations and a declared relationship where relevant.
- Do not rewrite a page date to feign freshness. “Updated” should reflect meaningful content maintenance.
- Schema, crawlability, canonical URL, pagination and sitemap checks require the actual published site/config; missing evidence is not a textual error.

## Paired judgments

**Edit — title overpromises answer**
- Title: “The complete guide to every LXD installation problem.” Article explains one missing prerequisite.
- Suggested title: “Fix a missing prerequisite when installing LXD” when consistent with the specific body and audience. Don't promise completeness unsupported by content.

**Leave — concise answer before detail**
- Heading: “How do I reset the token?” The next sentence answers directly, followed by a verified procedure. Don't add an SEO-flavoured introductory paragraph.

**Leave — natural synonyms**
- A well-understood term and its normal synonym do not need to be repeated verbatim to satisfy a supposed keyword density formula.

**Edit — weak heading with a clear, supplied intent**
- “Other things to know” → “When the setup token expires” *only when that section is actually about expiration*. A search-oriented edit must be truthful.
