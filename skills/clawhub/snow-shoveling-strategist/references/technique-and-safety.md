# Technique & Safety — Reference

## Why shoveling breaks hearts (literally)

Three mechanisms stack:

1. **Cold-air vasoconstriction.** Breathing cold air constricts coronary
   arteries and raises BP; the heart pumps harder against resistance.
2. **Static isometric grip.** Squeezing a shovel handle is a static
   contraction — it spikes blood pressure without the vasodilation that
   dynamic leg work produces. Arms raised + gripping is the worst combo.
3. **Upper-body inefficiency.** Arm work at a given heart rate does less
   useful work than leg work, so perceived effort understates cardiac
   load. Studies have recorded >85% of age-predicted max HR in sedentary
   adults within 2 minutes of heavy shoveling.

The population signal is real: storm days show measurable bumps in
cardiac hospitalizations, concentrated in middle-aged men who don't
exercise regularly. The countermeasures are equally real: cap effort,
cycle work/rest, warm up, breathe through a scarf, and know the exit
criteria.

## Effort model (what the script computes)

- Age-predicted max HR: **Tanaka** (208 − 0.7 × age) — more accurate than
  the 220−age folklore.
- Effort cap: 50% MHR (low fitness) / 65% (moderate) / 75% (high); any
  cardiac risk factor caps at 55%.
- Cycle: 2–3 min work / 2 min rest, timer on. The talk test is the field
  check: full sentence while working = roughly in zone; can't = over.

## Biomechanics that spare the back

- **Lever arm is everything.** A 1.8 kg wet load at 60 cm from the spine
  loads the L5/S1 disc with ~6–10× that ( spinal compression mechanics);
  halve the distance, halve the moment.
- Hands 30–45 cm apart on the shaft; back hand low, load hand close.
- Lift with hips and legs, back neutral; the power chain is ankles-knees-
  hips, not lumbar erectors.
- **Never bend and twist under load.** Face the throw direction with your
  feet, then lift. Disc fibers fail in combined flexion+rotation.
- Walk the snow to the stack when tired — carrying beats throwing at the
  end of a session, when form decays.
- Ergonomic shafts (bent handles) reduce required trunk flexion ~10–15°;
  a $30 upgrade that outperforms most "back gadgets."

## Layered clearing (the deep-snow method)

1. Skim 10 cm (wet) / 15 cm (powder) per pass down the length.
2. Push each layer to the stack line — don't lift what can be pushed.
3. The final thin layer gets the pusher in one sweep.
4. Layers double the "starts" but halve the per-lift load; total time is
   similar, injury risk and cardiac load drop sharply.

## Clothing & preparation

- Layers: moisture-wicking base + insulating mid + wind shell; you'll
  shed the shell 10 minutes in.
- Boots with real tread or ice cleats; most shoveling injuries on icy
  days are falls, not strains.
- Scarf or balaclava over the mouth: warming intake air reduces the
  cold-air coronary hit.
- Warm up indoors 5 min: marching, arm swings, bodyweight squats. Cold
  muscle tears at 60% of the strain a warm one survives.
- Hydrate before; skip the pre-shovel coffee and cigarette (both stack
  the BP spike).

## The red-flag list (stop now, emergency services)

- Chest pressure, tightness, pain — even mild, even "probably nothing"
- Pain radiating to jaw, neck, shoulder, or left arm
- Unusual shortness of breath; can't finish a sentence
- Light-headedness, cold sweat, nausea
- Palpitations that don't settle within 30 s of full rest
- Sudden extreme fatigue, back pain, "feeling off" (women, diabetics,
  older adults — atypical presentations are typical for them)

Chew an aspirin while waiting IF not contraindicated and you can do it
calmly. Unlock the door. Sit, don't lie down.

## Special situations

- **Snowblower:** still cardiac work but ~40% of the load; the injury
  profile is different (hand injuries from clearing jams — NEVER by hand,
  even powered off: the auger stores rotational energy; use a stick/tool).
- **Plow ridge / apron:** densest wet snow, done last, in layers; its
  weight-per-liter is the highest of the day.
- **Roof rake work:** overhead static effort is the worst cardiac angle;
  if you must, shorter cycles, and never alone.
- **Neighbor check:** the elderly neighbor's driveway is where this
  skill's cardiac section earns its keep.
