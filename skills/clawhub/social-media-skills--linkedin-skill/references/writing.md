# Writing LinkedIn posts

Readers decide in the first line whether to tap "see more", then in a few seconds whether the post
was worth it. Every post needs something only this person could write: what they saw, did, got
wrong, measured or believe.

## Start from material, not from a topic

Ask for the raw thing: a call summary, a changelog, a customer email (anonymised), a number from
the dashboard, a mistake, a photo from the week. One piece of material, one post. "Write about
leadership" makes a generic post; "the junior engineer who found the bug we'd missed for a year"
makes a real one.

## The first line (under about 140 characters)

Put the specific thing in it. Patterns that work when they're true:

- A number with a result: "We cut our onboarding from 14 days to 3. The fix wasn't software."
- A moment: "A planetarium visitor asked me why the Moon looks bigger near the horizon."
- A plain opinion: "Most status meetings should be a written update."
- A before and after: "Last year I said no to every podcast invite. This year I said yes to all of them."
- A list promise: "Artemis II in four pictures, from the launch pad to the far side of the Moon."

Line 2 makes "see more" worth tapping: the stakes, the twist, or the first concrete detail.
Avoid: questions nobody would ask, "I'm thrilled to announce", "Here's the thing", "Unpopular
opinion:", anything that could open anyone's post.

## The body

- Whole sentences in short paragraphs (2 to 4 lines), not one sentence per line.
- First-hand detail: names (with permission), numbers, what was said, what went wrong.
- One idea. If it needs two, it's two posts.
- Lists only when the content is a list; numbered, one line each.
- Keep the user's habits from `voice.md`: their length, their words, emoji and hashtags only if
  they use them.

## The ending

A real question when the user wants answers ("Which part of a big project gets the least
credit?"), a plain last line otherwise. Never "comment YES", "like if you agree", "tag someone who",
"repost to help", "follow me for more".

## Company Pages

Write as the company but show people: who built it, what the customer said, the photo from the
floor. Name the team members in the post who agreed to be named (the API can't tag them; tag in the
app if it matters). Make it easy to reshare: a post an employee would add one sentence of their own
to.

## Images and video

- A real photo of a person or the work beats a designed card.
- Text on images: a few words, readable on a phone; the post carries the message.
- Image sets: one point per image, the same shape, in reading order.
- Video: captions burned in, the point in the first seconds, 4:5 or 1:1.

## Links

A link card is fine when the click is the point (a launch page, an article). Otherwise leave the
link out and say where to find it if asked. Don't paste a URL into the text.

## Never

Invented numbers or quotes, a stranger's story told as the user's, engagement bait, Unicode bold
letters, hashtag walls, the same post on profile and Page the same day, AI phrasing the user would
never say.
