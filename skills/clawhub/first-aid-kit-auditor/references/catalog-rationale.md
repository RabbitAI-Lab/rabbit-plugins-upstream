# Catalog Rationale — Why Each Item Earns Its Slot

The catalog extends ANSI Z308.1 (workplace minimum) and Red Cross family-kit guidance to real
households. Baseline quantities are calibrated for a 4-person household and scale with headcount.

## Wound care (the daily-driver layer)
- **Adhesive bandages assorted (50)** — the highest-turnover item; buying 20 means running out
  mid-summer. Scale with kids.
- **Sterile gauze 4×4 + rolled gauze + tape** — covers anything bigger than a bandage: pressure
  for bleeding, dressing for abrasions. Gauze is the single most versatile item in the kit.
- **Self-adherent wrap** — holds dressings without tape (skin-friendly for kids/elderly).
- **Antiseptic wipes + antibiotic ointment + saline rinse** — the irrigation-first doctrine:
  clean tap water works for most wounds; saline for eyes and when water isn't at hand.
- **Hydrogel burn dressing** — kitchen burns are the #1 home injury; hydrogel cools and protects
  without the ice-pack frostbite risk. Burn gel for smaller areas.
- **Tweezers** — splinters, ticks (fine-point, not the eyebrow pair).
- **Instant cold pack x2** — bumps and sprains; no freezer required.
- **Nitrile gloves x6+** — body-substance isolation; latex allergies make nitrile the default.
- **Eye wash** — the most under-stocked item until the day it's needed.

## Adventure layer (medium/high profiles)
- **SAM splint + triangle bandage + finger splints** — immobilization for limbs in the field.
- **Hemostatic gauze + tourniquet** — for remote settings where EMS is >20 min; both demand
  training (Stop the Bleed course).
- **CPR pocket mask** — barrier device; makes bystander CPR more likely to happen.
- **Mylar blanket** — shock management and heat retention, weighs nothing.

## Medications (OTC, with expiry)
- **Acetaminophen + ibuprofen (adult and children's)** — the two workhorse antipyretics;
  children's versions are weight-dosed, check the label by kg not age.
- **Antihistamines (adult + children's)** — allergic reactions and itching; the oral route is
  the kit's route; severe allergy needs the Rx epinephrine plan.
- **Aspirin 325 mg** — chewed for suspected heart attack (unless anaphylaxis is suspected and
  an epinephrine auto-injector is in play — aspirin is not a substitute for EMS).
- **Anti-diarrheal + oral rehydration salts** — the travel pair; ORS matters more than the
  loperamide, especially for kids.
- **Anti-nausea** — motion sickness and stomach bugs.
- **Hydrocortisone 1%** — eczema flares, insect bites, contact rashes.

## Rx-flagged (conditions-driven; clinician required)
- **Epinephrine auto-injector** — any household with a known serious allergy; check the dose
  band (15 mg/0.15 mL junior vs 30 mg/0.3 mL) with the pediatrician; expires ~12–18 months.
- **Rescue inhaler** — asthmatic households; also worth a spacer for young kids.
- **Glucose gel** — diabetic households; know the choking rule: never give anything by mouth
  to a person who can't swallow.

## Pets (vet conversation first)
- **Vet wrap, styptic powder, muzzle/soft cone** — injured animals bite even beloved owners;
  the cone/muzzle protects both sides. Dosing pet antihistamines is a vet question, not a
  label-guess.

## The info layer (free, and the most skipped)
- Emergency contact card (family + pediatrician + vet + poison control number).
- Notepad + pencil — times, doses, mechanism of injury; gold for EMS handoff.
- First-aid handbook — read before you need it.

## Sizing Notes
- 1–2 people: baseline × 0.75 (floor at package sizes).
- 5–6 people: × 1.25–1.5 on consumables (bandages, wipes, gauze, gloves).
- 7+: split into two boxes per floor.
