# The Coach's Own Practice — Review, Supervision, Credentials, Burnout

Coaching is a craft with almost no feedback loop: the client thanks you either way, and the only honest verdict — did their behavior change — arrives months later, entangled with everything else in their life. Years of practice therefore do not make a coach better by themselves. A measurement, a supervisor, and a rubric do.

**Before any development decision**, read `## Craft Log` in `~/Clawic/data/coach/memory.md` (or `craft-log.md` once it has split out) and `## Due`. The last measured talk ratio, the last supervision date, and the running credential total are the three facts that decide what to work on — and all three are invisible from inside the sessions.

## Why Self-Assessment Fails

- **You cannot hear your own session.** In the room you are tracking the client, so the memory you review afterwards is a memory of your intentions, not of your behavior. Every coach who measures their talk ratio for the first time is surprised, and always in the same direction (SKILL.md Rule 2).
- **Client satisfaction is not an outcome measure.** Clients rate sessions on relief and rapport, both of which peak when you talk more and reassure faster — the two habits that reduce the effect of the work.
- **Experience alone does not compound.** In psychotherapy, the nearest craft with real outcome data, Goldberg's longitudinal analysis of therapists found client outcomes did not improve with years of practice and drifted slightly downward; there is no reason to expect coaching to be kinder. What improves practitioners is feedback on specific behavior, which is Ericsson's distinction between deliberate practice and repetition.
- **The consequence is a rule, not a mood**: one measured recording a month, one supervision slot per cadence, and a written verdict. Everything else in this file hangs off those two habits.

## The Recording Review

Monthly, twenty minutes, one recorded session, consent given and a deletion date set (`intake.md`). Do not review the session that felt interesting — review the one you have avoided thinking about.

Score the same six things every time, as numbers, not impressions:

1. **Talk ratio**: `coach_minutes ÷ total_minutes`. Above 30% is consulting, above 50% is lecturing (SKILL.md Rule 2). Sample the middle 20 minutes if timing the whole session is impractical.
2. **Long questions**: count questions over ~12 words or containing a comma. Each one carried your answer inside it (SKILL.md Rule 3).
3. **Longest silence you allowed**, in seconds. Under 3 means you are filling; the material that matters arrives at 5-7.
4. **Advice given without naming the switch** (`referral.md`). Count instances, not minutes — one unnamed instance is the failure.
5. **Rescues**: moments where they were uncomfortable and you softened, changed subject, or reassured.
6. **Commitments leaving the session** with action, date, and observable, out of the commitments set (SKILL.md Rule 6).

- **Write the six numbers and one sentence** to `## Craft Log`. Impressions ("felt scrappy") are unusable next month; `talk ratio 38%, too high in Options` names a target.
- **Work one number at a time, for a month.** Two targets produce a session where you monitor yourself instead of the client, which is worse than either fault.
- **The audio of your own voice is the entire intervention.** Coaches who review transcripts only miss pace, interruption, and the moment their tone changed — which is where the tell usually is.

## What To Work On Next

| What the recording shows | The underlying competency | The drill |
|---|---|---|
| Talk ratio above 30%, concentrated in Options | Not trusting the client to generate | Ban your own options for four sessions; ask "what else?" three times instead |
| Questions with commas, questions with a suggestion inside | Question discipline | Write each question down before saying it, for one session; cut to under 12 words (`questions.md`) |
| No silence over 3 seconds | Tolerating discomfort | Count to seven in your head after every question, for one whole session |
| Sessions run over, close is rushed | Time discipline | Set a timer for the Way Forward phase at 75% elapsed (`session.md`) |
| Commitments without an observable | Contracting the close | Refuse to write any commitment lacking the third field (SKILL.md Rule 6) |
| The client's energy dropped and you continued | Noticing over interpreting | Name the observable and stop talking: "your energy just changed" |
| Every session ends in insight, nothing changes | Confusing depth with movement | One month where every session ends in a commitment, no exceptions |
| You worked harder than the client | Your material, not theirs | Take it to supervision before changing technique (below) |
| Anything else | Unknown until measured | Pick the number furthest from target and change one behavior for a month |

## Supervision

Supervision is a structured conversation about your work with an experienced practitioner: what you did, what you missed, and what belongs to you rather than to the client. It is standard in the helping professions and optional in most of coaching, which is precisely why it distinguishes practices.

- **Cadence**: EMCC guidance is roughly one hour of supervision per 35 hours of coaching practice, with a floor of a few hours a year; the Association for Coaching's minimum is quarterly. Pick one, put it in `## Due` as a recurring row, and treat a missed slot as overdue rather than skipped.
- **Bring the client you avoid thinking about**, the one you feel oddly warm toward, and the one who bores you — not the interesting case. The material you would rather present is the material you have already processed.
- **Anonymize, and tell clients at intake that you do** (`intake.md`). Supervision is a genuine limit on confidentiality and clients almost never know it.
- **Group supervision is cheaper and shows you other coaches' errors**, which are easier to see than your own; one-to-one goes deeper on your patterns. Most practices need both at different stages.
- **Choose a supervisor trained in supervision**, not simply a more senior coach — the skill is different, and a senior coach without it delivers mentoring, which is a different room (below).
- **Mandatory triggers, whatever the cadence**: any Red Flags event or crisis (`referral.md`), an engagement ending badly, a dual-relationship question (`referral.md`), a client you have started dreading, and any urge to rescue a prospect (`intake.md`).

## Four Different Rooms

Coaches routinely try to get all four from one relationship and get none of them.

| Room | Object | Who it is with | Take there |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supervision | Your work with clients, ethically and technically | A trained supervisor | Cases, boundaries, patterns across clients, the ones you avoid |
| Mentor coaching | Your coaching against a competency model | A credentialed mentor coach | Recorded sessions, credential requirements, specific skills |
| Your own coach | Your goals — practice, income, direction | Another coach | The business decision, the niche, the thing you keep not doing (`practice.md`) |
| Therapy | Your own history and symptoms | A clinician | What surfaced in you, grief, anxiety, anything that predates the client |

- **The tell that you are in the wrong room** is repetition: the same theme returning across three supervision sessions with no movement usually belongs in one of the other three.
- **Coaching cannot be supervised by the client.** Asking a client for feedback on your coaching mid-engagement moves them into a role that costs them the work.

## Credentials

Credentials buy market access — corporate procurement uses them as a filter — and they do not predict client outcomes (SKILL.md, Where Experts Disagree). Choose accordingly, and log the hours into `## Craft Log` from day one whether or not you intend to apply.

The shape of the ICF route, which most corporate buyers recognize. Bodies revise the numbers: verify with the body before planning around them.

| Level | Coach-specific training | Client coaching hours | Mentor coaching | Also required |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ACC | 60+ hours | 100 hours, 75+ paid, 8+ clients | 10 hours over 3+ months | Performance evaluation and credentialing exam |
| PCC | 125+ hours | 500 hours, 450+ paid, 25+ clients | 10 hours over 3+ months | Performance evaluation and exam |
| MCC | 200+ hours | 2,500 hours, 2,200+ paid, 35+ clients | 10 hours with an MCC | Performance evaluations and exam |
| Renewal | Continuing education units on a three-year cycle | — | Required at first ACC renewal | Lapse means reapplying, not renewing |

- **EMCC's EIA runs four levels** (Foundation to Master Practitioner) and requires evidence of ongoing supervision and CPD — the practical difference from the ICF route, and the reason European corporate buyers sometimes ask for it specifically.
- **The credential is a floor for procurement, not a differentiator for private clients**, who buy evidence of outcomes and a referral (`practice.md`).
- **The performance evaluation is the part that actually develops you** — a recorded session scored against competencies by someone who is not you. Coaches who never apply can still buy that assessment once.

## Logging Hours

- **Log the same day, into the `sessions/<year>.md` row.** Hours reconstructed from a calendar two years later are an estimate, and every credentialing body asks for client counts and paid/unpaid splits that a calendar does not hold.
- **The fields that matter**: date, client, minutes, paid or pro bono, individual or group. `sessions/<year>.md` already carries the first four; the running total belongs in `## Craft Log` (`memory-template.md`).
- **Pro bono hours count toward most requirements but are capped**, and the cap is the reason people fall short at the end. Keep the paid and pro bono split in `## Craft Log`, not just the total.
- **Group and team sessions count differently** depending on the body and the number of participants (`teams.md`). Record the format in the `sessions/<year>.md` row so the recount is possible.
- Credential hours are working data, not a secret: names, dates, counts, and hours all belong in the file. Portal logins and credentials do not (`memory-template.md`).

## Your Own Material in the Room

Every coach has clients who pull something out of them. The pattern is visible in your behavior, not in theirs.

- **The rescue urge** — the client you want to help rather than coach. It produces advice, softened challenge, and eventually resentment. Named at intake, it is a fit signal (`intake.md`).
- **Needing to be liked** shows up as a missing challenge: you know what to say at minute 30 and you say the friendly version. The client pays for the version you did not say.
- **Competing with the client** — over-contributing expertise with a high-status client — turns the session into a demonstration of you.
- **Boredom** is data about the contract, not about the person: nothing is at stake in this goal, or you have been running sessions where nothing was ever decided.
- **The mirror client**, whose problem is your problem, produces coaching aimed at yourself. Advice you find yourself giving urgently is usually addressed inward.
- **The tell is always your own energy** — dread, excitement, relief when they cancel. Notice it, do not interpret it in the session, and bring it to supervision.
- **Working harder than the client** is the summary symptom of all of these. If it persists across two sessions, re-contract or end (SKILL.md Traps).

## Burnout and Caseload

- **The load is emotional, not temporal.** Six sessions of held attention costs more than a ten-hour day of most other work, which is why an honest caseload ceiling is lower than the calendar suggests (`practice.md`).
- **Early signals, in the order they usually appear**: reading notes minutes before the session instead of the day before; relief when a client cancels; sessions blurring together; giving advice because it is faster; skipping the check-in because it takes energy (SKILL.md Rule 5).
- **A ten-minute gap between sessions is a corridor, not a recovery.** Twenty minutes with the screen off, and never more than two back-to-back.
- **Coaches with financial pressure keep dependent clients** — the mechanism is invisible from inside, which is why dependency belongs on the supervision agenda and not only in the review (`progress.md`).
- **The crisis you handled has a cost you have not paid yet.** Debrief within days; coaches who absorb these quietly are the ones who leave the profession (`referral.md`).
- **Recovery is structural**: fewer slots, a real gap between engagements, a week with no clients in the calendar at all. Motivation interventions on yourself are the same mistake you would name in a client.

## Isolation and Peer Practice

- **Solo practice hides errors for years.** The cheapest correction is a triad — rotating coach, client, and observer, fifteen minutes each, with feedback on the coaching (`teams.md`).
- **One honest colleague beats a large community**: someone who will say "you interrupted her four times" and does not need you to be good.
- **Coach for free, deliberately, when you are working a specific skill.** A pro bono client who knows they are part of your development is a legitimate arrangement and produces feedback a paying client will not give.
- **Watch other coaches work.** Recorded demonstration sessions from any school, watched with the six numbers in hand, teach faster than reading about competencies.

## Staying Sharp

- **Depth in one modality beats collecting certificates.** Buying another training is the coaching equivalent of the client who plans instead of starting (`stuck.md`) — it feels like development and changes no behavior in the room.
- **Read outside coaching**: negotiation, motivational interviewing, clinical supervision literature, behavior change research. The field's own literature is thin and self-referential; the adjacent ones are where the techniques come from.
- **Re-read your own oldest client file once a year.** It is the clearest available evidence of what you have and have not learned.
- **Retire a technique when you notice you use it because it is yours**, not because this client needs it. Signature moves are how a coach stops adapting.
- Any rubric, development plan, or session-scoring sheet you will re-read belongs in `~/Clawic/data/coach/artifacts/<kebab-name>.md`, not loose in the log.

**After every recording review, supervision session, mentor-coaching hour, or block of credential hours**, write it to `## Craft Log` in `~/Clawic/data/coach/memory.md` — the date, the six measured numbers with the one behavior you are changing, what came out of supervision (never the client's disclosure), and credential hours as a running total with the paid and pro bono split — and move the section to `~/Clawic/data/coach/craft-log.md` with its `## Boxes` line the moment it crosses the split threshold. Put the next supervision date, the next recording review, and any credential deadline in `## Due` in the same turn (`memory-template.md`).
