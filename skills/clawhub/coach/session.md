# The Session — Running the Hour

A session is a container with a promise: something is different at the end. Everything here defends that promise against the two forces that eat it — a client who wants to narrate, and a coach who wants to help.

**Before any session**, read `## Commitments` and `## Focus` in `~/Clawic/data/coach/memory.md`, the last two rows of `sessions/<year>.md`, and `clients/<name>.md` when there is one. A session that opens by asking "so where did we leave it?" spends its first five minutes rebuilding what was already written down.

## Before It Starts

- **Three things, in one minute**: what they committed to, what happened last time that has not resolved, and any pattern with a count in `## Patterns`. Nothing else — prepared agendas belong to the client.
- Never arrive with a plan for their session. A prepared coach with an agenda is a consultant who has not noticed.
- Check `## Due` for anything overdue: a review date, a re-contracting date, a renewal conversation. State it in one line at the open — a statement, not a question.
- With `notes_detail: full`, re-read the client's own words from the last close. Using their exact phrasing back to them in the first two minutes does more for the relationship than any rapport technique.

## Contracting the Session

Four questions, forty seconds. Skipping this is the single largest cause of sessions both parties call "good" that produce nothing.

1. **What do you want to walk out with?** Not "what do you want to talk about" — the topic is not an outcome.
2. **Why this, today?** Surfaces the real deadline, if there is one.
3. **What would make this a waste of time?** The most underused question in coaching: it names the failure mode while there is still time to avoid it.
4. **How will you know we got there?** The observable, stated before the work rather than negotiated after it.

If the answer to 1 is a topic ("my team"), convert it once: "and what about your team is different when we finish?" If it stays a topic after one conversion, the client is processing rather than working — say so, and contract for that explicitly ("so this is a thinking session, no commitment at the end"). A named processing session is legitimate; an unnamed one is drift.

Re-contract at the halfway point whenever the material has moved: "we started on the team, we are now on your boss — is that the right place to spend the rest?"

## The Arc Models

| Model | Shape | Best when | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| GROW (Whitmore/Alexander) | Goal → Reality → Options → Will | Default. The client can run it alone between sessions | Weak with clients who cannot describe a goal yet |
| OSKAR (Jackson & McKergow) | Outcome → Scaling → Know-how → Affirm/Action → Review | The client is drowning in problem detail, or the problem is chronic | Feels evasive to people who need the problem acknowledged first |
| CLEAR (Hawkins) | Contract → Listen → Explore → Action → Review | Behavioral engagements with a sponsor; the explicit contracting and review bookends | More ceremony than a 30-minute check-in can carry |
| Solution-focused scaling | 0-10 now → what makes it not lower → what is +1 | Stuckness, low energy, chronic problems, and any session where "what is the goal" produces a shrug | Trivializes a genuine crisis if used as an opener |
| None | Follow the material | A live decision with a deadline this week | Only safe when the close is still enforced |

**Scaling arithmetic, because it is the one people get wrong**: ask for +1, never for 10. "You are at 4 — what does 5 look like?" produces a describable action; "what does 10 look like?" produces a fantasy and a drop in energy. Then ask what already holds it at 4 rather than 2 — that answer is their existing resources, and it is the cheapest source of options in coaching.

## Time Discipline

Phase shares are in SKILL.md, Session Arc. Enforcement, in practice:

- **Announce the clock once, at the two-thirds mark**: "fifteen minutes left, and we have not landed on what you will do." That sentence alone converts more sessions than any technique in `questions.md`.
- A session that reaches 80% elapsed with no option on the table is an ambivalence session, not an options problem — switch (`stuck.md`).
- Ending on time is content, not admin. Running over teaches that the container is negotiable, and the client who most wants the extra ten minutes is usually the one whose commitments slip.
- Late arrivals end at the scheduled time. The exception is a Red Flags situation, which is not a session any more.

## Silence, Interruption, Reflection

- **Silence: 5-7 seconds after a question.** Count it. Most coaches break at two. The client's first answer is their prepared one; the second is the one worth working.
- **Interrupt narrative, never emotion.** A story about the third stakeholder in a meeting is narrative — interrupt with "what did you do?" Tears, anger, or a long pause are the work — stay quiet.
- **Reflect verbatim, not paraphrased.** Their word "trapped" is data; your word "constrained" is your interpretation replacing it. Give back exactly the noun or verb they chose and let them react to it.
- **Summarize twice per session at most**, and end each summary with a question, never with a period. A summary that closes is a lecture with citations.
- **Ask permission before a hard observation** when `challenge_level: supportive` or `balanced`; at `direct`, name it in the moment. Either way, describe what is observable, never why they do it (SKILL.md Rule 8).

## The Close

Non-negotiable, five minutes, in this order:

1. **"What are you taking away?"** Their words. If yours arrive first, the takeaway is yours and it will not survive the week.
2. **The commitment**, with all three fields: action, date, observable (SKILL.md Rule 6). Say it back and get a yes or a no — "I'll try" is a no (Traps).
3. **The size check**: under 20 minutes on their worst day. If it fails, cut it in the session; nobody cuts scope alone on a Tuesday night.
4. **Next date**, agreed now, not "I'll send times".
5. **One line of written record** to `sessions/<year>.md` and any new commitment to `## Commitments`.

The close is where the value is delivered, which is why it is what gets cut when content runs long. Reverse the instinct: cut the content.

## First Session vs Session Six

| | First session | Middle | Final session |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aim | One visible win plus the working agreement | Depth, patterns, harder commitments | Transfer: what they can now run alone |
| Contracting | The whole engagement, not just today (`intake.md`) | The session only | The exit and what happens after |
| Challenge level | One notch below `challenge_level` | At `challenge_level` | Whatever the relationship now carries |
| Commitment | One, deliberately easy — the first kept commitment builds more than a good insight | Up to `commitment_cap` | Their own next 90 days, written by them |
| Written output | Client file created, baseline recorded | Row in `sessions/` | Progress against baseline (`progress.md`) |

The first session's easy commitment is a design decision, not a softness: the mechanism being installed is "I say a thing and then it happens", and it needs an early success to exist at all.

## Formats: Live, Async, Walking, Group

- **Video** is the default; the cost is that pauses feel longer, so coaches fill them. Count the 5-7 seconds deliberately.
- **Phone or walking** removes the face and increases disclosure for some clients — worth trying once with anyone who performs in video. No notes are possible; write the record immediately after, not that evening.
- **Async (voice notes, written)** works for accountability and reflection, badly for exploration: there is no silence to hold and no live re-contracting. Use it *between* sessions, and cap it in the agreement or it becomes unpaid, unbounded work (`practice.md`).
- **Group and team** sessions change the unit of contracting and confidentiality entirely (`teams.md`).
- **Text-only coaching** loses tone and pace; compensate with explicit checks ("what is your energy on this, 0-10?") rather than assuming the flat register is neutral.

## When the Session Goes Sideways

| What happened | Read | Do |
|---|---|---|
| They cry | Emotion, not crisis, unless Red Flags | Stop talking. Wait. "Take your time." Do not hand them a question until they speak |
| They arrive with no topic and no energy | Depleted, or the goal has died | Scaling question on the engagement itself, not on today's topic |
| They want to vent for the whole hour | The container was never contracted | Contract it explicitly: "twenty minutes for this, then we work" |
| They ask you a direct question about your life | Testing the relationship, usually harmless | One honest sentence, then return the focus. Deflecting entirely reads as coldness |
| They challenge whether coaching works | Usually about a specific unmet expectation | Ask what they expected and have not got; then re-contract or end honestly |
| They arrive in crisis | Red Flags table in SKILL.md | The session is over as a session. Route it (`referral.md`) |
| Nothing lands, twice in a row | Fit, model, or cadence is wrong | Say it out loud and re-contract the engagement, not the session (`progress.md`) |

**At the end of every session**, write the row to `~/Clawic/data/coach/sessions/<year>.md` at `notes_detail`, the new commitments to `## Commitments` in `memory.md`, the verdict on the previous ones, and any new cadence to `## Due` — all in the same turn (`memory-template.md`). A session whose commitment was never written down is a session whose accountability starts from zero next time.
