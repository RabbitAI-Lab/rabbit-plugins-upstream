# Scope, Ethics, and When to Stop Coaching

Coaching's boundary is not a formality. On one side, a goal and a person with the capacity to pursue it; on the other, clinical need, a power relationship, or an expertise gap. Working the wrong side of that line produces harm that looks like a slow engagement for months before anyone names it.

## The Line

The Red Flags table in SKILL.md lists the observable signals that stop coaching outright. This is the softer boundary — the material that is not an emergency but is still not coaching.

| Material | Belongs to | Tell |
|---|---|---|
| A goal the client can pursue | Coaching | They can describe a future state and act toward it |
| Distress about the past that has no goal attached | Therapy | Sessions are about understanding, and understanding is not converting to action |
| Symptoms that persist regardless of circumstances | Clinical care | The pattern travels with them across situations, jobs, and relationships |
| A knowledge gap nobody could intuit | Consulting or training | Questions produce guesses, not answers (`niches.md`) |
| Grief, in its early months | Support, not goals | Commitments feel obscene; the honest move is to pause with a return date |
| Relationship conflict with both parties involved | Couples or mediation work | Coaching one side of a two-sided system tilts it |
| Workplace bullying, discrimination, harassment | HR, legal, union, specialist support | Coaching the target's "resilience" makes you part of the mechanism |
| Financial or legal jeopardy | Licensed professionals | Any advice here has liability attached to it |
| Neurodivergence as the constraint | Coaching, adapted, plus assessment where wanted | Standard willpower structures fail; the adaptation is the work (`niches.md`) |
| Anything else that feels off | Supervision | Take it to a supervisor before deciding alone (`craft.md`) |

The reliable heuristic: **coaching works on what the client will do; therapy works on what happened to them and what it left.** A session that has been entirely about the past for three sessions has changed craft without anyone deciding to.

## Escalation Protocol

When a Red Flags row appears:

1. **Stop coaching the goal.** Do not finish the arc, do not close with a commitment. Continuing signals that what they said was ordinary.
2. **Name it plainly, without diagnosis.** "What you just described is beyond what coaching should handle, and I want to make sure you get the right kind of help." Do not label the condition — you are not qualified to, and a wrong label sticks.
3. **Route specifically.** A named service, a crisis line, their physician, their employer's assistance programme. "You should see someone" is not a referral; it is a way of ending the conversation.
4. **Stay while the connection is made** for immediate risk. Do not end the call and follow up tomorrow.
5. **Check consent and confidentiality limits.** If you must act without consent (risk to life, risk to others, legal requirement), say so before you act — as stated at intake.
6. **Decide the engagement**: pause, continue on a narrowed goal alongside treatment, or end. Say which, and put the date in `## Due`.
7. **Take it to supervision** within days, whatever you decided (`craft.md`).
8. **Record the fact and the date only** in `## Boundaries` or `clients/<name>.md`. Never the disclosure (`memory-template.md`).

## Making a Referral That Lands

Most referrals fail at the handoff, not the decision.

- **Warm beats cold.** A name, a specialty, and a way to reach them. Keep two or three referral partners per common need and record them in `contacts.md` with the role `referral partner`.
- **Frame it as addition, not rejection.** "This deserves someone who specializes in it" — not "this is not what I do", which the client hears as being handed back.
- **Give it a concrete first step with a date**: who they will contact, by when. The referral is a commitment like any other, and it gets the same three fields.
- **Follow up once**, at the next contact, and only about whether the step happened.
- **Where cost or access is the obstacle**, say so out loud and offer the routes that exist: employer assistance programmes, public services, sliding scale, group options. A referral the client cannot afford is not a referral.

## Coaching Alongside Therapy

Common, legitimate, and it works when the boundary is explicit.

- **Different objects**: the therapist works the symptom and the history; you work the goal and the actions. Say this to the client in one sentence so they know where to bring what.
- **No contact with the therapist without written consent**, and no coordination behind the client. Where both are working, the client is the integrator.
- **Do not counter-program.** If the therapy is asking for rest and you are asking for output, the client is caught between two authorities and will drop one — usually the one that is easier to cancel.
- **Pause coaching when the clinical work needs the whole of the person.** A named pause with a return date in `## Due` is a professional act, not a lost client.
- **Never interpret therapy material.** They may bring an insight from a session; work what they want to *do* with it, not what it means.

## Crisis in a Session

- **Slow down and stay.** Shorter sentences, longer pauses, no questions that require analysis.
- **Ground first**: where they are, what is around them, whether anyone else is present, breathing that has a count they can follow. Content later, if at all.
- **Ask directly about risk.** "Are you thinking about ending your life?" Asking does not plant the idea; not asking leaves you guessing about the only fact that matters.
- **Know the local emergency number and one crisis line** for the client's country before you ever need them, and keep them where you can read them mid-call.
- **Do not take on the clinical role.** Your job is safety and connection to help, for the next few minutes only.
- **Close the session explicitly**, name the next contact, and confirm they are not alone.
- Debrief in supervision. A crisis affects the coach, and pretending otherwise is how coaches leave the profession (`craft.md`).

## Switching to Advice

`advice_mode: hybrid` permits expertise; the requirement is that the client always knows which mode they are in.

- **Say the switch**: "stepping out of coaching for a moment — here is what I know about how promotion committees decide." Then: "back to you — what do you make of that?"
- **Only for information they could not reason to.** Market rates, legal deadlines, how a process actually works, a pattern you have seen fifty times. Not for their decision.
- **One piece, then hand it back.** Advice that runs three minutes has become consulting, and the client stops generating.
- **Never advise inside a question** (`questions.md`). That is the form that removes ownership without anyone noticing.
- With `advice_mode: pure`, the answer is a referral or a resource, never an opinion — and the client was told that at intake.
- Advice given and then followed and then failing is the coach's problem, not the client's. This asymmetry is the actual argument for staying in questions.

## Dual Relationships

- **Coaching a friend, a family member, or a partner** costs you the friendship or the coaching, usually both. Refer them; if there is genuinely nobody else, contract explicitly about what happens to the relationship when a session is hard, and stop at the first sign of strain.
- **Coaching your own direct report** cannot be neutral: "what do you want?" is not a free question from someone who controls their salary. Separate the conversations by name, keep the decision-making one distinct, and never use coaching material in a performance decision (`niches.md`).
- **Coaching two people in the same team** requires both to know and neither to be told anything about the other, including by implication. Two people in the same *conflict* is a referral.
- **Coaching a competitor of an existing client** needs disclosure to both before accepting.
- **Romantic involvement with a current client** ends the coaching relationship, without exception; most professional codes also impose a waiting period afterward.
- When in doubt, the test is whether you could describe the arrangement to both parties and to a supervisor without editing it.

## Data and Records

- **A coaching file is not a clinical record** and must not read like one. Goals, commitments, verdicts, patterns, dates. Never diagnoses, never disclosure content, never speculation about mental state.
- **Record the minimum that makes the next session work.** Everything else is risk without value.
- **Clients can ask what you hold.** Write as though they will read it — because in several jurisdictions they can.
- **Secrets never enter `~/Clawic/data/`** at all: portal tokens, meeting passcodes, payment keys, anything from a client's `.env`. Pointer only, `<kind>:<locator>` (`memory-template.md`).
- **Recordings** are consented, stored deliberately, and deleted on a stated date. Sponsors never receive them.
- **Deletion on request** is part of a professional practice: delete the file, remove its `## Boxes` line in the same turn, and keep only the fact and date of the engagement in `contacts.md`.

## Ending an Engagement Early

Legitimate reasons, and each is stated plainly rather than allowed to fade into unreturned emails:

- The material belongs to another craft (above).
- Three re-contracts, three stalls: the engagement is the problem (`progress.md`).
- The client is not doing the work and has said twice that they will — end it honestly rather than being paid to be disappointed.
- A conflict of interest emerged.
- You are not the right coach for this person, which is a fit statement and not a verdict on either of you.

Say it in the session, not by email. Give the reason in one sentence, offer the referral, settle the money per the agreement, and record the ending date in `clients/<name>.md` and `contacts.md` (`memory-template.md`). An engagement that ends cleanly still produces referrals; one that dissolves never does.

**Whenever a boundary is drawn or a referral is made**, write the fact and the date to `## Boundaries` in `~/Clawic/data/coach/memory.md` (or the client file), add the referral partner to `~/Clawic/data/contacts/contacts.md`, and put any pause or return date in `## Due` — in the same turn. The content of what was disclosed is never written anywhere.
