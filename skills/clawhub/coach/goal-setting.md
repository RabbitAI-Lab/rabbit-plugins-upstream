# Goals — Setting Ones Worth Pursuing

Most coaching failure is upstream of effort: the goal was unmeasurable, borrowed, purely outcome-shaped, or too far away to act on. Fixing the goal is cheaper than motivating the client toward a bad one.

**Before setting or revising a goal**, read `## Focus` in `~/Clawic/data/coach/memory.md` and any 90-day plan in `artifacts/`. A goal that is being set for the third time in a year is a different conversation from a first one — and the count is only visible in the record.

## The Four Tests

Run all four before any planning. A goal that fails one is repaired here, not compensated for later.

| Test | Question | Fail looks like | Repair |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ownership | "Tell me the goal as you would tell a close friend" | The shape changes, or the energy drops | Origin question, then the permission move (`stuck.md`) |
| Measurability | "How will you know you have it?" | Adjectives, no observable, no date | Convert to an observable with a number and a date |
| Control | "What part of this do you control?" | The whole goal depends on other people's decisions | Split into outcome and process (below) |
| Proximity | "What happens in the next two weeks?" | Nothing until month five | Chunk to `horizon_days` and define this fortnight's action |

## Outcome, Process, Identity

Three layers, and a working goal has all three. The most common defect is an outcome goal alone.

| Layer | Example | Controlled? | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outcome | "20 paying users by 15 October" | No | Direction and the honest scoreboard |
| Process | "4 customer conversations a week, one release every Friday" | Yes | Where all commitments come from |
| Identity | "Someone who ships weekly" | Becomes true through the process | What survives a bad month |

- **Commitments only ever come from the process layer.** A commitment attached to an outcome ("get 20 users this month") is unkeepable by construction, and missing it teaches the client that commitments are unreliable.
- **The outcome stays visible anyway.** Deleting it to protect feelings removes the only feedback that tells you the process is wrong.
- **Identity statements outperform goal statements** because they generalize: "I'm someone who ships weekly" answers hundreds of small decisions that "I want 20 users" does not. Get the client to state it in the first person, in their words, and record it verbatim in `## Focus` — the phrasing is the asset.
- **Evidence, not affirmation.** An identity claim needs a growing list of instances ("I shipped on 6 of the last 8 Fridays"), otherwise it is a slogan that collapses at the first miss.

## The Goal Behind the Goal

Ask "what will having that give you?" three times, then "and what would you do with that?" The chain terminates in a value — autonomy, security, respect, contribution, freedom, belonging.

Why it pays:

- **It reveals cheaper routes.** "I want to make partner" → "so I'm taken seriously" → the client discovers three ways to be taken seriously, two of which do not cost five years.
- **It survives failure.** When the outcome slips, a client anchored to the value keeps the process; a client anchored to the outcome quits.
- **It exposes borrowed goals.** A chain that terminates in "then my father would stop asking" is a goal about someone else, and it needs the conversation in `stuck.md` before any plan.
- Record the value and the client's own phrasing in `## Focus`. Six months on, it is what you use to test whether a new opportunity is aligned or merely attractive.

Escape hatch: with a client in a live decision this week, skip the ladder. It is an exploration tool, not a triage tool.

## Horizon and Chunking

- **Default chunk: `horizon_days` (90).** Long enough for real change, short enough that the deadline is emotionally present. Twelve months is a direction, not a goal — nobody acts differently on a Tuesday because of something due next June.
- **Structure**: annual direction → 90-day goal → weekly process commitment → this week's action. Each level is derived from the one above; a weekly action that cannot be traced upward is busywork with a good conscience.
- **Two checkpoints per 90 days** (day 30 and day 60), written into `## Due`. Their job is to test whether the process is producing outcome movement, not to check effort.
- **The abandon condition is set at the start**, not improvised at day 75: "if the process ran for six weeks and the outcome moved zero, the goal is wrong, not the effort." Writing this into the plan is what makes an honest stop possible later, when sunk cost is loud.
- Short-horizon exception: clients whose constraint is executive function do worse with 90-day frames and better with 7-14 day cycles (`niches.md`).

## Making It Measurable

- **The observable test**: could a stranger, given only your definition, say yes or no without asking either of you? "Be healthier" fails. "Walk 8,000 steps on 5 days a week, per the phone" passes.
- **Leading before lagging.** Lagging indicators (revenue, weight, offers) move late and demoralize; leading indicators (conversations, sessions, applications) move immediately and are what the client controls. Write both into `## Focus`, and let the leading one drive the check-ins (`progress.md`).
- **A baseline or the number is meaningless.** Record where they are in `## Focus` (or the baseline table in `clients/<name>.md`) on the day the goal is set — including a 0-10 subjective rating where nothing objective exists. Without a baseline, month three's "I think it's better" cannot be verified or celebrated.
- **Use a scale where the thing is not countable**: confidence delegating (0-10), energy at 18:00, how often the intrusive thought arrives per day. A subjective number recorded consistently beats an objective number nobody collects.
- Where the metric is a body measure, it belongs in the shared health box, not in the coaching files (`memory-template.md`).

## Competing Goals

- **Three simultaneous goals is the practical ceiling** for a single person, and even that assumes they draw on different hours and different energy. Four is a list, and the list produces zero.
- Make the trade explicit: "if this month goes to the business, what visibly does not happen at home?" A trade named in advance is a decision; the same trade discovered in week three is a failure.
- **Sequence rather than parallelize** when two goals compete for the same hour, and set the date the second one starts. "Later" without a date means never, and the client knows it.
- Watch for the goal that is quietly funded by sleep. Goals financed from recovery time come due, and the bill arrives as the "motivation problem" in month two.

## Revising vs Abandoning

| Signal | Read | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Process ran, outcome did not move, 6+ weeks | The theory of change is wrong | Revise the process, keep the goal |
| Process never ran | Sizing or ownership, not the goal | `accountability.md`, then `stuck.md` |
| Circumstances changed materially | The goal was right for a world that ended | Rewrite it, and say plainly that it is a new goal |
| Energy has been flat for three sessions | Probably not theirs | Ownership test (`stuck.md`) |
| Goal reset for the second time, same shape | Person-first read is correct | Stop working the goal; work the pattern |
| It has been achieved but nobody said so | Common, and demotivating | Name it, mark it in `## Focus`, and set the next one deliberately |

Abandoning well is a coaching outcome, not a failure: state what was learned, what it cost, and what it frees. An unabandoned dead goal sits in the background of every future session and quietly taxes each new commitment.

## Goal Frameworks, Honestly

- **SMART** is a checklist for measurability and nothing more. It says nothing about whether the goal is theirs or worth wanting, which is where coaching engagements actually fail.
- **OKRs** are an organizational alignment tool: objectives plus a few measurable key results, deliberately ambitious. Ported to an individual, the "70% is success" norm reads as permission to under-deliver, and the individual has no one to align with (`okr` covers the org use properly).
- **WOOP / mental contrasting** (Oettingen) — wish, outcome, obstacle, plan — is the most useful of the three in a coaching session, because the obstacle step forces the if-then that `accountability.md` needs anyway.
- **Word of the year, vision boards, manifestation**: they set direction and mean something to the client. They contain no process layer, so treat them as the identity layer and build a process underneath rather than dismissing them.
- Framework choice matters far less than the four tests. A goal that passes ownership, measurability, control, and proximity works in any notation.

**When a goal is set, split, revised, or abandoned**, write it to `## Focus` in `~/Clawic/data/coach/memory.md` — outcome, process, identity, the value the ladder terminated in, in the client's words — with the baseline and the date. Put the 90-day plan, its checkpoints and its abandon condition in `~/Clawic/data/coach/artifacts/<kebab-name>.md`, add its `## Boxes` line in the same turn, and put the checkpoint dates in `## Due` (`memory-template.md`). A plan nobody can find on day 60 is a plan that was written for the session, not for the goal.
