# Groups, Teams, and Cohorts

One-to-one mechanics do not survive contact with a group. Confidentiality has more parties, air time is scarce, the client may be the system rather than any person in it, and someone who is not in the room is usually paying.

**Before a group or team session**, read the `clients/<name>.md` file for the engagement and `## Due`. Group work has more dates that matter — sponsor checkpoints, cohort milestones, the mid-programme review — and missing one is visible to everybody at once.

## Group vs Team vs Facilitation

| Form | The client is | Success looks like | Wrong tool when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Group coaching | Each individual, who happens to be in a room with peers | Individual goals move; peers supply perspective and accountability | The members have to work together afterwards |
| Team coaching | The team as a system | The team's collective way of working changes, not five people improving separately | There is no shared task or shared outcome |
| Facilitation | The meeting's output | A decision or an artifact exists by the end | The need is development rather than a deliverable |
| Training | The curriculum | A skill is demonstrably learned | The gap is action, not capability |

Sponsors ask for one and mean another routinely: "team coaching" that means five individual engagements, or "coaching" that means facilitating a strategy offsite. Name which one you are selling before the proposal (`intake.md`).

## Contracting With a Group

Everything from one-to-one contracting, plus four clauses the group needs and never invents on its own.

- **Confidentiality inside the group.** The standard line: what is said here stays here, what is learned here goes home. Say it in the first session and repeat it at the start of each of the first three.
- **Attendance.** A member who attends half the sessions damages the others' trust more than their own progress. State the minimum and the consequence at the start.
- **Air time is a shared resource** and the contract says so, which makes managing it later an agreement being honored rather than the coach interrupting.
- **What the sponsor receives**: attendance, themes, and progress against the agreed objective — never who said what. Write the exact sentence and read it to the group in session one (`intake.md`).
- **Opt-out.** For any group the members did not choose to join, a real way to leave without career damage — otherwise participation is compliance and the material is performed.

## Air Time

Arithmetic first: **per-person speaking time = (session minutes − structure minutes) ÷ members.** A 90-minute session with 20 minutes of opening and closing structure and 8 members gives under 9 minutes each. Design against that number rather than discovering it.

- **6-8 members is the working ceiling** for a group where everyone speaks meaningfully; beyond that, break into pairs or trios for the working part and reconvene.
- **Round-robin openers** guarantee a floor: one sentence each, in order, no discussion. Fast, and it means nobody spends the session deciding whether to speak.
- **Hot-seat format** gives one member depth while the rest observe and contribute questions — rotate on a published schedule so the quiet members are scheduled rather than volunteering.
- **The dominant member is a design problem, not a personality problem.** Structure absorbs it: time-boxed turns, "let's hear from someone who has not spoken", pairs. Address it privately only if structure has already failed twice.
- **The silent member** may be processing, may be unsafe, may be out of place. Ask privately after the second session, never call on them cold in the room.

## Group Session Shapes

| Shape | Structure | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Round + hot seat | Check-in round, one member coached for 25 min, group questions, commitments round | Peer groups with a shared context |
| Action learning set | One member presents a real problem; the group may ask **questions only**, no advice | Managers and professionals; the no-advice rule is the whole method |
| Triads | Rotating coach / client / observer, 15 min each, observer gives feedback on the coaching | Teaching coaching skills; also the best cheap practice for coaches (`craft.md`) |
| Mastermind | Each member presents progress and one ask; group commits to specific help | Founders and independents, where the network is half the value |
| Themed workshop plus coaching | Short input, then applied coaching in pairs | Cohort programmes with a curriculum |

The no-advice rule in action learning is what makes it work and what everyone breaks in the first session. Enforce it out loud the first three times; after that the group enforces it.

## Team Coaching

The client is the system. Individual brilliance is not the goal, and coaching five people separately does not produce it.

- **Diagnose the team first**: purpose, clarity of roles, decision rights, how conflict is handled, how they talk about people who are not present. That last one predicts more than any survey.
- **Work in the live meeting.** Team coaching that happens in a workshop and never in a real meeting changes the workshop. Observe a real one, then intervene on the pattern in the moment.
- **The leader is inside the system, not above it.** A leader who commissions team coaching and exempts themselves has commissioned an expensive complaint session; contract for their participation explicitly.
- **Team commitments need a named owner and a date**, same three fields, and they get checked at the top of the next team session exactly like an individual's (SKILL.md Rule 6).
- **The absent stakeholder** — another team, a customer, the board — belongs in the room as a perspective: "what would they say about this decision?"
- **Conflict is the material, not an interruption.** A team that never disagrees in front of you is managing you.
- Individual sessions alongside team work are legitimate and require a stated rule about what crosses between them. Usually: nothing, unless the individual says it.

## 360 Debriefs

- **The report is the start of a conversation, not the product.** A debrief that walks the numbers page by page produces defensiveness and no action.
- **Sequence**: their prediction first ("what do you expect this to say?"), then the data, then the surprises, then two themes, then one behavior to change. Prediction first is what makes the surprises land.
- **Aggregate, never attribute.** Even when everyone knows who said it, naming sources ends the possibility of honest feedback next time.
- **Outliers are data, not verdicts.** One harsh rater in a set of eight is a relationship to look at, not a judgment on the person.
- **Close with one behavior, one audience, one review date.** "Ask a question before giving my view, in the leadership meeting, reviewed in six weeks" beats a development plan with nine themes.
- Baseline and re-run the same instrument at the end of the engagement — it is the strongest evidence executive coaching produces (`progress.md`).

## Cohort Programmes

- **Fixed start and end dates** create the accountability a rolling programme lacks; a cohort that anyone can join at any time has no shared stage and no peer momentum.
- **Curriculum and coaching alternate.** Content-only becomes a course nobody finishes; coaching-only leaves people without shared vocabulary.
- **Pair members for between-session accountability** and rotate the pairs at the midpoint. Peer pairs deliver most of the between-session adherence in a cohort.
- **Attrition concentrates after session two and again after the midpoint.** Design a check-in from you personally at both points — an individual message at the right week saves more places than any content change.
- **The community outlives the programme** and is often what participants valued most; decide before launch whether you are running it afterwards, because an abandoned community damages the programme's reputation.
- Cohorts are a `projects/` file in the shared box, with the roster referencing each person in `contacts.md` (`memory-template.md`).

## When a Group Goes Wrong

| Symptom | Cause | Move |
|---|---|---|
| One person dominates every session | No structural time boxing | Round-robin and pairs; private conversation only if structure fails twice |
| Nobody says anything real | Confidentiality is not believed, usually because a sponsor is in the room | Remove the sponsor, restate the boundary, and accept it takes two sessions to recover |
| Members give each other advice constantly | The no-advice rule was never enforced | Re-contract in the moment; model the question form |
| Attendance decays after session two | The value was not visible early enough | Front-load a visible win in session one; individual check-in at week two |
| Two members are in conflict | The group is now their arena | Take it out of the group; group work cannot resolve a two-party conflict |
| The team performs harmony for you | Fear of the leader, or of consequences | Work in the live meeting instead of the offsite; ask what does not get said |
| Anything else | The contract is stale | Re-contract with the whole group out loud, not with individuals |

**After any group or team session**, write the session row to `~/Clawic/data/coach/sessions/<year>.md` with the group as the client; the team or group commitments with their named owners to `clients/<name>.md`; the sponsor-facing summary — themes and attendance only — to the same file so the next report is consistent; and any programme milestone or sponsor checkpoint to `## Due`. A cohort or paid programme also gets its file in the shared `~/Clawic/data/projects/` (`memory-template.md`). Never record who said what in a group.
