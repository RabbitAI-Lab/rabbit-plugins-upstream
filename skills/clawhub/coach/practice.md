# Running the Practice — Pricing, Packages, Capacity, Churn

A coaching practice fails on arithmetic far more often than on craft. The coach who cannot price, cannot say no, and cannot see the capacity ceiling ends up delivering good sessions to too few people for too little, and calls it a marketing problem.

**Before any pricing, capacity, or renewal decision**, read `## Practice` in `~/Clawic/data/coach/memory.md` (or `business.md` once it has split out) and the fee and cadence lines in each `clients/<name>.md`. A rate set without the current caseload and the current renewal dates in front of you is a guess dressed as a decision.

## What You Sell

- **The unit of sale is a container, not an hour.** An hour invites comparison with every other hour on the market and caps income at time. A container — six sessions over three months, biweekly, with a defined between-session channel and an exit condition — is a thing only you are selling (SKILL.md Traps).
- **Name the outcome in the buyer's words and the container in yours.** The buyer decides on the outcome ("stop doing every review yourself") and signs the container. Reversing this produces a price conversation about session length.
- **Everything the container includes is a cost you carry**: prep, notes, async replies, scheduling, the free chemistry call, and the no-shows. Price the container against total time, never against delivery time (see the effective-rate formula below).
- **The free chemistry call is marketing, not a discount** — 20 minutes, about fit (`intake.md`). A free hour of real coaching prices the next one at zero.

## Setting the Rate

Work from your own required revenue, not from what a peer charges. Their cost base, caseload, and buyer are all invisible to you.

```
annual need      = take-home target + annual practice costs + tax provision
billable sessions = weekly delivery slots × working weeks × 0.8
session floor     = annual need ÷ billable sessions
```

- **The 0.8 is not pessimism**: it is holidays, illness, the gap between one engagement ending and the next starting, and the client who pauses. Everyone plans at 1.0 and lands near 0.8.
- **Worked example**: 60,000 take-home + 15,000 costs = 75,000 need. 6 delivery slots a week × 44 weeks × 0.8 = 211 sessions. Floor ≈ 355 per session, so a 6-session package prices from ~2,130 upward. Currency goes in the figure every time it is written down (`memory-template.md`).
- **Annual practice costs are never zero**: supervision, training and CCE hours (`craft.md`), professional indemnity insurance, scheduling and video platforms, accounting, website, and whatever marketing you actually do. Put a number on the list once a year; a rate set without it is priced against a cost base you have never seen.
- **Effective hourly rate** is the only figure that tells the truth: `package fee ÷ (session hours + prep + notes + async + admin + unpaid chemistry calls + no-show slots)`. Compute it once per package type. The premium-looking rate that includes unlimited messaging is routinely the worst line in the practice.
- **Price by container and buyer, not by your seniority.** A corporate engagement carries procurement, reporting, and invoicing overhead that a private client does not; the same six sessions legitimately carry a different number.

## Package Shapes

| Shape | What it is | Who buys it | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed package | 6 sessions / 3 months, biweekly — the default arc (`intake.md`) | Private clients and most sponsored work | None serious; it is the default for a reason |
| Retainer | Access over a period: a session cadence plus a defined async channel | Founders and executives with volatile calendars | Unbounded access; cap the channel and the response time in the agreement |
| Intensive | A half-day or full-day on one decision, with two follow-ups | Buyers with a deadline and no appetite for a cadence | Sold with no follow-up, so nothing transfers |
| Programme or cohort | Fixed curriculum, group sessions, fixed dates (`teams.md`) | L&D budgets | Priced per head against solo delivery cost, which ignores design time |
| Hourly | One-off, priced *above* the per-hour rate inside a package | Returning former clients, second opinions | Becoming the default; then you are selling hours again |
| Anything else | Build it as a container: sessions, period, cadence, access, exit | — | If it has no end condition it is a subscription to you (SKILL.md Rule 9) |

- **One package as the default, one alternative, and that is the menu.** Three or more options moves the buyer from "do I want this" to "which one", and the comparison stalls the decision.
- **The shorter package is the answer to price resistance, never the discounted one** (`intake.md`). A discount tells the buyer the first number was invented.

## Capacity

```
active client ceiling = weekly delivery slots ÷ sessions per client per week
```

- **Worked example**: 6 slots a week, clients on a biweekly cadence (0.5 sessions per client per week) → a ceiling of 12 active clients. Weekly clients consume a full slot, so a weekly caseload halves that number.
- **A delivery slot costs ~1.5× the session length** in real time once prep, notes, and rescheduling are counted, so 6 sixty-minute slots is around 9 hours, not 6. In a solo practice, delivery is roughly a third of the working week and everything else is pipeline, admin, and craft.
- **The ceiling is emotional before it is arithmetical.** Find your real number by marking which session of the day you stopped listening properly; it is the same ordinal every week. That ordinal, not the calendar, is the ceiling (`craft.md`).
- **Never book back-to-back beyond two.** The third consecutive session is where the talk ratio climbs (SKILL.md Rule 2), because listening is the part that costs energy.
- **At the ceiling there are exactly four moves**: raise the rate, run a waitlist with real dates, convert some demand to group work (`teams.md`), or subcontract. Working more hours is not on the list and is the one everybody tries first.
- Write the ceiling and the current active count to `## Practice` the day either changes, so the next enquiry is answered from a number rather than from a feeling.

## The Pipeline

- **Measure your own funnel, never a published benchmark**: enquiries → chemistry calls → clients, counted per quarter. Two ratios matter, and both are diagnostic rather than aspirational.
- **Conversion falling against your own previous quarters** means the call is doing the wrong job — usually solving the problem instead of demonstrating fit (`intake.md`) — or that the enquiries arriving were never buyers, which is a channel fault, not a call fault.
- **Conversion near 100% is a pricing signal, not a skill signal.** If nobody ever declines, the number is below what this buyer would have paid.
- **Referrals convert several times better than any other source and are the least systematized.** Ask in the final session, specifically, while the evidence is fresh (`progress.md`); the 90-day check-in is the second-highest-yield thing a coach does.
- **One visible channel, done consistently, beats four done occasionally.** The channel matters far less than whether the buyer of your niche gathers there (`niches.md`).
- **Speaking, writing, and teaching sell coaching; advertising mostly does not** — the purchase is trust-shaped and the buyer needs evidence of how you think.
- Referral partners and past clients are people: they live in `~/Clawic/data/contacts/contacts.md` with their role, one row each, updated in place (`memory-template.md`).

## Cash and Terms

- **Private clients pay in advance** — the package upfront, or monthly in advance. Invoicing a coaching session in arrears makes cancellation costless and turns you into a creditor of someone you are also coaching.
- **Corporate pays on its own terms**, typically 30-60 days after invoice, and only against a purchase order. Do not deliver session one before the PO exists; without it, the invoice has nowhere to land.
- **Late payment has a stated consequence in the agreement** — sessions pause — and it is applied the first time, quietly and without moralizing (`intake.md`).
- **Every figure carries its currency inside the value** (`2,400 EUR`, `300 EUR/month`), because these lines are read a year later and by other skills (`memory-template.md`).
- Fees the user **charges** live in `clients/<name>.md` and, in aggregate, in `## Practice`. A coaching retainer the user **pays** is a row in the shared `~/Clawic/data/finances/subscriptions.md`.

## No-Shows and Reschedules

- **A missed slot is perishable inventory**: it cannot be resold after the hour passes, which is why the charge is not a penalty but the price of the reservation. Say it that way and the conversation ends.
- **Charge from the first instance or the window does not exist.** A 24-hour window waived once becomes a 24-hour suggestion, and the client who tested it is not the one who suffers — the next four are.
- **Cap reschedules per package** (two is common) and state what happens after: the session is forfeited or the package extends, not both.
- **Chronic rescheduling is coaching material, not admin.** Three moves in an engagement is a pattern about this goal; take it into the session and name it (`stuck.md`), then fix the container — the cadence or the length may simply be wrong (`accountability.md`).
- Corporate no-shows are the sponsor's problem: bill the session and report attendance, which was contracted at intake (`intake.md`).

## Churn, Renewal, Completion

```
renewal rate    = engagements renewed ÷ engagements reaching their renewal date
completion rate = engagements finished as contracted ÷ engagements started
```

- **Distinguish completion from churn.** An engagement that ends because the goal was met is a success and a referral source; one that dissolves mid-package is a failure with an early warning — adherence dropped weeks before the emails stopped (`progress.md`).
- **A high renewal rate is ambiguous, not good.** Renewal on a *new* goal is health; renewal on the same goal for the third time is dependency wearing a revenue disguise (SKILL.md Rule 9).
- **Model the practice on replacement, not retention.** If the standard engagement is three months, the year needs roughly four cohorts of clients — a practice whose pipeline assumes indefinite clients has no pipeline.
- **Exit interviews from dropped engagements are the cheapest research available** and almost nobody does them: one message, one question, no offer attached.
- Renewal, review, and package-end dates go into `## Due` the day they are agreed, because the renewal conversation happens two weeks before the last session or it happens under goodbye pressure (`progress.md`).

## Corporate Buyers

- **Three parties, one room**: the sponsor pays, the line manager has an opinion, the client is coached. Contract with all three at once (`intake.md`).
- **Procurement is a separate sale from the coaching sale.** Expect a supplier form, insurance evidence, a rate card, sometimes a panel or preferred-supplier list, and a data-protection questionnaire. Prepare the pack once and reuse it.
- **Rate cards compress rates and buy volume and predictability.** Accept the trade knowingly, and price the reporting overhead into the card rather than discovering it in month two.
- **What the sponsor receives is attendance, themes, and progress against the agreed objective — never content** (`intake.md`). Agreeing to more in a procurement conversation destroys the engagement you are being hired for.
- **Concentration risk**: one corporate client above ~40% of revenue is an employer with extra steps and no notice period. Track the share in `## Practice`.

## Feast and Famine

The structural failure of every solo practice: pipeline work stops when delivery is full, and the gap arrives one engagement-length later, exactly when the cash does.

- **A fixed weekly pipeline slot, protected like a client session**, regardless of load. It is the only known fix and it fails only when it is treated as optional.
- **The famine is caused by the feast three months earlier** — a delay long enough that most coaches blame the market instead of their own calendar.
- **Waitlists are worth more than rate cuts** during a strong period: they smooth the next trough and they price better than an empty week.
- **Do not fill a trough by discounting.** The discounted client sets the rate for their referrals, and the trough returns with a lower ceiling (`progress.md`).

## Raising the Rate

- **New clients get the new rate immediately; existing clients get it at renewal with one cycle of notice.** Never mid-package — a repriced package is a broken agreement.
- **A rate rise that nobody declines was too small.** Losing the most price-sensitive clients is the mechanism, not a side effect.
- **Raise on evidence, not on confidence**: a waitlist, a full ceiling, a conversion rate near 100%, or a documented outcome record (`progress.md`).
- **The increment that works is one that survives being said out loud without a justification attached.** Explaining a rate invites negotiation of the explanation.

**Whenever a rate, package, cadence, ceiling, or pipeline number changes**, write it to `## Practice` in `~/Clawic/data/coach/memory.md` — rates and package prices with their currency, the active client count and the ceiling with its arithmetic, renewal and completion rates with the period they cover, and the date of the change — and move the whole section to `~/Clawic/data/coach/business.md` with its `## Boxes` line the moment it crosses the split threshold (`memory-template.md`). Per-client fees and terms go in `clients/<name>.md`, never duplicated here; renewal, invoice, and package-end dates go in `## Due` in the same turn.
