# Starting a Client — Chemistry, Intake, Agreement, Baseline

Everything expensive later is decided here: who the client is, what the engagement promises, who else has a claim on it, what stays private, and how it ends. Four steps, in order, and none of them is the first coaching session.

**Before a chemistry call or a first session**, read `~/Clawic/data/contacts/contacts.md` — the person may already be there from another context, and arriving without knowing you have met is an avoidable start.

## The Chemistry Call

20 minutes, free, and it is **not a coaching session**. A free session that is really coaching gives away the product and selects for people who wanted the product free (SKILL.md Traps).

Structure:

1. **What made you reach out now?** The word "now" is the whole question — it surfaces the trigger, and the trigger is the real goal.
2. **What have you already tried?** Tells you what will not work and whether they act between conversations.
3. **What would make this worth it in six months?** The outcome, in their words, before any pricing.
4. **How I work, in ninety seconds.** Cadence, length, commitments between sessions, what coaching is not.
5. **Fit, both directions, said out loud.** Then the logistics.

Give one small piece of value — a reframe, a named pattern — and stop. Enough to demonstrate; not so much that the problem feels solved. Solving it in the chemistry call is the most common reason a good call does not convert.

## Who Says No

Declining badly-fitting clients is a professional skill, and the alternative is a hard engagement, a bad outcome, and no referral.

| Signal in the call | Why it fails | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Wants the answer, not the process | It is consulting or training, and they will be disappointed by design | Name the difference; refer or offer the other service if you provide it |
| Sent by their manager, arrives resentful | Coaching without consent does not work | Contract directly with them, and give them a real veto (`teams.md`) |
| Anything in the Red Flags table | Not a coachable goal | Refer, warmly and specifically (`referral.md`) |
| Wants you to fix another person | The client is not in the room | "What is yours in this?" — coach only if there is an answer |
| Haggles hard before value is established | Price will remain the conversation for the whole engagement | Hold the rate; offer a shorter package, never a discounted one (`practice.md`) |
| Cannot name any time they will do the work | No capacity, not low motivation | Postpone with a date, or decline |
| Already has a coach they have not told | Split loyalty, contradictory structures | Ask them to end or pause the other engagement first |
| You feel a strong urge to rescue them | Your countertransference, not their fit | Take it to supervision before deciding (`craft.md`) |

## Intake

A short form beats a long one: anything over ~10 questions gets answered performatively. Ask for what changes the first session.

- The goal in their words, plus what happens if nothing changes.
- What they have tried and what happened.
- What a good coach does, and what a bad one does, in their experience. This question predicts the relationship better than any other.
- How they want to be challenged, and what tips into unhelpful.
- What else is going on in their life this quarter (capacity, not curiosity — see `stuck.md`).
- Anything that should be off-limits.
- Whether they are in therapy or under medical care. Not to exclude anyone: coaching alongside treatment is normal, coaching instead of it is the harm (`referral.md`).
- Practicalities: timezone, preferred days, notice period, who pays.

Send it before the first session and read it before, not during. Reading in the session tells the client their answers were not worth twenty minutes of your time.

## The Coaching Agreement

Short, plain, and read together rather than emailed as terms. What must be in it:

| Clause | Why it exists |
|---|---|
| Sessions: number, length, cadence, over what period | The container. Open-ended engagements drift into dependency (SKILL.md Rule 9) |
| Fee, currency, schedule, what happens if a payment is late | Ambiguity here becomes the whole relationship |
| Cancellation and no-show: notice window, what is charged | The 24-hour window is the common standard; enforce it from the first instance or it does not exist |
| Rescheduling: how many per package | Without a cap, one client can consume the calendar |
| Between-session access: what channel, what response time, what is out of scope | Async support is real work and is unpaid unless it is written down (`practice.md`) |
| Confidentiality and its limits | Below — the limits are the part that matters |
| What coaching is not | The clearest possible prevention of the therapy/consulting complaint |
| Recording: whether, who holds it, when it is deleted | Consent is not implied by a video platform's record button |
| Ending early: by either side, with what notice and what refund | Both directions. A coach who cannot end an engagement is trapped in it |
| Review point and renewal decision date | Puts the exit conversation on the calendar before it is emotionally loaded (`progress.md`) |

Keep the signed terms — not the document text — in `clients/<name>.md`, and the reusable template in `artifacts/coaching-agreement.md`. It is not legal advice; where a jurisdiction, a corporate procurement process, or an insurer requires specific wording, that wording wins.

## Confidentiality and Its Limits

State the limits in one sentence, at intake, **before anything has been disclosed**:

> "Everything here is private, with three exceptions: risk to your life, risk to someone else, and anything a court or the law requires — and if any of those come up, I will tell you before I act."

- Promising unlimited confidentiality and then breaking it destroys the relationship and, in a corporate context, the practice.
- Limits vary by jurisdiction and by whether the coach holds another regulated role. Verify locally rather than copying a template's list.
- In a sponsored engagement, add the fourth limit: what the sponsor receives (below).
- Supervision is a limit too, and clients rarely know it: material is discussed anonymized with a supervisor. Say so (`craft.md`).

## Three-Way Contracting

When an employer pays, three parties have interests and only one is in the room.

- **One meeting with all three at the start**, not two separate conversations. What the sponsor wants, what the client wants, and where those differ gets said in front of everyone. Differences discovered in month three are unfixable.
- **Write down what the sponsor receives.** The standard boundary: attendance, themes at a level the client has approved, and progress against the agreed objective — never session content. Put the exact sentence in `clients/<name>.md`.
- **The client sees anything the sponsor sees, before the sponsor sees it.** No exceptions; this rule is what makes disclosure possible at all.
- **Coaching sent as remediation** needs the truth on the table: whether this is development or a documented step before dismissal. A client who suspects the second and is told the first stops speaking honestly in session one.
- **The sponsor's goal is not automatically the goal.** If the client's real objective is to leave, the engagement is still legitimate — the contracted objective becomes the one they both agreed, and the private one stays private.
- Sponsor and client both go in `contacts.md` with their roles; the engagement lives in `clients/<name>.md`, and a paid programme also gets a `projects/` file (`memory-template.md`).

## Baseline

Recorded in the first session, never later — a retrospective baseline is a memory, and memory moves toward the story of progress.

- **One or two objective markers** where they exist: hours, count, revenue, applications, percentage of reviews still done personally.
- **Two subjective 0-10 ratings**: confidence on the goal, and satisfaction in the domain. Both re-read on the same scale at each review (`progress.md`).
- **A verbatim sentence** describing where they are today. At the end of the engagement, reading it back does more than any chart.
- Optional instruments — wheel of life, values card sort, strengths or personality inventories, 360 feedback — are useful when they are the *start* of a conversation and inert when they are the product. Save any result the client will re-read to `artifacts/`, never as a numeric summary in `memory.md`.

## Designing the Arc

- **Standard shape: 6 sessions over 3 months, biweekly.** Long enough for behavior to change and be tested, short enough to be a decision rather than a subscription. Weekly for the first month when the behavior is new; monthly is maintenance, not change.
- **Session count is a promise about the exit** (SKILL.md Rule 9), so state what the client will be able to do without you.
- **Front-load the cadence**: sessions 1-2 close together builds the accountability mechanism; spacing widens as the client runs their own reviews (`accountability.md`).
- **Put the review point at the midpoint** and the renewal decision at least two weeks before the last session — a renewal decided in the final session is decided under goodbye pressure.
- Everything with a date goes into `## Due` the day it is agreed.

## Self-Coaching Intake

With `mode: act-as` there is no chemistry call, no agreement, and no sponsor — but three intake moves still pay:

1. **The goal, its baseline, and the value underneath**, written down on day one (`goal-setting.md`).
2. **Cadence and check-in day**, agreed once and recorded in `## Due`. An unscheduled check-in is not a cadence.
3. **The exit condition**: what makes this done, and what happens then. Self-coaching with no end condition becomes a permanently open self-improvement project, which is its own form of avoidance.

**At the end of intake**, create `~/Clawic/data/coach/clients/<name>.md` with the contract terms, the sponsor boundary sentence, the baseline table, and the goal in the client's words; add or update the person's row in `~/Clawic/data/contacts/contacts.md` with the identity key; write the review, renewal and first check-in dates to `## Due`; and add the `## Boxes` line for the client file — all in the same turn (`memory-template.md`). Fees the user charges belong in the client file; a coaching retainer the user *pays* is a row in the shared `finances/subscriptions.md`.
