# Patterns & Techniques — Agorism in the 21st Century, Vol. 3

## Logic-of-violence forecasting (O'Leary)
**When to use**: predicting which side gains power from a technology.
**How**: ask what changes in "the economic conditions under which violence pays" — seizure risk, cost of force, returns to theft. Favor technologies making wealth intangible and force scalable-by-the-small. Identify the blocking variable (today: surveillance) and treat it as the battlefield.
**Trade-offs**: incentive forecasts miss ideology's independent power (Dunkel's correction).

## Octopus-head placement (Jorjani)
**When to use**: designing any exit/secession architecture.
**How**: enclaves are tentacles; the head goes where no jurisdiction reaches (high seas, seabed, deep space). Secure the flag-state (island chains — Palau math), build on the durable stratum (ARPANET-grade), keep production decentralized (3D print → nano).
**Trade-offs**: capital-intensive; legitimizes the state/naval-power frame it opposes.

## Separability audit (Wilson)
**When to use**: any claim of IP over functional designs.
**How**: apply Star Athletica — is the artistic element perceivable apart AND independently protectable? If the only separable element is the license/copyright text, delete it (determinate negation) and the work becomes legally insignificant.
**Trade-offs**: invites litigation; demands case-law literacy (or an attorney).

## License-purpose check (Wilson)
**When to use**: choosing or respecting licenses in contested spaces.
**How**: FOSS licenses hack the IP regime (rights-inverting); CC licenses reproduce it (rights-reserving). Ask which the project intends. Never register files with the state to police the movement.
**Trade-offs**: ideological purity vs pragmatic protection — bankruptcy risk is real.

## Elite-capture detection (Rosenberg)
**When to use**: watching a live innovation (protocol, community, tool).
**How**: track who attributes the benefits. Beams arrive as regulation/licensing/"security" — a containment structure over the living organism. Intervene before sealing.
**Trade-offs**: some assimilation is symbiosis, not capture; distinguish carefully.

## Abstract-vs-concrete test (Rosenberg)
**When to use**: evaluating any political/ideological demand.
**How**: does the abstract (nation, higher purpose, model society) outrank concrete living persons? Abstractions above life license atrocity. Refuse the rank-ordering.
**Trade-offs**: some abstraction (law, contracts) genuinely coordinates; the test is rank, not existence.

## Two-readings split (Lujan)
**When to use**: analyzing "network state" projects.
**How**: is it recognition-seeking (Srinivasan) or permissionless (Crypto Libertaria)? Follow the May rule: many nations, cheap exit, self-selection. Assess exit-cost as the core metric.
**Trade-offs**: recognition may be tactically useful without being the goal.

## 4th-dimension warfare (Dunkel)
**When to use**: prioritizing what to build.
**How**: choose technologies that change the arena's physics, not just the weapons — info tech enabling stateless coordination. Developers' intentionality is the steering; ideology is the lens, not decoration.
**Trade-offs**: dual-use certainty; your tools arm your enemies too.

## Catalyst recruitment (Dunkel)
**When to use**: building movements.
**How**: center people fluent in BOTH tradition and modernity (Haiti, Zapatistas, PKK-confederalism precedent). Assimilated-only rebels beg for seats; tradition-only resisters can't win.
**Trade-offs**: both-worlds fluency is rare and often scarred.

## Fiction stress-test (McCarthy/Dávila)
**When to use**: validating strategy frameworks.
**How**: extrapolate to concrete texture — who runs the conference, what's injected daily, what's NFT-gated. If the details cohere, the framework survived; if they go soft, the framework has gaps.
**Trade-offs**: vivid ≠ true; keep the genre honest.
