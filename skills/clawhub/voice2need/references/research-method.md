# Research method

## 1. Define a decision, not just a search term

Describe the people, their real task, the stage of the task, and the decision to make. For example: “Which revision handoffs cause trouble for podcast editors, and should we test a version checker?” A field name alone is a starting point, not a customer segment.

Record the agreed window, source communities, query families, imported files, exclusions, and request/cost limits. Choose the window and reading effort for this question; neither thirty days nor a fixed percentage of a corpus is universal.

Distinguish:

- **Discovery:** find relevant discussions, direct competitors, alternatives and community vocabulary.
- **Deep collection:** recover the selected posts, replies, parent context and full available text.
- **Analysis:** read meaning and connect evidence to a claim.

Keep the queries and selections visible. Successful requests do not establish complete comments, useful transcripts, or a good sample. A failed or capped source has unknown coverage, not zero demand.

## 2. Make the discussion readable

Retain native IDs, authors, dates, URLs, conversation and parent IDs, and raw-file pointers. Deduplicate repeated retrieval of the same native record; preserve its occurrences. Similar complaints from different people remain different evidence. Matching names across platforms do not establish one identity.

Group by conversation. Read the parent of short replies such as “same here.” Keep historical or outside-window parents as labeled context. Do not use them as current-period behavior without saying so. If a parent is unavailable, describe precisely what can and cannot be interpreted.

Structural processing, packaging, keyword matching, actual reading and review are distinct states. Never claim that every collected or packaged record was read. Track the IDs actually read, including parent context.

Long discussions may span several packets. Read adjacent packets when a reply depends on later corrections, intervening speakers, or project details. A packet's complete text is not proof of complete collection for the conversation.

## 3. Read for tasks, wishes and consequences

Extract only what the text supports:

| Field | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Person and project | Role, customer/client relationship, scale, stage; otherwise unknown |
| Task | What they are trying to achieve and what triggered it |
| Problem and consequence | Failure, rework, time, money, missed delivery, quality loss |
| Workaround | What they actually tried; outcome and whether they kept using it |
| Wish and question | Explicit desired capability or request for help, even without payment |
| Alternatives | Competitor use, abandonment, praise, sufficient existing solutions |
| Money | Stated willingness, reported transaction, refund, cancellation; payer separate from operator |
| Counterexample | When the problem is small, the solution is enough, or the proposed feature fails |

“You could use a shared sheet” is advice. “I created one last week” is reported behavior. “I stopped updating it” is a later outcome that may overturn the recommendation. “Thanks” alone proves neither adoption nor success. A finished project does not prove tool payment. An employer-paid subscription does not establish the employee as the buyer.

Classify tutorial narration, promotion, secondhand reporting and actual customer experience separately. A creator's video is not dozens of independent users because it has many transcript segments or views. A tool-generated message quoting a user should lead back to that user where possible; do not automatically discard or accept it by source type.

## 4. Allocate attention and record limits

Prioritize material that can change the decision: rich task accounts, failures, workarounds, clear wishes, actual projects, direct competitors, strong praise and opposing experiences. Search keywords can identify leads, but they do not accept evidence or settle meaning.

Audit the sample by platform/community, date, discussion type and available role clues. Large short-message communities must not automatically dominate long task accounts from smaller sources. Conversely, do not call an unread platform quiet. Inspect ordinary/nonmatching content for missed vocabulary and needs.

For large corpora, choose a reproducible reading sample before reading: IDs, strata, seed if randomized, denominator, selection reason and stopping rule. Expand the affected stratum when it reveals a blind spot. Keep the original sample and denominator. Do not extrapolate targeted reading into market prevalence.

When enough reviewed evidence answers a question, synthesize and state decision-relevant gaps. Do not postpone every answer until the entire corpus is read. A saturation statement applies only to the described batches and strata, and requires repeated independent observations; it is not “nothing new in two messages.”

## 5. Answer fourteen questions

The software provides Q01–Q14. Customize their wording for the field while preserving coverage:

1. What people discuss.
2. Tools used, compared and abandoned.
3. Specific complaints.
4. Recurring problems and actual consequences.
5. Repeated questions.
6. Explicit wishes.
7. People, projects, budgets and stages.
8. Workarounds and costs.
9. Interest, willingness to pay, actual payment, operator and payer.
10. Sufficient alternatives, mild problems, nonpayment and poor fit.
11. Open, self-hosted or customizable alternatives.
12. Self-built tools and what happened to them.
13. Criticism of competitors and substitutes.
14. Praise for competitors and substitutes.

Each answer needs linked findings, the relevant population/task boundary, counterevidence, and an honest gap. Unknown is a useful answer when the source cannot settle it.

## 6. Review decisions and preserve useful disagreement

Review the exact record and its context, not merely whether a URL opens. Prioritize consequential claims, representative cases, numbers, payment, changes of tool, and counterexamples. Compare independent reviewers' evidence rather than their agent names. A reviewer can also make errors; corrections need the same scrutiny as original claims.

Keep a key-finding destination table. Every important accepted case must appear in a conclusion or have an explicit reason for deferral. Recurring omission of a platform or task can invalidate a synthesis even if each remaining quote is correct.

Finish with a concrete next test: who to interview or observe, which task/prototype to compare, what would change the decision. Scores are optional decision aids with human-assigned dimensions and reasons. Missing dimensions keep the total unknown. They are not market probabilities and must not erase adverse evidence.
