# Feline Inappropriate Elimination — Rules, Causes, Protocols

## 1. Why this problem matters
House-soiling is among the most common behavioral reasons for cat surrender to shelters and, in crowded shelters, euthanasia. Behavioral literature and shelter intake surveys consistently rank litter-box problems at or near the top of owner-surrender causes — ahead of aggression in many cohorts. The brutal part: the large majority of cases are resolvable with (a) a veterinary exam, (b) box-management fixes, (c) soiling-trigger analysis. The ladder's order is the treatment.

## 2. Medical differentials (rule out FIRST)
| Condition | Signature | Why it breaks box habits |
|---|---|---|
| FIC (feline idiopathic cystitis) / FLUTD | Frequent small attempts, straining, blood-tinged urine, vocalizing in box; stress-linked | Pain is associated with the box itself; cat seeks "new" surfaces |
| Urethral obstruction (MALE) | Straining with NO urine, crying, hiding, vomiting, licking genitals | **Emergency — 24–72 h to death. ER immediately.** |
| Uroliths/crystals | Straining, hematuria, recurrent UTI signs | Same pain-association mechanism |
| CKD / diabetes / hyperthyroid | PU/PD: huge clumps, dilute urine, weight loss, appetite change | Volume exceeds box tolerance; cat prefers absorbent alternatives |
| Constipation/megacolon | Dry stools, straining to defecate, vomiting | Defecation pain → box avoidance |
| Arthritis (seniors) | Hesitates at high entry, stops covering, defecates just outside | Box walls are a pain wall |
| GI/dietary | Diarrhea urgency | Can't reach the box in time |
Minimum database for a sudden-change case: urinalysis (+ culture if indicated), age-appropriate bloods (CBC/chem/T4 for seniors), pain/arthritis assessment. "He's fine, it's behavioral" without a UA is a guess.

## 3. Box-management doctrine (evidence-based conventions)
- **Count**: the cats+1 rule (Ohio State Indoor Pet Initiative, AAFP housing guidelines) — resources should not be guardable bottlenecks. One box per social group minimum, distributed across zones, not lined up in one room (a row of boxes = one territory gate, not five resources).
- **Scoop daily at minimum.** Studies of cat preference show strong aversion to used boxes after a few deposits; 2×/day during resolution. Full dump+wash 1–4 weeks with mild unscented soap; never pine/citrus disinfectants or ammonia.
- **Litter preference research** (Borchelt, Neilson, AAFP summaries): fine-grain unscented clumping clay is accepted by the largest share of cats; scented litters, crystals, and pellets are recurrent refusal triggers. Attraction additives exist (Cat Attract-class herbal blends) for refusers.
- **Size**: 1.5× nose-to-tail-base length; many retail "large" boxes fail this for average cats. Storage-bin conversions and plywood-lined boxes are legitimate fixes.
- **Hoods**: privacy for some cats, but trap odor, block sightlines, and turn the box into an ambush point in multi-cat homes. Offer one open + one hooded and let the cat vote with its feet.
- **Location**: quiet, predictable, night-lit, away from appliances (washer spin = terror), away from food/water, with clear escape routes. "Dark scary basement" and "noisy utility room" are the two classic failures.
- **Depth**: 2–3 inches clumping. Liners and covered automatic boxes each have their own refusal cohorts; during a crisis, return to boring: open box, plain clumping, quiet corner.

## 4. Spraying vs toileting (the diagnostic fork)
| Feature | Spraying (marking) | Toileting (latrine behavior) |
|---|---|---|
| Posture | Standing, backing to surface, tail quiver | Squatting, digging, covering |
| Surface | Vertical (walls, furniture edges, curtains) | Horizontal (floors, beds, piles) |
| Volume | Small, aromatic | Full bladder/bowel volumes |
| Location logic | Territory boundaries: doors, windows, new objects | Latrine logic: quiet soft surfaces, far from box, or right beside a rejected box |
| Meaning | Anxiety/territory communication | Box rejection or medical urgency |
| Treatment | Trigger reduction (outdoor-cat sightlines, inter-cat tension), pheromones, clean with enzymatic neutralizer, severe cases vet-discussed anxiolytics | Fix the box/medical problem; the behavior collapses |
The fork decides the plan: spraying fixes are environmental-psychological; toileting fixes are box-medical. Both need enzymatic decontamination of old sites.

## 5. The 7-day litter transition protocol
Day 1–2: 75% old / 25% new, PLUS one fallback box of pure old litter elsewhere. Day 3–4: 50/50. Day 5–6: 25/75. Day 7: 100% new; keep the fallback box another week. Any refusal/refusal-sign (circling, jumping out fast, half-in posture) → roll back one stage. Never transition litter *and* move boxes *and* introduce a pet simultaneously — one variable at a time, 5–7 days apart.

## 6. Decontamination chemistry
- Urine = water + urea + uric acid + salts + pheromone-marking compounds. Bacteria decompose urea → **ammonia**. Uric acid crystallizes and is insoluble in water and resistant to bleach, detergents, and UV; it re-activates with humidity for years. Cats can smell it long after you can't (olfactory receptors ~200M vs human ~5M).
- **Enzymatic cleaners** (bacteria-produced enzymes: urease, uricase, proteases, lipases) digest the uric acid and pheromone molecules — the only household class that removes the signal, not masks it. Saturate to the urine's depth (carpet pad included), keep wet per label (often 10–15 min covered), air-dry, repeat.
- **Never**: ammonia-based products (advertises "cat toilet here"), bleach (doesn't touch uric acid; toxic mixed with urine), vinegar-only (masks briefly), steam before enzymatic treatment (heat can bond proteins into fibers — enzymatic first, always).
- Blacklight (365 nm) fluoresces urine salts; scan in darkness for the full site map — the visible stain is usually the smaller sibling of the real deposit.
- Mattresses/padding: inject enzymatic cleaner into the deposit depth with a syringe; treat both faces.

## 7. Follow-up ladder (case not resolving)
1. Week 0: vet exam + UA; management fixes all at once (count/scoop/location/litter).
2. Week 2 no improvement: re-check medicals (occult constipation, early CKD); add attraction strategies (different litter in a second box, box at preferred site then migrate 30 cm/day).
3. Week 4: inter-cat tension assessment (resource blocking, staring contests); pheromone diffusers; environment enrichment (play therapy reduces anxiety-marking).
4. Week 6+: behaviorist referral + vet discussion of anxiolytic support (fluoxetine-class for refractory spraying has evidence); re-houseotyping decisions only after this rung.
