# Symptom Router — Reference

## The core discriminator: SOIL MOISTURE at symptom time

Most houseplant symptoms are non-specific (yellowing, wilting, brown tips)
— but combined with the wetness of the substrate they split cleanly into
two opposite management tracks. Watering a wilting-overwatered plant "to be
safe" is the single most common fatal move.

## Master table

| Observation | Soil wet | Soil dry |
|---|---|---|
| Wilting | Root rot: roots dead, can't drink. STOP water, unpot, trim roots, repot. | Straight underwatering (or hydrophobic mix). Soak. |
| Lower leaves yellowing | Classic overwatering signature | Underwatering OR normal oldest-leaf aging |
| Overall yellowing | Chronic saturation + nutrient lockout | Deficiency (feeding washed out) or drought stress |
| Brown crispy tips | Root stress OR salt/fluoride buildup — flush 2–3× pot volume | Chronic low water or low humidity (high VPD) |
| Black mushy stems | Root/stem rot advanced — take cuttings NOW, topsoil plant is lost | — |
| New growth small/pale | Roots suffocating, can't feed | Feeding gap during growth season |
| Fungus gnats | Larvae in wet topsoil — see protocol | (they vanish) |
| White crust on substrate | Salts from hard water/overfertilizing — flush; switch to filtered | — |
| Leaf drop (sudden) | Shock from saturated+cold root zone | Drought shock |
| Leaf drop (gradual) | Slow rot or light starvation (check light first) | Slow chronic drought |

## Fungus gnat protocol (lifecycle break)

1. **Dry-out discipline:** let the top 3–4 cm dry between waterings. Eggs
   and larvae need continuously damp topsoil; this alone breaks the cycle.
2. **Bottom-water 3 weeks:** fill a basin, sit the pot in it 20–30 min.
   Topsoil stays dry, deep substrate waters. Adults can't lay.
3. **Yellow sticky traps:** catch adults, monitor progress (count drops
   weekly).
4. **BTI** (Bacillus thuringiensis israelensis): granules or bits in the
   watering can — larvicide, harmless otherwise. For heavy infestations.
5. **Grit top-dress (1 cm sand/fine gravel):** physical egg-laying barrier.
Timeline: lifecycle 3–4 weeks → adults gone in 2 generations IF topsoil
discipline holds. Gnats are a symptom of the watering regime, not bad luck.

## Root rot rescue, staged

1. **Suspect** (wet > 10–14 days + wilting/yellowing): stop watering,
   move to brighter/warmer spot, wait for dry-down to normal check rhythm.
2. **Confirmed** (swamp smell, black mushy roots): unpot entirely, hose
   roots clean, trim all black/mushy with sterile scissors, dust cuts with
   cinnamon or skip, repot in FRESH airy mix (never the old wet mix), water
   lightly once, then normal discipline.
3. **Advanced** (stems rotting): take healthy cuttings, propagate, let the
   parent go. Rooted cuttings outrun a rescue.

## Repotting rules that change the watering math

- Pot up ≤ 5 cm diameter at a time: a too-big pot = wet soil volume the
  root system can't occupy or dry → rot nursery.
- Fresh mix changes WHC: re-run compute after every repot.
- After repotting, water once thoroughly, then HOLD — damaged roots need
  oxygen first, water second. Expect a 1–2 week slower cycle.
- Root-bound plants dry 1.5–2× faster (roots displace substrate, foliage
  keeps transpiring) — that's your repot signal, not calendar.

## Salt buildup (the silent one)

Every watering that doesn't flush moves dissolved salts upward into the
root zone. Symptoms: brown tips + white crust + poor growth despite
"feeding properly." Fix: monthly flush with 2–3× pot volume of water
(do it in a sink), let drain fully. Filtered/rain water if your tap is
hard. Fertilize at half-strength twice as often beats full-strength.

## Quarantine discipline for new plants

New plant = 2 weeks isolated + a compute() run + a pest inspection
(undersides, stem joints, soil surface). Most infestations and gnats arrive
on new plants; most deaths arrive on new watering schedules applied to
plants that didn't need them.
