What does a brand identity cost?

A full identity system runs from $12,000. Packaging from $8,000, a campaign from $6,000. Every job is a fixed price agreed before a single marker comes out.

How long will it take?

Six to eight weeks for an identity, three to four for packaging. I take on two projects at a time so those dates hold.

Do you work with brand-new businesses?

Half of my clients have not launched yet. Bring a product and a point of view; the name and the logo are things we can make together.

What will I receive at the end?

Every file in every format, a short brand book a printer can follow, and the original marker drawings, framed if you want them.

Can we work remotely?

Yes. Kickoff and reviews happen on a call with the drawings held up to the camera. Rivergate clients get the studio and the good coffee.

How to build a brand personality people actually remember

May 12, 2026 · 5 minute read

A useful brand personality guides choices. A list of broad adjectives rarely does.

Start with a person, not a mood board

Describe how the brand would behave: what it says, what it notices and how it responds when something goes wrong.

A personality you cannot act out is a personality you cannot design.

Pick one exaggeration

Choose one characteristic to emphasise. A tea collection might express a slower evening through warm colour, names and illustration.

Write the voice before the logo

Test the voice in a pack description, an email and a sold-out sign. They should sound like the same person.

Make it survivable

Carry that character into ordinary details, from delivery labels to instructions. Recognition grows through those repeated choices.

Identity

Good design is more than a pretty logo

Apr 28, 2026 · 4 minute read

A logo is one part of a brand’s visual language.

What people actually recognise

Cover the mark on a familiar pack. Colour, type, illustration and voice should still help you recognise it.

Nobody has ever fallen in love with a brand because of its kerning.

The five things that do the work

  • Colour. Two colours you own on your shelf, used with discipline.
  • Type. One display voice and one quiet voice, and the rule for when each speaks.
  • Illustration or photography style. The thing that makes a picture yours before anyone reads a word.
  • Voice. The sentence on the back, the sign on the door, the reply to a complaint.
  • Behaviour. What the brand does when nobody is looking: the invoice, the return label, the delay email.

So what is the logo for?

Design those elements together, then test the system on the places people will actually see it.

Three ways brand consistency builds serious trust

Apr 10, 2026 · 4 minute read

Consistency helps people recognise the same care in different places.

1. Repetition is recognition

Repeat a few distinctive elements across the sign, the pack and the product. Each encounter reinforces the previous one.

2. Consistency signals care

Match the voice and detail of practical materials to the main design. Instructions and labels are part of the experience too.

Small brands cannot outspend anyone. They can out-consistent everyone.

3. Rules make room for surprises

A clear system also creates room for play. One unexpected shape stands out when the surrounding rules are familiar.

The practical version

Write down the few things that stay fixed: core colours, drawing style, type and voice. Let the applications adapt around them.

Questions people ask

How long does a project take?
Strategy alone is about three weeks. An identity is around seven, packaging seven per SKU system, and a combined brand-plus-pack project usually lands at eleven to fourteen weeks. I book two projects at a time so those numbers hold.
Do I get all the files?
Yes. Editable source files, print packages, screen exports and the illustration library, all named sensibly and handed over with a short call so your team knows what is what.
Can you work with our printer?
Happily, and I would rather talk to them early than late. If you do not have one yet I will introduce you to a few I trust and stand next to the press on the day.
What if we do not like the direction?
Because you see marker sketches early, wrong turns are caught in days rather than weeks. Two rounds of refinement are built in, and if we are genuinely off, we go back to the roadmap together and re-aim before any more drawing.
Do you take on brands outside food and drink?
Yes: kids' goods, outdoor, small hospitality and anything else sold in a row of near-identical boxes. If the project needs to be seen first and remembered second, it is a fit.
