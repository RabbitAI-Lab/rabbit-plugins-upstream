# Parent-Teacher Conference Prep — Reference

## Why conferences underdeliver
The median US conference is 15-20 minutes, with 20-30 families per
teacher per night in elementary, and per-subject 5-10 minute slots in
secondary. Under that pressure, teachers default to summary language
("doing fine", "a pleasure to have") unless a specific question forces
specifics. The information asymmetry is structural: the teacher sees
30 hours/week of your child in a peer context; you see evenings. The
conference is the only scheduled transfer point — and most parents
arrive unstructured.

## The dossier discipline
Evidence you bring beats memory both ways:
- Portal grades + attendance (print or screenshot — the teacher may
  not have them at hand).
- Last two work samples per problem subject: shows WHERE errors happen
  (careless vs conceptual), which changes the question entirely.
- Your home observations, dated: "homework takes 2h and ends in tears
  since September" is a different conversation than "writing is hard".
- What the child says about school, quoted: teachers can triangulate
  ("she says recess is the worst part" → social lane question).

## Question bank design notes
- Every question is phrased to force an EPISODE, not an evaluation.
  "How is she doing?" → adjective. "What does she do when she's stuck?"
  → a story, and stories contain the real information.
- The four lanes map to the four things report cards don't carry:
  academic mechanics, social position, behavior patterns, and the
  home-school contract.
- The time math: ~2.5 minutes per question including answer. A 15-min
  slot realistically fits 5-6 questions plus teacher opening remarks.
- The skip-list protects the slot from low-yield spends.

## Stage tuning rationale
- **K-2:** early literacy/numeracy is skill-mechanics (phonemic
  awareness, number sense) — measurable and intervention-sensitive.
  Social basics (sharing, transitions) predict classroom life.
- **3-5:** the "fourth grade slump" — comprehension demand jumps while
  decoding kids can mask gaps. Work habits calcify here.
- **6-8:** organization systems (planner, binder, late-work) become
  the grade determinant; identity ("not a math person") forms.
- **9-12:** it's credits and trajectories; per-teacher "what does your
  best student do" yields concrete strategy, and parents' job shifts
  from help to logistics + advocacy.

## Jargon decoder — the principle
Teacher comment language is professionally constrained: it must be
true, kind, and non-diagnostic. That forces euphemism density. The
decoder lists the plausible referents for common phrases and the
DISAMBIGUATING question for each — because "needs to stay focused" has
four different fixes, and only the teacher's episode description tells
you which.

Universal fallback: "Help me picture that — what does it look like in
class?" Works on any phrase not in the table.

## The follow-up email
Why it matters: teachers juggle 100+ families; verbal agreements
genuinely evaporate. A 5-line email that (1) thanks, (2) lists
agreements as bullets, (3) invites correction, converts a chat into a
shared record. Teachers overwhelmingly welcome it — it also protects
YOU: if support was promised ("we'll move his seat", "I'll send the
missing-work list"), the email is the artifact.

## Conference log across years
Keep one file per child: date, teacher, phrases used (verbatim — decode
later), agreements, what worked. Patterns across years are the highest
-value signal ("every teacher since grade 2 says 'rushes'" is data no
single conference yields).

## Special situations
- **Split/multi-teacher formats:** run `questions` per subject teacher;
  prioritize the subject where the grade and your gut disagree.
- **IEP/504 or equivalent meetings:** this skill is the PREP layer
  (dossier + log); the legal-procedural layer needs
  jurisdiction-specific advocacy resources.
- **Virtual conferences:** same structure; notes discipline matters
  more (no physical handoff of work samples — send photos in the
  follow-up email).
- **Bad news conferences:** the plan command still applies — convert
  to agreements; if none are offered, that itself is the finding:
  "what would you do if this were your child?" is a fair closer.

## Cultural note
Conference norms vary by country/system (some systems have no
conferences, some have monthly books). The lanes and dossier transfer;
formats adapt.
