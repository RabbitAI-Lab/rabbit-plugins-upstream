# Planning framework

All numbers are starting points for a first plan, not benchmarks. Replace them with the user's own data.

## 1. Capacity and cadence

Ask: who creates (strategy, writing, design, video, community), hours per week for each, tools, approval steps, and holidays or travel ahead.

Rough effort per piece for a small team (adjust to the user's reality): text post 20 to 40 minutes; carousel 1 to 3 hours; short video 2 to 5 hours with script, shoot and edit; long video many hours; community replies 15 to 30 minutes a day.

Method: total hours per week divided by effort per piece gives the number of pieces; keep 20% slack for replies, fixes and reporting. Offer two versions:

| Version | Use when |
|---|---|
| Minimum viable | solo founder or small team: 1 to 2 platforms, 3 to 5 posts per week in total |
| Stretch | dedicated creator or team: more platforms or higher frequency, only after 4 weeks of hitting the minimum |

Prefer one platform done consistently over four done poorly. Say so when the requested cadence is beyond capacity.

## 2. Pillars and mix

Use the strategy's pillars. A common starting split is education 40 to 50, proof or stories 20 to 30, community 10 to 20, offers 10 to 20; change it by goal (sales-led accounts carry more proof and offers, but constant selling tires audiences). The check command warns when a pillar is more than 10 points off its target.

## 3. Days and times

- Choose days the team can produce for, not days that look good in an article.
- Use the audience's time zone, not the team's. For audiences across Pakistan, the Gulf, India and the USA, either pick the main market or alternate slots; note that evening hours differ by country and by season.
- Treat times as experiments: pick 2 or 3 slots, keep each for 4 to 6 weeks of posts, then compare with analytics. A few posts per slot is not enough to conclude.
- Avoid fixed claims such as "best time is 7 pm"; platforms and audiences differ and change.
- Ramadan, Eid, school exams, weekends and public holidays change online habits in many audiences; check the dates for the year from a current source and adjust cadence and tone (for example lighter, kinder, evening-focused content) only when the audience data or the user's experience supports it.

## 4. Campaign and launch timeline

Phases (shorten for small launches): research and prepare, tease, announce, proof and education, urgency (only if real), last call, follow-up and thanks, wrap-up report. Place pieces by phase, not equally. Typical weights: more education and proof before the offer, offers concentrated around the announce and last-call days, community content throughout. Include owned channels (email, website, stories) and paid or collaboration slots.

## 5. Production workflow

Statuses used by the tool: idea, draft, review, approved, scheduled, posted, skipped.

Work backwards from the post date (offsets are starting points):

| Step | Offset before posting |
|---|---|
| Brief | 10 to 14 days |
| Draft copy or script | 7 to 10 days |
| Visual assets or video | 4 to 7 days |
| Review and approval | 2 to 3 days |
| Scheduled / ready | 1 to 2 days |

Batch similar work: write a week of captions in one session, shoot several videos in one day, build carousels from one template, then schedule in one pass. Keep a buffer of 3 to 5 approved posts.

## 6. Approval and ownership

Name an owner per item and one approver; regulated topics need a reviewer with the right expertise. Record approval in `status` and notes. Never schedule an unapproved post.

## 7. Risk and sensitivity

Check current news before launches. Pause scheduled posts during tragedies, disasters and national mourning. Avoid promotions on solemn dates. Keep a short "pause protocol": who can stop the queue and how.

## 8. Review cadence

- Weekly (15 minutes): did we post as planned, what slipped, what needs a shift, what is the buffer.
- Monthly: results vs plan (reach, engagement quality, clicks, leads), what to repeat, stop or test, next month's pillars and dates.
- Quarterly: revisit goals, pillars, platforms and cadence; retire what does not pay off.

Use `analytics` for the numbers; change one variable at a time so you know what caused the difference.
