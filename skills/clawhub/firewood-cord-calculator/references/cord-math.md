# Firewood Measurement, Energy & Seasoning Reference

## The measurement shell game

| Unit | What it is | True cords |
|------|-----------|------------|
| **Cord** | 128 ft³ stacked, classically 4'×4'×8' | 1.00 |
| **Face cord / rick** | one 8'×4' wall at one log length | 0.33 @ 16" depth |
| **Loose / thrown cord** | same volume, unstacked | ~0.65–0.70 |
| **Pickup "cord"** | full 8' bed, thrown | ~0.5 (½-ton), ~0.65 (¾-ton, sides up) |
| **Truckload** | undefined — measure, don't convert | ? |

Legal note: most US states define the cord as the only legal firewood unit
(some allow face cord IF the log length is stated). Sellers who dodge the
word "cord" are dodging the standard.

Packing factors (fraction of stack volume that is wood, not air):
tight machine-stacked 0.95–1.00 · typical hand-stack 0.90 · thrown/loose
0.65–0.70 · crossed/interlaced (worst) ~0.60.

Softwood doctrine: fine for fast hot fires and kindling; risky as
overnight damped fuel in a cool chimney (creosote). Chimney sweep cadence:
any significant softwood burning → annual inspection.

## Moisture physics

Every lb of water in the load absorbs ~1,050 BTU evaporating before any
heat reaches the room:

| MC (wet basis) | Available heat | Behavior |
|----------------|----------------|----------|
| ≤ 20% | 100% (reference) | clean burn, visible flame heat |
| 25% | ~92% | workable, more creosote |
| 35% | ~78% | steaming, glass blackens |
| 50% (green) | ~55% | hissing, smoky, chimney danger |

Measuring honestly: split a piece, press the meter pins into the FRESH
split face (not weathered surface), average 3 pieces. Weathered outside
surfaces read artificially low.

## Delivered heat and the efficiency factor

Wood BTUs ≠ room BTUs:

- Modern EPA non-catalytic stove: 63–78%
- Catalytic stove: 75–83%
- Older non-EPA airtight: 50–60%
- Open fireplace: −10% (it draws heated house air up the flue)

Delivered MMBtu/cord = table MMBtu × moisture factor × efficiency.
Red oak at 22% MC in a 70% stove: 24 × 0.97 × 0.70 ≈ 16.3 MMBtu.

## Fuel cross-pricing (per delivered MMBtu)

- Propane: $/gal ÷ 0.0916 MMBtu/gal ÷ furnace eff (~0.90)
- Natural gas: $/therm ÷ 0.1 ÷ eff (~0.95)
- Resistance electric: $/kWh ÷ 0.003412 (eff = 1.0)
- Heat pump: $/kWh ÷ 0.003412 ÷ COP (seasonal 2.5–3.5)
- Wood: $/cord ÷ delivered MMBtu/cord

Rule of thumb: at $3/gal propane, wood is cheaper than propane up to about
$400/cord delivered-stacked oak; at $1.30/therm gas or a COP-3 heat pump,
gas/HP usually beat bought wood, and wood wins on independence and
outage-resilience instead.

## Seasoning mechanics

Water leaves wood through end grain and faces by diffusion + evaporation.
The four levers:

1. **Split size** — thin splits (≤4") dry months faster than rounds; big
   rounds of oak can sit 2+ years.
2. **Airflow** — single rows, spaced, ends facing prevailing wind. Stacked
   against a closed wall or double rows = +months.
3. **Sun + top cover** — sun drives evaporation; a top-only cover (sides
   open) prevents re-wetting without blocking flow. Full tarps wrapped
   around = humidity tent (worse than nothing).
4. **Off the ground** — pallets or rails; ground contact wicks moisture
   back in and rots the bottom course.

Back-planning: date you want it ready minus species months (± split-size
adjustment) = split-by date. White oak for November 1: split by ~May the
year before (18 months).

## Annual consumption sanity check

House demand ≈ ft² × HDD65 × ~10 BTU (adjust with real fuel-bill data),
× wood-share fraction, ÷ delivered MMBtu/cord → cords. Multiply by 1.4
for the real first year (fires get lit for pleasure). Typical well-built
2000 ft² house in a 5000-HDD climate, wood covering 60% of heat: ~3.6
theoretical → ~5 cords actual.

Storage sizing: 1 cord single-row 4' tall ≈ 32 linear feet of wall; 5
cords ≈ 160 ft of row, or an 8×10 covered rack area stacked 6' high.
