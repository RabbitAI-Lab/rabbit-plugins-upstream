# Wear Life, Hygiene, and the Open-Date Clock

## Why the open date is the only date that matters

A contact lens carton carries two dates people confuse:

1. **Sterile shelf life (the printed expiry).** How long an *unopened*
   blister stays sterile in the packaging. Typically 3–5 years from
   manufacture. It says nothing about how long you may *wear* the lens.
2. **Wear life (the replacement schedule).** How long an *opened* lens
   may be worn, by class: daily (1 day), biweekly (14 days), monthly
   (30 days), quarterly (rare, 90 days), conventional/yearly (365 days).

The moment the foil is peeled, oxygen, finger flora, and lens-solution
chemistry start degrading the material: lipid and protein deposits
build, surface wetting angle worsens, micro-tears form that shelter
bacteria. Deposit load correlates with days since opening much more
strongly than with hours worn.

## What the surveys find

CDC-style behavioral surveys of lens wearers consistently report:

- Roughly **40–60% admit stretching lenses past the schedule** —
  "a few more days won't hurt" is the modal failure.
- Sleeping in lenses and overdue replacement are the two modifiable
  behaviors most associated with **microbial keratitis** — a
  sight-threatening corneal infection whose incidence is low (~2–4 per
  10,000 wearers/year for daily wear, far higher for overnight wear)
  but whose consequences (corneal scarring, graft) are catastrophic.
- Extended-wear and overnight use multiply risk 5–10×.

## Calendar-day vs day-of-use counting

Most FDA-cleared disposable schedules are **calendar-day** counts from
opening: a "2-week" lens opened Monday is due 13 days later whether you
wore it twice or fourteen times. A minority of brands market
day-of-use counting. The safe default (and this skill's assumption) is
calendar-day. If your brand's insert explicitly permits day-of-use
counting, treat the printed class as an upper bound anyway — deposit
chemistry doesn't read marketing.

## Cost reality check (approximate US street prices)

| Modality | $/lens | Life | $/day (both eyes) | $/year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Daily | $0.50–1.00 | 1 d | $1.00–2.00 | $365–730 |
| Biweekly | $1.20–2.00 | 14 d | $0.17–0.29 | $63–105 |
| Monthly | $2.50–4.50 | 30 d | $0.17–0.30 | $61–110 |
| Yearly (conventional) | $15–30 | 365 d | $0.08–0.16 | $30–60 |

Add for reusable lenses: multipurpose solution (~$0.15–0.30/day) and a
case every 1–3 months. Dailies need none. Annualized, monthlies are
cheap but the gap narrows; the real argument for dailies is hygiene
(never stored, never re-worn) — relevant for occasional wearers and
allergy sufferers.

## Occasional-wearer inversion

If you wear lenses 2–3 times a week, dailies frequently win on *both*
cost and safety: a monthly opened for 8 wears still expires in 30 days,
so you throw away ~60% of its life, while dailies are consumed exactly
per use. Rule of thumb below ~3 wear-days/week, price it out with the
`cost` subcommand before assuming monthlies are cheaper.

## Red flags — bypass this tool entirely

Any of these = stop wearing lenses and call an eye care professional
same-day (urgent if pain/photophobia/vision change):

- Red eye with pain, or pain on lens removal
- Light sensitivity (photophobia)
- Decreased or blurry vision that persists after removal
- Discharge, tearing, or the sensation of something in the eye
- A white spot on the cornea (visible in mirror, often with fogging)

Remove lenses at once; do NOT re-insert a lens from an eye with any of
the above; bring the lens + case + solution bottles to the appointment
(culture material). If you ran out of solution, never rinse with tap
water — it can carry *Acanthamoeba*, which is devastating and is
strongly associated with water exposure of lenses.

## Case hygiene (the untracked half of the calendar)

- Replace the case every 1–3 months — the case is a biofilm reservoir
  that re-inoculates every "clean" lens.
- Rub-and-rinse even with "no-rub" solutions (15 s each side) unless
  the IFU explicitly forbids.
- Air-dry the case between uses, cap off, on a clean surface — never
  sealed wet.
- Never top off solution; discard old solution and refill fresh.
- Keep solution bottles capped; do not touch the nozzle.
