# Rental Claims Playbook — How the Other Side Works

## Damage Recovery Economics

Post-return damage claims are often processed not by the rental company
but by a damage-recovery unit whose economics reward volume: the claim
chain typically stacks:

1. Repair cost (often list-price body-shop rates, sometimes inflated
   vs market)
2. "Loss of use" (days the car couldn't rent, at full daily rate)
3. "Diminished value" (the car is worth less post-repair)
4. "Admin fee" (processing the claim against you)

A $150 scuff becomes a $700 invoice. Each layer is disputable — but
only with evidence. This is why their process starts photographing at
check-in and yours must too.

## What They Need to Make a Claim Stick

- Condition record at YOUR return (their inspection — which for
  after-hours returns happens next morning, unsupervised)
- Repair estimate/invoice
- Proof the damage wasn't pre-existing (their check-in record)

Your timestamped pickup photos attack point 3 directly. Their claim
documents (demand them in the dispute letter) frequently fail on
point 2 — estimates that never became invoices are a common
weakness. Ask for the ACTUAL invoice, not the estimate.

## Insurance / CDW Interactions

- **Your credit card's rental coverage (secondary/primary CDW)**:
  if a damage claim is legit, this is what pays — file with the card
  within their window (often 30-60 days, needs the rental agreement,
  the claim documents, photos). Deny-and-tender: don't pay the rental
  company out of pocket before exhausting this.
- **Your personal auto policy**: collision often extends to rentals
  (US); liability via your policy regardless.
- **Their CDW/LDW bought at counter**: usually kills their damage
  claim entirely — if you bought it, say so in the FIRST response and
  the claim usually dies fast (the one scenario where the upsell
  defends you).
- **Third-party travel insurance CDW**: cheaper, but slower; start
  the notification immediately.

## Regional Notes

- **EU**: pre-existing damage must be recorded on the check-out sheet
  you sign; unmarked = presumed new. Hence: never sign a blank/sparse
  damage diagram. Deductions from deposit happen fast; dispute window
  short. Cross-border one-way rentals: photograph extra hard.
- **US**: chargeback leverage is strongest; keep states' AG consumer
  complaint as escalation.
- **Airport vs neighborhood branches**: airport returns skew
  after-hours and rushed (highest claim rates). Neighborhood counters
  usually walk the car WITH you — use them when prices match.

## The Escalation Ladder

1. Counter/location manager (call while evidence is fresh)
2. Corporate customer care (the letter — written trail starts here)
3. Card issuer chargeback (60-day clock; the real teeth)
4. State AG consumer division + BBB complaint (regulatory paper trail;
   companies respond because these get counted)
5. Small claims (for large claims with solid evidence; file where the
   company does business or per contract's forum clause)

## Evidence Quality Rules

- Timestamps ON (check phone camera settings before the trip)
- One slow narrated walk-around video beats 50 chaotic photos
- Size reference in damage shots (coin, fingertip)
- Archive off-phone (email to self / cloud) same day — phones get
  lost on vacation, which is when the claim arrives
- Name files: `pickup-front-left-rim.jpg`, `return-fuel-gauge.jpg`

## Fee Menu Decoder (what's contestable)

| Fee | Contestable? | How |
|---|---|---|
| Fuel service | Yes, partially | gauge photo + receipt; dispute the rate even if level conceded |
| Cleaning/detailing | Yes | interior photos at both ends |
| Late return | Sometimes | grace-period policies; flight-delay documentation |
| Additional driver | No (if unbought) | avoid by registering drivers at pickup |
| Toll "convenience" ($/day program) | Partially | opt out, pay tolls directly; some programs cap — read the cap |
| Smoking fee | Yes | photos of ashtray/interior at pickup |
| Damage + loss-of-use + admin | Yes | the whole playbook above |
