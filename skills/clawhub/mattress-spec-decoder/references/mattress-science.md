# Mattress Materials Science — The Reference

## Why specs beat brands

Mattress makers sell the same bed under different model names per retailer
(Serta's "Perfect Sleeper" line has dozens of retailer-exclusive names). The
purpose is to defeat price comparison. The fingerprint that survives renaming
is the spec stack: layer materials, densities, coil type/count/gauge, and
layer order. Two "different" mattresses with identical specs are the same bed.

## Foam density (lb/ft³) — the single best lifespan predictor

Density is mass per volume. Higher density = more polymer per unit = slower
compression fatigue = slower impression formation.

| Density (memory foam) | Verdict | Typical life |
|---|---|---|
| < 3.0 | Avoid | 1–2 years to visible impressions |
| 3.0–4.0 | Acceptable | 3–6 years |
| 4.0–5.0 | Sweet spot | 5–8 years |
| > 5.0 | Excellent | 8–10+ years, sleeps warmer |

**The base-foam bait-and-switch:** brands advertise the comfort layer density
and quietly drop the support/base foam below 1.5 lb/ft³. You feel the comfort
layer in the store; you sleep on the support layer for the next 8 years.
Always ask for BOTH numbers. A manufacturer that refuses to disclose densities
is telling you what they are.

Trade-offs of high density: heavier, slower response (some hate the "stuck"
feel), more heat retention, higher cost, longer off-gassing.

## ILD / IFD (Indentation Load Deflection)

Force (lbf) needed to compress a 4-inch sample by 25% (ILD) or 1 inch (IFD).
Roughly: 10–15 soft, 15–25 medium, 25+ firm for comfort layers.

Rarely published (it would let you compare across brands — see above). When
you can get it, the useful number is not the comfort layer alone but the
effective stack ILD, which depends on layer order (see below).

## Layer stack order matters as much as the layers

- Comfort layer over firm base = classic: pressure relief + support.
- Thin comfort (< 1.5") over firm base: you'll bottom out at shoulders/hips
  if you're heavier or a side sleeper.
- Thick soft over soft ("deep plush"): alignment failure for back/stomach.
- "Euro-top vs pillow-top": euro-top is sewn flush (denser feel); pillow-top
  is a flap (looser, can shift). Marketing difference is mostly cosmetic.
- Zoned sections (firmer center third): genuinely useful for back sleepers
  and heavier bodies; verify the zone map exists, not just the word "zoned."

## Coils

- **Pocketed (individually wrapped):** best motion isolation + point
  conformity. The default good choice for couples.
- **Offset (Bonell-adjacent, hinged):** traditional feel, durable, transfers
  motion.
- **Bonnell:** hourglass, cheap, squeaks with age, transfers everything.
- **Continuous (Leggett & Platt style):** durable, firm, poor conformity.
- **Count:** queen sweet spot ≈ 480–900. Past ~900 you're paying for a
  number; gauge and pocket quality matter more.
- **Gauge:** 12.5–13 = firm, 14–15 = softer. "Zoned gauge" (firmer middle) is
  a real feature, not marketing.

## Latex

- Dunlop: denser, firmer feel, more durable per dollar.
- Talalay: airier, more consistent, springier, pricier.
- Density 4.3–5.9 lb/ft³; lifespan 8–12 years — the most durable comfort
  material commonly available. Heavier and runs warmer than foam.
- Natural vs blended vs synthetic: durability differences are modest;
  "natural" matters for off-gassing preference, not longevity.

## Hybrid vs foam vs innerspring vs latex

- **Hybrid (coils + foam comfort):** best generalist. Support from coils,
  pressure relief from foam. Breathes better than all-foam.
- **All-foam:** best motion isolation and contour; worst thermally; lifespan
  set by foam density; BMI ≥ 30 needs 1.8+ base.
- **Innerspring (traditional):** cheap, breathable, bouncy; poor pressure
  relief and motion isolation.
- **All-latex:** premium durability; heavy; firm-ish feel; expensive.

## Heat physics

Memory foam softens with temperature and re-emits stored heat → the "sleeping
hot" complaint. Mitigations in order of actual effect: gel infusions are
mostly cosmetic (phase-change materials buy minutes, not nights); open-cell
and ventilated foams help somewhat; hybrid coil cores and wool covers help
most. A breathable mattress protector and linen/cotton sheets do more than
any "gel" claim.

## Off-gassing (VOC)

Foam beds release VOCs from manufacturing; odor peaks in the first 24–72
hours. Certifications: CertiPUR-US (foam, US), Oeko-Tex (textiles),
GOLS (latex organic). Unpack in a ventilated room, leave 1–3 days.

## Lifespan table (comfort-quality years, not "usable" years)

| Build | Expected good years |
|---|---|
| Low-density all-foam | 3–4 |
| Mid-density all-foam | 5–7 |
| High-density all-foam | 7–10 |
| Budget hybrid | 5–7 |
| Good hybrid (4.0/1.8/800 pocketed) | 7–9 |
| All-latex | 8–12 |

Replace-when signals: visible no-load sag > 1", waking with new pain that
fades over 30 minutes, sleeping better away from home, edge collapse.

## Firmness science summary

- Side sleepers: shoulder needs to sink ~1–2" for spinal alignment → softer.
- Stomach: hip sink hyperextends the lumbar spine → firmest.
- Back: between, with lumbar support.
- BMI shifts the effective firmness of any given surface: the same bed feels
  and performs firmer-under-load for heavier bodies → go firmer and denser.
- Aging (60+) generally prefers slightly softer surfaces for joint pressure;
  add support via density, not softness.

## Rotating and foundations

- Rotate head-to-foot every 3 months (memory foam hybrids), 6 months (latex).
  Never flip one-sided beds — most modern beds are single-sided.
- Slats: gap ≤ 3" (queen center support required for widths > 5 yr warranties).
- Box spring only for innerspring; platform/foundation for foam+hybrid.
  Wrong foundation is warranty-void #2 after stains.
