# 01 — Initial Client Communication

> **Trigger**: Client proactively reaches out / channel referral / proactive outreach
> **Preparation**: Understand basic client background (industry / scale / publicly available information)
> **Deliverables**: Client communication notes + preliminary assessment (whether to pursue, and if so, what positioning)

---

## 1. Pre-Meeting Preparation (15 minutes)

### 1.1 Quick Background Research

| What to Check | Channel | What You Learn |
|------|------|------|
| Store count / locations | Google Maps / Yelp / website | Scale, distribution, average ticket |
| Brand / company info | Crunchbase / LinkedIn / company website | Founding date, investors, related entities |
| Online channels | Website / Amazon / Shopify / TikTok / Instagram | Online maturity |
| Reviews / reputation | Yelp / Google Reviews / Trustpilot | Customer feedback, common complaints |
| Industry news | Google News / trade publications | Funding / expansion / negative press / competitors |

### 1.2 Prepare Question List

Based on research, prepare 5-8 questions that demonstrate "you've done your homework."

---

## 2. Four Client Types & Communication Strategies

### Type A: Small Retail Owner (Mom-and-Pop / Single Store / Small Chain)

**Characteristics**: Pragmatic, cost-conscious, no patience for technology, believes in "seeing is believing"
**Communication Strategy**:
- Use their language: "cash register" not "POS terminal," "selling online" not "omnichannel"
- Do the math first: "Invest $500/year, sell an extra $30/day via delivery apps, payback in under 1 month"
- Tell stories: "The shop down the street, just like yours, after adopting the system..."
- Don't give more than 2 solution options — choice paralysis = no decision

**Opening Script Template**:
> "Hi [Name], your store is in a great location — roughly what's your daily revenue? ... What cash register system are you using now? ... How long does end-of-day reconciliation take you?"

### Type B: Regional Chain Management (10-200 stores)

**Characteristics**: Some IT awareness but limited, focused on management efficiency, constrained IT budget, multiple systems coexisting
**Communication Strategy**:
- Start from "management frustrations": "Is inter-store inventory transfer often inaccurate?"
- Use data: benchmark against industry standards ("Industry inventory accuracy benchmark is 95% — where are you now?")
- Emphasize "integration": "Are your POS and e-commerce data connected right now?"
- Offer phased approach: don't require a one-shot solution; provide a 3-year roadmap

**Opening Script Template**:
> "[Name], what systems are your [X] stores using right now? Can you see data across all stores in real time? ... When was the last time inaccurate inventory caused an oversell?"

### Type C: Large Retail / Brand (200+ stores)

**Characteristics**: Has an IT team, focused on strategic ROI, long decision chain (3-6 months), clearly defined needs
**Communication Strategy**:
- Do your homework: review public information (financial reports / news / job postings)
- Show depth: use industry benchmarks and global best practices to spark interest
- Talk strategy not features: "Your competitor [X] is already doing AI-driven assortment planning"
- Respect the internal team: "We're here to accelerate your team, not replace them"

**Opening Script Template**:
> "[Name], I noticed your company opened [X] new stores this year — rapid global expansion. What do you see as the biggest challenge on the digital side? ... Is real-time global inventory visibility (T+0) important to you?"

### Type D: Franchise Brand

**Characteristics**: Franchisees are the resistance point, control is the core need, supply chain is the entry point
**Communication Strategy**:
- Start from "franchisee pain points": "Do franchisees often say 'the system is too hard to use'?"
- Lead with supply chain, then systems: "Centralized procurement can save franchisees 10-20% — that's the easiest win to push"
- Talk "empowerment" not "control": "The system helps franchisees earn more — it's not surveillance"

## 3. Eight Must-Ask Questions

| # | Question | Why Ask | What to Listen For |
|---|------|------|------|
| 1 | "What systems are you using now? Are you satisfied?" | Understand current state + pain signals | Satisfied = doesn't need you; dissatisfied = find the pain point |
| 2 | "What's your biggest headache?" | Find the most acute pain point | Prioritize: which is most painful, which is second |
| 3 | "If this problem isn't solved, what does it cost you per year?" | Make the client calculate their own ROI | The number they say = your value anchor |
| 4 | "Have you tried to solve this before? Why didn't it work?" | Avoid repeating past mistakes | Was it a selection error? Implementation failure? People resistance? |
| 5 | "Do you make this decision, or are others involved?" | Understand the decision chain | CEO / CFO / CTO / franchisees... who has the final say? |
| 6 | "What's the rough budget range?" or "What do you think it's worth?" | Probe budget | Don't directly ask "what's your budget" |
| 7 | "If this succeeds, what does it mean for you personally?" | Understand personal motivation | Promotion / cost savings / peace of mind / competitive edge / reputation |
| 8 | "What's your expected timeline?" | Understand urgency | Urgent = fast decision; not urgent = need to create urgency |

---

## 4. Meeting Structure (Recommended 60 minutes)

| Time | Content | Key Points |
|------|------|------|
| 0-5 min | Icebreaker + self-introduction | "I'm [Name], specialized in retail digital transformation. I've previously helped [X] retailers with..." |
| 5-10 min | Understand company background | "Tell me about your business — what's the situation?" |
| 10-30 min | Deep inquiry (8 must-ask questions) | Listen more, talk less; use follow-up questions to go deeper |
| 30-45 min | Share initial thoughts | "Based on what you've shared, I have 3 initial ideas..." |
| 45-55 min | Align on next steps | "I'd suggest we start with a quick diagnostic — within 2 weeks I'll deliver an assessment report" |
| 55-60 min | Relationship building + close | "Can I connect with you on WhatsApp/LinkedIn? I'll send over some industry reference data" |

---

## 5. Post-Meeting Actions (Within 24 Hours)

1. **Send a thank-you message** (WhatsApp/LinkedIn/email): Thank them for their time + 1-sentence summary + confirm next step
2. **Write meeting notes**: Client background / pain points / budget / decision maker / urgency / next steps
3. **Internal assessment**: Is this client worth pursuing? (Budget mismatch / needs aren't real → cut losses early)
4. **If a proposal is needed**: Proceed to Phase 01-03

---

## 6. Absolutely Do Not Do

| Do NOT | DO Instead |
|------|------|
| Quote a price in the first meeting | Understand needs first, then give a range |
| Explain your technical architecture | Explain the value in terms they understand |
| Criticize the client's current system | Say "it could be even better" not "this is terrible" |
| Offer 5 solution options at once | Maximum 2 (recommended + alternative) |
| Promise things you're not certain about | Honestly say "we need further assessment" |
