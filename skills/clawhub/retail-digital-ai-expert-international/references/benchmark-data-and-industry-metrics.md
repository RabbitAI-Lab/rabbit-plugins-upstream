### 7.1 EU AI Act — Retail Implementation Guidance

> The EU AI Act (effective August 2024, phased enforcement through 2027) is the world's first comprehensive AI law. It adopts a risk-based tiered approach with direct implications for retail AI deployments.

**Risk Classification for Retail AI Scenarios**

| Retail AI Scenario | EU AI Act Risk Tier | Key Obligations |
|------|:---:|------|
| AI-powered demand forecasting & replenishment | **Minimal / Limited** | Transparency recommended; no specific obligations unless safety-critical |
| Product recommendations & personalization | **Limited** | Transparency disclosure (user must know AI is used); opt-out mechanism |
| AI chatbot / customer service (non-high-risk) | **Limited** | AI interaction disclosure; human escalation path |
| AI dynamic pricing (general product) | **Limited** | Transparency (algorithmic pricing disclosure may be required under DSA) |
| AI employee scheduling & workforce management | **Limited → High** | If used for employment decisions → potentially High-Risk; requires conformity assessment, human oversight, accuracy documentation |
| AI-powered credit scoring / BNPL approval | **High-Risk** | Conformity assessment, risk management system, human oversight, accuracy/robustness documentation, fundamental rights impact assessment |
| AI candidate screening (HR/recruitment) | **High-Risk** | Full compliance: CE marking, quality management system, incident reporting, transparency |
| Emotion recognition in-store | **Prohibited/High-Risk** | Prohibited in workplace context; restricted in consumer context with explicit consent |
| Social scoring / consumer profiling | **Prohibited** | Prohibited if based on social behavior or inferred personal characteristics over time |

**Retail Compliance Action Plan (12-Month)**

```
Month 1–3: AI Inventory
  → Map all AI systems in use (including vendor-provided AI features in POS/ERP/CRM)
  → Classify each by EU AI Act risk tier
  → Identify High-Risk and Prohibited applications

Month 4–6: Gap Assessment
  → For High-Risk systems: conduct conformity assessment gap analysis
  → Review data governance practices (training data quality, bias testing, documentation)
  → Assess human oversight mechanisms

Month 7–9: Remediation
  → Implement required documentation (technical docs, risk management records)
  → Establish AI governance board / AI officer role
  → Train staff on AI literacy (mandatory under Article 4)

Month 10–12: Ongoing Compliance
  → Set up post-market monitoring and incident reporting process
  → Register High-Risk systems in EU database
  → Integrate AI Act compliance into vendor procurement and RFP templates
```

### 7.2 NIST AI RMF — Retail Implementation Guidance

> The NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0, January 2023; updated 2024) provides a voluntary but widely adopted framework for managing AI risks. It is increasingly referenced in US procurement requirements and corporate governance.

**NIST AI RMF Core Functions Applied to Retail**

| NIST RMF Function | Retail Application |
|------|------|
| **Govern** | Establish AI governance structure: AI ethics committee, risk appetite for AI in pricing/recommendations/hiring, vendor AI audit policy |
| **Map** | Catalog AI systems across retail value chain; document data sources, model types, decision impact (e.g., "promotional pricing model affects 500K SKU-price points daily") |
| **Measure** | Quantify AI risk — bias in product recommendations (demographic fairness), accuracy drift in demand forecasting (MAPE trend), fairness in credit/loyalty tier assignment |
| **Manage** | Implement risk controls: model rollback procedures for pricing errors, human override thresholds for inventory decisions, A/B testing with guardrails for personalization |
| **Continuous** | Monitor model drift, incident tracking, periodic third-party audits, integrate learnings into governance |

**Retail-Specific NIST AI RMF Mapping (By Department)**

| Department | Key AI Systems | Primary NIST Functions | Key Metrics |
|------|------|------|------|
| **Merchandising** | Demand forecasting, assortment optimization, pricing | Map → Measure → Manage | MAPE drift, bias by category/region |
| **Marketing** | Personalization, churn prediction, LTV modeling | Govern → Map → Measure | Fairness across demographics, opt-out rate |
| **Supply Chain** | Inventory allocation, DC routing, supplier scoring | Map → Manage → Continuous | Error rate in demand signals, supplier diversity |
| **Store Operations** | Labor scheduling, loss prevention CV, planogram compliance | Govern → Manage | Employee fairness, false positive rate in LP |
| **E-Commerce** | Search ranking, product recommendations, visual search | Map → Measure | Ranking fairness, explainability |
| **HR** | Resume screening, shift assignment, performance prediction | Govern → Measure → Manage | Hiring bias (4/5ths rule), transparency |

**Crosswalk: EU AI Act + NIST AI RMF Integration for Global Retailers**

| Area | EU AI Act Requirement | NIST AI RMF Mapping | Practical Step |
|------|------|------|------|
| Risk management system | Mandatory for High-Risk | Govern + Map + Measure | Unified AI risk register covering both frameworks |
| Data governance | Training/validation data quality | Map + Measure | Standardized data cards for all retail ML datasets |
| Transparency | User disclosure + documentation | Govern + Map | Standardized model cards, customer-facing AI notice |
| Human oversight | Mandatory for High-Risk | Manage | Human-in-the-loop for pricing, hiring, and credit decisions |
| Accuracy/Robustness | Required for High-Risk | Measure + Manage | Continuous monitoring dashboard for all production models |

> **Note**: For retailers operating in both EU and US markets, building a unified AI governance program that satisfies both frameworks is more cost-effective than maintaining separate compliance tracks. The NIST AI RMF's voluntary nature makes it an ideal foundation, with EU AI Act mandatory requirements layered on top.
