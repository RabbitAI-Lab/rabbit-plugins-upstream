# Engagement — Participation, Discussion, and Group Work That Works

Engagement is an output of task design, not an input of enthusiasm. A room that will not participate is usually a room where participating is risky, pointless, or optional.

**Before rearranging groups or seating**, read `## Dynamics` and `## Seating` in that group's class file: the pairs that do not work and the arrangement that settled them are recorded there, and rediscovering them costs a lesson each time.

## The Three Causes of Non-Participation

Diagnose before intervening; the three have opposite remedies.

| Cause | Signal | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| **Cannot** — the task is above their current access point | Starts nothing, copies, asks procedural questions, avoids eye contact | Lower the entry, not the objective: worked example, sentence stem, first item done together (`differentiation.md`) |
| **Will not risk it** — being wrong in public is expensive here | Answers privately but not publicly, hedges, "I don't know" from a student who does | Change the risk: think time, pair rehearsal, whiteboards where everyone shows at once, no opt-out normalised |
| **No reason to** — nothing follows from participating | Answers when asked, initiates nothing, work is minimally compliant | Make the response consequential: it is used, checked, built on, or visibly changes what happens next |

Treating "cannot" as "will not" produces defiance; treating "will not risk it" as "cannot" produces boredom and insult.

## Making Participation the Default

- **Response formats where everyone responds at once** remove the social cost entirely (`checking.md`). This is the single largest lever and it is mechanical, not motivational.
- **Think time before any first response.** Fast-hands cultures teach the rest of the room that thinking slowly means being wrong.
- **Rehearsal before exposure**: pair-share before a whole-class answer means nobody speaks an unrehearsed idea to thirty people.
- **Sentence stems** for anything academic: "I agree with X because…", "the evidence for that is…". Students often have the idea and not the register.
- **No opt-out as a norm**, taught explicitly: not knowing is a stage, and you will come back to them. Applied warmly it raises participation; applied as a gotcha it destroys it.
- **Praise the process publicly, the person privately.** Public praise for ability makes the next hard task a risk to identity.

## Motivation That Survives the Novelty

Three levers, in the order that they actually move:

1. **Competence.** Success at something that was recently hard is the most reliable motivator in a classroom. Design for a success rate around 80% in guided practice (Rosenshine) — consistently at 100%, the work is not worth doing; consistently at 40%, effort looks pointless.
2. **Autonomy.** Real choices that do not compromise the objective: which three of five practice items, which context for the same task, the order of two components. Fake choices ("do you want to work hard today?") are detected immediately.
3. **Belonging.** Names, being noticed for work rather than only for infractions, and a visible position in the group's work. The quiet compliant student is the most under-noticed person in most rooms.

Extrinsic rewards do work in the short term, and they reliably erode intrinsic interest in tasks that were already interesting. Reserve them for volume-of-practice behaviours nobody claims to love — spellings, drills, retrieval streaks — never for reading, creating, or investigating.

## Discussion That Is Not Four People Talking

- **Discussion is thinking, not encoding.** Nothing is retained unless something is written or retrieved afterwards (`explaining.md`). Close every discussion with three written questions.
- **Structure before openness**: everyone writes a position for two minutes, then pairs, then a whole-class round. Cold openness selects for confidence.
- Use a protocol when the stakes are high: talk tokens (each student has two contributions to spend), a round where everyone speaks once before anyone speaks twice, or a silent written discussion on a shared document.
- **Track the distribution.** A tally against the seating plan for one lesson is usually more persuasive than any advice here.
- Teach disagreement explicitly: disagree with the idea, name the reason, offer the alternative. Without teaching, disagreement is either absent or personal.
- Socratic seminar works with a text everyone has read, prepared questions, an inner and outer circle, and a job for the outer circle. Without those four, it becomes an unstructured chat with a classical name.

## Group Work

Group work fails by default. Two conditions make it work (Johnson and Johnson), and both must be engineered:

- **Positive interdependence**: the task cannot be completed alone. Split resources, assign distinct sub-questions, or require a product that needs every part.
- **Individual accountability**: each member is assessed on something only they produced. Without it, one student works and the rest observe — and the grade lies about all four.

Practical rules:

- **Three to four members.** At five, someone is a passenger; at two, it is pair work with different dynamics.
- **Assign roles and teach them**: facilitator, recorder, checker, presenter. Rotate them so roles are not identity.
- **Groups are assigned, not chosen.** Self-selection maximises comfort and minimises range, and it isolates the students least able to afford isolation.
- **Task first, groups second.** If the task can be done alone, it should be.
- Give a visible timer and interim checkpoints; groups spend the first third socialising unless the first checkpoint lands early.
- Grade the individual component; the group product can be feedback-only. Group grades are the fastest way to make cooperative work resented.

## Group Structures Worth Knowing

| Structure | Shape | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Think-pair-share | Individual, pair, whole class | Any question worth more than a one-word answer |
| Jigsaw | Each member masters one part in an expert group, then teaches their home group (Aronson) | Content that splits into independent parts; forces individual accountability by construction |
| Numbered heads | Group agrees the answer; a random member reports | Ensures everyone can explain, not just the fastest |
| Rotating stations | Groups move between tasks with a time box | Limited equipment, or several short practice types |
| Gallery walk | Work displayed; groups annotate each other's with a criterion | Peer feedback without peer grading |
| Fishbowl | Inner group performs the task, outer group observes against a checklist | Modelling a discussion or a procedure before everyone attempts it |

## Competition, Points and Games

- Team competition raises effort on low-stakes retrieval and damages the room when the same students lose repeatedly. Randomise teams; keep rounds short; reward improvement over absolute score.
- Quiz games are retrieval practice with a scoreboard: keep the retrieval and cap the scoreboard. If a game generates more noise than answers per minute, a plain retrieval starter beats it.
- Points systems decay. Assume any economy stops motivating after a few weeks and design so the underlying routine survives its removal.

## Phones and Devices

- School policy usually settles this, and a teacher who fights it alone loses. Record what the policy is under the behaviour posture preference area in `config.yaml` and apply it consistently.
- Where a device is needed for the task, name the exact tool and the exact window: "laptops open for the next 12 minutes, on the simulation only". Ambient device availability with no stated purpose is a permanent parallel channel.
- Screens down and away for anything read aloud, modelled, or discussed. Split attention between a screen and a demonstration means neither is processed.
- Notes by hand for anything to be remembered; typed notes are fine for anything to be searched.

## The Disengaged Individual

Work down this list before concluding anything about attitude:

1. Can they access the task? Reading level, prerequisite, missing accommodation (`differentiation.md`).
2. Have they had a success in this subject in the last two weeks? If not, engineer one that is real, not a token.
3. Is anything outside the classroom in play — attendance, home, health, another class? (`struggling.md`, and the Red Flags table in `SKILL.md`)
4. Do they know you noticed them for something other than a correction this fortnight? A thirty-second private, specific comment is the cheapest intervention available.
5. Is the disengagement subject-wide or global? Global is a pastoral conversation; subject-specific is usually about a failure they can name if asked privately.

**When a technique visibly moves participation with a group**, write it to `## What Works With This Group` in `memory.md`. **When a grouping or seating arrangement works or fails**, update `## Dynamics` and `## Seating` in that class file, and put the rotation on a `## Due` row. **When a structure and its brief are worth reusing** — a jigsaw split, a station rotation, a seminar protocol — save it in `~/Clawic/data/teacher/artifacts/<kebab-name>.md` with its `## Boxes` line, in the same turn (`memory-template.md`).
