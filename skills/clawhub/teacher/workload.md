# Workload — Making the Job Survivable Without Teaching Worse

Teaching has no natural stopping point: there is always another resource to make and another script to mark. The constraint is therefore a decision, and unmade decisions get made by exhaustion.

**Before deciding what to cut**, read `## Due` in `~/Clawic/data/teacher/memory.md` and the assessment dates already committed. Most workload crises are visible in a calendar six weeks earlier, and the cheap fix is available only then.

## Where the Hours Actually Go

Audit before optimising: one week, tallied in 15-minute blocks. The usual result surprises people, and it is the only way to know which lever matters for *you*.

| Category | Typical share | Compressible? |
|---|---|---|
| Teaching contact | Fixed by timetable | No |
| Marking and feedback | Frequently the largest non-contact block | **Yes, largest lever** |
| Planning and resource making | Large in year one, should fall sharply after | **Yes, via reuse** |
| Admin, data entry, reports | Fixed volume, movable timing | Partly, by batching |
| Communication | Expands to fill the available channel | **Yes, via boundaries** |
| Meetings and duties | Mostly fixed | Rarely |
| Behaviour follow-up | Falls as routines take hold | Yes, indirectly (`classroom.md`) |

The three compressible blocks are marking, resource making and communication. Working faster at the incompressible ones is not a strategy.

## The Triage Rule

Rank every recurring task by **what students act on**, and cut from the bottom.

| Tier | Examples | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Non-negotiable | The check that decides tomorrow's teaching; feedback that gets acted on; the accommodations; safeguarding duties | Never cut |
| High return | Retrieval starters; the exit ticket; whole-class feedback; the worked example | Protect |
| Medium | Individual written comments on formative work; detailed plans for lessons you have taught before | Reduce or convert |
| Low | Presentational marking; decorative displays; re-typing resources; deep plans for a lesson taught tomorrow and never again; duplicate record-keeping | Stop |

The test for any task: **if I stopped doing this, what would a student notice?** If the answer is nothing, and it is not required by policy, it is not teaching.

## Marking Is the Biggest Lever

The full arithmetic and the alternatives are in `grading.md`; the workload framing is:

- Run the budget arithmetic when the **assessment calendar** is written (SKILL.md Rule 8). Once the work is collected, the only remaining choices are bad ones.
- Sample marking plus whole-class feedback is usually a 60-70% saving with more student action, not less.
- Self-marking against a key for closed-answer work, teacher reading only the misses.
- Live marking during independent practice removes the pile for that piece entirely, at 6-8 students a lesson.
- **Stop marking presentation, titles, dates and copying accuracy.** None of it is feedback and it consumes a surprising share of the pile.
- Fewer, larger, better-marked pieces beat many lightly-marked ones on every axis including student outcomes.

## Planning Once, Teaching Many Times

- **Plan the unit once, the lessons in batches.** Sequential nightly planning is the most expensive possible order and produces the least coherent sequence.
- Plan the parallel classes together: same objectives, adjusted entry points from each class file.
- Adapt, never create from blank. Department schemes, last year's artifact, a colleague's resource — steal the structure and rewrite the parts that do not fit your group.
- **Timebox planning explicitly.** Under `plan_format: brief`, a lesson in an existing unit should take 15-20 minutes to plan. A plan that takes longer than the lesson is not sustainable at four lessons a day.
- Resist making beautiful resources. Presentation quality has no measured effect on learning, and decorative graphics measurably reduce it (`explaining.md`).

## The Reuse System

The single largest time source in teaching is the work you already did. It only exists if it was written down where you will find it.

- Everything reusable goes to `~/Clawic/data/teacher/artifacts/<kebab-name>.md` with a line saying when to read it and its `## Boxes` index line, in the same turn it is produced.
- **Name by content, never by date or week.** "Unit plan — bonding, Year 10" is findable in eighteen months; "week 7 lesson 3" is not.
- **On reuse, add one line**: what you changed and why. Three years of those lines is a better professional record than any portfolio.
- The two highest-return records are `## Explanations That Landed` and `## Misconceptions` — they turn next year's hardest lesson into a ten-minute preparation (`explaining.md`).
- Rubrics, comment banks and blueprints are the artifacts that repay most; they are reused every cycle and are painful to rebuild (`assessment.md`, `grading.md`).

## Batching

Context switching is the hidden cost in a day cut into five-minute pieces.

- **One slot for guardian communication**, one for admin, one for marking. Reacting to arrivals means every task pays the switching cost.
- Marking in blocks of about 25-30 minutes with the anchors re-checked at the start holds the standard better than a long unbroken session, where standards drift with fatigue (`grading.md`).
- Batch the same question across scripts rather than the same script across questions — faster and more consistent.
- Put the slots in `## Due` where they are cadences, so they survive a busy fortnight.

## Communication Boundaries

- **One published channel with a stated response window** — "email, two working days, term time" — and hold it. Without one, the boundary is set by whoever messages latest.
- No school communication on a personal phone number or personal account (`parents.md`).
- Do not answer at 11pm. Every out-of-hours reply resets the expectation for everyone, including colleagues, and the expectation is what becomes unsustainable.
- Draft difficult replies and send them in the morning. Nothing consequential is improved by being sent tired.

## Saying No Upward

- Ask what it replaces. "I can take that on — which of these three should drop?" is a workload conversation rather than a refusal, and it makes the trade-off visible to the person who owns it.
- Ask what problem it solves and how it will be measured. Initiatives with no measure are usually the ones quietly abandoned in February, after everyone has built the resources.
- Offer the cheaper version: a sample instead of everything, an existing artifact instead of a new one, a template instead of a bespoke document per class.
- Where a request duplicates a record that already exists, say so with the location. Duplicate record-keeping is the most common recoverable waste in schools.
- Some things are not negotiable — statutory duties, safeguarding, access arrangements. Know which, so the negotiable ones can actually be negotiated.

## The Calendar Is the Intervention

Most workload crises are calendar collisions, visible weeks ahead:

- **Stagger assessments across classes** by at least a week (`curriculum.md`). Four sets arriving together is one destroyed weekend, guaranteed months in advance.
- Check the whole-school calendar for reports, parents' evenings, trips and exam weeks before setting anything.
- Put the **return date** in `## Due` the day work is collected, derived from `grading_turnaround_days` — the deadline you can see is the one you can protect.
- Leave the week after a reporting deadline light. Everyone plans as if that week is normal, and it is not.

## Warning Signs

Observable, not emotional; act on any two together:

- Marking older than two weeks that is still described as "coming back soon" — feedback past that point is a grade with words (`grading.md`).
- Planning the same lesson from scratch for the second time because last year's version was never saved.
- Working every evening and still starting the week behind.
- Guardian emails answered after 10pm as normal practice.
- Cutting independent practice or the exit check because the lesson is not ready — the first things to go are the ones that were doing the teaching.
- Sunday consumed as the default operating assumption rather than as an occasional crunch.

Each of these has a structural fix above. Working harder is the response that makes all of them worse, and it is the default.

## What Not To Cut

- The checks that decide the next lesson. They are five minutes and they prevent teaching into a void.
- Feedback that students act on, with the time to act on it.
- Accommodations. They are an obligation, not an enhancement (`differentiation.md`).
- The early positive guardian contact. Two minutes now prevents a two-hour meeting later (`parents.md`).
- Writing down what worked. Cutting this is borrowing from your future self at a very poor rate.
- Sleep, food and the weekend. A tired teacher makes worse decisions in every category above, and the decision quality is the job.

**When a workload decision is made** — sample marking on a piece, a cadence changed, a task stopped, an assessment moved — record it as a decision in `~/Clawic/data/teacher/artifacts/<kebab-name>.md` with the arithmetic behind it, plus its `## Boxes` line: it will be re-litigated at the next timetable change, and the arithmetic is what settles it. **When a cadence is agreed** — marking slot, communication slot, guardian contact, coverage audit — it becomes a `## Due` row. **When an artifact is reused**, add the one line about what changed; that line is the whole reuse system working (`memory-template.md`).
