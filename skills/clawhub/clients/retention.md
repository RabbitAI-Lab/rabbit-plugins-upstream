# Retention — Renewals, Expansion, Referrals, Win-Backs

Scope: keeping a client, growing them, and getting the next one out of them. Ending well is `offboarding.md`; deciding whether you *want* to keep them is `portfolio.md`.

## Ask at Value, Not at Need

The single organising rule of this file. The moment to ask for a renewal, a referral, a testimonial or a rate rise is when you have just delivered something they are visibly pleased with — not when your pipeline is empty.

Two reasons it works, and one that matters more: goodwill is at its peak and the evidence is fresh; and an ask that arrives at your moment of need is legible as need, which changes what you are worth. Put the asks on dates in `## Due` so they happen on the delivery calendar rather than the anxiety calendar.

## The Renewal Conversation

Start it 30-60 days before the end date held in `## Due`. A renewal discussed in the final week is a renewal negotiated under time pressure, by you.

1. **Open with what happened**, in their metrics, from the project file: what was delivered, what changed, what it was worth. Three lines, no slides.
2. **Show the change log** — including everything done free (`scope.md`). This is the moment it earns its keep: "last term included 38 hours outside the agreed scope" reframes the next number as accuracy, not an increase.
3. **Propose the next term as a specific shape**, not an open question. "Same retainer, plus the reporting work that kept coming up, at X" beats "would you like to continue?", which invites a review of whether they need you at all.
4. **Apply the rate change here** if one is due, with notice already given (`pricing.md`). Renewal is the correct moment; mid-term is not.
5. **Name the decision date and the consequence.** "I'm holding your slot until the 15th" is true if you hold slots, and should not be said if you do not.
6. **If they decline, ask what would have made it a yes.** The answer arrives honestly at this moment and at no other, and it is the most valuable market research available to a small supplier.

Renewal is also a filter. Check the client against `portfolio.md` before proposing: a renewal you would rather not have is a slot that cannot be sold to someone better.

## Expansion

Growing an existing client is cheaper than finding a new one, and the mechanism is specificity:

- **Propose the adjacent thing you already saw**, not a menu of services. "While doing X I noticed Y is costing you a week a month; I can fix it in three days for Z" converts because it is evidence-based and small.
- **Land-and-expand deliberately**: a small first engagement that is genuinely complete on its own, then the larger one once trust exists. A first engagement designed to be incomplete without the second is transparent and resented.
- **Expansion inside a retainer needs a cap change**, not absorption. More work at the same fee is a rate cut disguised as growth.
- **Watch the concentration limit.** Expanding your largest client past `concentration_limit_pct` makes the business more fragile even as revenue rises (`portfolio.md`).
- Time expansion proposals to their budget cycle. Ask once, early, when the fiscal year starts and when budgets are set — a great proposal in the wrong month waits a year.

## Referrals

- **Ask specifically.** "Do you know anyone who needs X?" gets a polite nothing. "You mentioned your counterpart at Y has the same reporting problem — would you introduce me?" gets an introduction, because it requires recall of one person rather than a search of everyone they know.
- **Make it effortless**: write the forwardable paragraph for them. Two sentences on what you did and one on what you are looking for. Most referrals die from the friction of composing the email.
- **Name the client you want.** "Companies about your size, with the same stack" produces better leads than "anyone".
- **Ask right after a delivered win**, and put the ask in `## Due` so it happens.
- **Close the loop.** Tell the referrer what happened, whether it converted or not. Referrers who never hear back stop referring, and it is the cheapest relationship maintenance there is.
- **Reciprocate.** A referral relationship that only runs one way ends quietly (`pipeline.md`).

## Testimonials and Case Studies

- **Ask at the moment of praise.** When a client says something good in writing, reply: "Would you mind if I used that as a testimonial?" Consent at the moment of enthusiasm is nearly automatic.
- **Draft it for them.** A busy client will approve a good draft and will not write one. Send three sentences with the numbers in them and ask them to edit.
- **Numbers, not adjectives.** "Cut onboarding from 11 days to 3" outperforms "a pleasure to work with" by a wide margin.
- **Get the permission in writing**, including whether their name, logo and metrics can be used. Enterprises often permit an anonymised version — "a mid-sized logistics operator" — when they refuse a named one; negotiate that at signature, when goodwill is highest (`stakeholders.md`).
- Keep the approved text in `artifacts/case-study-<client>.md` with the permission scope recorded. Six months later nobody remembers what was allowed, and the artefact goes unused for lack of certainty.

## Dormant Clients

A past client is the highest-converting lead source there is, and the only thing standing between the user and that pipeline is a date.

- **Put a re-contact date in `## Due`** for every client that ends well — a quarter after the end for project work, sooner if their cycle is fast.
- **Re-contact with something, not with "checking in".** Something you saw that is relevant to them, a result from similar work, a change in their sector. The message should be useful whether or not they hire you.
- **No guilt, no apology for the gap.** "It's been a while" invites a reply about how busy everyone is. Open with the useful thing.
- Two attempts, spaced, then move them to an annual rhythm. A client who has ignored two useful messages is not dormant, they are done — record it in the client's `## Roster` row (status and date) and stop.

## Win-Backs

For clients who left, including ones you fired:

- **Wait for the trigger**, do not push: their new supplier fails, your old champion moves into a bigger role, or the problem you solved recurs. Most win-backs are events, not persuasion.
- **Acknowledge the ending in one clause, no more**, and never re-litigate it: "I know last time ran over on scope — I've changed how I run those projects."
- **Name what changed on your side**, concretely. It is the only thing that makes the second attempt different from the first.
- **Re-qualify from scratch** (`pipeline.md`). A returning client is not automatically a good client, and a client you fired is not automatically fixed.
- **Requote at current rates**, never at the old ones. Returning at the old price re-establishes the old price permanently.

## Churn Signals

Ranked by how early they appear. Any two of these on the same client is a renewal at risk and a rescue conversation now, not at renewal (`difficult-clients.md`).

| Signal | Lead time before churn |
|---|---|
| Approval times doubling, and the champion attending fewer meetings | Longest — often a full quarter |
| A reorg, a new manager above your champion, or your champion leaving | Long, and it resets everything (`stakeholders.md`) |
| Questions about how you work rather than about the work | Long — someone is building a comparison |
| Budget described as "under review" or moving to annual approval | Medium |
| Scope requests stopping entirely on an active engagement | Medium — disengagement reads like calm |
| Payment slowing on a client who never paid late | Medium (`getting-paid.md`) |
| Being asked for documentation, handover notes, or credentials unprompted | Short — a transition is being prepared |
| Anything else that changes from a pattern you had recorded | Whatever the record says; that is why the contact log exists |

**Write before you move on:** renewal outcomes, new terms and new end dates go into `## Roster` and `## Due`; every renewal, referral ask, testimonial ask and re-contact goes into `contact-log/<client-slug>.md` with the date and the response; an approved testimonial or case study goes to `artifacts/case-study-<client>.md` with its permission scope and its `## Boxes` line; a referral that converts starts a row in `## Pipeline` with the source named, because source-to-conversion is what `pipeline.md` ranks on; a dormant client gets its re-contact date in `## Due` the day the engagement ends, or the date will never exist; a dormant client who has ignored two useful messages has that outcome written into its `## Roster` row with the date, and its re-contact row in `## Due` moved to an annual rhythm or dropped.
