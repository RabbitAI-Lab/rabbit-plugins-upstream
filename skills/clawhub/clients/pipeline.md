# Pipeline — Leads, Qualification, and Saying No

Scope: from "someone enquired" to "we are writing a proposal" or "we declined". Pricing the work is `pricing.md`; writing the document is `proposals.md`.

## The Order That Saves the Most Time

Budget and authority before scope, scope before price, price before a document. Every reversal of that order costs unpaid hours:

1. **Disqualify** against the no-go list and the decline table — thirty seconds, no call.
2. **Budget range and decision process**, in the first reply. Not "what's your budget" as an ultimatum; "projects like this usually land between X and Y — is that the right order of magnitude?" A range you name is answerable; an open question invites a pass.
3. **Discovery call** only if the range survives.
4. **Proposal** only after discovery, and only if you can name the deliverables (`proposals.md`).

A proposal written before a budget conversation is a free consulting document. If they will not discuss money before seeing a document, that is the answer to the qualification question, not an obstacle to it.

## Qualification in Four Questions

| Question | What a good answer sounds like | What a bad one means |
|---|---|---|
| What happens if this does not get done? | A named consequence with a number or a date attached | No consequence = no budget will materialise, however enthusiastic they are |
| Who signs, and who else has to agree? | One name, plus a specific approval step | "I'll just need to run it past a few people" = a committee you cannot see (`stakeholders.md`) |
| What is the money already allocated to this? | A figure, a range, or "it comes out of the X budget" | "We'll find it if the case is strong" = you are writing their business case unpaid |
| Why now, and why us? | An event — a launch, a departure, a failed vendor, a deadline | Vague interest = a discovery call that goes nowhere and a proposal that dies in silence |

Three good answers out of four is a qualified lead. Two or fewer, and the correct move is a paid discovery or a decline — never a full proposal.

## Decline List

Anything on `no_go_list` is declined before this table is even reached. Then:

| Signal | Why it ends badly | Exception |
|---|---|---|
| Budget an order of magnitude below the scope | Every hour after this is spent teaching them what things cost | Sell one small, complete piece at full rate — never the whole thing cheap |
| "This should be quick for someone like you" | Expertise being used as an argument for a discount; it will recur at every invoice | None |
| Wants a free sample, spec work, or a pitch against unpaid competitors | The work is the product; giving it away sets the price at zero | A tiny paid trial with a real fee |
| No written scope will be tolerated | Every disagreement becomes your word against theirs | None |
| Previous freelancer or agency described as an idiot, unprompted | You are next in that sentence; find out what actually happened before believing it | They can describe their own contribution to the failure |
| Sets your hours, requires your presence, provides the equipment, forbids other clients | This is employment with none of its protections, and misclassification exposes both sides — the IRS common-law test turns on behavioural control, financial control, and the nature of the relationship, and the equivalents elsewhere ask the same three things | Negotiate the control terms out; if they are the point, it is a job offer, so treat it as one |
| Asks you to start before the contract "as a show of good faith" | Good faith is what the deposit demonstrates | None |
| Pays in equity, exposure, or future work | Illiquid, unenforceable in practice at small holdings, and it converts a client into a partner you did not choose | Equity **on top of** a rate that covers costs |
| Anything else that trips two rows of the Warning Signals table in SKILL.md | Two independent signals before the money has moved is the cheapest decline you will ever make | Ask about both directly; a real answer to both resets the count |

## The Discovery Call

Thirty to forty-five minutes, and its purpose is disqualification as much as discovery.

- **Open with the agenda and the end time.** "Twenty minutes on the problem, ten on how I work, five on next steps." Structure signals what working with you is like more than any credential does.
- **Spend the first two-thirds on their problem, not on your work.** The single most common mistake is presenting a portfolio to someone who has not finished describing what is wrong.
- **Ask what they have already tried and why it stopped.** The answer names the real constraint — usually a person, rarely a technology.
- **Say the number out loud on the call.** A range, verbally, with the shape of what it buys. Sending numbers only in writing delays the disqualification by a week and adds a document nobody reads.
- **Close with the next step, its owner and its date.** "I'll send a proposal Thursday; can you confirm by the following Wednesday?" A call that ends in "let me know" ends.
- **Record it the same day** in `contact-log/<client-slug>.md`, even for a lead — that file becomes the client's history if they convert and the reason you did not chase them if they do not.

## Where Leads Actually Come From

Ranked by conversion rate, which is roughly the inverse of how much attention each gets:

| Source | Why it converts | How to work it |
|---|---|---|
| Past clients | They already paid; the trust question is settled | A dated re-contact in `## Due`, not a mood (`retention.md`) |
| Referral from a past client | Arrives pre-sold, price rarely litigated | Ask at the moment of delivered value, and name the kind of client you want |
| Referral from a peer who does adjacent work | Same trust transfer, plus they filter for fit | Reciprocate deliberately; a one-way referral relationship stops |
| Someone who saw the work | Self-qualified on capability, not yet on budget | Make the work public with the problem attached, not just the artefact |
| Inbound from content or search | Volume, low qualification | Put the price range on the page and half the bad leads disqualify themselves |
| Marketplaces and platforms | Highest volume, lowest rate, price-led buyers | `upwork` covers the platform mechanics; treat it as lead generation, not as a business |
| Cold outreach | Lowest conversion, highest control over who you talk to | Only worth it when narrowly targeted at a problem you can name in their words |

The concentration lesson from `portfolio.md` applies to sources too: a pipeline that is entirely referrals from one happy client is one relationship away from empty.

## Following Up Without Begging

- Three follow-ups, spaced roughly 3, 7, and 14 days after the proposal, each carrying something new — a clarification, a relevant example, a date that is about to move.
- The third is the close-the-file message: "I'm assuming the timing isn't right — I'll close this off unless I hear otherwise, and I'd rather that than keep landing in your inbox." It gets more replies than the two before it combined, because it costs them nothing to answer and it is honest.
- Never send a follow-up whose entire content is that you are following up. It teaches the reader that your messages can be ignored without cost.
- A lead that goes silent after a proposal is not lost data: record the stage it died at in `## Declined Leads` with the reason and the re-open condition.

## Declining Well

The decline is a marketing act — the people you turn down refer you, and the ones you turn down badly warn people off. Three sentences: a clear no, one true reason that is not their fault, and a door.

> "I'm not the right fit for this one — the budget and the scope are far enough apart that I'd be setting us both up to be disappointed. If the scope tightens or the budget moves, I'd be glad to look again. In the meantime, [name] does this kind of work well."

Never decline by going quiet, never decline with a fake reason a mutual contact can disprove, and never counter-offer a discount in the same message as the decline.

**Write before you move on:** every lead gets a row in `## Pipeline` in `memory.md` (or `~/Clawic/data/clients/leads.md` once it has split out) with its source, stage, estimated value and next date; a decline gets its row in `## Declined Leads` with the reason and the re-open condition; a discovery call gets its entry in `contact-log/<client-slug>.md`; a lead that converts gets its roster row and its people written into `~/Clawic/data/contacts/contacts.md`. A pipeline held in the user's head is the thing this skill exists to replace.
