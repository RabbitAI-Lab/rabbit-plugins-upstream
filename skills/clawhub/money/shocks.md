# Shocks — Job Loss, Illness, Divorce, Death

**Before answering**, read `## Situation`, `## Money Shape` and `## Goals` in `~/Clawic/data/money/memory.md`, and `artifacts/shock-playbook.md` if `## Boxes` lists it. A plan written in calm is worth more here than anything composed during the event.

In a shock the normal ladder is suspended. The objective is not optimization, it is **runway and housing**: how many months of core spending exist, and whether the household stays where it lives. Everything else waits.

## The First 72 Hours

Same sequence for every shock, adapted at the edges:

1. **Stop the outflow.** Cancel or pause every non-essential recurring payment (`finances/subscriptions.md`), suspend automated investing and extra debt payments, and reduce standing transfers to the minimum. This is reversible, immediate, and buys weeks.
2. **Count the runway.** `runway months = (liquid cash + reliable incoming) ÷ core monthly spend`. Liquid means available this week: buffer, current accounts, redundancy pay. Not pensions, not property, not the vested shares that need a sale window.
3. **Protect the housing payment first** (tier 1 in `debt.md`). Rent or mortgage before every other creditor, without exception.
4. **Claim what exists.** Statutory support, redundancy entitlements, sick pay, insurance policies already held (`insurance.md`), employer benefits that continue past the leaving date. Claims almost always have deadlines and are almost never backdated; this is the highest-value hour of the week.
5. **Tell creditors before missing a payment, not after.** Forbearance, payment holidays and hardship schemes exist and are granted to people who call early. A missed payment with no call is an enforcement track; a call before the date is an arrangement.
6. **Do not liquidate anything yet.** Nothing sold in the first 72 hours is sold well, and most of it turns out to be unnecessary.

## What Never Gets Liquidated First

Ordered from "use it" to "last resort":

| Source | Position | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency fund | First. This is the event | It exists for exactly this and costs nothing to use |
| Taxable investments, sold with the tax position checked | Second | Costs a realised gain or loss, no penalty |
| Selling assets that carry running costs | Second | Cuts the outflow as well as raising cash |
| Interest-free or low-rate borrowing already arranged | Third, deliberately | Cheap and reversible; not a solution, a bridge |
| Retirement money | Very late | Penalty + tax + lost compounding, three costs where people count one (`retirement.md`) |
| Borrowing against the home | Last, and only with advice | Converts unsecured stress into a repossession risk (Red Flags) |
| High-rate credit to cover living costs | Only knowingly, with an exit date | It is a countdown, not a bridge; write the date down |

## Job Loss

- Runway first, then the search. Both, in that order, in the same week.
- The severance package is a buffer, not a bonus (`windfalls.md`). Check the payment date and whether any of it is taxed differently in `country`.
- **Benefits stop with the job**: health cover, income protection, death-in-service, employer pension contributions. Establish the exact end date for each, and price replacements for anything the household cannot be without (`insurance.md`).
- Continuing a workplace pension contribution personally is often possible and is usually the last thing to cut, not the first — but liquidity outranks it during the gap.
- Sign nothing on the day. Settlement agreements, restrictive covenants and waivers are negotiable and are presented as standard; in many jurisdictions independent legal advice is required and paid for by the employer.
- Accepting a lower-paid role to stop the bleed is a legitimate move, and it resets the salary base for years (`income.md`). Weigh it against the search runway, and prefer a bridge income that does not close the search.

## Illness or Injury

- Income and expenses move in opposite directions at the same time. Model both: sick pay tapering, then statutory support, against the new costs — treatment, transport, adaptation, care.
- Check every policy already held before buying anything: income protection, critical illness, cover bundled in a mortgage or card, employer schemes, and travel cover if it happened abroad.
- Deadlines on claims are short and enforced. Notify early, even if the claim is uncertain.
- If a carer in the household reduces their hours, that lost income is part of the shock and is routinely left out of the arithmetic.

## Separation and Divorce

- The financial split is a legal process and the rules differ enormously by `country` and by marital status. **Cohabiting partners frequently have no automatic rights at all**, which is discovered at exactly the wrong moment. Route the settlement itself to a qualified professional; what belongs here is the money hygiene around it.
- Immediate: know what exists. Every account, debt, pension and policy on both sides. A list made now is worth more than any argument later.
- Joint debt does not split when the relationship does. **Both parties remain liable to the lender for the whole of a joint debt regardless of any agreement between them.** Separating joint borrowing is a refinancing, not a conversation.
- Break financial associations at the credit bureaus once accounts are separated, or one file keeps affecting the other (`credit.md`).
- Update beneficiaries, wills and powers of attorney. Beneficiary designations override wills in many systems, so an unchanged one pays an ex-partner (`insurance.md`, `household.md`).
- Pensions are frequently the largest asset in the split and the most often overlooked because they are not visible in a bank account.
- Two households cost more than one. Rebuild both budgets from zero rather than halving the old one (`budget.md`).

## A Death In The Household

- Nothing is decided quickly, and nothing needs to be. Estate processes are slow by design.
- Immediate cash access is the practical problem: joint accounts usually remain available, sole accounts often freeze. Knowing which is which in advance is one of the strongest arguments for the estate checklist.
- Claim what is due: life cover, death-in-service, survivor pensions, statutory payments. Each has its own process and its own deadline.
- Household spending does not halve. One income may stop entirely while housing and fixed costs continue (`retirement.md`, survivor terms).
- Do not make an irreversible decision — selling the home, moving, giving money away — inside the first year unless cashflow forces it.
- Beware the advisers who arrive at this moment. Grief plus a lump sum is the highest-risk combination in the domain (`scams.md`).

## Write The Playbook Before You Need It

The highest-value artifact in this whole skill, written on a calm afternoon and read on the worst day:

- Runway today, and the figure it must not fall below
- The cut list in order, with amounts: what gets cancelled in hour one, week one, month one (`budget.md`, ordered by money saved per unit of life quality lost)
- Which creditor gets called first, and the account references
- Which policies exist, with the claim route for each — this is where the coverage map earns its cost (`insurance.md`)
- Which assets are liquid, in what order, and what each costs to touch
- What is untouchable, and why
- Where the documents are, as pointers rather than contents (`file:~/Documents/...`)
- Who to call: employer contact, adviser, the free statutory or non-profit advice service in `country`

**Write it down.** The playbook is an artifact at `~/Clawic/data/money/artifacts/shock-playbook.md`, created before the shock and reviewed annually in `## Due`, with its `## Boxes` line the same turn. During an event: the new runway figure and the change in circumstances go to `## Situation` and `## Money Shape` in `~/Clawic/data/money/memory.md`; anything cancelled goes out of `~/Clawic/data/finances/subscriptions.md`; every arrangement made with a creditor — the terms and the date — goes to `## Debt Plan` and its account row in `~/Clawic/data/finances/accounts.md`.
