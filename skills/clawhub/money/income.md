# Income — The Lever That Outranks Every Optimization

**Before answering**, read `## Money Shape` and `## Situation` in `~/Clawic/data/money/memory.md`. A raise recommendation that ignores a savings rate already at 30% is the wrong advice for that household.

A 10% pay rise, saved, moves the finish date further than any plausible improvement in fees, allocation or discipline — and unlike those, it has no ceiling. Spending has a floor; income does not. That is the whole argument for putting this above `investing.md` in effort, and it is why an agent that spends an hour on fund selection for someone underpaid by 20% has optimized the wrong term.

## Split the Raise Before It Arrives

The mechanical rule that beats every intention: decide the split **while the raise is still a number on paper**, and set the standing order the day the first higher payslip lands.

- Default split: `savings_rate_target_pct` of the gross increase goes to savings, before anything else changes. A stated preference overrides it.
- Aggressive version: 100% of the increase to savings for three months, then take a share back. Three months establishes that the old budget still works, and it converts a raise into a permanent rise in savings rate rather than a permanent rise in fixed costs.
- The failure mode is not indulgence, it is **fixed-cost creep**: a raise absorbed by a bigger flat, a longer car finance term or a school is not reversible next year. Variable spending is; fixed spending is a new floor.
- Percentage rises compound against a rising base, so an early raise is worth multiples of a later one of the same size. That is the argument for doing the uncomfortable conversation now.

## Where Raises Actually Come From

| Route | Typical magnitude | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Annual review increase | Usually tracks a budgeted pool set before the conversation | Low effort, low ceiling |
| Internal promotion | A band change, so a step rather than a percentage | Slow, and often requires a vacancy |
| Changing employer | Consistently the largest single-step increase available in most markets, because it reprices to the external market rather than to last year's salary | Search effort, tenure reset, risk |
| Repricing without moving — a competing offer | Real, and routinely resented; some employers match once and reclassify the person as a flight risk | Relationship cost, sometimes permanent |
| Scope change: more revenue responsibility, a scarcer skill, a harder market | The only route that raises the ceiling rather than the position on it | Months to years |

Prepare the conversation as evidence, not as need: what changed in the scope, what it produced in figures, what the market pays for it. Personal need is the weakest argument in the room and it is the one most people bring. The conversation itself — anchoring, timing, the counter — is `negotiate`.

## Total Compensation, Not Salary

Compare offers on the whole package converted to an annual figure in `currency`:

- Employer pension or retirement contribution — a matched contribution is immediate return, and the match ceiling is the number that matters (ladder step 1)
- Health, dental, life and income-protection cover, priced at what buying it privately would cost (`insurance.md`)
- Bonus: state the **expected** value, not the maximum. A "up to 20%" bonus with a historical payout of 8% is an 8% bonus
- Equity, valued below
- Pay-adjacent costs that differ by employer: commute, mandatory office days, required travel, professional fees, equipment
- Leave, notice period and redundancy terms — notice and severance are buffer months, and they reduce `emergency_fund_months` (`emergency-fund.md`)

## Equity Compensation, Honestly

| Instrument | What it is | The trap |
|---|---|---|
| RSUs | Shares delivered on vest, taxed as income at vest in most regimes | Withholding at vest is often a flat supplemental rate below the recipient's marginal rate; the shortfall is due at filing and nobody has reserved it (`taxes.md`) |
| Options (ISO/NSO/EMI and local equivalents) | The right to buy at a strike | Exercising is spending real money on an illiquid asset, and in several regimes the tax falls at exercise, not at sale — a tax bill on a gain that cannot be sold |
| Private-company equity | A number on a slide | The strike, the preference stack and the last round's terms decide the payout, not the headline valuation. Common shares behind a large liquidation preference can be worth zero in a sale that makes headlines |
| ESPP | Discounted purchase, often with a lookback | Usually the highest risk-adjusted return in the package if sold promptly; the risk is holding it and compounding concentration |

Two rules that survive every market:

1. **Vested public shares are cash that has decided to stay in one company.** The test: with that cash in hand today, would you buy that stock at that price, in that size? If not, sell. Holding is an active decision.
2. **Cap employer concentration.** Salary, bonus, pension and stock all failing together is the single largest correlated exposure most households own. A common ceiling is ~10% of investable assets in the employer; anything above that is a position, not a benefit (`investing.md`).

## Second Incomes and Side Work

Compute the **marginal net**, which is routinely a fraction of the headline:

`marginal net = gross − income tax and contributions at the marginal rate − childcare − transport − any withdrawn means-tested benefit`

Benefit withdrawal is the invisible term: in several jurisdictions a band of income is taxed and simultaneously withdraws support at a combined effective rate above 70%, so a second job in that band can add almost nothing (`taxes.md` — benefit cliffs). Establish `country` before advising a second earner to take work.

Ranked by return on effort, over a year:

1. Repricing the main income (above) — the same hours, more money
2. A skill that raises the main income's market rate — slow, then permanent
3. Contract or freelance work in the same skill, billed at a professional rate (`self-employed.md`)
4. Selling assets and unused items — one-off, but immediate and untaxed in most regimes below a threshold
5. Low-skill gig work — reliable, capped, and it competes with sleep. Correct as a bridge in a shock, wrong as a strategy (`shocks.md`)

## When Income Is Variable

Bonus, commission and overtime are not salary and must not fund fixed costs. The rule: **the budget is built on the floor month; everything above the floor is allocated the day it arrives**, by the ladder, not by the mood. Treat a recurring bonus as a windfall each time (`windfalls.md`), and never let a bonus season set the standing orders.

**Write it down.** A salary, bonus expectation, employer match ceiling or income change goes to `## Money Shape` in `~/Clawic/data/money/memory.md`, with its `As of` date; the shape of the household's income (single, dual, freelance) goes to `## Situation`.
