# Viral Reels: faceless reels, YouTube Shorts and TikTok videos with AI

This is a method for making viral reels without showing your face or filming anything. You pick a format that viewers already know, write a 1-second hook, build the reel script as Paradox, Escalation, Pivot, Payoff, give every line its own picture, and end on a comment word. The same vertical video works as an Instagram reel, a YouTube Short and a TikTok. Use it with any AI reels maker. The last step shows how to make it in Ziztu Reel Factory.

No format guarantees views. What the method does is remove the usual reasons a faceless reel fails: a slow start, several ideas in one line, pictures that do not match the words, and a video that looks nothing like the last one.

You are the writer and the director. By the end you hand over a script, a shot list, a caption and a comment word, ready to paste into a reel maker.

When the user leaves something open, use these defaults and say them in one line instead of asking a list of questions:

| Choice | Default |
|---|---|
| Shape | Vertical 9:16, one spoken voice, no face on screen |
| Length | 30 to 45 seconds. Shorter is fine if the idea ends sooner |
| Platform | Write once for Instagram reels, YouTube Shorts and TikTok together |
| Language | The user's language |
| Format | Pick from step 1 by the topic. When the topic fits two, give both and recommend one |

Ask a question only when the answer changes the reel. Ask at most two, then write.

## Step 1. Pick a format

A format is a repeatable shape: the same kind of picture, the same kind of script, a new topic each time. Viewers who liked one episode know what the next one gives them. Pick the format from the topic, not the other way round. Full beat templates are in `references/formats.md`.

How to choose:

1. If the topic is a number or a scale, use How deep is the ocean or What-if stages.
2. If it is one surprising fact about a living thing, use Animal facts.
3. If it is a choice, use Would you rather.
4. If it is a story with a person and a problem, use Claymation, Stickman or Block-world.
5. If it happened in the past, use the 2.5D history cartoon.
6. If it is a thing being made, use Diorama build.

If the user points at a reel they like, study its structure, not its words. Write down: the hook type, how many lines, what each picture showed, how it ended. Then write a new script on a new topic. Never reuse another creator's script, voice or footage.

## Step 2. Pick the topic

A good reel topic has a surprise you can say in one sentence. Test: write "Most people think ___, but ___" or "___ is bigger, older, deeper, faster or stranger than ___". If you cannot finish the sentence, the topic is not ready.

Then check three things:

- **True.** Every fact is something you can source. Look it up before writing. Do not invent numbers, quotes or dates.
- **Visible.** Every line of the script can be drawn as a picture. "Inflation affects purchasing power" cannot be drawn. "A shopping cart that gets smaller every year" can.
- **One idea.** The whole reel is one idea. Two ideas are two reels.

## Step 3. Write the 1-second hook

The hook is the first spoken line plus the first picture. Together they have about one second to say "stay". It does three jobs: it makes a promise, it shows a picture that matches, and it starts mid-moment. Rules:

1. Start on the surprise. No greeting, no "in this video", no channel name, no "did you know".
2. The first line is 8 words or fewer. The first word should carry the point.
3. The picture and the line are the same thing. If the line says "Everest vanishes", the picture shows a mountain going under.
4. Do not explain. The hook raises a question; the rest of the reel answers it.
5. Make the caption word match the first spoken word.
6. Never promise what the reel does not deliver.

Hook types (examples and more in `references/hook-ideas.md`): the impossible comparison, the number with a thing attached, the broken rule, the stage clock ("one minute after"), the either-or choice, the myth you were taught, the ending first, the scale shock.

Write five hooks and keep the one that is most concrete. Concrete beats clever.

## Step 4. Write the reel script: Paradox, Escalation, Pivot, Payoff

Every script has the same four parts. They are in this order because each one answers the question the last one raised.

| Part | Job | Share of the reel |
|---|---|---|
| **Paradox** | A statement that cannot be true, or a question with no easy answer. This includes the hook | First 15 to 20 percent |
| **Escalation** | The same idea, bigger each line. A new number, a new place, a new consequence. Never a new topic | About 40 to 50 percent |
| **Pivot** | A turn that changes what the viewer thought the reel was about | About 15 percent |
| **Payoff** | The answer that resolves the paradox, plus the comment word | Last 15 to 20 percent |

Rules for the lines:

- **One idea per line.** A line is one sentence, ideally 5 to 10 words. If a line has "and" in the middle, it is probably two lines.
- **Plan about 2.5 spoken words per second.** A 30 second reel holds roughly 75 words. Count them. Cut until it fits.
- **Each line is bigger or stranger than the last.** If a line could be swapped with the one before it, the escalation is not working.
- **The pivot is a reframe, not a new fact.** "But depth is not the danger. Weight is." The viewer now reads everything again.
- **The payoff closes the loop opened in the paradox.** Use the same words where you can, so the viewer feels the circle shut.
- **Plain words.** Short sentences. Say numbers the way you would say them out loud. Cut every word that only announces.
- **Spoken, not written.** Read it aloud. If you stumble, rewrite.

### Example: "How deep is the ocean" (about 34 seconds)

The numbers here are well-known, rounded figures. Check every number in your own script against a source before you post.

| # | Part | Line |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Paradox (hook) | Everest vanishes down here. |
| 2 | Paradox | The ocean is deeper than the tallest mountain is tall. |
| 3 | Escalation | At 200 metres, the sunlight fades. |
| 4 | Escalation | At 3.8 kilometres, you find the Titanic. |
| 5 | Escalation | That is only a third of the way down. |
| 6 | Escalation | Keep going. It is pitch black. |
| 7 | Escalation | The deepest point is about 11 kilometres down. |
| 8 | Pivot | But depth is not the danger. Weight is. |
| 9 | Pivot | Here the pressure is over a thousand times the surface. |
| 10 | Payoff | And still, something lives here. |
| 11 | Payoff | Tiny shrimp-like animals called amphipods. |
| 12 | CTA | Comment DEEP for part two: the creatures. |

Notice how line 1 is the hook (4 words), lines 3 to 7 only go deeper, line 8 turns the reel from "how far" to "how heavy", and lines 10 and 11 answer "what could possibly be there".

## Step 5. Write the shot list: a picture for every line

The picture must show what the line says, in the same second. Viewers with the sound off read the picture and the caption only. Use `assets/templates/reel-script.md` to lay it out.

Rules:

- **A picture for every line.** Name the subject, the action and the setting in one clause: "a clay shark circling a tiny clay submarine in dark blue water".
- **Cut about every 2 seconds.** A line of 3 seconds gets two pictures. A line of 2 seconds gets one. Never hold one picture for the whole reel.
- **Motion in every shot.** A moving camera, a moving subject, or both. A still picture with captions reads as a slideshow.
- **One style for the whole reel.** The same materials, palette and lighting in every shot. Say the style once in a style line and repeat it in every picture prompt.
- **Keep the cast small.** Name every character and give each one a fixed look in under 20 words. Repeat that look word for word in every shot they are in.
- **Put text on screen as a caption, never inside the picture.** AI pictures draw text badly. Add depth labels, dates and numbers as captions.
- **Show the noun.** If the line says "shark", the shark is on screen, not a sea.
- **The last picture ends the idea.** Hold on it for the comment word.

### Example shot list (first six lines of the ocean reel)

| Shot | Line | Picture | Motion | Seconds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Everest vanishes down here. | A model of Everest sinking into dark blue water, bubbles rising | Camera follows the peak down | 1.5 |
| 2 | The ocean is deeper than the tallest mountain is tall. | Cutaway of the sea: the Everest model on one side, a deep dark trench on the other | Slow pan down the trench wall | 2 |
| 3 | (same line) | The Everest model lowered into the trench, the water still over it | Camera pulls back | 2 |
| 4 | At 200 metres, the sunlight fades. | A beam of light thinning into blue, then dark | Camera sinks | 2 |
| 5 | At 3.8 kilometres, you find the Titanic. | The wreck on the sea floor in the lamp light of a small submersible | Slow push in | 2.5 |
| 6 | That is only a third of the way down. | The wreck shrinking above as the camera keeps falling | Camera sinks fast | 2 |

## Step 6. Voice, captions and music

- **Word captions.** Show the spoken words on screen, one to three words at a time, in time with the voice. Put the caption in the middle band of the frame, away from the platform buttons and the bottom text. Use a large bold font with high contrast. Highlight the key word of each line.
- **One voice.** Pick a voice that fits the format and keep it for the series. Write numbers and names the way they are said.
- **Music under the voice.** One bed that is quieter than the voice and never covers a word. No music-only reels: the voice carries the idea.
- **Keep sounds simple.** A soft tick on a cut is enough. Do not add sound effects that cover words.
- **No dead air.** The voice starts on the first frame and the last word lands on the comment word.

## Step 7. The comment-word CTA

End with one ask: "Comment ___ and I'll ___". The word is short, easy to type and tied to the topic: DEEP, CLAY, RAT. Rules:

1. **Promise something you will actually send or make.** Part two, the full list, the next episode. If you will not follow up, ask a question instead.
2. **One word, one ask.** Do not ask for like, follow, share and comment in one breath.
3. **Say it in the voice and show it as a caption.**
4. **Repeat it in the post caption and pin a first comment** that says the same word.
5. **Answer the comments.** Replies are the cheapest way to see what the next episode should be.

## Step 8. The caption and the cover

The post caption restates the hook in one sentence, adds one line of context, then the comment word. Put the search words people use (the topic, the format) in the caption in plain language. Write a cover with 2 to 4 words that match the hook, large and readable on a phone. Check each platform's current limit on hashtags before you add any.

## Step 9. Turn one reel into a series

A format works when it repeats. Plan the series before you make the second reel:

- **Fix the style.** One format, one look, one voice, one caption style, one music mood. Change the topic, not the style.
- **Fix the shape.** The same four parts, the same length, the same kind of ending.
- **Number or name each episode.** "Part 2" or "Episode 2: the creatures" tells a new viewer there is more.
- **Plan eight episodes at a time.** List the eight topics before you write any. Each should stand alone and point to the next.
- **Let comments choose.** The comment word from episode 1 is the topic of episode 2.
- **Do not recycle.** If two episodes could swap places, one of them is not needed.
- **Watch which episodes get replies and watch time,** then make more like those.

Use `assets/templates/reel-script.md` for each episode and keep a one-page series sheet: style line, voice, caption style, music, comment-word list, episode topics.

## Step 10. The AI label and the checklist before posting

### The AI label on each platform

Each platform asks you to label realistic AI-made content. Menus and wording change, so open the app's post screen and read what it asks.

| Platform | Where to look |
|---|---|
| Instagram reels | The post's advanced settings, an AI label option. Instagram may also add its own label |
| TikTok | The "More options" part of the post screen, an AI-generated content setting |
| YouTube Shorts | The upload details, the altered or synthetic content question. Read https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491 |

A good rule: if a viewer could take the video for real footage of real people, real places or real events, label it. A clay, stickman or block-world cartoon is clearly not real, but the platform's current rule has the last word. When in doubt, label.

### Check list before posting

Run it on the finished video, with the sound off first and on second.

1. The first spoken line is 8 words or fewer and starts on the surprise.
2. The picture in the first second matches the first line.
3. The script has all four parts: Paradox, Escalation, Pivot, Payoff.
4. Every line has one idea.
5. Every line has its own picture, and the picture shows the noun.
6. A cut or new motion about every 2 seconds.
7. Word captions are on, in time, in the middle band and readable on a phone.
8. The voice never competes with the music.
9. Every fact and number is checked against a source.
10. One style from the first frame to the last.
11. The video ends on the comment word and you will deliver what it promises.
12. The caption restates the hook, and the cover is 2 to 4 words.
13. The AI label is set on every platform you post to.
14. It is not a copy of another creator's script, voice or footage.
15. Mute test: does the idea still land from the pictures and captions alone?

If a check fails, fix that part and run the list again. Do not post a reel that fails 1, 2, 9 or 14.
