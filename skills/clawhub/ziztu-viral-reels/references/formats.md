# The nine reel formats

For each format: the idea, how Paradox, Escalation, Pivot and Payoff look in it, what to put in the pictures, and the usual mistakes. Every script uses one idea per line and one picture per line.

Style pages on Ziztu Reel Factory are linked in each section. Write the script first, then use the page to make it.

## 1. Diorama build

https://getziztu.com/reel-factory/diorama

A small world that looks handmade, built in front of the camera one piece at a time.

- **Paradox:** the finished thing, shown for a second, with a claim: "This whole city fits on a desk."
- **Escalation:** each line is one build step, and each step makes the world bigger or stranger: the base, the walls, the roads, the lights.
- **Pivot:** the detail that changes the meaning of the build: "And every window has a person in it."
- **Payoff:** the full reveal, a slow camera move around the finished piece, then the comment word.
- **Pictures:** hands or tools placing pieces (not faces), close macro shots, a tabletop and a soft light. Keep the same table, light and materials in every shot.
- **Mistakes:** steps that do not change the picture, a finished world that looks nothing like the opening flash, narration that explains how to build it instead of what it is.

## 2. Claymation

https://getziztu.com/reel-factory/claymation

Clay characters in a tiny scene. Good for a short story or a joke with a problem.

- **Paradox:** a character with an impossible problem: "This clay fox has one day to cross a river with no bridge."
- **Escalation:** each line is an attempt that goes worse or gets bigger.
- **Pivot:** the character stops fighting the problem and does something unexpected.
- **Payoff:** the result, in one picture, and the ask: "Comment FOX if it should try again."
- **Pictures:** one to three characters at most, each with a fixed look (colour, shape, one marking). Simple shapes are easier to keep the same from shot to shot.
- **Mistakes:** too many characters, a scene that changes place every line, a problem that needs dialogue to understand.

## 3. Animal facts

https://getziztu.com/reel-factory/animal-facts

One animal, one fact that sounds false and is true.

- **Paradox:** the fact stated flat: "[Animal] can [surprising thing]." The fact must be true. Look it up.
- **Escalation:** each line adds a more extreme detail about the same ability.
- **Pivot:** why it happens, or what it costs the animal.
- **Payoff:** the result for the animal, or the comparison to humans, then the comment word.
- **Pictures:** the animal doing the thing in each shot. Never a generic jungle.
- **Mistakes:** facts that are half true, a list of unrelated facts about one animal, a fact told with no picture of it happening.

## 4. Would you rather

https://getziztu.com/reel-factory/would-you-rather

Two options. The viewer picks.

- **Paradox:** two options that both sound good (or both bad): "Would you rather ___ or ___?"
- **Escalation:** one line per side, each adding a cost to its own option. Keep the two sides the same length.
- **Pivot:** a twist that changes the choice: "But there is a catch for the one you picked."
- **Payoff:** the vote ask. The comment word is the answer: "Comment A or B."
- **Pictures:** a split screen or two clear scenes, one per side, with the same style on both.
- **Mistakes:** one option clearly better, options with no picture, a long setup before the question.

## 5. How deep is the ocean

https://getziztu.com/reel-factory/how-deep-is-the-ocean

A camera that sinks (or climbs, or travels) past labelled steps on a scale.

- **Paradox:** a comparison that cannot be true: "Everest would vanish here."
- **Escalation:** each line is the next marker on the scale, with something found there.
- **Pivot:** the idea changes from "how far" to "what is it like at the bottom".
- **Payoff:** the last marker plus the surprise that lives there.
- **Pictures:** one continuous direction of travel. Add the marker as a caption: depth, height, distance or year.
- **Mistakes:** markers out of order, numbers not checked, a camera that turns back.
- **Works for other scales too:** height of the sky, size of the universe, age of the Earth, speed of things.

## 6. What-if stages (Zack D. Films style)

https://getziztu.com/reel-factory/zack-d-films-style

A "what if" played out in stages, usually with a time or size label on each stage.

- **Paradox:** the "what if" question: "What if the Moon disappeared?"
- **Escalation:** numbered stages, each worse or stranger than the one before: after one minute, one day, one year.
- **Pivot:** the stage where the effect nobody expected arrives.
- **Payoff:** the final state, and one fact about why it matters today.
- **Pictures:** one scene that changes with each stage, with the stage label as a caption. Keep the same planet, room or city in every stage so the viewer sees the change.
- **Mistakes:** stages that are not in order, claims that are not science, a scene that resets each stage. Mark guesses as guesses.

## 7. Stickman

https://getziztu.com/reel-factory/stickman-animation

A plain stick figure acting out an idea.

- **Paradox:** a rule that looks wrong: "This stick man gets stronger by doing less."
- **Escalation:** the figure runs into one example after another that shows the rule.
- **Pivot:** the moment the figure sees the rule from the other side.
- **Payoff:** the lesson in one line and the figure's final pose.
- **Pictures:** one figure, a plain background, simple props with high contrast. Show process, comparison and cause and effect.
- **Mistakes:** background clutter, abstract ideas with nothing to draw, a lesson that is advice you cannot back up.

## 8. Block-world animation (Minecraft-style)

https://getziztu.com/reel-factory/minecraft-animation

A blocky, game-like world for a story, a build or an explainer.

- **Paradox:** a goal that seems impossible in this world: "He has one night to build a castle with no tools."
- **Escalation:** each line is a bigger obstacle or a bigger build.
- **Pivot:** the idea that changes how the problem is solved.
- **Payoff:** the finished build or the escape, from a wide shot.
- **Pictures:** one blocky character with a fixed outfit, a recognisable biome, and clear block shapes. Use wide shots to show scale.
- **Mistakes:** a story that needs the viewer to know the game, too many characters, cutting away from the build before it finishes.

## 9. 2.5D history cartoon

https://getziztu.com/reel-factory/2-5d-animation

Flat cartoon people in a layered, slightly 3D set. Good for history told in order.

- **Paradox:** the outcome that seems impossible: "Eight hundred soldiers held a pass against an army of thousands." Use only true, sourced events.
- **Escalation:** the events in plain time order, each line a place and a date.
- **Pivot:** the decision or accident that turned the event.
- **Payoff:** what happened next, and why it still matters.
- **Pictures:** one place per line, with the date as a caption and a map when the place is far from the last one. Characters keep the same costume in every scene.
- **Mistakes:** skipping the date or place, jumping between years, inventing quotes, showing real people in harmful ways.
