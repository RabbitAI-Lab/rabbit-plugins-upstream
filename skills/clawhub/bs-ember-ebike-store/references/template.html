A few useful answers.

Before your first ride.

Can I test ride before buying?

Yes, at any Ember dealer. Book a slot online, bring a helmet or borrow one, and take the bike out for forty minutes on your own roads.

How do I pay?

Card, bank transfer or monthly instalments over 12, 24 or 36 months with no interest. The instalment option appears at checkout.

Can I fit my own accessories?

Standard mounts throughout: rear rack, bottle cage, child seat and pannier rails all use common fittings. The equipment page lists what we have tested.

A few practical details.

Which model should I start with?

Urban 02 for the daily commute, Easy Step if you want to ride in whatever you are wearing, Carry More if there are children or shopping involved. A dealer can settle it in ten minutes.

Do I need a licence or insurance?

No licence for any of our bikes; they assist up to 25 km/h. Insurance is optional and our partner covers theft and damage from $9 a month.

What happens when the battery ages?

After roughly 1,000 full charges it will hold around 80% of its original range. Replacement packs are $520 and the old one is recycled through the dealer.

The long way home

A new turn on a familiar commute.

Follow a shorter route

A coast offers a changing line of water, rock and sky. Choose a small stretch and give it time rather than treating every stop as a view to collect. Repeated visits to the same edge reveal changes in light and movement.

Slow down enough to notice the edge.

Look away from the horizon

The wide view is only part of the story. A path, a wall or a weathered surface can carry the character of a place at a smaller scale. Let those close details sit beside the landscape instead of competing with it.

Leave the place as you found it

Use established access and respect local guidance. Give other people room to enjoy the setting, and take your belongings with you. A slower visit leaves space for the place to be itself, rather than becoming a backdrop for a rushed itinerary.

A day with room for more

Make space for the everyday.

Begin with a small outcome

Choose a task that can be understood from beginning to end. Write down what a person should be able to do when it is finished. This makes it easier to tell the difference between useful progress and activity around the work.

Finish one useful thing before widening the scope.

Review the working version

Share a small, usable version early. Ask someone to follow the intended path and watch where the explanation becomes necessary. A short observation can be more useful than adding another round of visual polish.

Leave a clear stopping point

Record the decisions made, the known limits and the next task. Keeping the handover small makes it easier to resume without rebuilding the whole context. The routine should support the work, not become a second product to maintain.

The details you touch

The parts you notice on every ride.

Choose a material vocabulary

Paper, cloth, metal and ceramic each suggest a different kind of handling. Select a small set that fits the subject, then keep the scale and light consistent. More textures rarely make the page more convincing.

Texture works best when it has a reason to be there.

Keep the type precise

A tactile image can do the expressive work while the typography stays clear. Give captions and headings enough contrast, and avoid roughening every element to match the surface. The contrast between a precise layout and an imperfect material can be enough.

Review the whole sequence

Look at the images as a set, not only one at a time. Alternate close views and wider compositions where the story benefits. Repeated mockups with different colours can feel less rich than a smaller set of genuinely distinct perspectives.

QUESTIONS AT
THE BIKE RACK.

Range, charging, servicing and the rest, answered plainly. For anything about your route, a dealer will talk you through it.

All questions
How far will it really go?

Between 60 and 120 km on a charge, depending on mode, load and hills. Every model page shows range at three assist levels so you can plan on the honest number.

How long does a charge take?

About three and a half hours from empty with the supplied charger, or 80% in two. The battery lifts out, so it can charge at your desk while the bike stays downstairs.

Can I ride it in the rain?

Yes. Motor, battery and display are sealed to IP65. Rinse the drivetrain after a salty winter ride and it will thank you.

What about theft?

Every bike ships with a frame lock and a GPS tag registered to you. If the worst happens, the tag reports its position and the insurance partner steps in.

How is the bike delivered?

Assembled and tuned by your nearest dealer, then delivered to your door or collected in store. Either way, someone sets the saddle height before you ride off.
