# Interview Forge — Deep Reference

## The question taxonomy (and the stories that answer them)

Nearly every behavioral question is one of ~12 archetypes wearing different clothes:

| Archetype | Sample phrasings | Theme to pull from |
|---|---|---|
| Failure | "failed", "mistake", "would do differently" | failure |
| Conflict | "disagreement", "difficult person", "pushback" | conflict |
| Leadership | "led", "took ownership", "rallied" | leadership |
| Ambiguity | "unclear requirements", "no direction", "competing priorities" | ambiguity |
| Achievement | "proudest", "biggest impact", "accomplishment" | achievement |
| Influence | "convinced", "without authority", "alignment" | ambiguity/leadership |
| Learning | "learned fast", "outside comfort zone" | achievement |
| Integrity | "pressure to cut corners", "ethical" | conflict (refusal stories) |
| Resilience | "setback", "didn't go as planned" | failure |
| Mentoring | "developed someone", "junior" | leadership |
| Customer | "angry customer", "user feedback" | achievement/conflict |
| Process | "improved", "inefficiency" | achievement |

The mapping is many-to-many on purpose: one strong conflict story can answer archetypes 2, 8, and 10 with a different emphasis in the Result line.

## STAR budgets — why 15/10/50/25

Interviewer scoring sheets weight Action heaviest: the action is the evidence of skill. Situation is context (needed, but scored ~0). Result is proof. The budget is a forcing function against the universal failure mode: front-loaded stories where the setup eats the answer.

Diagnostic: read only your A section aloud. If it wouldn't impress standing alone, the story is weak — no Situation can rescue it.

## The follow-up chains interviewers actually run

Layer 1 (probe the action): "What exactly did you do first?" / "Walk me through how you convinced them."
Layer 2 (probe alternatives): "Why not X instead?" / "What would you do differently?"
Layer 3 (probe the result): "How did you measure that?" / "Who verified it?" / "What happened to [thing] after?"
Layer 4 (probe the person): "How did that land with your manager?" / "What did the other side think?"

A story that can't survive layer 3 has an unverified result — flag it and prepare the bridge: "The tracked metric was X; the $ figure is my estimate from it."

## Question prediction deep rules

1. **The first bullet in Requirements is the first behavioral question.** Interviewers screen for what the req lists first.
2. **Repeated phrases are strengths-gaps**: "fast-paced" twice = they got burned by someone slow. Prep the prioritization story as your opener.
3. **"Nice to have" bullets = stretch questions.** One story that touches it beats claiming fluency.
4. **Company-section phrases predict culture-fit probes.** "Move fast" → process-vs-speed tension story; "customer trust" → integrity story.
5. **Tenure patterns**: role open <6 months or team doubled = chaos story needed ("dropped into a fire").
6. **Level calibrates scope**: senior = stories with org-level results; junior = effort + learning rate. Don't bring an org-level story to a junior loop as your only one — it invites "so what was YOUR part" you can't answer.

## Mining prompts — the extended set

Career archaeology (run any time, 20 minutes):
- The thing you fixed that stayed fixed
- The time you were right but had to lose the argument gracefully
- The worst handoff you ever received — and what you did in the first week
- The deadline you moved, negotiated, or missed honestly
- The rule you broke for the right reason (and the one you refused to break)
- The quiet work that made someone else's headline possible
- The number on a dashboard somewhere that is still your number
- The tool/process you killed
- The time you taught a non-technical person something technical
- The user/customer you personally turned around
- The bug/incident/outage you'll never forget
- The scope you cut to hit a date — what you cut and why it was right

Each answer is one bank line. Don't judge quality while mining; judge coverage after.

## Answering the salary question (early rounds)

Deflect-with-research pattern: "I'm flexible on structure and want to make sure the overall fit is right — for calibration, roles like this in [city] are posting in the $X-$Y band, and I'd want to be in the upper part of that band given [specialization]. What range is budgeted?" Never a bare number first; never "whatever you're offering."

## Post-interview debrief ritual

Within 2 hours, log: questions actually asked vs. predicted (feeds prediction accuracy), answers that landed (keep), answers that rambled (re-budget), follow-ups that stung (new weak-spot flags), interviewer names/roles. After 3-4 interviews the debrief log is worth more than any prep doc.

## Red flags in your own answers (self-audit)

- "We" density > "I" density in Action
- Result with no number, band, or timeframe
- Situation longer than Action
- Villain story (coworker/manager is purely wrong) with no empathy line — always include what the other side wanted
- Failure story where nothing actually failed
- Any claim you can't defend one layer deeper
- Present-tense lesson ("I need to be better at...") instead of changed behavior ("Since then I...")
