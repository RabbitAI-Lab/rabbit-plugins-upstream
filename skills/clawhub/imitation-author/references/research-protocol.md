# Research Protocol

Use this protocol for every Imitation Machine run. It turns "search the web" into an auditable corpus-building process.

## 1. Define The Research Brief

Record:

- target and disambiguation details;
- authorization status;
- intended outputs and requested topic;
- style domains and material channels;
- known time span and likely evolution;
- source hints;
- standard or Deep Mode;
- success criteria and excluded uses.

## 2. Map The Source Universe

Search for all applicable source families before drawing style conclusions:

| Family | Examples |
|---|---|
| Official identity | Home page, biography, verified accounts, organization pages |
| Complete inventories | Archive pagination, sitemap, RSS, bibliography, catalog, episode index, channel uploads |
| First-party long form | Essays, letters, newsletters, books, scripts, talks, reports, documentation |
| First-party short form | Posts, captions, threads, comments, short videos, product microcopy |
| Spoken and performed | Interviews, talks, podcasts, transcripts, captions, live appearances |
| Visual/product artifacts | Screens, campaigns, layouts, motion, packaging, interface states |
| Craft and process | Interviews about writing, editing, influences, routines, audience, design choices |
| Requested topic | Target plus the user's topic, synonyms, adjacent concepts, and relevant works |
| Time slices | Early, middle, current, and known transition periods |
| Reception and criticism | Profiles, reviews, critiques, corrections, parodies, reversals, controversies |
| Genre/topic baseline | Comparable artifacts by adjacent authors, publications, brands, or creators |

Use platform-native search where useful, general web search, archive navigation, feeds, transcript search, and user-provided sources. Record unavailable platforms instead of pretending they were covered.

## 3. Run A Query Matrix

Log representative queries and result pages. Deep Mode should cover these query families:

1. `target official`, official domain, archive, sitemap, RSS, bibliography, catalog.
2. `target + channel`: essay, newsletter, podcast, transcript, video, social, interview, talk, product, campaign.
3. `target + craft`: writing process, editing, influences, style, audience, creative process.
4. `target + requested topic` plus three to eight topic synonyms or adjacent concepts.
5. `target + time period` for early, middle, recent, and transition years.
6. `target + critique/review/analysis/controversy/correction`.
7. Exact-title searches for major works and missing artifacts.
8. Adjacent-author or genre searches for the counter-corpus.

Do not equate search rank with representativeness. Search snippets are discovery aids, not style evidence.

## 4. Classify Every Source

### Authorship

- `authored`: directly created by the target.
- `coauthored-edited`: target contribution is real but another hand may affect the surface form.
- `spoken-transcribed`: target speech, with transcription or editing uncertainty noted.
- `official-associated`: official publisher, employer, campaign, or product material not proven to be authored by the target.
- `quoted`: target appears only in selected quotations.
- `secondary`: reporting, review, criticism, or analysis by others.
- `unknown`: attribution cannot be established.

### Access depth

- `A`: complete or substantial first-party artifact suitable for structural and language analysis.
- `B`: verified short sample, authorized excerpt, transcript segment, interface sequence, or observable artifact suitable for limited claims.
- `C`: metadata, summary, snippet, listing, reception, or work-level context. Not suitable for sentence-level claims.

### Corpus role

- `inference`: used to build claims.
- `held-out`: reserved before synthesis for evaluation only.
- `context`: establishes biography, catalog, chronology, reception, or gaps.
- `counter`: same genre or topic from another source.

One source can have only one corpus role for a run. A held-out artifact cannot later be cited to rescue an inference.

## 5. Build The Corpus Manifest

For every artifact, record:

- Source ID, title, creator/byline, URL or citation, publication date, access date;
- channel, genre, topic, time band;
- authorship label, access tier, corpus role;
- complete/partial status and approximate analyzable length;
- duplicate group or source relationship;
- usefulness, limitations, and access gaps.

Deduplicate mirrors, syndication, book excerpts reprinted as articles, transcript copies, compilations, and snippets of a longer source. Count the underlying artifact once.

## 6. Balance The Inference Corpus

Avoid letting one easy archive define the whole target.

- Sample across time, topic, and channel.
- Weight full authored artifacts above metadata or recollections.
- Separate spontaneous speech from edited prose.
- Separate individual style from house style when editors or teams are involved.
- Preserve real frequency. A rare memorable trick should not become a default rule.
- If the intended output is narrow, keep a broad core corpus and add a topic/output-specific slice.

For a well-documented writing target in Deep Mode, aim for 20-50 Tier A/B authored artifacts, three or more time bands, all material writing modes, and a small same-genre counter-corpus. More is useful only when it changes or calibrates the model.

## 7. Reserve Held-Out Evidence

Before writing claims, reserve at least 20% of Tier A/B evidence, with a minimum of four artifacts in Deep Mode. Stratify across important channels, topics, and periods. List only metadata in the working taxonomy; do not read the held-out content during synthesis if the workflow can practically avoid it.

If prior research already exposed every artifact, create the cleanest available holdout and label contamination risk. Do not claim a blind evaluation.

## 8. Extract Claims, Not Impressions

Give each material claim an ID such as `SEL-01`, `REA-02`, `COM-03`, or `LIN-04`.

| Layer | What to observe |
|---|---|
| Selection | Chosen subjects, scale, scenes, people, facts, omissions, recurring tensions |
| Worldview | Values, assumptions, audience relationship, certainty, emotional range |
| Reasoning | Evidence order, causal logic, analogy, contrast, qualification, counterexample, conclusion |
| Composition | Opening type, macro-structure, section functions, transitions, paragraph roles, endings |
| Language | Diction, function words, syntax, length distribution, cadence, punctuation, questions, repetition |
| Variation | Differences by channel, genre, topic, audience, period, collaboration, spontaneity |

A high-confidence stable claim normally requires three supporting inference artifacts across at least two topics, periods, or contexts. Record disconfirming examples. Narrow the scope instead of averaging contradictions away.

## 9. Separate Style From Content And Genre

Ask of every apparent trait:

- Does it survive when the topic changes?
- Does it also occur in the counter-corpus?
- Is it a house/genre convention rather than the target?
- Is it driven by proper nouns, subject vocabulary, or repeated source stories?
- Is it stable across periods or a temporary mode?
- Is it frequent, or merely memorable?

Quantitative features can help: sentence and paragraph length distributions, section length, question frequency, first/second-person use, hedges, quotations, lists, punctuation, repeated transition shapes, and opening/ending types. Metrics support interpretation; they do not replace close reading.

## 10. Prove Saturation

Counts are necessary floors, not a stopping rule. Deep research can stop only when:

- every applicable source family has coverage or an explicit gap;
- finite archives or catalogs have been mapped, including pagination/date range;
- topic-specific discovery is complete;
- time, channel, craft/process, criticism, and counter-corpus passes are complete;
- two consecutive, meaningfully different discovery passes add no new high-confidence trait, material exception, time shift, or source class;
- the Claim Registry is stable enough that new evidence mostly confirms or narrows existing claims;
- blocked and weak areas are recorded with their impact.

In the Saturation Log, state the last useful discovery, the two no-novelty passes, remaining gaps, and the exact coverage claim. Say "broad search reached saturation under the recorded protocol," never "the entire web was searched."
