# HACCP / Codex / ISO 22000 — Reference

## Codex HACCP Principles (CXC 1-1969, Annex)

### Principle 1 — Hazard Analysis
- Identify all hazards reasonably expected to occur at each step.
- Assess significance (likelihood x severity).
- Consider: biological (pathogens, parasites), chemical (allergens, toxins, residues, cleaning agents), physical (glass, metal, bone).
- List control measures for each significant hazard.

### Principle 2 — Critical Control Points
- Apply the CCP decision tree to each step with a significant hazard.
- A CCP is a step where control is essential to prevent/eliminate a hazard or reduce it to an acceptable level.
- Distinguish CCP from PRP (Prerequisite Program).

### Principle 3 — Critical Limits
- Must be measurable (temperature, time, pH, aw, concentration).
- Must be validated (scientific basis, regulatory standard, or expert advice).
- Example: pasteurization 72°C for 15 seconds (milk).

### Principle 4 — Monitoring
- Define: what to monitor, how, frequency, who.
- Monitoring should ideally be continuous; if not, frequency must be sufficient.
- Records must be signed/dated by the person doing the monitoring.

### Principle 5 — Corrective Actions
- Predefined for each CCP deviation.
- Must address: (a) disposition of affected product, (b) cause correction, (c) record of action taken.
- Affected product must be evaluated for safety before release.

### Principle 6 — Verification
- Activities that determine whether the HACCP system is working effectively.
- Includes: CCP monitoring review, calibration checks, sampling/testing, internal audits.
- Validation (initial scientific justification) is distinct from verification (ongoing confirmation).

### Principle 7 — Documentation and Records
- HACCP plan, hazard analysis, CCP determinations.
- Monitoring records, corrective action records, verification records.
- Records retained for an appropriate period (often ≥ product shelf life).

## Common Critical Limits Reference

| Process | Typical Critical Limit |
|---------|------------------------|
| Pasteurization (milk) | 72°C / 15 s (HTST) |
| Cooking (poultry) | 74°C internal |
| Cooling | 60°C→20°C within 2 h; 20°C→5°C within 4 h |
| Acidification | pH ≤ 4.6 |
| Water activity | aw ≤ 0.85 |
| Metal detection | Fe ≥ 1.5 mm, non-Fe ≥ 2.0 mm, SS ≥ 2.5 mm (typical) |

## Allergen Management (often a CCP or PRP)
- The major allergens vary by jurisdiction (e.g., US: 9 major; EU: 14 listed).
- Cross-contact control, cleaning validation, and label accuracy are key.
- "May contain" statements must be based on risk assessment, not used as a substitute for control.
